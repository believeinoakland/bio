Purpose: citing is one act, "this is why I think that": material or a question becomes part of what a case or question rests on. On a case (`project`) a citation is a `cites` edge in `references[]`; on a question (`inquiry`) a leg of `basis[]`, with the target in its references too. The module holds that act, its withdrawal and restoration on a case (sever, reinstate: status changes, never deletions), and the one rule of what may be cited now: nothing the group has retired. A question may also rest on another team's accepted finding at a named edition (R16) and take that leg back (R17). It judges nothing of a leg's grammar, grade or the basis graph: it composes legs, and `inquiry` judges them at the write.

Terms: the citing object is the bundle cited into, named by `project` whether a case or a question (legacy `focus`/`problem` read through `normalizeType` as a question). A selection is `retrieval`'s (its R19–R20), named by `handle`. An edge is a `references[]` entry `{rel: "cites", target, status, note, extent_capture?}`, status `proposed`, `confirmed` or `severed`. A leg and its role are `inquiry`'s (`BASIS_ROLES`: `supports`, `cuts_against`). An imported leg is a leg on an imported finding reference (`inquiry-grammar` R11: target `imported:<import>/<finding>`, positive `target_edition`, no grade, axis, source or part, not in `references[]`). A part is a leg's `extent_*` fields or `content_id`, in `content`'s extent grammar. Viewer and identity are control-plane stamps read through `membership`. Every refusal names a `reason`; one with a catalogue row carries `check` and `translation`; one about members names every offender (`offenders`, sorted) and never narrows the call.
