## tasks (own requirements)

### Public

Purpose: the obligation inbox: tasks routed from captures whose authority is undetermined, drained from capture's queue under the task grammar (C-19.1), listed to the members who may see their subjects, and forwarded or resolved by their assignee, anyone when unassigned, or an administrator.

**The obligation inbox: taskDrain, taskList, taskForward, taskResolve** (`op=taskdrain`, `tasks`, `taskforward`, `taskresolve`)

- **R1** `taskDrain({limit, actor, now})` takes up to `limit` (1-500, default 50) queued capture events in order. An event whose capture is filed in no bundle is kept with its attempts counted (`waiting`). One with a live task (open or forwarded) of its kind on that bundle adds a `folded` entry to the task's history. Otherwise a task is created, routed to an active owner of the bundle when it is a project, else to an active owner of the project the bundle is filed in (`record-core.bundleInfo().project`), else to an active owner of the first project (by id) with a live `cites` edge to it (`connections.edgeSevered`), else to the earliest active administrator (the first member of `activeAdmins` after the founder, `member-roster` R29; `group-admin`), else `unassigned`. An ungrammatical task (C-19.1) is dropped and reported under `refused`. An exhausted id space keeps the event, its `waiting` entry carrying `record-core.mintExhausted("TASK")`'s `code`, `check` and `detail` (its R62; N322). Answers `drained`, `created` (each route's basis), `folded`, `waiting`, `refused`, `remaining`, `limit`. A `scheduler` consumer runs it while events wait. The drain asks capture's queue only for events of kind `authority-undetermined` (`taskEvents({limit, kind: "authority-undetermined"})`, capture R45's optional `kind`), and its `remaining` and the consumer's wait count only that kind (`taskEventCount({kind: "authority-undetermined"})`), so an event of any other kind (`archive-unpack` among them) is never taken, routed, folded, refused or counted here. It reads capture's queue in pages, oldest first: an event kept `waiting` (capture filed in no bundle, or id space exhausted) does not use up `limit`; while fewer than `limit` events have been created, folded or refused and a page came back full (as many events as it asked for), it reads the next page, passing `after`, the `cursor` of the page's last event. No event is read twice in one drain; `waiting` names every waiting event read. The administrator fallback picks only an administrator `membership` R80 admits to the subject at sight (invited, joined, or the project discoverable), else `unassigned`. Once the instance is claimed the founder (`admin`) is an active member (`memberFacts` answers her facts) and leads `activeAdmins`, but is no routing target: the route skips her, and the earliest active administrator is the first after her; with none the task is `unassigned`. Test: claimed and unclaimed, with and without another administrator.
- **R18** (scheduler R15) The drain's wake is bounded. After a `task-drain` tick that drained nothing, the consumer's `wake` is the earliest instant any waiting event R1 reads is next due: an event tried `a` times (capture R45's `attempts`, `lastTry`) is due at `lastTry` + min(`TASK_DRAIN_BACKSTOP_MS` x 2^(a-1), `TASK_DRAIN_MAX_WAIT_MS`), so at the retry limit and beyond the gap is one hour and a timer is held for every queued event whatever its tries (R25); with no event waiting, no wake. Such an event is kept (R1), retried on any drain that runs for another reason; the drain is re-armed at R1's short delay when capture enqueues an event and when a promotion commits (`promotion.onCommitted`, its R45). A tick that drained something keeps the short delay. `TASK_DRAIN_BACKSTOP_MS` (60,000), `TASK_DRAIN_RETRY_LIMIT` (8) and `TASK_DRAIN_MAX_WAIT_MS` (3,600,000) are exported by name. The wake's reading (`#backoffWake`) pages with `after` as R1 does, so the earliest due instant is over every waiting event of R1's kind.
- **R2** `taskList({assignee, status, refersTo, limit, viewer})`: tasks on subjects the viewer may see, newest first, filtered as asked, at most `limit` (1-1,000, default 200), `truncated` measured one past the cap, per-status counts over the visible set plus the queued-event count.
- **R3** `taskForward({id, to, actor, now})`, refusals in this order: `NO_ACTOR`; `MACHINE_CANNOT_FORWARD` (C-32.10); `NO_SUCH_TASK`; `ALREADY_RESOLVED`; `TASK_NOT_YOURS` (C-76.1; its own code, apart from intent's C-111.15) unless the actor is the assignee, the task is `unassigned`, or the actor is an administrator; `NO_SUCH_MEMBER` (an active member), answered through `noSuchMember` (`member-roster` R49; C-96.47; K231: one code, one site) with `member` the `to` asked; `ALREADY_THEIRS`. Reassigns with role `member`, status `forwarded`, and appends `forwarded` with the actor to the history. `taskResolve({id, actor, now})`: `NO_ACTOR`; `MACHINE_CANNOT_RESOLVE` (C-32.11); `NO_SUCH_TASK`; an already resolved task answers `already: true` and writes nothing; `TASK_NOT_YOURS` as above; then status `resolved`, `resolved_at`, and `resolved` with the actor appended. With `items`, both run per item through `record-core.perItem` (its R50-R52). On a task of kind `check-requested` (R14), `taskForward` is refused `CHECK_NOT_FORWARDED`: a check request is addressed by expertise and sight, or to a member its requester names (R13), never reassigned (DEC-135); `taskResolve` by an addressee who has not taken it closes that addressee's own To do only, the request staying open to the others, and the taker's To do closes only by R15's record. An administrator's override also requires that the actor sees the subject (R80); one who does not is answered `NO_SUCH_TASK`, never `TASK_NOT_YOURS` naming the assignee.
