# Diagnosis R6 memory: why the object dies reading a large PDF (K4426; read-only; measured 2026-10-22, tranche/T51 tip dd8f7104ba)

## Verdict
**Root cause: certain for memory, probable for the exact kill.** The walker's file is not a scan. 0111 (digest 6e0b3dc5, 15,932,155 B, downloaded from govinfo and sha-checked) is BUDGET-2024-APP: a 1,354-page **text-layer** volume. Reading it needs more than the object's 128 MB once the plane's own idle heap is counted, and it takes about 22 s of CPU in one request with 30 s allowed. Both limits are met or crossed on one tick, so Cloudflare kills the isolate (`exceededResources`, about 37 s wall, eight times, K4561). The CPU half is probable, not certain: no live `last_ms`/`last_mem` was ever recorded (no row has run since the deploy).

## Evidence
**Code path.** `pending.mjs:152-155` `reading` -> `extraction/index.mjs:681` `readHeld` -> `:743` `ev.head` (no bytes) -> `:750` `this.read(..., budget: CAPTURE_TEXT_CAPTURE_BOUND, pieces: {cursor: null})` -> reading-pipeline `read` loads the whole file (`bytesOf`, ceiling `WHOLE_READ_MAX` 24 MiB, `:48`) -> pdfstructure walks all 1,354 pages -> all text units in memory -> `listPieceAnswer` (`reading-pipeline:2035-2041`) keeps the first piece (about 2 MB of 13.7 M chars) -> `writeReading` and the index. Held at once in one request: the 15.2 MB bytes, the parsed objects, the glyph and layout structures of every page, the whole `all` unit list (13.7 MB of text, doubled in UTF-16), then the SQLite and FTS writes.

**Every later piece repeats it.** `#continueText` (`extraction/index.mjs:785-790`) calls `read` with the cursor; for a PDF the cursor branch `reading-pipeline:1872` is skipped (that shortcut is only for plain text, `textRead`), so each of about 7 pieces re-parses all 1,354 pages (`:2035-2041` rebuilds `pieceUnitsOf(i2text)` and then slices). The text is read "whole, in the background" (K4571) at the price of 7 full parses of a 15 MB PDF.

**Measured, same file.**
- Node, uncapped, reading-pipeline `read` alone: 20-21 s, peak 258 MB above idle. CPU profile (`pdfstructure.mjs` `matMul` :1922, `run` :2360, `binaryString` :140, `tokenizeContent` :1423, GC 1.9 s): spread over page extraction, no single hotspot. Under a 100 MB heap cap the same `read` takes 29.4 s (GC thrash); at 70 MB it dies.
- Raw workerd (Durable Object with SQLite, `--max-old-space-size` as in `workerd-ceiling.probe.mjs`), `readHeld` tick only: **22-23 s per tick** at every cap that survives. With extraction and record-core only: survives at 96, dies at 80 (heap out of memory). With every plane module also loaded (`plane/register.mjs`, no Store, no traffic): survives at 144 and 160, **dies at 128, 112 and 96**. Add the 15 MB of bytes beside the heap and the isolate overhead, and the object's 128 MB is out of reach by about 30 MB.
- Through the assembled plane in node (the existing l77 harness, real file, cap = idle + 88 = 176 MB): peak above idle 99 MB (the gate assumes 88), **6 ticks, 234 s, the row still owed** (about 39 s a tick under cap). At a 128 MB total cap it dies.
- The plane's idle heap: 6.9 MB bare node, 73 MB after loading all modules, 87-103 MB with a Store built.
- A scanned-style PDF (100 image pages, 20 MB, JPEG noise): `read` takes 0.13 s, peak 28 MB with its bytes, no units; the OCR hand-off sends only the sha (`askMemberPerPage`, `reading-pipeline:346-350`), never bytes. Scans are not the memory problem; their problem was the dead alarm.
- The 40 MB `keep`: `putFromParts` (`record-core/index.mjs:1904-1935`) reads one 8 MiB part at a time, hashes in JS (40 MB in 0.4 s, no growth), uploads each part; the stand-in ran it in 0.6 s. Then `readNow` is called in the same tick but a 40 MB capture is over `WHOLE_READ_MAX` and is stated from its size (`extraction:737`), nothing loaded. **Probably safe** (design holds one part; not measured on real R2).

## Why the tests missed it
1. `plane/l77-size.test.mjs` gates at `idle + 88 MB`: it treats the 128 MB as free beyond the plane's own idle (73-103 MB). The real budget is 128 total. The 15.9 MB case in that file is "printed, no cap" and is a synthetic of small objects, not the 1,354-page volume.
2. `reading-pipeline/memory.test.mjs` does read the real 15.2 MiB volume, but gates "work under 64 MB above its bytes" in a child with idle + 64, again outside the plane's heap, and reads one piece only.
3. `extraction/c5b-memory.test.mjs` caps at 88 MB old space on a **synthetic** 1,300-page, 21 MB file whose pages are plain Type1 text (cheap); extraction alone, no plane modules. Real govinfo pages cost more.
4. Miniflare and node enforce no CPU limit (the l77 header says so); no test asserts a time per tick; every fixture reads one tick, none the seven pieces.
5. Nothing ran the real 0111 on workerd with the plane's modules under a 128 MB flag.

## Fix (which module, what)
A reading must fit both limits as a bounded step, not as one parse of the whole file.
1. **pdf-reader (`pdfstructure.mjs`) with reading-pipeline `read`: read a PDF by page ranges.** Parse the cross-reference once (cheap, about 1 s), then extract N pages per tick (cursor = page number), emit that range's units, drop the page structures. Each tick holds the bytes and one range, about 1/30 of the work; a tick costs about 1 s of CPU and 10 MB, and the total is one parse, not 7. This replaces `listPieceAnswer(pieceUnitsOf(i2text))` for PDFs (`reading-pipeline:2035`) and removes the `#continueText` re-parse. Ownership: pdf-reader (page-range entry) and reading-pipeline (cursor shape "p"); extraction's `#continueText` needs no change beyond passing the cursor.
2. **Interim guard if (1) cannot enter this round: a measured ceiling by pages, not bytes** (the open point in `cause-reading-4.md`): refuse as `final` with the page count, before text extraction, any PDF over about 600 pages. It states the reading honestly; it does not read 0111.
3. **Reading-pipeline `bytesOf`:** read the whole only after `head` and the used-page rule; unchanged otherwise.
4. Do not rely on the retry: `reapInterrupted` (`pending.mjs:141-148`) ends the row stated after three kills, so 0111 would end "the reading did not finish; the object stopped" in 3 ticks. That is the live outcome the walk will show unless (1) or (2) enters.

## Failing-first test on the walker's case
`bio-plane/test/m/plane/l77-size.test.mjs` (or a new file beside it): the real 15.2 MiB BUDGET-2024-APP volume (the cache and sha pin in `memory.test.mjs`), promoted as the capture screen does, `reading` owed, alarm fired until the ledger is empty. Run the child with `--max-old-space-size = 128 - 15` (no "idle +" term), and per tick assert wall time under 10 s and the sum of ticks under one parse's time x 1.5. Today it fails: dies at 128 (verified in workerd above) and runs 6+ ticks at about 22-39 s each. Add the 40 MB five-part `keep` in the same file (peak array buffers under 24 MB).
