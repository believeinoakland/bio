## 1. Rules every job in this round carries

- **A test at the module's interface, through the real path** (K3810, K4162). A provider's test calls its op as the door calls it (the module's `*Ops` map with `url` and `body`, or the method a door calls) over a real fixture of the provider's own neighbours. A screen's test presses the act in `interface-shell`'s harness and asserts the request sent; because that harness scripts the plane (`screens-publish/fixtures.mjs` answers `publish: ok(...)` whatever it is sent, which is how C1 and C60 got through green), the screen job's guard against a lie is the `OP_FIELDS` check of section 4. No test copies a sentence: keys, or the word table (K3810).
- **One owner per fix** (K4164): a cause needing edits across layers is fixed by moving the ownership; where two modules are truly needed the provider is named first and merges first.
- **A requirement sentence before the code** (README conventions 2, 4, 5): written in the file's style, an outcome and not an implementation, an id first on its line; a sentence that only a caller can keep goes in the caller's file.
- **Fewer tiny-change fan-outs** (K3268): a test never hand-lists another module's ops, rows or words; a job re-points a used service only where its own entry touches it.
- **Models**: haiku for a one-line send or render fix with its test, a link or door of a few lines, or a declaration with its honesty test; sonnet for anything with a new control, a new read, two modules, or a contract change.

### 2a. The NEW requirement sentences

Written in the file's style (an id, an outcome, no implementation); the numbers are proposals.

- **`case-authoring` R78** (amends R9; `review` R4) When `draft` names a draft that the caller may read and that belongs to the project, `publish`, `publishpreflight` and `publishtensions` take, for each field of the draft's `params` the call does not itself send, the draft's value; a field the call sends is used as sent, and nothing is written to the draft. A `draft` not held, or of another project, leaves the call as sent and R9 refuses it as before. So a pre-flight and a publication over the same draft judge the same words the review copy's `missing` judged (`review` R13).
- **`case-authoring` R79 and the same sentence in each declaring module**: *states, as `OP_FIELDS`, for each op it serves that a screen sends, the fields the op accepts and the fields it requires (an entry of `requires` may be a list of alternatives); a test holds the declaration true by calling the op.* (Section 4.)

## 4. Fragility: the smallest durable design (goal 1)

### The structural cause, as the code shows it

Five causes in this round (C60, C1, C34, C35, C71) and four more (C25, C28, C54, C66) share one cause: **a screen act and the op it calls agree on a request's fields only by hand, and nothing ever compares them.** The evidence is on both sides of the line:

- *Screen side.* An act is `{ op, send(view, el, cs) → {body, query} }` (`screens-publish/ceremony.mjs`:300; the shell's act runner `interface-shell/browser.mjs`:1818 sends what `send` returns, adding only the reason field). Its tests run through `interface-shell/test/m/.../harness.mjs`, whose `fetch` answers from a **scripted** plane (`screens-publish/fixtures.mjs`: `publish: ok({...})`), so a screen test cannot fail because the real op wants something else. `queue-states.test.mjs`:203 pins the wrong spelling (`definition_version`) and is green.
- *Plane side.* A module's ops map reads its fields by hand from `url` and `body` (`caseAuthoringOps`, `case-authoring/index.mjs`:2010; `provenanceOps`; about 40 ops in `control-plane/owner-ops.mjs` through `pick(b, keys)`). The one list of what an op accepts is its handler's code; `op-declarations` declares classes, stamps and weights, never the fields.
- *Already half-built.* `case-authoring` exports `AUTHORED_FIELDS` and `ACT_FIELDS` and refuses any other field `CASE_FIELD_NOT_ALLOWED`; `review` keeps a second list (`REVIEW_DRAFT_FIELDS`) that differs from it by `whatChanged`, `account` and `statementCites` (that is C3's account loss); `queue` already answers `requires: ["definitionVersion"]` in its items. The facts exist; nothing joins them.

### The design

1. **Declare at the owner.** Each module that serves an op a screen sends exports, beside its `*Ops`, a frozen `OP_FIELDS` keyed by the op's door name (the name the screens and the registry use; `publish`, not `publishcase`):
   `{ publish: { accepts: [...], requires: ["project", ["draft", "targets"], ["draft", "statement"], ...] } }`. A `requires` entry may be a list of alternatives (any one satisfies it); that is how C1's decision is written down: the call must name the project and either the draft or the words. `accepts` is built from the module's own exports where they exist (`case-authoring`: `AUTHORED_FIELDS` plus `ACT_FIELDS` minus the stamps), so it cannot drift from what the handler refuses. Location (query or body) is not declared; stamps (`viewer`, `author`, `by`, `identity`, `proposer`) and the shell's `reason` field are not listed by the screens (the declaration lists `reason` only for an op that reads one).
2. **One check, in `interface-shell`'s harness.** Every `screens-*` test already routes every request through `boot()`'s `fetch` (`harness.mjs`:93). Add there: look up `OP_FIELDS[op]`; if present, compare the sent `query ∪ body` keys with `accepts` and `requires`; a mismatch throws a `FieldMismatch` naming the screen's op and the field, which fails the test that pressed the act (the page shows "no answer" and the assertion fails). If absent, the request is counted undeclared. The table is assembled once, with no hand list: read `build/modules.json`, take the files of each module's `paths` whose text contains `export const OP_FIELDS`, import them, merge, and fail on a duplicate op. A new `declared.test.mjs` in `interface-shell` (a) plants a wrong field and a missing required field and asserts the guard fires (the control), and (b) enumerates every act of every screen module and prints `declared N of M ops` (today M is about 300 ops across 426 acts; N is 0).
3. **Each declaring module holds its declaration true** with a short test of its own, written with the `test-support` helper (job 1): call the op through its ops map with only the `requires` satisfied (no "missing field" or "unknown field" refusal), then without each required entry (refused, naming the field), then with a field outside `accepts` (dropped or refused as the op does). That is the only thing keeping the declaration honest; it never imports another module's declaration (K3268).
4. **What it catches, concretely.** C60: `queue` declares `proposedispose` accepting `definitionVersion`; the screen's `definition_version` is an unknown field at the first press. C1 before the fix: `publish` sent without `draft`, `statement` or `scope` fails `requires` in the first `screens-publish` ceremony test. C34: `narrow` without `version` and `ord`. C35: `hypothesishold` without `about`. C71: `filingapprove` refused without `resolutions`. The walk and the live release then find only what the harness cannot, not these.

### Modules touched, increments and cost

- **Touched in this round:** `test-support` (one helper), `interface-shell` (the harness check and one test), and the owners of the ops in the causes above, each adding a declaration only because its job touches it or its op is the subject of a cause: `case-authoring`, `review`, `member-roster`, `connections` (with their jobs), and `progressions`, `hypotheses`, `basis-versions`, `filings`, `monitoring`, `queue` (declaration-only haiku jobs 8, 9, 10, 14, 15, 17). Not touched: `op-declarations`, `admission`, `control-plane`, `plane`, any screen module. No sweep: about 290 of the 300 sent ops stay undeclared and the check reports so.
- **Incremental.** Yes. An op with no declaration is skipped and counted; a module declares more ops the next time its own entry touches it (K3268's "re-point when next touched"). The count is the metric the census can print before each release (K4164: "sent fields checked against what each op requires").
- **Cost.** Harness: about 40 lines; `declared.test.mjs`: about 80; helper: about 40; per op: 6 to 15 lines of declaration and 10 to 20 of its honesty test. The scan imports only files that contain the export (a text search first), so the suite's time is not touched by modules that have not declared. Per-request cost is one lookup and a loop over the fields.
- **What it does not do (stated so it is not mistaken for more).** (a) It cannot prove a handler really reads a declared field; the honesty test and the plane tests do. (b) `accepts` ignores placement (query versus body), so a field sent in the query that the arm reads from the body is caught only by the module's honesty test through the real arm. (c) A screen that builds its body from a response (the shell's `reason`, the plane's own `requires`) is checked on the final request, which is what is wanted.
- **The stronger form, later and not proposed now.** Make the declaration drive extraction (the op arm reads `pick(b, OP_FIELDS.x.accepts)`, as `owner-ops.mjs` does for its ops), so that a screen field outside the declaration is dropped by construction. Adopt it per arm when the arm is next touched.

### Next fragility seen, not in this round

Exposing one op today costs four touch points: the owner's arm, `op-grades` (a rung, `op-grades/t41.mjs`, `t51.mjs`), `op-declarations` (a share, `t51.mjs`, and its seven tranche test files, commit `8850f69b2a` touched 14 files for nine ops), and `control-plane/owner-ops.mjs` or `plane/store.mjs` for the arm. C20, C23 and C65 pay that now (jobs 5, 6, 11, 16, 18, 27, 28). That is the central hand list K3268 wants assembled from each owner's own declaration; the `OP_FIELDS` convention is its seed (an owner could also declare class, weight and stamps there). Recommended as the next round's subject, after this round's causes are closed.
