## 1. What it must achieve
A team, a project or a member can choose the AI behind Civicsmith's assistant from the providers, plans and types they already have or can get, through a connection Civicsmith explains or walks them through; every claim Civicsmith makes about an AI carries its evidence; each team decides how much privacy its work needs; and the assistant keeps working, fully and economically, whichever AI is chosen.

## 2. Providers, plans and types [AIR1, AIR12, AIR20, AIR23, AIR34, AIR41]
1. Any legitimate provider can be supported: proprietary or open-weight; cloud, hosted or local; direct provider, aggregator (OpenRouter), gateway (Cloudflare AI Gateway), built-in (Cloudflare Workers AI), or a custom OpenAI-compatible endpoint.
2. **Anthropic is a provider like any other** [Bob] (supersedes K3717's exclusion for this design).
3. Adding a provider needs one adapter and one catalogue entry; nothing outside them names a provider. Adding a model at a supported host needs only a catalogue entry with its evidence. One generic adapter serves the OpenAI-compatible family through per-host profiles; bespoke adapters only where an API genuinely differs.
4. **API accounts and subscriptions are both supported** [Bob]: the support is broad because teams and members want to use provider relationships they already have. A plan entry carries the provider's stated restrictions (who may use it, for what, where its runtime and token may live), each with quote, page and date, or OPEN; an unconfirmed point restricts nothing (K3540).
5. **Personal subscriptions** [Bob, AIR41]: a member decides whether to use their own personal subscription for the work they run. It is personal because the member runs it; its results are that member's work and may be shared. It is never a project's or team's account, and no member other than its owner can run the assistant on it, including as a failover account, whatever opt-in is given. The product shows the provider's stated restrictions at connection (and again when they change) and records the member's choice; the only rules it enforces are that level rule and the privacy and origin checks in force where the work is done (§4), which bind a personal subscription like any account.
6. **Sequencing** [Bob]: OpenRouter support (its sign-in flow and host pinning) is built first, so an OpenRouter account can be opened through the product's own path and the open OpenRouter tests run (`TESTS.md` L2).

## 3. Evidence and the catalogue [AIR11, AIR13, AIR14, AIR17, AIR18, AIR24, AIR33, AIR36, AIR38, AIR39]
1. Every statement about an AI or plan carries a **grade** (Reproduced / Reported / Stated), its source and date; OPEN restricts nothing.
2. **The offered list is closed** [Bob]: a model can be selected only if it is in Civicsmith's catalogue; an aggregator's full list is never exposed. Each entry carries its answers to the seven checks (§4), its Gate 1 verdicts per part of the assistant, and its characterization.
3. An entry is a **(model, host) pair**, and the host is pinned: through an aggregator each request is locked to the vetted host with no fallback; a response from another host is refused and recorded.
4. **Gate 1 · capability** (`SUITE-REQUIREMENTS.md`): a Civicsmith-owned, versioned suite scored by code, extending the existing test bar (D14; `run-rules` R19, R29; `ai-runs` R75) to a verdict per (entry, part): passes / passes with limits / not suitable. A part the chosen AI has not passed is refused by name for accounts using it. It includes "civic work not refused" for every part that reads records [Bob]; a person's reasonableness reading is a separate labelled note that never changes a verdict [Bob]. Pass marks are Bob's after the first measurements [Bob]. Gate 1 runs on any entry whatever its privacy answers, because its content is synthetic. Called the "Gate 1 suite", never "conformance" (an existing module).
5. **Characterization** [Bob]: session start cost and time; turn latency; consistency; plan limits; stated and effective context window; all-in cost of standard tasks; uptime evidence with its period; privacy answers from §4.
6. **Catalogue updates are available at all times**, independent of releases [Bob]: a signed, versioned data channel every copy pulls, with last-good copy, rollback, schema version and a "needs a newer release" state. The nearest existing mechanism is the daily release-notice check (`instance-setup` R88–R90, `signatures` R45–R48). Bob decides what enters it.
7. **Drift and retirement:** exact model versions; listed → watch → lapsed → retired; canaries on Civicsmith's accounts, deprecation dates, served-model mismatch, divergence of team results, opt-in content-free health reports [Bob]; signed advisories; a lapsed entry refuses new runs but never alters results already produced.
8. Terms bearing on use (political, legal and high-risk limits, indemnities, no SLA) are recorded per plan with quote, section, version and effective date, including future dates, and shown at connection.
9. **Results library** [Bob, AIR36]: every team sees, per AI, the parts it passed and its limits, measured speed and cost of standard jobs, who tested it (Civicsmith on its own account, or a team not yet repeated by Civicsmith) and the date. Word-for-word test records, and the name of a team that sent results unless it agrees, stay private. Bob's analogy is filing templates; those are each team's own library today, so the library borrows their record rules and the delivery channel of §3.6.

## 4. Privacy and origin checks: each team decides [AIR42, AIR43]
1. **Seven checks**, each answered for every entry (met / not met / OPEN, with quote, page and date): (P1) no training on content; (P2) no human reading of content by default; (P3) a stated retention period, zero-retention used where offered; (P4) where it runs and under which country's law; (P5) no hidden onward passing (host pinned); (P6) works with the team's keep-away limits and the recipient notice; (P7) not developed by a Chinese organisation (judged by developer) [Bob]. Shown, never required: whether the provider's terms permit this use; breach-notification commitments.
2. **All seven are required by default.** The team's **administrators** decide which stay required for the team; those are locked for every project and member. A check the administrators untick may be ticked or unticked by a project owner or a member [Bob]. Some teams work anonymously, some in a potentially more public view: that is the team's decision.
3. A check a **project owner** ticks binds all work done **directly in that project**, whoever does it and on whichever account; it does not reach work in the record or other areas that are not project-specific [Bob]. Which acts are "directly in the project" follows the project scope the account cascade already uses (BOB's mapping).
4. An entry failing or OPEN on a check in force is not offered for that work. Every change to a check is recorded (who, when, which check, at which level). Keep-away limits still bind every account (K2352): unticking P6 never lifts a limit.
5. **Free tiers** that read or train on content are usable only after a plain warning is shown and accepted, the acceptance recorded [Bob].

## 5. Testing by Civicsmith and by outside teams [AIR15, AIR16]
1. Civicsmith's own tests run on accounts it controls and yield Reproduced results; sample accounts are Bob's to obtain.
2. Outside teams test a plan not in the catalogue only through a **locked, digest-pinned Civicsmith container** [Bob] that holds the key in memory for one run, limits egress to the provider under test and Civicsmith's issuer, uses synthetic items whose answers stay with Civicsmith, and seals the results bundle inside itself (manifest shown to the team first). Team results are Reported until reproduced.
3. The container runs in the team's own account or machine (the machine route serves local models); running it in Civicsmith's account for outside teams is deferred [Bob].
4. A team's own unlisted or local endpoint reaches the assistant only through the test flow until a release lists it [Bob].
5. The test cost is estimated and shown before a run, with a spending ceiling; the suite is tiered (connect, screen, full, characterize).

## 6. Connection and credentials [AIR2, AIR3, AIR9, AIR30–AIR32, AIR37]
1. **Guided connection always** [Bob]: the product describes or walks the person through every step; a plan without guidance is not offered.
2. **How teams connect** [Bob]: a pasted key, the provider's own sign-in, or the AI built into the team's own Cloudflare account; built-in is the default for a team with no outside AI account.
3. **Plan scope is data and enforced:** a one-person plan, including every personal subscription, cannot be a team's or project's account. The cascade (project, else member, else team) is unchanged.
4. **How a credential is held** [Bob: references allowed]: (a) **gateway-held**: the provider API key is stored by Cloudflare AI Gateway in the team's own Cloudflare account and added to requests by the gateway; Civicsmith's code and Civicsmith the company never hold it (proven by live test L1, `TESTS.md`); (b) sealed by the team's copy, as today; (c) a reference to a password or secrets manager, resolved at call time (it keeps the key out of Civicsmith's records, not out of its memory); (d) a provider-issued token from a documented sign-in flow. Each plan states which means it allows.
5. **Gateway-held keys** (from L1): the proposed default for team and project API keys. **A key reaches the gateway either through Civicsmith's own connection screen, which passes it straight to Cloudflare's store and keeps nothing (the default), or by an administrator in the Cloudflare dashboard, where it never touches Civicsmith** [Bob: both, screen as default]. Each account holding an API key has its own gateway (through the Worker binding one gateway cannot choose among keys; Secrets Store's beta cap is 100 secrets per account). On every call the product verifies from the gateway's log that the account's own stored key served it and that Cloudflare's own credits did not (a missing key on Unified Billing endpoints falls through to Cloudflare's credits), and refuses or flags otherwise. The gateway's log holds full prompts and answers in the team's own account and falls under the team's privacy settings. Sign-in tokens are not covered and use (b) to (d).
6. Signup: guided links and, where documented, a provider's connect flow; the product never signs anyone up, never handles payment details, never states an unconfirmed price; special Civicsmith pricing only once an arrangement exists.

## 7. Using the assistant with the chosen AI [AIR5–AIR8, AIR25–AIR28, AIR40]
1. **Full function:** every role runs through the same harness, control table, checks and fences for every AI; the canon's boundary rules hold unchanged.
2. **Token efficiency:** adapters expose each provider's savings (caching, batch, trimmed context); usage in a provider-neutral shape, null never zero where unstated; the gateway's per-call cost fills gaps.
3. **Sessions:** turns kept in one session where efficient; the plane's run record is the source of truth, so a run resumes on another AI or later; provider-held state only as an optimisation, never where zero-retention or keep-away limits forbid.
4. **Model and effort** offered exactly as each provider offers them, per model.
5. **Cost visibility:** per account, owner, member and run: spend against limit, estimate before, actual after, the added cost of a switch; "not measurable in money" for plans reporting none; alerts at owner-set thresholds.
6. **Budgets and ordered accounts** [Bob: failover allowed, an exception to K2352]: Civicsmith-side limits plus provider-side limits recognised as limits reached. Who may set an ordered list [Bob]: administrators for team accounts, project owners for project accounts, members for their own; another owner's account appears only with that owner's standing opt-in (off by default); never a personal subscription. Work moves down the list only at a step boundary, only on a limit, outage, lapsed model or capability gap, **never for a use its owner switched off** [Bob], and only to an entry meeting every check in force for that work. Every switch is recorded and shown, the member can decline, each result keeps the label of the AI and account that produced it, and an exhausted list refuses in plain words naming who can act.
7. **Disclosure per provider and host** [Bob]: before material first goes to a recipient not yet disclosed, including after a switch, the notice is shown and the step waits; a non-interactive run waits rather than proceeding.
8. **Outages:** classified errors with defined handling (bounded retries honouring retry delays, circuit breaker, health state, failover per §7.6); interactive asks fail fast with plain choices; non-interactive runs pause and resume; partial results survive; retries never duplicate writes; never a silent degrade.
9. **Streaming** [Bob]: where an AI cannot stream, turn-level progress is acceptable and the adapter says so.
10. **High-risk civic output:** where a provider's policy requires qualified human review and AI disclosure (legal, public-benefit, adjudication advice), the product states what is asked of the team once at connection; Civicsmith does not itself advise on a person's legal matter.

## 8. Configuration by leaders and members [AIR10]
Administrators choose which AIs, models and plans the team's members may use and which checks are locked; a project owner may narrow for work in the project; a member chooses among what is allowed and adds their own where the team permits; narrowing only; keep-away limits bind every AI; every change recorded with who and when.
