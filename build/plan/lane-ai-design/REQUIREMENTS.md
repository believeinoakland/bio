# AI-DESIGN requirements for multi-AI support

**Status.** Drafted 2026-10-17 by AI-DESIGN #1 from the direction given in the lane session (the words below, kept verbatim). These are the requirements the design (Q3 boundary, Q5 UX) must meet and Q4's tests must prove. They are not rulings: Bob rules requirements (P17), so BOB takes this to him with the questions in §4 and records his answer as K rulings. Until then they are the lane's working bar.

## 1 · The direction, verbatim
> We need to be specific about the requirements that need to be met.
> The general AI provider support must be general enough and the interface abstracted sufficiently to...
> - enable any AI provider to be used
> - enable the user to see, understand, compare pricing of, and choose the pricing plans available as allowed by the providers terms of service for use by the Civicsmith workflow - understanding that some plans may fit for multi-user scenarios (team, and possibly projects) while others might only work for single-user cases (per member plans)
> - enable the UX of choosing and using their chosen AI provider to be optimally presented and following best practices for such experiences.
> - support the complete functionality required of an assisted Civicsmith experience with all the capabilities/demands of the workflow
> - support the optimal token efficiency of while using the AI
> - whenever possible, appropriate, and efficient, allow a series of turns within a single AI session, preserving session context across turns
> - enable the selection of model and effort levels that are offered by each AI provider
> - whenever reasonably possible, support the ability for the selection and signup of AI provider accounts, including special pricing plans that may be offered to Civicsmith teams and members.
> Include an optimal UX for team leaders and individual users to configure which AIs to list as options that can be used in the Civicsmith workflow.

## 2 · The requirements, made specific
Each has: what must hold, how it is checked (a test or an inspection), and what today's design does about it (from `NOTES.md` §Q1).

| **AIR7** | **Multi-turn sessions that keep context** where the provider supports it and it is efficient: the adapter exposes whether a provider offers server-held conversation state or only a resent history, and the harness uses whichever is cheaper while keeping the plane's own run record as the source of truth (a run must resume on another AI or another day without the provider's state). | Test: a run resumed after a break gives the same record; a session-capable provider is shown to cost less than resending where claimed. | Runs resume from the run log (neutral); provider session only via Claude Code. |
| **AIR8** | **Model and effort selection** is offered exactly as each provider offers it: the catalogue lists the provider's models, each with its stated context, output limit, price, capabilities and the effort/reasoning controls that model has (none where none). | **AIR10** | **Configuring the list of offered AIs.** A team leader sets which AIs (and models, plans) the team's members may use; a project owner may narrow; a member may choose among those allowed and may add their own where the team permits. **Constraints that carry over unchanged** (from the canon and rulings): the boundary rules of the canon §3 hold for every AI (machine proposes; labelled; no machine credential attests; endpoint surface is the fence); a limit reached refuses rather than moving on (K2352); disclosure before first paid use names the provider (K2352, D311 generalised); credentials sealed and never shown; progress shown live and answers checked (K3602); "team" in words people read (K3472); no Anthropic in the supported list (K3717).

## 3 · What the existing research already answers, and the gaps these requirements open
Answered in part by `NOTES.md` §Q2: provider connection ways, models and contexts, prices, limits, data terms, scope-of-use clauses (resale/transfer).
**Gaps now needed (new research, Q2b, workers sonnet/medium, same rules as Q2):**
1. **Plans per provider** (AIR2): every plan or tier a provider offers that an application could use, with price, included usage, limits and **the plan's terms on who may use it and through what** (consumer subscriptions: allowed through an app or not; team/business/enterprise plans: seats, sharing; API tiers; free tiers; non-profit, startup, education or civic discounts and partner programs, AIR9). Provider pages only; consumer-subscription routes (ChatGPT, Gemini app, Mistral Le Chat, Cohere, Grok) are expected to be mostly OPEN or barred and must be confirmed.
2. **Session and caching facilities** (AIR6, AIR7): per provider, whether conversation state is held server-side (e.g. stored responses/conversation objects), what caching exists (automatic/explicit, minimum size, discount, lifetime), batch discounts, and what each costs.
3. **Model-by-model controls** (AIR8): effort/reasoning parameters and their allowed values per model, max output, vision, tools, schema, with the model lists per provider kept current at build time.
4. **Signup and partner programs** (AIR9): documented signup flows, referral/partner/affiliate or "app developer" programs, and any rule on an app linking to signup.
5. **The two blocked pages and the Cloudflare per-model gaps** from A2 (a person must read the blocked pages).

## 4 · Questions this raises for Bob (via BOB), with the lane's recommendation
1. **Are AIR1–AIR10 the requirements?** Recommended: yes, ruled as the bar for the multi-AI assistant, with AIR2's "OPEN restricts nothing" (K3540) and AIR9's "never signs up on their behalf, never touches payment" as written.
2. **Single-user plans (AIR2, AIR3):** may a member use their own personal subscription for their own asks where the provider's terms allow it, while team and project accounts must use plans whose terms allow multi-user use? Recommended: yes; that is K1755's shape (member's own subscription serves only that member) generalised, and the scope rule makes it safe.
3. **Weaker AIs (AIR5):** may a team offer an AI that serves only some roles (e.g. ask and draft, not investigate)? Recommended: yes, the product states what each AI cannot do and refuses by name.
4. **Who maintains the catalogue (AIR2, AIR8):** Civicsmith releases carry a vetted provider catalogue with dates; stale entries are shown as "last confirmed <date>". Recommended: yes, updated by release, never edited live by the AI.
