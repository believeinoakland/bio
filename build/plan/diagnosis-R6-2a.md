## What would still stop a row (pacing, caps, accounting)
1. **Same-instant stall (new, latent).** `scheduler/index.mjs` `#holding` (lines 477-505) holds a consumer whose wake is unchanged and in the past after its tick. Three overdue heavy rows with the **same `due_at`**: the first runs, the other two never do, firing after firing; the cron's `watch()` does not release it (`why:null`, because `last_run` is fresh). `ttie.mjs` SAMEMS=1 shows it; with distinct instants all three end. The known registration paths (R89 repair, separate requests) give distinct milliseconds (`trepair.mjs`: 4 of 4 distinct), so it did not cause the walker's rows, but a batch registered in one millisecond would stall until the next `arm()`. Owner: **scheduler** (release the hold when the row that set it ended) or **plane** (`wake` strictly moving).
2. **Order.** Rows run oldest `due_at` first, one heavy per firing: a new upload waits behind every older heavy row; offline that is 0-7 firings of milliseconds, live it is the sum of the older rows' times (0111: up to 3 kills).
3. **No attempts cap** (backoff 15 s to 1 h, never given up); the only ends are done, a stated `final`, or three interruptions (`pending.mjs:51,141-146`); `interrupted` resets when a piece moves a cursor.
4. **Memory class** is not separable in this harness; every size-dependent live-only item above rides on G26.
