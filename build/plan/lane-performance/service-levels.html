How far a team's copy can grow on Cloudflare

For Bob · from the PERFORMANCE lane (PERFORMANCE #2), 2026-10-23 · measured on biosmoke8 (release 0.86.0) and Cloudflare's own pages read today · record: build/plan/lane-performance/HANDOFF.md P17

Short answer to your first question: yes. Workers Paid ($5 a month) is the plan every copy needs today (your ruling D5, K3060), and the installer refuses a Free account.

What I found.
  • A team's copy is limited first by Civicsmith's own design, not by the Cloudflare plan.
  • Today a team's whole record lives in one Durable Object: one "room" on Cloudflare with one worker inside it and a 10 GB shelf.
  • No Cloudflare plan, Enterprise included, makes that room faster or its shelf bigger.
  • What the higher plans buy: uptime guarantees, support, larger uploads, control over where data is kept, and higher limits on other services.
So a service level has two parts: how Civicsmith stores the team's record, and which Cloudflare plan the team pays for. The first decides how big and how responsive a copy can be. The second adds guarantees.

Does upgrading make it faster?

Mostly no. No Cloudflare plan offers faster processors for Workers or Durable Objects; every plan runs on the same machines. Enterprise differs in billing, limits and support, not in speed. Today's response times come from three things in Civicsmith's own design. All three are fixed by changes that cost nothing on any plan.

Found today: the test team's room is in Frankfurt.
  • Cloudflare creates a team's room near whoever first asks for it, and it never moves. Over the past week, Cloudflare's analytics show 466,628 calls to the test team's room, all in Frankfurt, Germany.
  • Each call from the Worker serving a member crosses the Atlantic. Measured from the US east coast, a call costs 110–135 ms; from California it is likely more.
  • A screen makes 6–15 such calls one after another, so 1–3 s of a screen's time is travel, not work.

2. What it costs

Money is not what limits a small or medium team. On Workers Paid, the test copy's busiest day stays inside the $5 allowance for requests, CPU, object time, rows read and storage. The first charge past the $5 is rows written: 50 million a month are included, then $1 per million. Writing is heaviest when large documents are read in. Sustained, the test copy's busiest day would add about $20 a month. Stored files cost $0.015 per GB a month, so 50 GB is $0.75, with no charge for sending them out. Browsers and the file scanner each cost a few dollars a month at Civicsmith's own caps.

3. Three service levels

Each level names the product's design stage and the Cloudflare plan it needs. Team sizes are my estimates from today's measurements. They should be confirmed by a load test on a scratch copy before any figure is shown to a team.

Level 1 · Team

available now (alpha)
  • Design: one room per team (today's).
  • Cloudflare: Workers Paid; any domain.
  • Size: up to about 50 GB of captured files (8,000–12,000 documents).
  • Responsiveness: about 15–25 members working at once today. Roughly 2–4 times that after the fixes already proposed (fewer calls per screen, cheaper reads), which change nothing a member sees.
  • Files: uploads to 100 MB.
  • Cost: $5 to about $25 a month.

Level 2 · Organization

needs the beta's split record (N1271)
  • Design: record split across rooms (per project, with bulk text in file storage), so projects no longer wait on each other.
  • Cloudflare: Workers Paid; Business zone optional, for the uptime guarantee and 200 MB uploads.
  • Size: about 10 GB of database per project; hundreds of GB in total.
  • Responsiveness: each project gets its own line, so the ceiling grows with the number of active projects.
  • Cost: about $10–60 a month, plus $200–250 with Business.

Level 3 · Coalition / institution

beta and later
  • Design: Level 2, with very busy tables split further and reads cached.
  • Cloudflare: Enterprise contract.
  • Adds: uploads to 5 GB; data kept in chosen regions (for teams with legal or funder requirements); higher limits on request; 24x7 support; strongest uptime credits.
  • Cost: by quote from Cloudflare (not published).

A team moves up by changing its own Cloudflare plan (its own act, N1265). The move from Level 1 to Level 2 also needs the record moved into the split design, which must be built as an upgrade step the copy runs on its own data.

4. Your decision

Decided by Bob, 2026-10-23: "A for both questions". Service levels are defined by what a copy can do (A), and a team's room is created near the team, chosen at install (a). BOB records the ruling and plans the work.

Question: how should Civicsmith define the service levels a team can be at?

  1. A. Three levels defined by what the copy can do (record size, members working at once, upload size), each naming the design stage and the Cloudflare plan it needs, as in section 3. Recommended.
    • It tells a team the truth: buying Enterprise does not by itself make a one-room copy faster.
    • It fits your K4635 (one room now, split for the beta), so N1271's capacity study measures against these levels.
    • The figures are confirmed by measurement before a team sees them.
  2. A second question: where a team's room is created. Today it is wherever the first request happens to come from, which put the test team in Frankfurt.
    • (a) Near the team, chosen at install from the founder's location and shown on the install screen; a team with a legal requirement can pick a region. Recommended.
    • (b) One region for all copies (for example western North America).
    • (c) As today: wherever the first request lands.
    A room cannot move once created, so this also decides whether the test copy is re-created near you. It is a development copy you have said will be wiped.
  3. B. Levels named after Cloudflare's plans (Paid / Business / Enterprise). Simpler to say, but it promises what the plans cannot deliver: a team that upgrades without the split record gets guarantees and support, not speed or space.
  4. C. One level for the alpha; define levels only after N1271's capacity study. No commitment now, but teams and the installer have nothing to plan around, and the study has no targets.

5. What happens without asking you (technical; BOB's)

  • Cheaper calls (raises Level 1). Each read makes 2–5 calls to the room, and some reads scan thousands of rows. Folding the checks into one call and caching counts are already proposed (P11, G80).
  • Start-up margin (P16). About a quarter of the 1 s start-up is four large data tables compiled as code. Packaging them as data instead is a contained change.
  • Placing the room near the team. Cloudflare lets a room be placed in a region (for example western North America) when it is first created; it cannot move afterwards. This is free on every plan. The lane will measure the gain once BOB allows a throwaway test (P15). Where a team's records live may later be yours to rule, if teams need a choice.
  • Confirming the team-size figures. A load test on a scratch copy (never the shared test copy) replaces my estimates with measurements before any number reaches a team.

Method and caveats: the copy's figures cover 2026-10-22 and 2026-10-23, with a few walkers active. The 15–25 figure assumes about 2 screens a minute per working member, 20–40 room calls per screen, and the measured 30 ms per call; queues lengthen well before 100% use. The 50 GB figure assumes today's ratio of database to file bytes (19%).
