# Measures for DEC-406 (T52): M-R1, M-R2, M-R5, M-R6, and the two legal texts

Date read, for every fact below: **2026-10-21** (UTC), unless a row says otherwise. Place: a team in Oakland, California. Read-only research by a worker session; nothing in the repository was changed except this file. M-R3 and M-R4 are BOB's and are not measured here.

Conventions: "not confirmed" means I could not establish it from the source itself; nothing marked so is a guess. Sizes are bytes from `Content-Length` (MiB = 1,048,576 bytes; 256 MiB = 268,435,456 bytes) unless stated. A third-party description is labelled as such and is never used as a fact.

Limits of this research, stated once:
- `cal-access.sos.ca.gov` answers with a bot-protection (Incapsula) script page, so I did not use it; I used the Secretary of State's own CDN file and the documentation packed inside it. I did not work around the protection.
- `leginfo.legislature.ca.gov` answered 403 to both curl and the fetch tool; not worked around, so California Government Code text is **not confirmed** (see M-R6).
- `uscode.house.gov` showed "Site is currently under maintenance" on the date; the U.S. Code text below is therefore from the Government Publishing Office (govinfo), 2024 edition, not the House's current "prelim" text.
- The NetFile public portal (`netfile.com/public/COAK/...`) is a script-built page; my fetch tool returned only its title, so what it offers beyond that is **not confirmed**.
- One slip to report: a request for the first 300 KB of the OakData Schedule A CSV ignored the Range header and ran to a 40 s timeout, receiving 17.8 MB before it stopped. That is over the "few MB" limit. The partial file was deleted at once and nothing was kept; a size estimate was taken from it (see M-R1 row 1).

### Legal texts, verbatim

**11 CFR 104.15**, from the eCFR (https://www.ecfr.gov/api/versioner/v1/full/2026-10-19/title-11.xml?section=104.15 ; the eCFR "versions" call for this section shows one version, dated 2016-12-23, so the text below is the current text; read 2026-10-21; the most recent issue date of Title 11 was 2026-10-19):

> § 104.15 Sale or use restriction (52 U.S.C. 30111(a)(4)).
> (a) Any information copied, or otherwise obtained, from any report or statement, or any copy, reproduction, or publication thereof, filed under the Act, shall not be sold or used by any person for the purpose of soliciting contributions or for any commercial purpose, except that the name and address of any political committee may be used to solicit contributions from such committee.
> (b) For purposes of 11 CFR 104.15, soliciting contributions includes soliciting any type of contribution or donation, such as political or charitable contributions.
> (c) The use of information, which is copied or otherwise obtained from reports filed under 11 CFR part 104, in newspapers, magazines, books or other similar communications is permissible as long as the principal purpose of such communications is not to communicate any contributor information listed on such reports for the purpose of soliciting contributions or for other commercial purposes.

## What this changes in the draft's options

**B-2, what "whole" means.**
- Option B as the draft wrote it ("FEC by cycle and state") cannot be done as stated for FEC: the bulk files are sliced **by cycle only**, never by state, and every cycle's zip is 1.5 to 5.5 GiB (2.17 GiB for the current cycle), over 256 MiB. A place slice for FEC means either streaming the cycle zip and keeping only matching rows (the file is pipe-delimited with a separate header file, so it can be streamed), or the API with a free key (1,000 calls an hour; filter parameters not confirmed).
- CAL-ACCESS is one 1.49 GiB zip with no place or year slicing, but its members can be fetched singly by Range using the zip directory; six tables exceed 256 MiB uncompressed and the biggest (receipts, expenses) are over 4 GiB. "As published" (B) means a 1.49 GiB fetch; a useful import is a per-table import with a place filter, which is C.
- The Oakland-local sources are small: OakData Schedule A is 73,043 rows (about 20 MB estimated), the largest I measured; the lobbyist sets are a few thousand rows. For these "whole" (A) costs nothing, so the options can differ by register: whole for Oakland sources (A), cycle-or-table slice with a stated place filter for FEC and CAL-ACCESS (B/C). The recommendation "B" holds for the large sources only if B is allowed to include a place filter; BOB should restate B so that its slice for FEC is "cycle, filtered at read time to the team's place".
- E5b (the conditional acquisition job) is **triggered**: both FEC and CAL-ACCESS files exceed 256 MiB.
- DCA and Form 700 sizes are not confirmed and cannot yet be used for the cost report.

**B-3, FEC purpose limit.**
- The rule text is now verified (above). The bar is on selling or using information copied from filed reports "for the purpose of soliciting contributions or for any commercial purpose"; 104.15(c) permits use in "newspapers, magazines, books or other similar communications" if the principal purpose is not to communicate contributor information for solicitation or commercial purposes. The statute is the Commission's duty section; the regulation states the restriction. The draft's "I recall but have not verified" is now settled: 52 U.S.C. 30111(a)(4) reads as quoted, and it also lets committees seed 10 pseudonyms, which is why a copied contributor list can be traced.
- The FEC pages say the same in plain words and name "names and addresses". The FEC bulk file has no street address or phone, but name, city, state and 9-digit ZIP, employer and occupation are the "information" the rule covers.
- The rule is federal and covers FEC (and any federal-committee) reports. CAL-ACCESS and OakData are state/local filings: whether a California statute imposes a like limit is **not confirmed** (code site closed to me); OakData's CC0 licence does not settle it. The draft's guard (no list export of contributor identity, a notice on every row, no soliciting feature) fits the text. Whether publishing a case that names an FEC donor falls in 104.15(c) is a question on the principal purpose of the publication; the text does not answer it, so the draft's "counsel confirms before beta" stands.

**B-5, K1485 versus "each import a capture".**
- The conflict is narrower than the draft assumed. The FEC file and the published CAL-ACCESS contributor/expense tables **carry no street address or phone**, and the Oakland Schedule A has its address columns present but empty in all 73,043 rows. So for the contributor registers the served file is already without home addresses; a reduced capture (option B) drops nothing there, and a sealed whole copy (option A) has nothing to seal.
- Where personal contact data is in the served file: CAL-ACCESS filer, cover and lobbying tables (phone, fax, email columns, filled in the rows read, 20 or more columns across about 15 tables); the OakData lobbyist-client set (street address in all 2,912 rows, phone in 1,452); the DCA address-of-record lines (layout only). For these, B (store the reduced file, record the original's digest and the dropped column names) still applies, and the list of columns to drop in M-R2 is now concrete.
- Because CAL-ACCESS is fetched as a zip and read by member, option B costs one pass per table. Form 700, bizfile and OpenCorporates layouts are not confirmed, so B-5 cannot be closed for them.

**B-6, company officers and fee-bearing sources.**
- No free, machine-readable officer source is **confirmed**. The Secretary of State's bulk order is placed through a bizfile Online account (fee and file contents not confirmed); its API portal has sign-up (key, fee and limits not confirmed from the state's own pages). OpenCorporates' free tier is restricted to journalists, NGOs and academics doing public-benefit research, at its discretion and with share-alike and attribution; automated extraction is prohibited outside the API; paid API plans start at £2,250 a year.
- So the draft's recommendation ("include only a free source; otherwise defer that register and say so") leads, on this evidence, to **defer officers** unless the state's API portal turns out to be free on a plain sign-up. That is one cheap check for the walker that I could not complete (the portal's pages did not render for me). If a team qualifies as an NGO, OpenCorporates free access would be a member's act with their own account (K1449), not an unattended service.

**B-7 (not asked, but M-R5 bears on it).** Polling is feasible only at "tens of dockets, not hundreds" in the draft's words: at 125 requests a day per account, about 5 dockets hourly or about 125 dockets once a day. Webhooks are a paid organisational feature. The docs do not describe polling alert results; a poll would be a repeated entries request per docket. Whether a free token can read `/docket-entries/` at all is **not confirmed** and needs one authenticated call by a member with their own token.
