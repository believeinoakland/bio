# Load management: a copy that queues, tells the member, and keeps advancing (draft)

**Status** · DRAFT, not READY: the numbers (the bound, the thresholds) need the load test and the memory study. PLATFORM #3, 2026-10-28, revised after Bob's second direction (`DECISIONS.md`: queue, never "try again"; priority by whether a member waits and the act's weight; background work advances and is never stuck; advance acts that could time out; a visual clue that fits the design principles). Bob's three directions are the requirement (the third: avoid resets by a sense of the risk of one, and the waiting act is brief and never blocks; Bob confirmed "Not be wordy"); §2-§5 are proposals for BOB.

## 1. What is read and measured (sources named)
- **Today** (read in `bio-plane/src`; not every file): `admission` decides who may call an op and has one window per source for credential-free ops (R21). A search of `plane`, `store-assembly`, `control-plane` for in-flight or concurrency limits found none on member calls. The one limiter seen is Cloudflare's own shed, "Durable Object is overloaded. Requests queued for too long." (115 events in the first ramp, `study/s1-results.md`), which answers the member a 502/503.
- **What breaks** (measured on `plat-s1`): errors rise at 15 members with an administrator walker per ten and an upload a minute (PLATFORM #2: 1.24 %; this session: 10.5 % of requests after a driver mistake was removed). The room, one Durable Object, resets when it exceeds its memory limit and kills every request in flight (227 resets in 36 h); most began in the alarm's reading of promoted documents (22 of 34 reset alarms, `pending-work`). The holder is not yet named; the room cannot read its own memory (`memoryUsage` answers zeros).
- **So** the limit is work in flight in the room, not a member count; and a reset ends every waiting request, so a queue that lives in the room itself is lost at the moment it is needed.

## 2. The queue (Bob's direction, made concrete)
1. **Nobody is told "try again".** Every call is admitted to a line and answered when its turn comes. When the line makes a call's expected wait longer than the screen's time budget (`time-budgets-draft.md`'s class ceiling), the member is told, in the team's words, that the copy may be a bit slow and that the work is in line; the call stays.
2. **A call that cannot finish inside its own time bound is acknowledged and finished in the background** (the pattern PL's AI-act and Gate 1 classes already use: acknowledged within 2 s, completion stated, a kept answer), so no member's call waits until a timeout.
3. **Order by three things, in this order of weight** (proposal; "several factors to balance"): (i) **a member waiting**: a member's act or screen read before any background item; (ii) **the act's weight**: its class from the time budgets (a cheap read before a heavy one: `heldcaptures` and list reads yield to acts and cheap reads); (iii) **slack to deadline**: among those, the call nearest to missing its bound goes first, which is how acts that could time out are advanced. A call whose slack runs out is lifted to the front and, if it cannot be done in time, is acknowledged and continued (item 2); it is never dropped.
4. **Background work advances and is never stuck**: each background item (reading, derive, copy, scan) gains priority with age until it is served, and the room guarantees background a minimum share of its time (for example one slice of at most 100 ms, PL7, in every K seconds while anything waits, K from the load test), so a busy hour slows reading but does not stop it. The alarm starts no reading run while it would break the byte budget (§4).
5. **Nothing a member submitted is lost**: a held file or act survives a room reset (the gate of §3 retries an act that has an idempotency key; a read is retried freely); T53-585's failure-in-words stays for what cannot be.

## 2a. Avoid the reset: a sense of its risk sets how much runs at once (Bob's third direction)
A reset is the costly event: it kills every request in flight, and the work it leaves has to be rebuilt (the ledger re-runs an interrupted reading, `store-assembly/pending.mjs`: it "counts an interrupted run" and halves the unit after two kills), which loads the room again at its worst moment. So the queue's concurrency limit is not fixed; it follows the risk.
- **Signals the system has or can have** (each named with its source; none is a memory reading, which workerd does not give): (i) **the bytes admitted in flight** by class (an upload's size, a reading's window, a heavy list read's rows), a reservation made when a call is admitted: proactive; (ii) **recent resets**: an object that starts young (`object-age` in the answer's Server-Timing, read in the study) and the ledger's `interrupted` counts and the scheduler's heartbeat (`running_since`, `scheduler/index.mjs`), which already notice a run that did not finish; (iii) **near-misses**: calls answered `502` or "overloaded", or taking several times their class's median; (iv) **the mix**: a reading run in the alarm together with uploads and heavy reads is the shape that preceded resets in the study (22 of 34 reset alarms began in `pending-work`).
- **The rule (proposal):** admit heavy work only while the reserved bytes stay under a budget and the risk is low; after a reset or a near-miss **halve** the number of heavy calls admitted at once and **raise it slowly** while none occurs (the way a network connection finds its rate); the budget's size and each class's cost come from the memory study (`proto/levels-2-3-mem`, running), which is how the sense is calibrated.
- **No stampede after a reset:** the calls the gate holds are released in steps, not all at once, so the room does not fall further behind while it warms up (the first calls after a start were the slowest in the study: not measured separately).
- **State that survives a reset**: the risk level and the recent-reset record live in storage (the gate, §3 B, or a small table in the room), not in memory.
- **Honest limit:** this reduces the chance of a reset; it cannot promise none until the holder is named. **The test** therefore counts resets, not only errors: resets per step at and below N should be 0 (from the ramp, 1 to 5 a step today).

## 3. Where the queue lives (architecture: options for BOB; Bob's if he sees it as high-level)
- **A. In the room**: simplest; it keeps no line across a reset and cannot reorder what the platform already holds in its own input queue. Not recommended alone.
- **B. A gate in front of each room** (a small Durable Object per room, or per team): holds the line, orders it, releases N calls at a time, retries across a reset; costs one more hop per call (the hop is 50-57 ms on `plat-s1`, `study/s1-results.md` §1a; the gate's own cost is not measured). **Recommended** for Level 2 and 3 and for Level 1 once measured, because a reset is exactly when the queue must survive.
- Either way the order and the bound N (work in flight, from the measured break less a margin) are the same; §5 states the test.

## 4. The byte budget
The room cannot measure its memory, so admitted work is held to a budget by size (an upload's bytes, a reading's window); the budget's figure and its holder come from the memory study (`proto/levels-2-3-mem`, running). Until then it is stated as not measured.

## 5. The member's sign that the copy is under load (a need to UX-DESIGN, through BOB)
Not a design: the needs only. Bob (third direction): **the waiting act is brief, not wordy, and blocks nothing the member is doing on the screen (reading, scrolling, typing); it informs and is never modal.** (a) **A quiet state shown to every member when the expected wait passes the screen's bar**, cleared when it passes; it takes no focus, covers no content, needs no dismissal, and is one short line at most. (b) **On the act that is waiting**, a short mark that it is in line, with the screen still usable around it. (c) **The administrator also sees what is deferred** and its age, on a screen of its own, not in the way. (d) Not by colour alone; words only through the catalogue; it must fit the design principles UX-DESIGN holds (I have read none and name none). What PLATFORM supplies: the signal (queue wait over the class ceiling) and a threshold per service level.

## 6. Test and ownership
Per level, at the ramp's mix (PL40-PL45): **errors 0 and resets 0 at every step up to N, and past N only the stated slow-state, never a 5xx**; the median and p90 of each class stay within its ceiling up to N and degrade as the line, never as a 5xx; no background item waits past a stated bound; the load sign appears and clears at the stated wait. Owners for PLANNING: the door and the gate (`plane`, `control-plane`), the scheduler (guaranteed background share, idle slices), reading (the byte budget). **Not measured:** N; the gate's hop; the background guarantee K; whether queuing alone stops the resets (one reading may break the limit by itself, so the budget may be needed too).
