# S0: what the record's shape means for splitting it (draft)

**Status** · S0 RESULT, PLATFORM #1, 2026-10-27, for `levels-2-3-study-plan.md`. Source: `table-inventory.md` (and the TSV), read from the real assembled test store's `declaredTables()`: **616 tables**. The scope column is the inventory worker's heuristic (no source declares a table's scope); sizes per table are not in any source and are measured in S1.

## 1. What the inventory shows

- Only about **106 of the 616 tables are project-scoped** (keyed by a project, or belonging to a project-only module). **293 are team-wide** (members, credentials, entities, people, events, standards, capture queues, ledgers) and **217 are mixed**, mostly keyed by a document (`bundle_id`) and reachable to a project only through `bundles.project`, which can be empty.
- **Work that crosses projects is built into the design, not incidental:** `connections` and `contradiction_*` pair documents from different projects (the contradiction rule tells each project only its own side until all holders ask); the **entity registry** is one per team and joined to every project's readings; a captured document has **one home** and appears in a second project only as a `seen_in` row; shared questions are drawn on by several projects; Home, Queue and Projects are unions over a viewer's projects; a published case can name several projects; **sight** (who may see what) is SQL over `bundles.project` applied to every read.
- **The strongest constraint is atomicity.** `transact` (record-core R32) is one transaction over the whole store, and a promotion commits everything it writes in one transaction, including listener writes to `refs`, `readings`, projections, `connection_dirty` and entity resolution; ids come from one ledger (`seq`, `minted_ids`); purge and expunge span every table. Which promotions touch tables outside their own project is UNCLEAR in the sources and decides the cost of a per-project split.

## 2. What that does to the candidate splits (for Bob, with the study)

| split | what it buys | what it costs | verdict from S0 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. A room per project, a team directory** | a thread per project: concurrency grows with active projects; each project's record is its own 10 GB | breaks whole-store `transact` across rooms; every cross-project read (connections, contradictions, entities, search, sight, Queue, Home) becomes a fan-out or a roll-up kept in step; project-less documents have no room; a promotion's listeners may write outside their room; the id ledger and purge/expunge need a coordinator | **the deepest change.** Bob's own remark (K4747: "adding support for more than one room will be non-trivial") is borne out. Not first. |
| **B. One relational core, bulk out** (K4635 option B) | the core room stays small and fast: move `capture_text` (535,983 rows on the test copy; the growth term, FILES F5), the indexes (`capture_text_fts`, `bundles_fts`, `bundle_projection`, `bundle_terms`), `readings` JSON, `reading_history` and blobs to R2 or another store; keeps one transaction domain | a **search index outside SQLite** (FTS5 cannot be assumed to leave the object: UNCLEAR) so search runs in a different place and, per Bob's factor 3 (K4635), may answer in the background; the core still has one thread, so concurrency does not grow | raises the **record bound**, not the members-at-once ceiling. Probably the first step of Level 2. |
| **C. Read through caches** | the shared reads that dominate (Queue counts, project roster, `home_looks`, standards, published cases) answered from a Worker-side cache with a stated staleness, so the room's thread is spent on writes and changed reads | staleness the screens must state; cache invalidation by promotion | raises **members-at-once** without splitting; cheapest; helps the budget at Level 1 as well |
| **D. Member-private rooms** | member-private tables (`queue_state`, `hypotheses`, `member_notes`, `home_looks`, mutes) move to a room per member: no cross-member contention | `sight` owner-keyed tables are few; the gain depends on how much of the call volume they are (unmeasured) | a candidate if S1 shows they dominate |
| **E. A thread for background work** | reading and landing (the large-file work that holds the room: P24, PL7) moves out of the member-facing room | landing still must write to the core | partly done by PL7 (yield); full separation is a design step |

**What this means for the levels.** Level 2 (Institution) is probably **B plus C (and D if measured)**, a core room with bulk and indexes outside and shared reads cached, and **not** A. A is the way to a thread per project and is a re-architecture of the transaction model, to be taken only if S1 and S2 show B and C do not reach the Institution's range. Level 3 (Enterprise) adds what Enterprise adds (larger uploads, region-held data, higher limits on request) on the Level 2 design.

## 3. What S1 and S2 must now measure (changes to the plan)

1. **S1, on the seeded Level 1 scratch copy:** bytes per table (SQLite `dbstat`) so B's gain is known (what share of the 416 MB is `capture_text`, the FTS tables, `readings`); rows read per op by table, so C's and D's gain are known (which reads dominate the 4,800 rows a call); the share of calls that are member-private (D) and that are shared and cacheable (C); and which promotions write outside their project (A's cost), by instrumenting the plane's `transact` call sites in the prototype only.
2. **S2, the prototype, becomes two small prototypes:** (i) **B**: a team's text units and indexes held outside the room with search answered from an external index, timing a search and a landing; (ii) **C**: a Worker-side cache of the shared reads, timing Home and Queue with the room under a ramp. **A is prototyped only as a paper costing** unless S1 shows B and C fall short: a prototype of A would have to reproduce `transact` across rooms, which is a design task, not a throwaway.
3. **What stays UNCLEAR and is S1's to settle:** whether FTS5 can leave the object, the per-table sizes, which promotion listeners cross projects, which room holds a project-less bundle.
