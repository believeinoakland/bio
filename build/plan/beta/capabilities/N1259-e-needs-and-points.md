## 2. What the `registers` screen needs from UX-DESIGN (needs, not designs)

Sent to UX-DESIGN through BOB. DEC-407 and DEC-411 are the drawing as it stands (67 keys, then about 39 added); each item below is something the requirements give the screen to say or do that the drawing does not yet have, or that it draws differently.

1. **Import progress.** States `queued`, `fetching`, `reading`, `indexed`, `failed`, `paused` (reasons `member`, `shelf`, `host_paused`, `host_cooling`), `stopped`; per-piece progress; a percentage only when the size is stated by the source; controls to pause, resume and stop. DEC-411 draws only "the team is told in the queue when it is ready".
2. **Counted or estimated, and what could not be known.** The delta and time estimate say per figure whether it was counted by the source, estimated, or not stated; "about" for time; a state for a probe that timed out ("could not reach the source in time; try again"); a shelf warning (70% and 90%) before an import that would use much of the team's space.
3. **The listing's figures with their basis** (stated by the source, counted, estimated, not stated) and the listing's other states: `not measured yet` (importable only through the team's own definition), `gated` (a member's own capture), `beta`. The mock lists Form 700 and the state licence files with sizes as importable; the profile will not (6.1).
4. **The filter** shows only criteria the chosen register supports (names, organizations, amounts, dates, filer numbers, forms, places, licence and docket numbers); what each prefilled criterion came from; what could not be applied (`left_out`).
5. **Which criteria are watched by themselves and which are not.** A filter with a name or organization is kept and shown but re-checked only when a member asks ("Check now"; `registerrefresh`); the same for a team's pre-read. The mock's row "Read in whole: watching looks for any change" and "names Martinez and Ortega" under "what watching looks for" cannot stand (6.2).
6. **The administrator-only acts**: the pre-read, connecting an outside alert service, and removing a row. What a non-administrator sees in their place ("alerts are off; ask an administrator"), and how many followed dockets the service's limit can serve and how often each is looked at (following R42).
7. **A team's own register** beyond the drawn door: after "Propose this register" (name, address, publisher), a second step: format, delimiter, the header, each column's role and keep or leave out (default leave out), the team's own statement of the register's terms, labelled "your team's own research, not checked"; the list of the team's proposals and withdrawal. DEC-411 draws only the proposal.
8. **Comparing two imports**: counts added, removed (or "possibly removed"), changed; a page of rows; "a difference is not a finding".
9. **A row**: its kept cells; "reduced copy" label and the dropped column names; the other imports' versions; the original's fingerprint and the file's; the limit's notice (FEC) on the row and on the extract; the states `NO_CRITERIA` and `RESULT_CAP` for a limited register ("add a name, amount, date or number to search"); the search's three endings: rows, "not in this import (source date ...)", and "stopped early; narrow the search" (never "nothing found" when cut).
10. **Queue items**: `register-changed-noticed` with its counts (added, changed, removed) and no names; `register-import-done`, `-stopped`, `-failed`.
11. **The scope choice** on an import: "for this project" or "for the team" (administrators), and what each means for who sees it.
12. **Removal of a row** (administrator): the grounds (unlawful, confidential, a court order, a lawful demand of a kind the profile lists), the consequence statement, and how a cited leg shows "removed".
13. **Words**: a `setup-words` row for every refusal code, state, reason and status in parts (a) to (c) (a missing sentence is a REPORT for the design, never inline English), and the keys `register.reduced`, `register.extract`, `register.beta.officers` and a limit's `text_key`.
14. **On a published page**: the extract's label, the two fingerprints, the dates and the row position; the limit's notice.
15. **"Follow from here"** on a register row (DEC-410/447): a row with no entity link offers only "Look for this name" (N1260's `screens-investigate`).

## 4. Needs from other lanes

**PLATFORM** (what a register import adds to the record and the load, per service level K4746; numbers marked "arithmetic" are mine from the measures and `service-levels.html`, not measured):
- *Sizes the measures give.* City open-data sets: campaign Schedule A 73,043 rows, about 20 MB as a file (an estimate of about 280 bytes a row, from 17.8 MB holding about 63,300 rows); Summary Totals 100,032 rows; Schedule E 28,292; the four lobbyist and behested sets 2,912, 5,501, 31 and 226 rows (a few hundred KB to a few MB). State raw data: one 1.49 GiB zip; the receipts member 571 MiB compressed (over 4 GiB uncompressed per its ZIP64 flag), expenses 381 MiB compressed. Federal individual contributions: 1.49 to 5.46 GiB per two-year cycle (2.17 GiB for the current one), uncompressed size and row count not measured. The mock's placeholders (FEC 9.8 GB and 41 million rows, state 1.94 GB and 6.31 million rows, the City's 212 MB and 48,210 rows) are not measurements.
- *Arithmetic for one team.* The shelf is 10 GB per object at every level today and an import competes with the document record (the test copy holds 416 MB). A small register (Schedule A) as a reduced segment plus index is of the order of 25 to 40 MB (about 0.3% of the shelf; unmeasured). A filtered federal slice of 1 million rows at about 300 bytes a row is of the order of 0.3 to 0.7 GB with its index. A full federal cycle is tens of millions of rows and cannot live in the team's object: this is why the storage options in section 5 include object storage. Rows written are the first charge past the $5 allowance (50 million a month included, then $1 per million, `service-levels.html`): an import of N rows with k index structures writes on the order of N × (1 + k) rows (my arithmetic; how an index write is counted is unverified), so 1 million rows with 4 indexes is about 5 million (10% of the allowance) and 40 million rows would be about 200 million (about $150 beyond the allowance); a refresh writes only its delta (part (b) R21). A team's filter and delta shown before an import let it choose by capacity.
- *Load.* A slice must yield: the record object has one worker (about 30 calls a second, 15 to 25 members working at once before screens slow, `service-levels.html`); a register import is background work in that room at Levels 1; at Level 2 (the split record, N1271) the rows of a register belong in their own storage unit so a search does not queue behind the team's screens; at Level 3 the same, per organisation. PLATFORM to say where a register's rows live at each level, and to supply: the shelf-free figure and warn marks (`rowStore.free`), the Worker CPU and wall limit per request and per alarm firing, the subrequest limit per invocation, and the per-level load test (N1274) with an import running while members work.
- *Time.* Section 3's budgets.

**FILES** (N1266, N1267, N1267a, N1268):
- *Overlap with `csv-reader`.* `csv-reader` reads a whole body up to the standard's bounds into a sheet (a document); `register-readers` streams a file of any size under a definition. They must share one record walker (`walkRecords` promoted to a Provides of `csv-reader`) so that comma, tab, semicolon and pipe, quoting and encodings are decided once; `csv-reader` R2 already reads a TSV by declared type; N1266's TSV work should add nothing to dialects that `register-readers` also needs. A register file a member uploads is a document read by `csv-reader` (K1468), not a register import; whether a member can bring such a file into `registers` is not in this scope (a question for FILES and BOB).
- *Bounds.* The alpha refuses a CSV over 17 MiB (N1267: the active figure, 128 MB memory), `csv-reader` R6 states the beta's 50 MB and 1,048,576 rows, archives are 500 MB as uploaded and 2 GB unpacked (K4831 (4)). A register piece exceeds all of them by design (a 5.46 GiB federal zip, read by range and never held), so it is bounded by its own `REGISTER_STREAM_MAX` (part (a) R126) and not by `READ_BOUNDS`; FILES to confirm that N1267's bounds apply to documents and not to `streamRegister`.
- *Zip by range.* A range-based central-directory lister and member stream with ZIP64 (part (a) R2): `ooxml.listArchive` is given bytes; N1266's "ZIP archives (each file inside read)" and this need one implementation, or `register-readers` has its own.
- *Corpus (N1268).* Fixtures: a pipe file with a separate header, a tab member in a zip, a ZIP64 directory over 4 GiB (sparse), a ragged row, a quote left open, a UTF-16 file.

**AI-DESIGN** (the assisted part: `registerask`, `registermatch`, `registerwatchpropose`; not built; hand over through BOB). What it will need from this design: (1) `searchRows` takes a structured query only, so the assistant turns plain words into criteria and no free text is stored; (2) matching a register's names to the team's subjects must be a proposal for a member to accept, never a link made by the assistant (hypothesis layer, K1473), and `entities` is the module that links; (3) a proposed watch is a proposed `registerinterest`, and the automatic-watch rule (`autoWatchable`) binds it: a proposed name watch is re-checked by a member's act only; (4) the assistant reads only rows the asking member may see (hidden projects, part (b) R19); (5) K1492 (6) says members' searches are not logged, while an AI-assisted ask is metered in `ai-use`: the two need an explicit rule; (6) importing stays mechanical; an assistant that wants more rows asks the member to start an import; (7) turning a row into a money fact or a line is a member's act (K1468), citing the row's extent.

## 5. Points that are Bob's, and storage options

Nothing here repeats an answer in K4403, K4405 (its ten points) or DEC-447. Each is put as P17 asks: what it is in plain words, options, a recommendation.

**P-1 · Company officers in the beta: from where, and at whose cost (money, legal exposure). ANSWERED, K5498 (C8-poll), option C, "as recommended".** A team connects California's state API with its own key where sign-up is free, read only when a member asks (a member-initiated read, not a background daemon read); a member may order the state's bulk file and bring it in; never OpenCorporates; a paid or account-gated source only by a member with their own account, price shown first, never in the background (K1449). BOB: the docket-poll lines are no longer conditional. Applied in part (a) R130, R131; part (c) R41, R42, R81; part (d) R134; points 4, 10, 16. The beta line (BETA-2): officers are read when a member asks and never watched by themselves.

**P-2 · What counts as a "home address" in an import (doctrine on personal facts, K1485). ANSWERED, K5499 (C9), option A.** In a donor list only the street lines of a home address are removed; ZIP codes, map coordinates and the city are kept as the source gives them (not the recommended B). Applied in part (a) R3 (a nine-digit ZIP stays nine; the search term `zip5` is its first five digits, R5). A home address or phone number a member keeps in their notes is still never published (part (d) R39, D408).

**P-3 · Does the donor protection reach California and Oakland donors? (legal exposure). ANSWERED, K5500 (C10), option A.** The donor-use guard covers federal registers only for now (11 CFR 104.15); whether California has a like rule goes on the list for a lawyer (see "Items for a lawyer" below). The guard is data on each system, so a later extension is a data change (part (a) R131; part (d) R66).

**Items for a lawyer** (asked before the first beta; none blocks a draft): (1) publishing a federal donor (K4403 (3)); (2) K5500: whether California has a rule like 11 CFR 104.15 for the state's filings, and whether it reaches the City's open data (a public-domain licence does not by itself settle it).

**P-4 · A donor named in a cited row and "every named person has a recorded basis" (doctrine, K1483 (a)). ANSWERED, K5503 (C11), option B, "as recommended".** A published case that cites a public filing gains a seventh recorded reason for naming a person, "named in the public filing the case cites" (`named_in_cited_filing`), which the cited row itself proves: the cited row of the register names the person. It is recorded and checked before publication like the six others (their act or position, a tie, an interest, their consent, an earlier publication of them, a private party with the words saying why), and is accepted only while a cited row names that person. It keeps K1483 (a) (a recorded, checked reason for every named person) and makes the honest reason cheap for a case citing many rows. Applied in part (d) R35 (a kind added to `peopleJudged`, case-disclosures R25) with its test. Not decided here: the Provides of case-disclosures R25, whose list of reasons lives outside this folder.
