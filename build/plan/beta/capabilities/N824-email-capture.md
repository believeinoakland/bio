## 0. What exists, and what this feature is

Today there is no email path at all: email has no reader (`reading-pipeline` R54 names an email by its bytes or `.eml`/`.msg` name and states it final, "Email is not read yet.", `UNSUPPORTED_FORMAT` kind `email`; there is no reader and no intake path for mail). A member can already bring a file she holds into the record (`capture` R86 `uploadCapture`: her statement of origin required, graded received and never fetched, `provenance` R63). The doorbell takes anything from anyone into an inbox a member empties into the record (`pullKnock`, R13), bounded by two rate buckets (R2) and a count-only tally (R19). The product sends no email (`screens-settings` R23) and holds no member email address that I found; the only email it touches is the stewards' notification (`stewards` R14, a `send_email` binding on Civicsmith's own account).

N824 (Bob, K2425 (5): "D42 - email: A", "later, after upload is in use", "also a deployment") is: an address that receives email and brings it into the record, graded like upload. This draft follows the ruling's words and the upload's shape. I could not find the text of D42's options (they are in no file I read); I take `next.md`'s wording ("a per-group forwarding address ... graded like upload") as the meaning of "A", and say so as contradiction 1 in the report.

**Split of work.** Reading a message (`.eml` and `.msg`, attachments included) is the FILES lane's (N1266). This draft states the interface it needs (section 3). It does not draft the reader.

## 1. Bob's points (each with an explanation a non-engineer can follow)

**B1. Where the address lives, and what it costs a team (architecture and money). ANSWERED, K5504 (C12), option A, "as recommended": E1 with E3 beside it.**
*What the problem is.* To receive mail, an address must belong to a web domain (like `ourteam.org`), and the domain's mail routing must be set up. Civicsmith's own pages live at an address ending `workers.dev`, which cannot receive mail. So a team must have, or buy, a domain, and put it on its own Cloudflare account; then it tells Cloudflare "mail to this address goes to my Civicsmith". Cloudflare's feature for this is called Email Routing. Facts about Cloudflare, each checked by the C7 reviewer against developers.cloudflare.com on 2026-10-27: (1) Email Routing is free (confirmed, "Available on Free and Paid plans", /email-routing/); (2) it needs the domain on Cloudflare DNS (corrected: "You must be using Cloudflare DNS", onboarding adds MX, SPF TXT and DKIM TXT records, /email-routing/get-started/enable-email-routing/; whether full name-server delegation is needed is not stated there); (3) a domain costs roughly ten to fifteen dollars a year (UNVERIFIED: a registrar's price, not checked); (4) a Worker can receive the routed mail through an `email()` handler (confirmed, /email-routing/email-workers/runtime-api/); (5) a message is limited to 25 MiB inbound, larger rejected (confirmed, /email-routing/limits/, which also gives 200 routing rules per domain and 200 destination addresses per account).
*Options.*
- **E1. The team's own domain, on the team's own Cloudflare account** (the same account its Civicsmith already runs in). Mail never passes through Civicsmith's people. Cost to a team: a domain (a new cheap one used only for mail is simplest, leaving its main website and mailboxes untouched) and about fifteen minutes of guided steps; the Cloudflare side is free if my assumption holds. Cost to Civicsmith: building the guided steps and the receiving code.
- **E2. An address on a domain Civicsmith runs** (for example `team@mail.civicsmith.org`), forwarded to the team's Civicsmith. Cheapest for a team (no domain, no steps). But every team's mail would pass through a service Civicsmith runs, which reverses the rule that a team's record is its own and nothing leaves by a system path (v0.2 rule 14, `BIO_Action_v0_2.md:64`; `stewards` Purpose), makes Civicsmith a party a court could order to hand over mail, and puts abuse of a shared mail service on it.
- **E3. No receiving address in the beta.** A member saves the message as a file (`.eml`) and uploads it; FILES' N1266 makes that read whole, attachments included. Nothing to set up, nothing to pay, no spam; one more step for each message.
*Recommendation.* E1 as an optional feature a team switches on, with E3 always available beside it (it exists the day N1266 lands, and needs nothing from this draft). Not E2. The draft below is written for E1; E3 needs none of it.
*Ruled (K5504):* the forwarding address lives on the team's own domain (the instance-setup mail domain line, R114; about $10-15 a year; Cloudflare's free email routing), and uploading a saved email (E3) is always available beside it. E2 and "E3 alone" are not taken.

**B2. Who may send, and what the address is tied to (privacy and policy). ANSWERED, K5505 (C13), option A, "as recommended": S3.**
*What the problem is.* The "From" line of an email is whatever the sender's program wrote; anyone can forge it. So "mail from a member's address" proves nothing by itself. And the product holds no member email address, so an allow-list of addresses would mean asking every member for one and keeping it.
*Options.*
- **S1. One address per team (what "per-group" says); anyone may send; mail waits in a team mail inbox like the doorbell's, and any member brings a message in.** Simple; open to spam and to anyone filling the inbox; the member who brings it in is attributed, as with a knock.
- **S2. One address per team, with an allow-list of senders' addresses members register.** Needs the product to keep addresses; a forged From defeats it unless the receiving side also checks SPF, DKIM and DMARC, and I could not verify Cloudflare hands those to the receiving code (UNVERIFIED; C7: the postmaster page says Email Routing itself rejects mail failing SPF and DKIM and enforces DMARC, but not that any result reaches an Email Worker, /email-routing/postmaster/).
- **S3. One private address per member, under the team's domain** (for example `m-7k3q9x2f@ourteam.org`, an unguessable code). Mail sent to a member's own address is hers: it waits in her private list, attributed to her by the address it was sent to, not by anything the message says. The product stores no email address of hers; she sets her own mailbox to forward to her private address; she can switch it off and get a new one at any time; mail to any other address at that domain is refused. If her code leaks, she changes it.
*Recommendation.* S3. It ties the mail to a member the way a signed-in upload is tied to her, needs no new personal fact, makes spam a per-member nuisance she can end, and fits "graded like upload". 
*Ruled (K5505):* each member has a private forwarding address of their own, so the address itself says who sent a message; Civicsmith holds no member's email address. S1, S2 and S1+S3 (a team-wide shared inbox) are not taken.

**B3. The statement of origin (evidence honesty). ANSWERED, K5506 (C14), option A, "as recommended": T1.**
Upload requires the member's own words on where the file came from (R86, `UPLOAD_NO_STATEMENT`). A forwarded email arrives with no such words.
- **T1. Nothing enters the record until she adds her statement.** Mail arrives in her private "mail waiting" list (stored privately, not in the record); she opens it, reads it, writes her statement (up to 2,000 characters, as upload) and brings it in. The same two steps as the doorbell (arrive, then pull) and the same rule as upload. Unconfirmed mail is deleted after a stated time.
- **T2. A standing statement she writes once** ("I forward mail from my own mailbox") is attached to every message automatically; mail goes straight into the record. Convenient, but the statement is then not about this message, which weakens it as evidence.
- **T3. No statement; the message's own headers are the origin record.** Weakest: headers are the sender's claims.
*Recommendation.* T1. It keeps mail out of the record until a member has read it (which is also the whole spam defence), and gives every captured message the same statement upload gives. T2 can be added later without changing the record.
*Ruled (K5506):* forwarded mail waits in the member's private list until the member adds a statement of where it came from, as an upload requires, and only then enters the record (R3, R5, R6).

**B4. What of an email may appear in a published case (privacy).**
K2334 (2) already removes a member-supplied file's own metadata from its published copy. An email's headers are metadata that name private people (the sender, every recipient and copied address) and contain network addresses; its body often holds a home address or phone (K1485: imports never copy these in bulk; DEC-408: a member may keep one in her notes, never published).
- **P1. A published copy shows the body and attachments only**; the headers are kept in the record for members, never published, except the date, and a sender's name when that sender is an official writing in the official role (K1485 row 8).
- **P2. P1 plus the subject line.**
- **P3. All headers.**
*Recommendation.* P1. This is an extension of an existing ruling to a new kind of file, so Bob decides whether the extension holds. It also needs the publication filter of N1259 (home address or phone never published) to run over the body, which that lane drafts.

**B5. How long unconfirmed mail is kept, and the limits (policy, figures).**
Mail not brought in has to go somewhere and must not pile up. The doorbell's discarded knocks are to be cleared after about a week (Bob's suggestion, DEC-108 (5); that clearing was owed and not built, `doorbell` R3; C9 drafts it as `doorbell` R29, in the companion file, and mail-door R6 is its twin). *Proposal:* 14 days (a member may be away), then deleted, with a count-only tally kept as the doorbell keeps one (R19). Limits per member address: at most 20 messages in any hour, and 200 a day for the whole team; one message at most 25 MiB, and never more than `CAPTURE_MAX`. These figures are proposals for Bob to approve or change; the code states them in words, as the doorbell's refusal sentence does (`doorbell` R9).

**B6. A new module (architecture).** Adding a module that carries product capability is Bob's (PROCESS-MECHANICS §7). I propose `mail-door` (layer 3, after `doorbell`), section 4. The alternative, putting the code inside `doorbell`, would cross `doorbell`'s purpose (anyone, no account) with a member-addressed door and grow a 35 KB file; I recommend the new module.

**B7. What does not change.** Never AI: no machine reads, sorts, summarises, scores or deletes a message (the doorbell's optional "gatekeeper", DEC-108 (4), is a machine spam filter labelled as machine work; **no** equivalent is proposed here; if Bob wants one it is a later request). Civicsmith sends no reply, no bounce text beyond the refusal the sending server itself writes, and no notification email.

## 2. Technical choices I made (BOB's, not Bob's)

1. **The receiving entry is the plane's mail handler** (Cloudflare's Worker `email()` handler, documented at developers.cloudflare.com/email-routing/email-workers/runtime-api/ with `from`, `to`, `raw` stream, `rawSize`, `headers`). The plane today exports `{ fetch, scheduled }` only (`bio-plane/src/plane/index.mjs:71`). UNVERIFIED: whether the plane's bundle and the installer's upload carry it. It is a MEASUREMENT for PLANNING, M1 in `N824-container-member-job.md` section D, on Civicsmith's own account `20b533579290b9b93168345edd3b7f72` with one probe Worker removed after and its pass bar set before measuring (C9 (5); C12 (a)).
2. **The raw message is the capture.** The bytes the handler receives are held whole under their own SHA-256 (as an upload's are), so the capture's identity is the message's. Parsed fields are recorded as facts about the message, never rewritten.
3. **Attachments are captured as members of the message**, filed at the message's grade and read separately, by the pattern of an archive's members (`provenance` R59, `acquisition` R38-R41): same grade, never stronger, each shown beside its message.
4. **Waiting mail lives outside the record** (private evidence store, one row per message), as `doorbell` R3 holds a knock; nothing about it is notified.
5. **Refusal at the door, not after.** A message over its bound, or to an address that is not a live member address, is refused during the mail conversation (the sending server writes the bounce). The refuse call is `message.setReject(reason)`, which "refuses the email and returns a permanent SMTP error with the reason you supply" (Cloudflare runtime-api page; C7 checked); `rawSize` gives the size before the stream is read. UNVERIFIED: that a rejected message leaves nothing in Cloudflare's logs that the product would hold.
6. **One clock** (`capture` R115) for every instant.

### 4.1 mail-door (NEW; layer 3, after `doorbell` in the order; B6)

**Uses:** `record-grammar`, `record-core`, `membership`, `credentials`, `provenance`, `acquisition`, `capture`, `runtime-limits`, `setup-words`, `mail-reader` (layer 1, FILES'; not yet in `modules.json`), `test-support`. **Layer check (C9 (3)):** every edge goes to layers 1-3 and earlier in the order (indices: `capture` 48, `doorbell` 49). `scheduler` (layer 10, index 140) uses `mail-door` and `doorbell`: allowed, a later layer using earlier modules, no cycle (`N824-container-member-job.md` section C.2 gives the whole arrangement). `mail-door` does NOT use `instance-setup` (layer 11) or `scheduler`: the mail domain and the switch are handed in by the composition as `mailDoorOf(ctx, deps)`'s `deps.settings()`. `op-declarations` and `control-plane` (layer 11) use it.

**Purpose.** A member's private address brings mail into her private waiting list; she reads it and brings it into the record with her statement. Anyone else's mail to the team's domain is refused. It is the mail twin of the doorbell and the upload, and files nothing itself: `capture` files.

- **R1** (T53; N824; K2425 (5), K2434; B2) *(not yet met: T53)* **A member's private address.** `mailAddressOf({member, viewer})`, `mailAddressMake({by, within})`, `mailAddressOff({by, within})`. A signed-in member with `contribute` (member-session fence as `capture` R86: `MEMBER_SESSION_REQUIRED` for a machine identity or no stamp; `contribute` is `member-roster` R1's capability, checked at the door by the op's class, as `captureupload` is, `capture` Suggestions T41 and `control-plane` R72, not by R86's own text) has at most one live address: a local part `m-` followed by 12 characters of at least 60 bits drawn from a cryptographic source, joined to the team's mail domain (set by instance-setup R114, read through `deps.settings()`; distinct from the website domain claim of `groupDomainSet`, instance-setup R7-R9). `mailAddressMake` makes a new one and turns the old one off in the same act; `mailAddressOff` turns it off. Only the member reads her own address; no administrator, no other member, no read for the record's sake reads it, and it is never written to a log, a receipt, a document or a published copy. The address's digest, not the address, is what the door matches. With no mail domain set, `mailAddressMake` is refused `MAIL_NOT_SET_UP`. Test: two members get two addresses; a new address turns the old off; a second member's read of the first's address answers nothing; a machine identity is refused `MEMBER_SESSION_REQUIRED`; no table row, answer or log line holds the clear address, and none holds any member's own email address (K5505: the product never asks for one or keeps one; only the digest of the private address is kept). Control: a member with no `contribute` is refused as the `captureupload` door refuses her.
- **R2** (T53; N824; K2425 (5); B2, B5) *(not yet met: T53)* **`receiveMail({raw, envelope_to, envelope_from, size, now})` takes mail at the door.** Refusals in order, each refused during the mail conversation and writing nothing but R9's tally: the door off (`MAIL_OFF`); `envelope_to` not the digest of a live member address (`MAIL_NO_SUCH_ADDRESS`, one answer for an unknown address and a switched-off one); the member's hourly or the team's daily bound reached (R8); a declared `size` over the mail bound (`MAIL_TOO_LARGE`, with size and bound, and judged before a byte is read); a read stream that passes the bound (stream cancelled); zero bytes (`MAIL_EMPTY`). Otherwise one waiting row (R3) is written and the message is accepted; `envelope_from` is read for nothing and kept nowhere, so no member's own address is stored. No reply, bounce text or notification is sent by the product. Test: a message to a live address is accepted and waits; to an unknown local part, to a switched-off address and to the right address on a closed door are each refused with the stated code and nothing written; a declared size of bound plus 1 is refused before reading; the stream cancel is asserted by a counting source. Control: a message exactly at the bound is accepted.
- **R3** (T53; N824; B3, B5; `doorbell` R3's pattern) *(not yet met: T53)* **The waiting list.** An accepted message writes one row `MAIL-<date>-<8 hex>`: owner (the member whose private address it was sent to: the address, not any header, says who forwarded it, K5505), the raw bytes' SHA-256, size, received instant, status `waiting`, `days_left`, and, from `mail-reader`, the headers as stated (R4); the raw bytes under a private evidence key (`bio/mail/<sha256>`), outside the record (K5506): no bundle, no receipt, no document, no grade, seen by its owner alone, until she brings it in with her statement (R5) or R6 deletes it. `mailWaiting({viewer, limit, after})` lists the viewer's own rows only, newest first, at most 200 per page with `next`; `mailRead({id, viewer})` answers one row's headers, text and attachment list (name and size as stated, never opened) to its owner only, `NO_SUCH_MAIL` for anyone else (one answer for absent and not hers). Test: a member lists only her own mail; another member's id answers `NO_SUCH_MAIL`; the bytes are under the private key and not in the evidence of any bundle. Control: after bring-in (R5) the row reads `brought_in` and the list still shows it, with its capture.

Deleting writes no record and no receipt. Test: a row received 14 days and 1 minute before the instance clock is gone after the tick, with its bytes; one received 13 days ago stays; `mailDelete` removes the row and bytes; `limit: 1` over three due rows clears one and states `truncated`. - **R7** (T53; N824; K1485, DEC-408; B4) *(not yet met: T53)* **No mail address of a person is published, and none is bulk-copied.** A captured message's headers are record-private: any published copy (`publication`, N1259's filter) shows the body and attachments only, plus the date and, for a sender who is a public official writing in the role, the sender's name as the message states it (P1 of B4; if Bob rules P2 or P3 this line is amended).
