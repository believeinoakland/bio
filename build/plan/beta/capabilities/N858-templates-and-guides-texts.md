## 0. What is built, what is missing

**Built (T45, T51):** a template reaches a member only through `filing-templates` R15: its attribution must name a writer, at least one public source, at least one `professional` review with outcome `no_concerns`, and an approver other than the writer; otherwise it is not offered and reads `TEMPLATE_NOT_REVIEWED` naming what it lacks. R28 refuses court-petition templates. R35 (`unofferedFor`) and `filings` R65 (`templatesForAction`'s `not_offered`, `lacks` any of `writer`, `sources`, `professional_review`, `second_approval`) tell a member what is "Not offered yet" and why. `reading-guides` R13 gives a profile procedure guide (items `{route, expect?, citation}`, `citation` required); R15 answers each profile guide with `status` and `basis`; `screens-action` R31 draws them.

**Missing:**
1. **No text exists.** The Oakland-and-Alameda profile holds no `templates` and no `procedure_guides` (searched). `@records-request` is unresolved (K2781 (6)); the machinery offers none and says so.
2. **Procedure guides are not gated.** `reading-guides` R13/R15 require a citation per item and a `status` and `basis`; they do **not** require a named writer, a professional review or a second approver. `screens-action` R31 draws any profile guide it is given. So the entry's rule ("each needing a named writer, the public sources and a professional's review") holds for templates and not for guides: the first guide put into the profile would be offered without review. (See the contradictions in the report; this is B1.)
3. **No written process** for who writes a text, from what, who reviews, who approves, how it enters the profile and when it is redone.
4. **No one acceptance test** that nothing reaches a member through any door without all three.

## 1. Bob's points

**B1. Do procedure guides need the same three things as templates? (policy).**
*Explanation.* A template is wording a team may send in its own name. A procedure guide tells a member "this is how this kind of request goes here: the routes, in the order the law gives, and what to expect". A wrong route could cost a team a deadline. Action v0.2 rule 11 says templates are "written by a named person from public sources, reviewed by a professional, approved and attributed", and that a guide is "sourced ... labelled legal information, not legal advice"; it does not say a professional reviews the guide. `next.md` N858 says both need all three.
*Options.* **G-a.** Guides need sources only (as built) plus the label. **G-b.** Guides need what templates need: a named writer, sources, a professional's review, an approver other than the writer (recommended). **G-c.** As G-b, but only for guides of Tier 2 and 3 kinds (higher stakes); Tier 1 guides need writer, sources and an approver.
*Recommendation.* G-b. The first profile has one kind in the beta's scope (B2), so the added cost is a single review of one guide beside the template review; and a team trusting "how it goes" is the same trust as a team sending the text. G-a leaves the built behaviour and an unreviewed guide on every action screen.
*Decision wanted:* G-a, G-b or G-c. G-b or G-c changes what `reading-guides` R13 means, so it is Bob's.

**B2. Which texts the beta carries (scope).** Texts are needed per kind of action and per purpose (`jurisdictions` R81: `initial`, `immediate_disclosure`, `follow_up`, `reconsideration` ...). The Oakland profile's Tier 1 kinds are the records request, the complaint to the civil grand jury, the referral to the State Controller, public comment to a body and media outreach; Tier 2 includes the demand under the Brown Act and a court petition to enforce a records request (R28: never a template until a licensed professional certifies, v0.2 §8). *Options:* **S-1** the records request only (its templates and its guide); **S-2** S-1 plus the grand jury, Controller, public comment and media (Tier 1); **S-3** everything Tier 1 and 2 except court petitions. *Recommendation:* S-1 for the beta: it is the kind the journey is built around, `@records-request` is the one named in the ruling, and each added kind multiplies reviewer time. Others go in as writer and reviewer are found, each its own small release. Court petitions: none.

**B3. The professional reviewer (money, liability, independence).** A reviewer is a licensed professional (for the records request: a lawyer who works on public-records law) who reads the final text and its sources and gives `no_concerns`, `concerns` or `changes_requested` on that exact text (the review is bound to the text's SHA; any later change needs a new review, `REVIEW_STALE`). The product records the reviewer's name, organisation, and the credential **as the reviewer states it, never verified** (`filing-templates` R9). *Questions that are Bob's:* who the reviewer is; whether the review is a gift or paid, and by whom (Believe in Oakland?) which is a money decision; whether the reviewer is independent of any group that will use the text; whether the reviewer agrees to a public line "reviewed by [name], [organisation], [date]" which every member who opens the template sees. *Options for the arrangement:* **V-1** a volunteer open-government attorney, named; **V-2** a paid review by a firm; **V-3** two reviewers (one for the text, one for the sources), recommended only for Tier 2. *Recommendation:* V-1 or V-2 as Bob finds one; the product accepts either, because it records what the reviewer states and nothing more.

**B4. The writer, and machines (doctrine).** The machinery already refuses a machine as author or reviewer of a template (`MACHINE_CANNOT_DRAFT_TEMPLATE`, `MACHINE_CANNOT_REVIEW_TEMPLATE`): "a machine proposes wording and nothing else". The process below applies the same to the profile's texts: the writer is a named person; if any wording was first proposed by a machine, the writer says so in the text's notes and is still the writer. *Questions that are Bob's:* the writer's name shown to every member (attribution), or "Believe in Oakland's staff" with a named person recorded; whether a member of the team that benefits may be the writer. *Recommendation:* a named person, shown; not a member of any listing group; machine help declared in the notes.

**B5. Keeping a text true (policy).** Laws and agencies' pages change. A reviewed text is true on its review date. *Options:* **M-1** re-review at every profile release that touches the kind; **M-2** re-review when a source's page changes (the link check, below, flags it); **M-3** a yearly re-review. *Recommendation:* M-1 and M-2: a release is when the profile is signed, and a changed source is when the text can be wrong. Who owns this is Bob's: until someone is named, a text older than 12 months at a release is shown to members with its review date and a plain "last checked" line (UX need). That line is a requirement of `screens-action` and is listed in section 3.

## 3. The process (the people's steps; BOB to place it in the repository's docs; not a requirement of any module)

**The text process, for a template or a procedure guide in a jurisdiction profile:**
1. **Choose.** Bob names the kind and purpose (B2). Nothing is written for a kind whose venue is a court (R28).
2. **Source sheet first.** The writer lists, before writing, every public source the text will rest on: citation, the address where one exists, the date it was read. Sources are public law and agencies' own published material only; nothing confidential, nothing from a member's record. Each item of a guide, and each legal statement of a template, traces to a source on the sheet.
3. **Write.** In plain words; blanks only from `FILING_BLANKS`; the text takes no position on policy (v0.2 rule 9); it is legal information, not advice; no place named in product code (the text lives in the profile, which is data). A template's `text` and a guide's `items` are what the writer produces; nothing else is a text.
4. **Self-check by machine.** The release checks reject: a blank outside `FILING_BLANKS` (`TEMPLATE_BLANK_UNKNOWN`), a court petition (`TEMPLATE_COURT_PETITION`), a guide item with no `citation` (`GUIDE_ITEM_UNSOURCED`), a guide that carries conduct (`GUIDE_CARRIES_CONDUCT`), a text with no source (`VALUE_INVALID`).
5. **Professional review** (B3) of the exact text and its sheet. A change after review needs a new review.
6. **Approval** by a person other than the writer: Bob (section 2, item 6), with his K ruling.
7. **Entry.** A module job writes the text into the profile with its attribution (section 4's fields). The profile release is signed as every release is. Nothing is offered by the machinery until all three exist in the profile's data, however the text was obtained.
8. **Keep true** (B5).

**What the machinery offers until texts exist** (all built, drafted here only to be stated and tested): `templatesForAction` answers no template and lists the profile's unreviewed or absent ones under `not_offered` with `lacks`; a court-filed kind answers `court_petition: true`; a member may write her filing with no template (K921, K924); a team may write its own template (project-scoped, reviewed by its members, with its own tier rule, `filing-templates` R3-R10) and its own guide (`reading-guides` R2, R3: another member approves; citations required); the Tier 3 counsel packet needs no template; the profile's laws, deadlines and offices are shown with their citations as facts (`screens-action` R31 (3)); the action screen says "No profile text for this yet" (UX need) rather than offering a generic fallback (DEC-340: "no generic fallback").
