## C. BOB's points (lower level; DEC-404 gives them to BOB)

**C1. How layers stack.** (K5452 settles that a team may hold several layers of one level; what remains is BOB's: the shape and its cost.)
- A (recommended). **A forest of chains.** Each layer names the layer it sits in (`within`, a layer of a higher level); the federal layer is the one root; below it any number of layers of any level. A state-wide footprint is a state layer with no county or city layer; a region is several county layers in one state; a footprint of a state and one city of another state is two branches; a city may sit directly in its state when no county layer is held (a team's own city), or in the federal layer when its state is not held. All active layers combine side by side, nothing overrides anything; a rule may count on a closure list a layer in its own branch holds. Names are layer-qualified (`<layer id>:<name>`), so two cities each holding a list called `city` cannot collide. Matches "a place is four layers", "only its city" and K5452's "any set of places". Lines R99, R101, R102, R104, R105, R113, `instance-setup` R107.
- **What A costs.** (1) The three refusals of a chain grow to seven (`LAYER_ID_TWICE`, `LAYER_FEDERAL_TWICE`, `LAYER_PARENT_MISSING`, `LAYER_PARENT_LEVEL`, `LAYER_CYCLE`, `LAYER_NO_FEDERAL`, `NAME_AMBIGUOUS`), each checked for every branch, and a `within` field. (2) A name-resolution rule: a bare name resolves to its own layer's, else its branch's, else the only active layer that gives it, else `NAME_AMBIGUOUS`; any module that stores a rule or list name must also be able to store the layer. (3) A team-wide one-value fact (time zone, weekend, locale) stated differently by two branches is withheld at team level while each rule is read in its own branch's value; a screen must say so. (4) The places screen draws a tree, not a line (E1), and the choice is a set, not three steps (E2). (5) Tests grow: the test forest (R118), the shipped layers in every combination (R113), the time of `combine` with 1, 3 and 8 layers (F5, F11).
- B. A flat set: any layers, no parent, each self-contained. Simplest in code (as today), several counties are trivial, but each layer must repeat the weekend and any list it counts on, and a team could pick Oakland without California.
- C. Precedence by level. DEC-404 says no ("not overriding").
- Sub-point, now settled by K5452: several layers of one level are allowed. Not yet settled: whether a team's several counties may be saved as a named footprint for reuse (a package, R114); not drafted.

**C2. A disagreement within one key** (two layers give different values to one thing, such as a zone, a kind's tier, a holiday year of one list).
- A (recommended as the rule). Withhold the key and report both values with their layers; consumers answer "undetermined" (today's R15). Never guess a law.
- B (recommended as the team's way out, lines to be written if chosen). The administrator records which layer's value the team follows for that key, with a reason; the choice is shown beside the value and edits no layer.
- C. The nearer layer wins; or D. the stricter value wins (shorter period). Both are overriding or ranking; C contradicts DEC-404.
- With it: a build check that the layers Civicsmith ships never disagree with each other (R113), so a disagreement can only come from a team's own entry (R117).

**C3. Who confirms a team's own layer.** A (recommended): each entry by a second member as today's rules (K3935), the qualification statement for Tier 2 and 3; a screen can confirm several in one visit. B: the layer approved whole by two administrators. C: none. Recommended A because it extends a ruled practice (DEC-340).

## D. Bob's points (policy, doctrine, what a requirement means)

DEC-404 answered: four layers, who ships what, read-only, report not edit, side by side, own research serving only the team. These three remain.

**D1. What Civicsmith promises about how current the layers are.** A law changes, an office is renamed, a year's holiday list is published late (in October 2026 neither the City's nor the court's 2027 list was published: `jurisdictions` R85). A layer is a snapshot that Civicsmith researched on a day; the team's deadlines rest on it.
- A (recommended). Every layer says, in plain words beside each rule, where it comes from ("read from <source> on <date>") and how far its calendars reach; a count past that is shown as "not known", never guessed. Civicsmith promises the day it read, not that the law has not changed, and says "not legal advice". Updates arrive with releases, and an administrator is told once which rules changed.
- B. A stated promise of a delay (for example "within 30 days of a change"). Needs people and a process Civicsmith may not have; a broken promise on a deadline is the worst failure.
- C. No dates shown. Cheap; a team cannot judge a rule.
Recommended A; it is what the draft's lines build (R108, `instance-setup` R108, `queue-producers` R58).

**D2. What "read-only" means for a team that knows a shipped fact is wrong today.** Reporting takes time; a deadline or a holiday may be wrong now. Today a member can correct a holiday year, an office's hours or the zone for her own team, marked as hers, with how she checked it and a source (K921, K925). DEC-404 says a team does not edit a read-only layer.
- A (recommended). The layer is never changed. For the calendar facts (holiday dates, hours, the zone) the team's own marked correction stays, because a closed office is a fact the team can see and a count must follow it; for laws and deadlines, the team can only report. The team can add a rule of its own under a different name beside the shipped one (side by side), never over it. Draft R25 builds A.
- B. Reports only, for everything; corrections of calendar facts go. Matches the words "does not edit" most strictly; a team with a wrong holiday waits for a release.
- C. The team may apply its own value over a shipped one, marked. Overrides: contradicts DEC-404.

**D3. A team whose work is focused outside the United States. ANSWERED, K5497 (C7 remainder), option A, "as recommended".** (K5452: a team merely LOCATED abroad, with its work focused on a United States jurisdiction, chooses the United States places of its work as any team does.) The four levels (federal, state, county, city) and the federal layer are the United States'; Civicsmith holds United States places only. A team whose work is in another country has no layer to choose; today it gets nothing filled in (DEC-150), and the draft preselects the federal layer for every team, removable. A team working in both (some United States places and some abroad) gets the layers of the United States places it names and names the rest as free-text places.
- Ruling (A): Civicsmith ships United States places only; the team is told so when choosing its places; a team whose work is outside the United States names its places (the free-text place) and adds their offices and rules itself, as its own research (DEC-150); the level names are unchanged (DEC-404). Applied in `installer` R94 (installer) and R107 (instance-setup) and `jurisdictions` R109. Option B (renaming the levels) was not chosen.
