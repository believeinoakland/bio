## 2. case-grammar (highest bold id R34; draft ids R35 to R36)

- **R35** (T53; N1259; K4403 (4); K1483 (a); amends R12; K2206's and G47's precedents) *(not yet met: T53)* **A cited register row travels as a labelled extract, and the source file does not.** A material row stays flat (K549: a block is rows of scalars and inline lists; `materialsLines` writes `MATERIAL_FIELDS` then optional flat fields) and keeps `kind` `document` (`MATERIAL_KINDS` is `document`, `observation`, pinned by `casefile.test.mjs:72` and read by the checker's specification test). A row for a cited register row states, as optional flat fields after `title`, as `obscured_copy` and its kin are: `extract_register`, `extract_file`, `extract_reduced_sha256`, `extract_original_sha256`, `extract_original_covers`, `extract_source_date`, `extract_imported_at`, `extract_src_row`, `extract_kept_columns`, `extract_roles` (the role of each kept column, `register-readers`' vocabulary), `extract_dropped_columns` (inline lists of names), `extract_header` and `extract_row` (inline lists of the kept columns' names and the cited row's kept cells, verbatim, as the segment holds them), `extract_label_key` (`register.extract`) and, for a limited register, R36's three `extract_use_limit_*` fields; the names are BOB's. The row's `sha` is the reduced segment's SHA-256, `text_sha` the SHA-256 of the extract text (`extract_header` and `extract_row` joined as the job states), `archived_copy` null, and `included` false whatever it is handed, as an `obscured` row's is (R12: the file is not carried; the extract is, in the row), `ref` the row's own (its content id), so two rows cited from one segment are two rows of one `sha` and distinct `ref`s (the checker joins files and attestations by `ref` and `sha`, `case-checker` R2, R3). The SHA-256s are 64 hex; `source_date` and `imported_at` are dates; `src_row` the row's number in the original piece. `materialsOf` reads the fields back as `extract: {…}` (null when none is stated). A document stating any extract field declares the next case-document format (`bio-case-document/10`, by the predicate `caseDocumentFormatFor` that declares `/9` for an attestation `sha`) and one stating none keeps `/8` or `/9` and its bytes, because a checker pinned before the fields would ask R8 for the segment's bytes and give a wrong verdict; `case-checker` R14's specification and `casefilespec` (R15) name it. K1483's "relied-on documents travel whole" is, for a register row only, "the cited rows travel as an extract; the file is not shipped" (Bob, K4403 (4)). `registerExtractRow(args)` builds the row and `materialsOf` reads it back; both pure and never throwing. A malformed extract is refused `EXTRACT_INVALID` naming the field. Test: a built row round-trips through `materialsOf`; each field absent, a short digest, a non-date `source_date` and a `src_row` of 0 are `EXTRACT_INVALID`; two rows cited from one segment are two rows of one `sha`; a document with an extract declares `/10` and one without keeps its bytes (the existing goldens, `complete-v7-golden.json` among them, are byte-equal). Control: a `document` row and an `obscured` row are read as before.
- **R36** (T53; N1259; K4403 (3)) *(not yet met: T53)* **A cited row of a limited register carries its limit.** When the register has a `use_limit` (`jurisdictions` R131), the row states `extract_use_limit_key`, `extract_use_limit_citation` and `extract_use_limit_text_key` (flat, R35; the case is read without the profile) and `materialsOf` reads them back as `extract.use_limit` `{key, citation, text_key}`. Test: the limited fixture's extract holds the limit; a limit-free one holds none. Control: removing the limit from a limited extract makes the checker's R30 below fail.

## 3. case-disclosures (highest bold id R34; draft ids R35 to R38)

- **R35** (T53; N1259; K1483 (a); amends R24) *(not yet met: T53)* **Every person named in a cited register row is a person the case names.** `peopleNamed` also lists, once each, every person named by a cell of a cited row's extract whose `extract_roles` entry is `name` (a donor, payee, lobbyist, filer, licensee or officer; `case-grammar` R35), with the place `register row <ref>`; a name the record does not resolve to a person entity is answered in `unresolved` with that place, never dropped and never resolved here. `peopleJudged` (R25) then asks a recorded basis for each, as for any named person, and a person named in a cited row has a seventh recorded reason, `named_in_cited_filing` ("named in the public filing the case cites", K5503), which the cited row itself proves: the case's cited row names the person in a `name` cell, recorded and checked like the other six; an organization in a row is not a person and is not asked. Test: a case citing two rows naming "A" and "B" lists both with their places; one without a basis is `PERSON_BASIS_UNRECORDED` naming the person to the publisher only; the reason `named_in_cited_filing` is accepted for "A" only while a cited row names "A" (a row removed, or never citing "A", is `PERSON_BASIS_UNRECORDED`); it cannot be recorded for a person no cited row names; a row naming an organization adds no person. Control: a case citing no register row lists the same persons as before.
- **R36** (T53; N1259; K1493; K1485; DEC-408; amends R26) *(not yet met: T53)* **A phone number or street address typed into the group's own words or an extract is refused before signing; a recorded public-office basis is the only way through.** Pre-flight runs `register-readers.contactShape` (a new `modules.json` edge from `case-disclosures` to `register-readers`, both earlier layers, BOB's) through a new `contactJudged(prepared, parts, contactBases, viewer)` called as `peopleJudged` is, over every authored statement, claim, account sentence, the text of an observation a member authored, `title` and each extract field's cells (UNVERIFIED: an observation is a material, R6, and relied-on materials travel whole under R26 and DEC-100 (2); R26 as written does not separate a member's own authored observation from a captured document, and the DEC-408 test below needs it to). A hit not listed in the owner's `contactBases: [{text, basis: "public_office", ref}]` (`ref` a held capture that states the number or address as a public office's own) is `CONTACT_SHAPE_IN_PUBLICATION`, naming where it is to the publisher only; a listed hit whose `ref` the record does not hold is `CONTACT_BASIS_NOT_STANDING`. The refusals are pre-flight answers and are never written into the document. The materials a finding relies on still travel whole (R26 / DEC-100 (2)): this rule governs the group's words and extracts, not relied-on documents. The detector's choices (what shape counts, the override) are BOB's, because a rule that refuses an office's address, or lets a home number through, is wrong either way; the false-positive rate is M-R10 (e §3). Test (the DEC-408 publication test): a sentinel phone and a sentinel street address in a member's note, turned into an observation by the member and cited in a case, are refused at pre-flight; the same sentinel in a question's text and in an extract cell are refused; a city hall address listed with a `public_office` basis passes; an unlisted one is refused; a sentinel in a `people` address fact is never passed to the document (R26). Control: the case without the sentinels passes.
- **R37** (T53; N1259; K4403 (3); K1492 (5); 11 CFR 104.15; `jurisdictions` R131) *(not yet met: T53)* **A case citing a row of a limited register meets the register's `on_publish`.** With `warn` (this release's value), pre-flight answers `USE_LIMIT_NOTICE` naming the register and the limit's citation, which the owner acknowledges (`limit_acknowledged: [register]`) by a member's act recorded with who and when, and the published case carries the notice by key beside the extract; with `refuse` it is `USE_LIMIT_REFUSES_PUBLICATION`; with `allow` nothing is asked, and the notice is still carried. - **R38** (T53; N1259; K1483 (a)) *(not yet met: T53)* **A register extract is bounded.** A case carries at most `EXTRACT_ROWS_MAX` cited rows from registers (a draft figure, 500, BOB's) in total; a case over it is `EXTRACT_TOO_MANY` naming the count and the bound, nothing partly signed. ## 4. case-checker (highest bold id R28; draft ids R29 to R32)

- **R29** (T53; N1259; K4403 (4); amends R4, R8, R14) *(not yet met: T53)* **The checker checks a register extract against what it can see, and says what it cannot.** For each material row stating extract fields (`case-grammar` R35, read by `materialsOf` as `extract`) it checks: (a) the row's `text_sha` equals the SHA-256 of the carried extract text, and the extract's header and row have the same number of cells; (b) every passage a finding relies on from the row is found in the extract text, and its `content_id` recomputes by `content.contentIdFor` from the reduced segment's `sha`, the extent and the chain; (c) `reduced_sha256`, `original_sha256`, `source_date`, `imported_at` and `src_row` are present and well formed; (d) the label `register.extract` is present; (e) when `use_limit` is held on the register's row, the notice is present (R30 below). A failure is a `differs` entry naming the field; a missing extract is `missing`. R8's presentability is amended as it was for an `obscured` row: a row stating extract fields is presentable, with `included: false`, when its extract is carried in the row, and neither the source file's bytes nor the reduced file's are asked (R8 otherwise answers a load-bearing chain reaching an `included: false` row a `differs` entry); the specification (R14, `casefilespec` R15) names `bio-case-document/10` and the extract fields. Test: each failure above is a `differs` or `missing` by name on a changed byte, a moved cell, a short digest, a removed label; a good extract passes. Control: a changed byte in an unrelated material still fails as before.
- **R30** (T53; N1259; K4403 (3)) *(not yet met: T53)* **A limited register's notice must be present.** An extract whose register had a `use_limit` and whose document does not carry the notice (`text_key`, by key) is `differs` naming it. Test: remove the notice, fail; restore, pass. Control: a limit-free extract needs none.
- **R31** (T53; N1259; K4403 (4); DEC-112; amends R1) *(not yet met: T53)* **The checker says plainly that the source file is not in the package.** Its answer for a case with extracts carries a key `unverifiable: [{ref, what: "row_in_source_file", why}]` for each extract: that the row exists in the file the fingerprints name cannot be re-derived from the package, and a reader who fetches the source can compare the original's fingerprint only if the source is unchanged (the original is re-fetched as it then is). "Recreating shows a case intact and consistent, not true." Test: a case with two extracts lists two `unverifiable` entries and the standalone program prints them after the findings; a case with none has no `unverifiable` key (the answer's key set is pinned, `check.test.mjs:23`). Control: the other checks' answers are unchanged.
- **R32** (T53; N1259) *(not yet met: T53)* **The standalone program is rebuilt with these checks.** R13's single file includes R29-R31 from the same code; the same build gives the same bytes. (`program.mjs` is a committed generated artifact, `build/manifest.md`: regenerated by `node bio-plane/src/case-checker/build-program.mjs` and verified by `program.test.mjs` at every merge.) Test: the program run on a fixture case with an extract prints R29-R31's results; its SHA-256 equals the rebuild's. Control: a case with no extract gives the former output byte for byte.

## 6. corpus-export (highest bold id R15; draft id R16)

- **R16** (T53; N1259; K4403 (2), (3); K1493; amends R9) *(not yet met: T53)* **A register is exported as its descriptors and tombstones, never as rows; a limited register's rows in no form.** For each import the export carries `{register, scope, filter, filter_fingerprint, source, source_date, imported_at, counts, segment_shas, original_fingerprints, reduction}` and each tombstone `registers` lists (table, key, ground, instant; none of the removed content); it carries no cell, no index row, no segment and no row of a register with a `use_limit`, and the export log records it. `corpus-export` R7 already carries a table declared `export: never` with no row, named in the manifest, and a `derived-rebuildable` one as its rule, so the descriptors are `registers`' `export: yes` tables and the cells and index rows need no code here. What R7 does not cover is the segments: a reduced segment is filed as a capture in a bundle (part (a) R125 and R133, part (b) R14), and R1 lists every bundle's files and R13 treats every registered capture, so a segment would travel as rows. This line therefore adds: R1 and R13 skip a capture whose provenance document states `origin: register_import` and the manifest names only its SHA-256, in the descriptor's `segment_shas` (UNVERIFIED: where the segments' bundle lives; and whether R3's byte-compare of every registered capture on import needs the same exemption). An import of an export re-creates the descriptors and shows "rows not carried: bring them in again from the source" and where a removal was; it restores no row and never a tombstoned key. Test: an export of a fixture instance with two imports holds two descriptors, one tombstone and no cell (a scan for the fixture's cells finds none); importing it shows both descriptors and the tombstone and no row; a limited register's descriptor holds no cell either. Control: other exported tables are carried as declared.
