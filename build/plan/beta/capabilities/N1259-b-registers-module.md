## Purpose

Holds the public registers a team has imported, whole or in part, by fixed steps with no AI: lists the registers known (from the profile) and the team's own; plans an import (a filter prefilled from the project, the delta against what the record holds, a time estimate); runs it in the background as resumable slices; keeps the rows searchably and the captures they came from; cites a row as a labelled extract; compares two imports; keeps the filter a project states as its interest so that change-watching is focused; holds no fact, score or judgment about anyone.

## Provides (names are the interface; the ops are (e) §1)

`registerList`, `registerPropose`, `registerDefine`, `registerFilter`, `registerDelta`, `importStart` (scope project, or team = the pre-read), `importStatus`, `importSet` (pause, resume, stop), `importDue`/`importWake`/`importTick` (for `scheduler`), `searchRows`, `rowOf`, `citeRow`, `compare`, `interestSet`/`interestOf`/`interestForget`, `refreshImport` and `changesSince` (for `following`), `rowExpunge`; the registrations `registerRowContext` (to `content`) and `registerHeldAs` (to `extraction`); `PRODUCT_PHASE`.

## Uses (earlier layers only; checked against `modules.json` order)

Not used: any `ai-*`, `agent-*`, `skills`, `retrieval` (register rows are not passages), `monitoring`, `scheduler`, `following` (later layers; they reach this module). New `modules.json` edges: `scheduler`, `following`, `notice-producers`, `corpus-export`, `case-disclosures`, `plane`, `control-plane`, `op-declarations`, `answer-envelope`, `journey-check` use `registers` (BOB's, P17).

**Terms.** *Held rows* are the kept cells of rows in the imports the viewer may see. *Scope*: `project` (an import for one project; its sight is the project's) or `team` (a pre-read; its sight is the team's). An *interest* is a filter a member attaches to a project and a register. *Slice*: one bounded unit of an import's work. Refusal codes are drafts; words are by `setup-words` key (design supplies the rows; a missing sentence is a REPORT, not inline English).

## 1. The listing and the doors

Sorted by name; the same call answers the same list. Reads the profile and this module's tables; fetches nothing; writes nothing. Test: the test profile's registers appear with name, source, URL and figures whose basis is shown; a figure with `not_stated` shows a null value, never 0; a register the viewer's team holds an import of shows it; the list is equal on two calls. ## 2. Planning an import: the filter and the delta

- **R8** (T53; N1259; K4403 (2); `register-readers` R6, R8) *(not yet met: T53)* **`registerFilter({register, project?, viewer})`: the filter, prefilled from the project and said so.** With a `project` the viewer may see, answers `{ok, filter, prefilled: true, basis, left_out, interest?}`: `filter` from `register-readers.prefillFrom` over the project's named subjects and identifiers (read through `entities`, only those the viewer may see), `basis` each criterion's source fact, `left_out` the criteria the register cannot apply, `interest` the project's saved interest on this register if any (R30). With no project, or one with no usable facts, `{}` (the whole register). The result is validated (`validateFilter`); it is not saved; it is a proposal the member edits. Nothing is inferred from a project's text; a person or organization enters only where the project already names it as a subject. Test: a project with a subject organization, a filer id and a period prefills `organizations`, `filer_ids` and `date` with their basis; the project's address fact and notes add nothing; an unseen project is `NO_SUCH_PROJECT` (one answer); no project gives `{}`. Control: an edit then validates or is refused by `validateFilter`'s code.
- **R9** (T53; N1259; K4403 (2); K4405 (6)) *(not yet met: T53)* **`registerDelta({register, filter, scope, project?, budget_ms, viewer})`: what the import would add, before anything is brought in, each figure saying counted or estimated.** Answers `{ok, new_rows, already_held, bytes_in, bytes_stored, seconds, shelf, source_date, probe, timed_out}`. `already_held` counts the rows matching the filter in the imports the viewer may see (counted from the index). `new_rows` is `{value, basis}`: `counted` only when `acquisition.probeRegister` (a R128) counted the rows for this same filter and the definition says the source is `append_only` (rows are never changed or removed at the source), the value being source rows less `already_held`, floored at 0; otherwise `estimated` from `register-readers.estimate` and the last import's selectivity (rows kept per row read), or `not_stated`. `bytes_in` the bytes the import would read (`stated` from the probe or the sum of pieces, else `not_stated`); `bytes_stored` estimated from rows and the bytes per row of the last import of this register, else of the definition's sample; `seconds` `{value, low, high, basis: "estimated"}` from `bytes_in` over the register's throughput, the median of its last three imports (`took_ms` per byte) or the constant `THROUGHPUT_DEFAULT` set from M-R7, never `counted` and presented by the design as "about"; `shelf` `{free_bytes, would_use_pct, warn}` from the storage port (R22). It makes the governed probe requests only (no body), writes nothing, records nothing, and stops at `budget_ms` (default 1,200) with `timed_out: true` and what it has, never a guess. Refusals as `importStart` for the register (R3-R5, `FILTER_*`). Test: a fake source with a count query and `append_only` gives `new_rows` counted and equal to count less held; with a name in the filter (unsupported by the count) it gives estimated; a source with no `Content-Length` gives `bytes_in` not_stated; a slow probe ends at the budget with `timed_out`; seconds equal the arithmetic and carry `estimated`; nothing is written (row counts of every table equal before and after). Control: a register whose last three imports are held uses their throughput.
- **R10** (T53; N1259; K4405 (6); DEC-411) *(not yet met: T53)* **An estimate is recorded against its outcome, so the next one is better.** An import records `took_ms`, `bytes_in` and `rows_in` when it ends; `registerDelta` uses them as R9 says. No estimate is ever shown as exact, and an estimate of `seconds` is never below the time already spent on a running import's last slice. Test: after three fixture imports of different speeds the next estimate equals the median rate's arithmetic; a failed import contributes none. Control: with none held the constant is used and `basis` says so.

## 3. The import

- **R11** (T53; N1259; K4403 (2); K4405 (3); K1449, K1492) *(not yet met: T53)* **`importStart({register, filter, scope, project?, author, viewer})` (`op=registerimport`; scope `team` is the pre-read, `op=registerpreread`): a member's act that queues a background import and fetches nothing itself.** Refusals in order, each writing nothing: a machine or absent author `MACHINE_CANNOT_IMPORT`; `NO_SUCH_REGISTER`; `REGISTER_NOT_IN_ALPHA` (R3); `REGISTER_NOT_MEASURED` (R4); `member_act_required` (R5); `FILTER_*` (`register-readers` R6); scope `team` by anyone but an active administrator `NOT_AN_ADMIN` (K4405 (3)); scope `project` without a visible project the author takes part in `NO_SUCH_PROJECT` (absent and unseen alike); `IMPORT_QUEUE_FULL` when `MAX_ACTIVE_IMPORTS` (default 2) are already queued or running in this instance. An import of the same register, filter fingerprint and scope that is already queued or running answers `existed: true` with it. Otherwise it writes the import `{id, register, filter, filter_fingerprint, scope, project, state: queued, author, queued_at}`, tells `scheduler` (R9's notice, so an idle instance arms its alarm), and answers within the act's time, never waiting for a byte. Test: each refusal by name with a control; a machine credential is refused; two identical starts leave one import; a third concurrent import is `IMPORT_QUEUE_FULL`; the fake network sees no request during the act; an idle instance has its alarm armed afterwards. Control: a member's project import lands.
- **R12** (T53; N1259; K4403 (2)) *(not yet met: T53)* **The import runs as resumable slices on the scheduler's alarm and ends with the same rows as an uninterrupted run.** `importDue(now)` is true while any import is queued, running or retrying and the host is not paused; `importWake(now)` the earliest instant one is due; `importTick(now, budget)` claims the oldest due import under the host's epoch (a retry never runs a slice twice), runs at most one slice (bounded by `budget_ms` and bytes, `register-readers` and `acquisition.streamRegister`, R125-R127), records progress, and answers `{import, state, progress, done}`. States: `queued`, `fetching`, `reading` (reducing and indexing the arrived rows), `indexed` (done), `failed` (with `reason` and `retries`), `paused` (with `reason`: `member`, `shelf`, `host_paused`), `stopped`. A restart between slices re-derives from the import's table and cursor; a slice that fails is retried up to 3 times with growing delay, then `failed` with its reason, and a new `importStart` makes a new import. Test: an import killed between each pair of slices of a 1M-row fixture ends with the same row-table digest, counts and segment digests as an uninterrupted run; a paused host runs no slice (`paused: true` answered); the slice respects its bound on the 1M-row fixture; `failed` carries the reason and the retry count. Control: with nothing queued `importDue` is false and `importWake` null.
- **R13** (T53; N1259; K4403 (2)) *(not yet met: T53)* **`importStatus({import, viewer})` and `importSet({import, action, author, viewer})`: progress to the member, and pause, resume or stop.** `importStatus` answers the import's state, its pieces (each `pending|reading|done|failed`), `progress` `{bytes_in, rows_in, rows_out, segments_stored, percent?}` (`percent` only when bytes are `stated`), `started_at`, `finished_at`, `took_ms`, `source_date`, `reduction` (R15), `reason` and `retries`; for an import the viewer may not see it answers `NO_SUCH_IMPORT`, the same as an absent one. `importSet` with `pause`, `resume` or `stop` is the starter's or an active administrator's act (`NOT_YOUR_IMPORT` otherwise; a machine author refused); `stop` ends the import, keeps what was brought in (segments stored and rows indexed so far) and says so (`stopped`, with counts); `resume` on `stopped` is refused `IMPORT_STOPPED` (start a new import). Test: status of a running fixture import shows the pieces and progress; pause then the next tick runs nothing; resume continues from the cursor; stop keeps the stored segments and their rows searchable and the state reads `stopped`; another member's hidden import is `NO_SUCH_IMPORT`. Control: stopping twice answers `existed: true`.
- **R14** (T53; N1259; K4403 (2)) *(not yet met: T53)* **Every import is a capture with its source and date.** Each segment of each piece is filed through `acquisition` and `promotion` (R125, R133) with the import as authority; the import row binds each piece to its segments and captures, and states per piece the source address, the retrieved-at instant, the source's own stated date (`Last-Modified`, the dataset's `updatedAt` or the file's own date, else `not_stated`) and the original's fingerprint (`covers` whole file or named member). The import's `imported_at` is the oldest piece's retrieval; its `source_date` the oldest piece's stated date. Test: an import of a 2-piece fixture shows both pieces with address, retrieved-at, stated date and fingerprint; `imported_at` equals the older retrieval; a piece with no stated date shows `not_stated`, never the retrieval time. Control: a second import of the same bytes shows `existed` captures and its own date.
- **R15** (T53; N1259; K4403 (5); K1485; DEC-408) *(not yet met: T53)* **The reduced copy on arrival is stated, never silent.** The import's `reduction` is `{kept_columns, dropped_columns: [{name, why}], withheld_cells, rows_in, rows_out, rows_undetermined, original_fingerprints, reason: "reduced under K1485"}` (names and counts only, no value) and the listing, status and every extract state "phone and address columns are left out as it arrives" by key (`register.reduced`). Test: a sentinel-bearing fixture import shows the dropped column names; no sentinel value appears in the import row, the status, the listing or any answer. Control: a register with no contact columns shows an empty `dropped_columns` for contact roles.
- **R16** (T53; N1259; K4403 (2)) *(not yet met: T53)* **Importing is mechanical: no AI call is made.** `registers` and `register-readers` list no `ai-*`, `agent-*` or `skills` module in `Uses`; a complete cycle (plan, delta, import, search, cite, compare, interest, refresh, watch change) on the fixtures makes zero calls through the AI boundary and leaves the `ai-use` ledger and every AI account's counters unchanged. Test: a spy on the AI port and on requests to any AI provider host records zero calls across the cycle; `modules.json` `uses` of both modules hold no AI module. Control: the spy is shown to count a call made by a deliberately AI-using fixture module.
- **R17** (T53; N1259; K4403 (2)) *(not yet met: T53)* **A retry or a second run never duplicates a row or a capture.** Rows are written by `(register, row_key, content_hash, sight scope)`; segments are content-addressed; a slice repeated after a failure writes nothing the first did. Test: a slice run twice leaves one index row per key and one object per segment digest. Control: a changed row writes one more version.
- **R18** (T53; N1259; K1492 (1), (4)) *(not yet met: T53)* **An import is the source's own fetch, polite and attributed.** Every request of a slice is `acquisition`'s (host-governor pacing, R9's agent naming the instance and purpose `register`, the definition's hosts only); a `HOST_COOLING_OFF` pauses the import with reason `host_cooling` and retries after `retry_in_ms`, not counted as a failure. Test: a fake governor cooling off pauses the import and resumes after the wait; the user agent of every request names the purpose. Control: an uncooled run is unchanged.
- **R19** (T53; N1259; K1489; K4403 (2), (7)) *(not yet met: T53)* **An import is seen as its scope is: a hidden project's import, its filter and its rows stay inside it.** A `project` import and its segments take the sight of the project (`membership`'s fence); a `team` import the team's. No list, count, delta, search, compare, status, extract, queue item or error names, counts or lets one infer an import, filter or interest the viewer may not see: `already_held` counts only visible rows; a row first stored in a hidden project's import is stored again (its own version) by a team import, not shared. Test: with a hidden project's import holding sentinel names, a non-participant's `registerList`, `registerDelta` (counts equal to a record without it), `searchRows`, `compare`, `importStatus`, `changesSince` and a team pre-read's delta show no trace; a participant's show it. Control: a team import is visible to every member.
- **R20** (T53; N1259; K4403 (2); K4354) *(not yet met: T53)* **An import is never in the way of the member's screens.** A slice's transaction batches are bounded so that no single write holds the object's one worker longer than `SLICE_TX_MS` (a draft figure, set with PLATFORM, 100 ms), and a slice yields between batches; no read op waits for a slice. Test: during a 1M-row fixture import, `registerList` and `searchRows` on a small register answer within their budgets; no transaction exceeds the bound (instrumented). Control: with no import running the same reads are no faster than within noise.

## 4. Storage

- **R21** (T53; N1259; K4403 (2) "storage designed for large datasets"; K1727's pattern) *(not yet met: T53)* **A row version is stored once; a re-import writes only what is new or changed; every version seen is kept.** For a sight scope, the cells of a row version live once, in the reduced segment of the import that first saw that version, and the index holds the row's terms (`register-readers` R5), `row_key`, content hash and a pointer (segment, row number, byte range); a later import of the same register and scope writes an index row and a segment row only for a key not held or whose content hash differs, and records `unchanged` for the rest (reported as `stored_rows`, `unchanged_rows`); an older version stays addressable by its import. Bytes stored per import are the delta's. Test: a second identical import writes zero index rows and zero segments beyond the descriptor; one changed row writes one; the old version is still returned by `rowOf` for the first import; the counts equal the arithmetic. Control: a different scope stores its own copy (R19).
- **R22** (T53; N1259; K4403 (2)) *(not yet met: T53)* **The row index is a rebuildable, bounded table behind one storage port.** The index is declared to `record-core` with `derive: derived-rebuildable` and a `rebuild(scope)` that recomputes it from the stored segments; `rebuildAndCompare` is `{same: true}` after any import sequence. The module reaches the index and the segments' rows only through one port (`rowStore`: put a delta, find by terms and range, get a row, list by key range, count, free bytes, rebuild); a contract test suite runs unchanged against every implementation of the port (an in-memory one and the team object's SQLite one; a partitioned one, if the platform design adopts it, must pass the same suite). Tables are declared with classes: index `purge: clear`, `expunge: tombstone`, `export: never`, `sight: source`; import and interest rows `export: yes` (descriptors, R41); proposals and own definitions `sight: group`. The module's choice among stores is the storage options in (e) §5; no requirement here depends on it except R20, R23 and R24's bounds. Test: after a random import sequence `rebuildAndCompare` is `{same: true}`; the contract suite passes on both implementations; every table is declared. Control: a deliberately corrupted index row makes `rebuildAndCompare` `{same: false, first}`.
- **R23** (T53; N1259; K4403 (2); K4354) *(not yet met: T53)* **Storage is bounded in memory and in time, and refuses before it overruns.** A slice holds at most `SLICE_MEMORY_MB` (set from M-R7; a draft 32 MB) beyond the reader's two windows. Before a slice writes, the module asks the port for free bytes; when the import's estimated stored bytes would pass the object's warning mark (the Settings screen's 70%) it pauses the import with reason `shelf` and the figures, and an administrator's resume is refused while it would pass 90% (`SHELF_NEARLY_FULL`); nothing is partly written. The row index answers a point lookup and a range query in time that does not grow with the number of imports held (an index, not a scan), shown by the M-R3 measurement at the sizes (e) §3 lists. Test: a fixture port reporting 5% free pauses a large import with `shelf` and writes nothing past the pause; a 1M-row index answers `rowOf` and a one-criterion search page within 1,500 ms on the test copy (M-R3). Control: with room the import runs.

## 5. Search, rows, citing and comparing

- **R24** (T53; N1259; K4403 (2); K1484, K1468, K1492 (6); DEC-406 I4) *(not yet met: T53)* **`searchRows({scope, name?, organization?, amount?, date?, filer_id?, form?, limit, after, budget_ms, viewer})` (`op=registersearch`): rows by person, organization, amount and date, from the held rows only.** `scope` is `{register}` (every visible import of it), `{import}` or all registers; criteria are `validateFilter`'s (a name matches on tokens, `register-readers` R5); `limit` is 1 to 200 (default 50, `BAD_LIMIT` otherwise); `after` the opaque `next` of an earlier page (`BAD_CURSOR`); `budget_ms` default 1,500, at most 10,000. It never fetches, never calls `entities` or resolves a name, creates no entity or link, and answers rows as written. Each row `{import, register, row_key, src_row, cells, imported_at, source_date, version, cited, use_limit?}`. Order: date descending (undetermined last), then `row_key`. Pages joined equal the unpaged set. `matched` is the exact count to 10,000 and `capped` beyond. Every answer states the imports searched, each with `imported_at` and `source_date`.
