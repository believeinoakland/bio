### Purpose

Reads the bytes of one email message (`.eml`, and `.msg`) into a plain answer: the headers as the message states them, the body as text, the attachments cut out whole, and the few facts a receiving door needs to decide what to do (whether the message says it is automatic, how deeply it nests). It holds no record, opens no connection, fetches no remote image or link, runs nothing, and writes nothing.

### Provides

**`parseMessage(bytes, {kind}) -> {ok, headers, nesting_depth, text, html_present, attachments, refusals}`**
- **R1** (K5448; N824; C9) *(not yet met: T53)* The answer is `{ok: true, headers: {from, to[], cc[], date, subject, message_id, received[], authentication_results, auto_submitted, precedence, list_id, return_path}, nesting_depth, text, html_present, attachments: [{index, name_stated, type_stated, bytes, size, inline}], refusals: [{code, detail}]}`. Every header is the message's own words as stated, or `null` when the message has none; a header that cannot be read is `null` with its reason in `refusals`, never repaired, guessed or filled from another header. Test: a message holding every header answers each as stated; a message with a malformed `Date` answers `date: null` and a refusal naming it. Control: a plain message answers `ok: true` with empty `refusals`.
- **R2** (C9 (1)) *(not yet met: T53)* **The four automatic-mail facts and the nesting count.** `auto_submitted` (the `Auto-Submitted` value), `precedence` (the `Precedence` value), `list_id` (the `List-Id` value) and `return_path` (the `Return-Path` value, with an empty `<>` answered as the string `<>`, distinct from `null` for none) are each answered exactly as stated or `null`; the reader never decides that a message is automatic, it only reports (the door decides, `mail-door` R10). `nesting_depth` is the number of messages inside messages along the deepest chain (a plain message is 0; one message attached as `message/rfc822` is 1), counted by this reader and stopped at `NESTING_READ_MAX` (4): at more it answers `nesting_depth: 4` and a refusal `NESTED_TOO_DEEP`, and opens none deeper. Test: a bulk-flagged message answers `precedence: "bulk"`; a message with `Auto-Submitted: auto-replied` answers it; an empty return path answers `<>`; a chain of 1, 3 and 5 nested messages answers 1, 3 and 4 with the refusal on the last. Control: a plain message answers `nesting_depth: 0` and all four `null`.
- **R3** (K5448; N1266) *(not yet met: T53)* **Bounds, and nothing executed.** A message over 25 MiB is `{ok: false, refusals: [{code: "TOO_LARGE", detail: {size, bound}}]}` before any part is decoded (the figure is `runtime-limits`', `boundOf("mail", "message_bytes")`, N824 R37); each attachment's `bytes` is its decoded content and `size` its length; an attachment over its own kind's bound (N1267) is listed with `size` and no `bytes`. No remote image, link or font is fetched, no script run, no macro opened. Test: a message of the bound plus 1 is refused naming size and bound, no part decoded (counting source); a body naming a remote image causes no request (a throwing fetch stub). Control: a message at the bound is read.
- **R4** (K5448) *(not yet met: T53)* **Pure and never throwing.** The same bytes always give the same answer; no store, no network, no clock in any output; every failure is a returned `{ok: false, refusals}` or a `refusals` entry, never an exception. Test: truncated, empty, binary and non-message input each answer `ok: false` with a code. Control: R1's well-formed message.

## C. The doorbell's discard clearing, and the scheduler's edges (C9 (3))

### C.1 doorbell (next free R29)

`doorbell` R3 says the discard archive's one-week clearing (DEC-108 (5)) "no requirement here yet provides"; the code has the guard (`mayClearDiscarded`, `doorbell/index.mjs:201`) and nothing that clears. This line is that requirement, owed since DEC-108.

- **R29** (T53; N824; C9 (3); DEC-108 (5); fills R3's "no requirement here yet provides it"; reads R3's guard; `scheduler` R34) *(not yet met: T53)* **Discarded knocks are cleared one week after receipt, and never while a hold is in place.** `clearDiscarded({now, limit})`, with `discardClearDue(now)` and `discardClearWake(now)` for the scheduler. A knock whose status is `discarded` and whose received instant is `DISCARD_DAYS` (7) days or more before `now` at the instance clock (`capture` R115) has its inbox row deleted, in one `record-core` transaction (R22), and then its bytes under `bio/inbox/<digest>`; bytes left by a kill between the two are removed by the next call. `DISCARD_DAYS` is `runtime-limits`' (`boundOf("doorbell", "discard_days")`, N824 R37), no literal here. Before it deletes anything it asks R3's `mayClearDiscarded()`, at every call and never remembered: `may: false` (no reader registered, a reader that throws or does not answer synchronously, or a hold in place) clears nothing and answers `{ok: true, cleared: 0, held: true, basis}`. Only `discarded` is ever cleared: `new` and `pulled` knocks never are, and a knock put back to `new` before the clearing is not. Bytes the record holds through a capture are under the record's own key and are untouched, though the inbox's copy of the same digest goes. It clears at most `limit` per call (default 200, clamped 1 to 1000, N90), oldest received first, answering `{ok, cleared, truncated, held}`; `discardClearDue(now)` answers the earliest instant a discarded knock becomes due, or `null` when none is, and `discardClearWake` is the same instant or `null`. The clearing is never done at read time (`inboxList`, `inboxGet`), writes no tally and no record of what it cleared beyond the count in its answer, leaves R19's tally unchanged, and never throws. The week counts from receipt, as DEC-108 (5) words it; so a knock discarded after its sixth day is cleared by the next call (BOB confirms this reading; counting from the discard is a one-line change). Test: with the reader registered and answering `false`, a knock discarded and received 7 days and 1 minute ago is gone with its bytes; one received 6 days 23 hours ago stays; `new` and `pulled` knocks received 30 days ago stay; with the reader answering `true`, throwing, answering a promise, or not registered, nothing is cleared and `held: true`; a hold placed between two calls stops the second; bytes also captured in the record survive in the record; `limit: 1` over three due knocks clears one and states `truncated`; a second call over the same instant clears nothing more. Control: `inboxList` and `pullKnock` answer as before, and R19's tally is the same after a clearing.
