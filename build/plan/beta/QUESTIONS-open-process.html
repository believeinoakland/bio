The process and its rules · from BOB #333

R1–R4 · The Civicsmith Rulebook: areas, the shape of the rules, proof at the merge, and the order of work

Your direction of 28 October: a few unambiguous, concise rules per area instead of a new ruling for each problem, with process and product kept apart. The proposal, with its evidence and today's audit, is the Civicsmith Rulebook. Four decisions:

  • R1 · The areas · recommendedTen process areas (Design, Requirements & architecture, Planning, Building, Integration, Release; Governance, Sessions, Repository & tooling, Usage) and ten product areas (Mission & scope; Teams, members & trust; The record; Intake & reading; Civic knowledge; Investigation; The assistant & AI; Publication & action; Experience & words; Platform & operations).
  • R2 · The shape of the rules · recommendedPrinciples, plus one rulebook per area of at most ten rules on one screen, rewritten in place and loaded by code; the ruling log goes to the archive; principles drafted for Design and Release.
  • R3 · Proof at the merge · recommendedEvery pull request reviewed by a fresh Opus reviewer against its requirements, and a check that refuses tests that cannot fail; job records become pull requests.
  • R4 · The order of work · recommendedBuild the new process and clean this repository in place together, prove it, then resume T53 (about a week, an estimate). Corrected 20:05 UTC: in place, not a new repository, because teams' copies and the installer fetch releases from this repository's address (bio-plane/src/setup.mjs:458, newgroup/src/steps/api.mjs:118-120).

The other options for each are on the proposal page.
