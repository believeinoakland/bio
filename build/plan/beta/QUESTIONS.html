Reading versus interpreting big documents · from the CAPABILITIES lane

Settled already: every document a member adds is kept whole and read whole, so every line can be searched and cited (K4571). Interpreting is a separate, selective act: turning parts of a document into structured facts, such as a sheet's figures, names or dates. You said deciding what to interpret "is itself an investigation of a different kind" (K4632). Today, a sheet's cells are interpreted only for the range a member opens or cites.

C2CAPABILITIES · N1272asked 27 Oct · rewritten 27 Oct for more context

How do members know which parts of a document were interpreted, and for which investigation?

Background. In C1 you decided that Civicsmith interprets only selected parts of a document (what a member opens or cites, parts a member marks, and later Civicsmith's own proposals). Every word of every document is still read and searchable. Interpreting is the extra step that turns a part into facts the record can use: amounts, names, dates, a sheet's figures. So a document can be fully searchable while only pages 3–9 have been turned into facts, and only for one investigation.

The risk. A member could assume that a document "in the record" has been fully worked through, when only a few pages were interpreted, and for a different investigation. You ruled that nothing is ever done silently (K4571), so the product has to say plainly what was and wasn't interpreted.

Example: the Sewer fund investigation interprets pages 3–9 of a 400-page city budget. Months later, a Pothole repairs investigation opens the same budget. Its member needs to see that the budget is fully searchable, that pages 3–9 were turned into facts for the Sewer fund (by whom, and when), and that nothing has been interpreted for Pothole repairs yet.

  • RecommendedA · A plain line on the document's page and in search results, with an offer to interpret moreThe line reads: "Read in full and searchable. Interpreted for Sewer fund: pages 3 to 9 (Rosa, 12 March). Not yet interpreted for Pothole repairs." Next to it is a button: "Interpret parts of this for Pothole repairs". Search results show a small "not interpreted for this investigation" mark.
  • B · A, plus a list on each investigation's pageThe list shows documents that matched the investigation's searches but haven't been interpreted for it, so a member can work through them. More helpful, but more on the screen. It could be added later if members ask for it.

Places in layers · from the CAPABILITIES lane

You decided (DEC-404) that Civicsmith ships four read-only layers of places and rules (federal, state, county, city). Teams report errors but don't edit a layer, and a team's own research sits beside a layer and serves only that team. Three points remain.

C5CAPABILITIES · N1257asked 27 Oct

What does Civicsmith promise about how current the layers are?

Laws change, offices are renamed, and holiday lists come out late. In October 2026, neither the City's nor the court's 2027 holiday list had been published. Teams' deadlines rest on these layers.

  • RecommendedA · Show beside each rule where it came from and on what dateFor example, "read from the Oakland city clerk on 3 October 2026", and how far its calendars reach. A count past that shows as "not known", never a guess. Civicsmith promises the date it read, not that the law hasn't changed since, and says "not legal advice". Updates come with releases, and an administrator is told once which rules changed.
  • B · Promise a delay, such as "within 30 days of a change"Needs people and a process Civicsmith may not have, and a broken promise about a deadline is the worst failure.
  • C · Show no datesCheap, but a team can't judge a rule.

The assistant's costs · from the UX design stream
U1UX-DESIGN · cost visibilityasked 27 Oct

Should team leaders see how much each member's AI work costs?

Today's drawing (DEC-420) shows administrators the cost per account and per project, without naming members. Each member sees their own use and cost. AI-DESIGN asks for cost per member as well. Who may see that about a member is your call.

Example: Lakeshore Tenants pays for one team account. Mai runs many long readings for "Pothole repairs"; Teo runs few. Rosa, an administrator, sees the month's total and Pothole repairs' share. Should she also see "Mai: $41, Teo: $3"?

  • A · Leaders see every member's cost by nameThe team can spot heavy use and talk about it. But members may hold back from work they feel is watched, and a member's pattern of work becomes visible to leaders.
  • B · Per account and per project only; each member sees their own (today's drawing)No member is singled out, and projects still show where the money goes. But an administrator can't tell who drives a project's spending without asking.
  • RecommendedC · As B, but when a team or project limit is reached, an administrator may open the per-member breakdown for that account and monthOpening it is recorded in the account's history, with who looked and why. Everyday use stays private, administrators get what they need when money runs short, and the look itself is on the record.
