Reading versus interpreting big documents · from the CAPABILITIES lane

Settled already: every document a member adds is kept whole and read whole, so every line can be searched and cited (K4571). Interpreting is a separate, selective act: turning parts of a document into structured facts, such as a sheet's figures, names or dates. You said deciding what to interpret "is itself an investigation of a different kind" (K4632). Today, a sheet's cells are interpreted only for the range a member opens or cites.

C2CAPABILITIES · N1272asked 27 Oct · rewritten 27 Oct for more context

How do members know which parts of a document were interpreted, and for which investigation?

Background. In C1 you decided that Civicsmith interprets only selected parts of a document (what a member opens or cites, parts a member marks, and later Civicsmith's own proposals). Every word of every document is still read and searchable. Interpreting is the extra step that turns a part into facts the record can use: amounts, names, dates, a sheet's figures. So a document can be fully searchable while only pages 3–9 have been turned into facts, and only for one investigation.

The risk. A member could assume that a document "in the record" has been fully worked through, when only a few pages were interpreted, and for a different investigation. You ruled that nothing is ever done silently (K4571), so the product has to say plainly what was and wasn't interpreted.

Example: the Sewer fund investigation interprets pages 3–9 of a 400-page city budget. Months later, a Pothole repairs investigation opens the same budget. Its member needs to see that the budget is fully searchable, that pages 3–9 were turned into facts for the Sewer fund (by whom, and when), and that nothing has been interpreted for Pothole repairs yet.

  • RecommendedA · A plain line on the document's page and in search results, with an offer to interpret moreThe line reads: "Read in full and searchable. Interpreted for Sewer fund: pages 3 to 9 (Rosa, 12 March). Not yet interpreted for Pothole repairs." Next to it is a button: "Interpret parts of this for Pothole repairs". Search results show a small "not interpreted for this investigation" mark.
  • B · A, plus a list on each investigation's pageThe list shows documents that matched the investigation's searches but haven't been interpreted for it, so a member can work through them. More helpful, but more on the screen. It could be added later if members ask for it.

Files and photos · from the FILES lane
F4FILESasked 27 Oct

When a case is published, does a photo in it show where the photo was taken?

Why this matters. Most phone photos carry the place they were taken, often to within a few feet. You ruled (DEC-460) that Civicsmith keeps that data beside the photo's safe copy, and the member sees it in their own record. A published edition is public. Showing the place there can reveal a member's home, a source, or where a witness was standing.

Example: Ana photographs a leaking gas line from her front porch and adds it to a case. If the case is published with the photo's place showing, anyone reading it can work out where she lives.

  • RecommendedA · The author chooses, per photo; off unless they turn it onNothing private leaks by accident; when the place is the point (the leak is at this corner), the author shows it.
  • B · Always shownSimplest, and the most evidence, but it leaks places nobody meant to publish.
  • C · Never shown in a published editionSafest, but an author can't show a place when it matters.
