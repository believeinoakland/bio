Reading versus interpreting big documents · from the CAPABILITIES lane

Settled already: every document a member adds is kept whole and read whole, so every line can be searched and cited (K4571). Interpreting is a separate, selective act: turning parts of a document into structured facts, such as a sheet's figures, names or dates. You said deciding what to interpret "is itself an investigation of a different kind" (K4632). Today, a sheet's cells are interpreted only for the range a member opens or cites.

Places in layers · from the CAPABILITIES lane

You decided (DEC-404) that Civicsmith ships four read-only layers of places and rules (federal, state, county, city). Teams report errors but don't edit a layer, and a team's own research sits beside a layer and serves only that team. Three points remain.

Templates and procedure guides · from the CAPABILITIES lane

A template is wording a team may send in its own name, such as a records request. A procedure guide tells a member how a kind of request goes here: the routes in the order the law gives, and what to expect. Templates already need a named writer, public sources, and a professional's review.

Following a court case · from the CAPABILITIES lane
C22CAPABILITIES · K5498asked 27 Oct

When a member follows a court case, may Civicsmith check it for new filings on a schedule?

Why this matters. On company officers you ruled (C8, K5498) that a team's own free key to an outside source is used "only when a member asks", never in the background. Following a court case is a similar use. A team connects CourtListener (the free public court-records site) with its own free key, and a member chooses to follow a case. Civicsmith then checks that case once a day and tells the team when something new is filed. Nobody presses a button each day, so it's unclear whether that daily check counts as "when a member asks".

Example: Marcus follows the lawsuit over the Coliseum lease. Two weeks later the city files a motion. With A, the team sees "new filing" the next morning. With B, nobody knows until a member opens the case and checks it by hand.

  • RecommendedA · Following a case is the member's askThe daily check runs only for cases a member chose to follow, on the team's own free key, and stops when they unfollow. Nothing is checked that no member asked for.
  • B · Only an explicit check countsNo scheduled check. A member opens the case and presses "check for new filings". Following still works, but the team learns of a filing only when someone looks.
Files and photos · from the FILES lane
F4FILESasked 27 Oct

When a case is published, does a photo in it show where the photo was taken?

Why this matters. Most phone photos carry the place they were taken, often to within a few feet. You ruled (DEC-460) that Civicsmith keeps that data beside the photo's safe copy, and the member sees it in their own record. A published edition is public. Showing the place there can reveal a member's home, a source, or where a witness was standing.

Example: Ana photographs a leaking gas line from her front porch and adds it to a case. If the case is published with the photo's place showing, anyone reading it can work out where she lives.

  • RecommendedA · The author chooses, per photo; off unless they turn it onNothing private leaks by accident; when the place is the point (the leak is at this corner), the author shows it.
  • B · Always shownSimplest, and the most evidence, but it leaks places nobody meant to publish.
  • C · Never shown in a published editionSafest, but an author can't show a place when it matters.
