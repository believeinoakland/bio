Which parts of the assistant does the beta offer?

You asked for "full support for the assistant". Already built and ready to switch on: asking, drafting, reading unclear pages, and checks and plans. Three more are built but switched off, each behind a bar it has to clear first:

  • Investigating is the assistant working through a question across the record and raising findings ("these two payments look connected"). Its bar comes from your investigation design (D11): at most 1 in 5 of the findings it raises may turn out to be wrong (at most 20% false alarms). A finding that's wrong costs a member time and can mislead a case, so this part waits until an AI is shown to stay under that limit.
  • Exploring is investigating started by Civicsmith itself on an open question, without a member asking, once a day at most and with the cost shown. Because it is investigating run on its own initiative, it stays off until investigating clears its bar.
  • Extracting is the assistant proposing exact quotes and readings from a document, for example the figures in a table, which a member then accepts into the record. Each quote is checked in code to really be in the document. It must pass its own accuracy test, and you ruled it never runs unattended: only when a member asks.

With your AI1 answer, each bar is now measured by Civicsmith's automatic test for each AI, at the marks you set after the first measuring run. The 20% false-alarm limit stands unless you change it.

  • RecommendedA · Every built part, plus three more: the intake interview, the case's account, and "what does this charge or rule mean" answersInvestigating, extracting and exploring stay behind their bars and switch on for an AI as soon as it clears them.
  • B · As A, and switch investigating, extracting and exploring on nowFaster, but members could meet wrong findings before any AI has shown it stays under the limit.

When a team lets members use one of its AI accounts on "their own projects", which projects count as theirs?

Why this matters. You ruled (K5430) that a team's AI account stays off every project's and member's list unless the account's owner switches on "Projects and members may add this account to their own lists". Once it is on, a member may add it to the AI list of their own projects. That decides whose AI account, and so whose money and whose privacy checks, a project's work runs on.

Example: Maria leads the "Rate case 2026" project. Dev has only joined it. The team's Gemini account has sharing switched on. Can Dev add the team's Gemini to "Rate case 2026", or only Maria?

  • RecommendedA · Only projects the member leadsThe person answerable for a project chooses what it runs on. Dev can ask Maria. This is how the drafts are written now.
  • B · Any project the member has joinedEasier for Dev, but anyone who joins could change which AI, and whose checks, a project uses.
AI12AI-DESIGNasked 27 Oct · from AI10

How does Civicsmith notice that an AI has changed or is being retired?

Why this matters. Providers change their models quietly and retire old ones on a date they announce. An AI that passed Civicsmith's test in October may answer differently in December. A team would keep trusting a label that no longer describes what it gets.

Example: a team uses "Mistral Large" for drafting letters. In January the provider swaps in a new version under the same name and starts refusing to quote meeting minutes. Without a watch, the team just sees more refusals and nobody knows why.

  • RecommendedA · Several signals, and the AI's status moves through stepsEach AI on the list names an exact version. Civicsmith watches four things: its re-tests (AI6), the provider's announced retirement dates, a different model answering than the one listed, and teams' results drifting away from Civicsmith's. Teams can also opt in to send counts of errors and refusals, never their content. An AI then moves from "listed" to "watch" to "lapsed" to "retired", under your standing rule (AI7). A lapsed AI takes no new work, but nothing it already produced changes. Gains: problems are caught early and shown to teams. Costs: more to build, and re-test spending under your cap.
  • B · Only the provider's announced retirement datesGains: simple, costs nothing. Costs: a quiet change in behaviour is never caught.
  • C · As A, without the teams' opt-in countsGains: no data from teams at all. Costs: slower to notice problems that show up only in real use.
AI13AI-DESIGNasked 27 Oct · from AI10

What does a team see about cost, before and after the assistant works?

Why this matters. Every ask and run is paid by someone's AI account, and prices for the same model differ up to tenfold between providers. Teams on small budgets need to know before they start, and after, so a limit is never a surprise. Some plans, like a monthly subscription, report no money per request at all.

Example: before a long check of a contract, Dev sees "about $0.40 to $1.10 on the team's OpenRouter account". Afterwards he sees "$0.62". On his own ChatGPT subscription the same work shows "not measurable in money: 41 requests".

  • RecommendedA · An estimate before, the actual cost after, and "not measurable in money" where a plan reports noneAlso shown: spend against each limit, alerts at levels the owner sets, and, if a run would move to another account, what that move adds. A subscription is never shown as $0. Who sees each member's cost follows your earlier answer (K5459). Gains: no surprises, and limits are managed early. Costs: estimates need measured figures for each AI, so a new AI shows "no estimate yet" until it has been measured.
  • B · The actual cost after, onlyGains: simpler, and never shows a wrong estimate. Costs: nobody knows before starting what a long run will cost.
AI14AI-DESIGNasked 27 Oct · from AI10

What happens when an AI provider is down or very slow?

Why this matters. Every provider has outages. The assistant should never silently switch where a member's material goes, never lose work, and never leave a member staring at a spinner.

Example: Maria asks a question while the team's provider is down. Overnight, the team's standing questions are due on the same provider.

  • RecommendedA · A quick, honest stop for a member who is waiting; a pause and resume for overnight workA member asking is told at once and offered the choices: wait, switch to another account she may use (a move to someone else's account asks her first, as you ruled in AI3), or continue without AI. Work nobody is waiting on, like standing questions, pauses and resumes by itself when the provider recovers. Brief errors are retried a few times. Nothing already done is lost, and a retry never writes anything twice. Gains: honest and safe. Costs: overnight answers can come late.
  • B · Always move to the next account in the listGains: fewer interruptions. Costs: material goes to another provider without the member choosing it, unless that provider was already shown to her.
  • C · Only show an errorGains: simplest. Costs: overnight work is lost and has to be started again by hand.
AI15AI-DESIGNasked 27 Oct · from AI10

Should a provider's terms that bind the team's work be shown when an AI is connected?

Why this matters. Some providers' rules touch civic work directly. OpenAI's usage policies limit political campaigning and lobbying uses. xAI's ask users not to provide sensitive information such as political opinions. Terms also change on announced dates. A team should know what it agrees to before its work depends on it.

Example: an administrator connects an OpenAI account. Before switching it on she sees two of OpenAI's rules that apply to an advocacy team, quoted with the page and the date, and a note that new terms take effect on a date in November.

  • RecommendedA · Show them, quoted with the page and the date, and restrict nothingThe terms that bear on civic work are quoted with page and date, shown at connection and again before a change takes effect. Civicsmith restricts nothing on their account (a point not confirmed restricts nothing). Gains: the team decides knowing the rules. Costs: Civicsmith has to keep the quotes current.
  • B · Show them, and also block uses a provider's terms forbidGains: protects teams from breaking terms. Costs: Civicsmith would be interpreting providers' terms for teams, and would often be wrong.
  • C · Link to the terms onlyGains: nothing to maintain. Costs: few will read them, so a team may never learn of a rule that affects its work.
AI16AI-DESIGNasked 27 Oct · from AI10

Should Civicsmith run one small service of its own, for the AI list and teams' sealed test results?

Why this matters. You decided that the list of AIs updates at any time, not only with releases, and that teams can send sealed test results to you. Both need somewhere outside any team's copy that every copy can reach: a place that publishes the signed list and receives the sealed results. Today nothing like that exists. Each team's copy is its own.

Example: on a Tuesday you approve "Llama 4 at Groq" for the list. Every team's copy picks it up the next day without a new release. A team in Fresno tests a local model and presses "Send the results", and the sealed file lands with you.

  • RecommendedA · One small Civicsmith service on Civicsmith's own Cloudflare accountIt publishes the list and its notices, all signed with a key kept offline (the service itself cannot sign anything), and receives sealed results it cannot open. Opening and scoring happen apart from it. You decide what enters the list (AI7's rule aside). Gains: the list updates any time, and teams' results reach you. Costs: one more thing Civicsmith runs and pays for, small Cloudflare fees, and every copy depends on it for list updates (each copy keeps its last good list if the service is unreachable, as you ruled in AI9).
  • B · Publish the list as a file in the public code repository, and receive results by emailGains: nothing new to run. Costs: list updates are tied to the repository, results arrive by hand, and teams have no "Send" button.
  • C · No central service; the list ships only with releasesGains: simplest. Costs: goes against your decision that the list updates at any time, and teams' results have no way to reach you.
