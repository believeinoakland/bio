# OPTIONS design brief (READING-ARCHITECTURE #1, the lane's own design; the writer expands it, never changes its substance)

Inputs, all committed in `build/plan/lane-reading-architecture/`: REQUIREMENTS.md (RR-, CF-, GP- ids), PLATFORM.md (W/D/R/B/CR/Q/WF/C/BR/AI ids, E1–E22, P-M1–P-M24), CHALLENGES.md (C1–C17, §7 obligations 1–17, §5 unknowns marked A).

## The shape every option shares (so options differ only where it matters)

These hold in every option, because CHALLENGES §7 makes them obligations whatever the carrier:
- S1 **Register, then answer, then read.** Every way in (upload, capture, pull, archive member, promote, "Read it") writes one durable READ JOB row in the team's record (by whole sha, kind, bound check, state) in the same transaction that files the capture, before the answer leaves. No member request reads more than a bounded first look (format detection by range reads; declared sizes). Nothing heavy inline: no OCR, no unpack, no whole parse in a member's request (obligation 3, 6; C4, C6). Acts answer within 2 s; web capture keeps K4562's at-once answer for large files.
- S2 **One identity:** the whole sha; parts are storage detail below R2 multipart (obligation 10; C9).
- S3 **One reader per kind,** read by range from R2 (never the whole file in an isolate unless the kind is small by declared size), a whole read = a one-piece range (obligation 1; C2, C3, C8). Per-step cost bounded by bytes decoded and objects walked, independent of file size; declared headers checked before any allocation (obligation 2).
- S4 **Pieces land as units in the record** in bounded batches (rows sized under the 2 MB value; text units, page boxes, cell ranges in their own rows), so the text index grows to the last line; the reading's completeness is ONE fact computed from the job's own counters (written, skipped, owed, failed with cause) and drawn by every screen (obligations 9, 11, 12; C11, C12).
- S5 **Progress monotone, failures classified:** each step advances a stored cursor or ends stated with a cause code; deterministic / transient / resource failures differ; a unit that kills its reader three times is stated for that unit only and the read goes on (poison rule); a transient failure at any tick is retried (obligations 4, 5; C5).
- S6 **Bounds stated once per kind** in one table derived from Bob's standard (100 MB or 5,000 pages; 50 MB or ~1M rows; 50 MB; 500 MB), in the unit that matters; inside the bound never refused or "kept, not read"; above it refused before work with size and bound (obligation 8; C7). Protective sub-bounds (pixels per page, decompression ratio, nesting) are K1881 and stated.
- S7 **What the member sees** comes from the job row: queued / being fetched / being read N of M (pages or rows) / read in part and why / refused with its bound / finished; a notice when finished or stopped; "by when" an estimate from measured rates (Bob's B-3). Search and cite work on the units already landed and say "searched so far" (DEC-418) (RR-I1, RR-J6, GP-32, GP-35).
- S8 **Safety decoupled:** the scan and safe view run beside the read on their own jobs; reading never waits on them; an unscannable verdict is its own state, never `clean` (obligation 14; C16). Whether a hold gates reading is Bob's (B-S7).
- S9 **Testing at the platform's shape:** a harness with real alarm/step semantics, a capped isolate counting buffers, the 2 MB value shim, kill/restart/store-failure injection; a seeded generated corpus per kind at the standard's sizes, judged on counts not time, through the real entry path; the live measurements P-M1–P-M24 that the chosen option relies on, run on a probe before building (obligation 15, 17; C13).

## The options (they differ in WHERE the read runs and WHAT carries it across kills)

### Option A — Hardened in-object (today's shape, made whole)
The team's Durable Object keeps carrying reading: the ledger (`keeps_pending`) becomes the single READ JOB table; the object's alarm runs one bounded firing (time budget ~10 s, rotation, every await bounded), the cron watchdog as K4566; readers stay the plane's JavaScript readers (tier 1) plus `pdf-worker`/`ocr-worker`/`sheet-worker` by service binding, each rewritten to read by range from R2 with a stored cursor. Memory: the read shares the record's isolate (128 MB, plane idle 50–87 MB → 40–78 MB for a read); CPU 30 s per alarm unless `cpu_ms` set (set it to 300 s); one thread shared with every screen.
- Meets the standard? Only if every reader can work in ~40 MB of headroom on a range at a time, including PDF object streams, xlsx shared strings (a 1M-row sheet's sharedStrings.xml can be tens of MB, must itself be windowed), zip central directories, image decode by strips. Possible in principle, unproven at the standard's sizes (CHALLENGES §4: nothing live above 41.5 MB).
- OCR of a 5,000-page scan: ~1.6 s/call wasm, one page per `ocr-worker` call, ≤32 invocations per request → hours of alarm firings competing with screens (C14).
- Removes C4, C6, C8, C9, C11 by the shared shape; does NOT remove C1, C2, C14 (memory and thread shared with the record), keeps C5 dependent on the self-built alarm/watchdog (G70 unknown cause).
- Effort: lowest new platform surface; but every reader rewritten to ranges; the held round-8 causes become its plan.

### Option B — A reader object per document (Workers only, off the record's thread)
A new Durable Object class `Reader` (one instance per read job, id = whole sha + team), with its own small bundle (only the readers it needs; idle a few MB, not the plane's 50–87 MB), its own SQLite (cursor, counters, owed units), its own alarm, its own 30 s–5 min CPU per invocation. The team object only registers the job (S1) and receives pieces by RPC in bounded batches (≤32 MiB serialized, in practice ≤1 MB per batch) into its index; the team object's screens never wait on a read (removes C14). The Reader drives `pdf-worker`/`ocr-worker`/`sheet-worker` by binding. Liveness: the Reader's alarm re-arms itself every step (D12's advice: catch, re-arm before returning); the team object holds the job row and the cron watchdog checks job rows whose Reader has not reported within N minutes and pokes the Reader (one RPC; K4566's "only checks and re-arms" kept).
- Caveat (PLATFORM D1/D2): instances of one class may share an isolate and its 128 MB, so concurrent Readers per team need a cap (e.g. 2–4) and each Reader's step must stay well under 128 MB / cap.
- Meets the standard if the range readers fit a step in ~60–100 MB of a nearly empty isolate; better headroom than A, same reader rewrites.
- OCR throughput as A (wasm), but off the record's thread; could run several Readers in parallel.
- Removes C14 and most of C1 (headroom), C5 still self-built but simpler (one job per object, its own alarm).
- Effort: a new class and a bundle; migration of the ledger's reading kinds; the readers' range rewrite as in A.

### Option C — Workflows carry the read (Workers readers)
Each read job starts a Cloudflare Workflow instance (id = team + whole sha, idempotent). Each step reads one bounded piece (a page range, a row window, an archive member, one OCR page or a batch) from R2, returns a small reference (≤1 MiB; units written to the team object by RPC inside the step, or to R2 then indexed), and the platform carries durability: completed steps are not re-run after a crash or deploy, a failed step retries with backoff (configurable, up to 10,000), sleeps are free, 10,000 steps default / 25,000 max per instance (5,000 pages fits; 1M rows must be windowed in ~100–1,000-row steps). The team object keeps only the job row and the units; no self-built ledger, alarm firing or watchdog for reading (the existing scheduler stays for the record's other consumers, smaller).
- Caveats: step memory is "a Worker's" (128 MB, inference G14); a step restarted by the engine redoes that step; deploy behaviour of a running step not documented (WF17, P-M14); concurrency 10,000 vs 50,000 (G6, irrelevant at team scale); billing per step ($0.80 per 100k beyond 500k/month: cents per read); Workflows run in Miniflare locally (to verify, spike).
- Removes C5 for reading (platform-managed liveness, the G70 unknown no longer on the reading path), C14, most of C1; C4 by S1.
- Effort: moderate; new product dependency; the readers' range rewrite as in A/B; installer must deploy the Workflow (a Worker binding).

### Option D — Workflow-carried, container-read heavy kinds (recommended candidate)
As C for the carrier, but the heavy kinds are read in a container (Cloudflare Containers, reached through a Durable Object; instance `standard-1` 4 GiB / ½ vCPU or larger), with mature native tools instead of hand-written JavaScript parsers: PDF text and structure (poppler/pdfium/mupdf), OCR (native Tesseract with language packs, several times faster than wasm, multi-threaded), office and legacy formats (LibreOffice headless for .doc/.xls/.ppt/.rtf, plus direct OOXML/ODF parsing), archives (libarchive/7z, TAR/GZ/7z/RAR), images (libvips/ImageMagick: TIFF, HEIC, WebP, GIF, CMYK, progressive JPEG). Light kinds (plain text, CSV/TSV, HTML, JSON, XML, small files under a declared-size threshold) stay in Workers steps (one path per kind: the kind decides, not the size). The container streams the file from R2 (outbound handler or S3 API), reads a range of pages/members per call, writes units back in batches; each Workflow step = one container call over one range, so a container killed by a deploy or host restart costs one range (C10, C14 caveats).
- Meets the standard most surely: 4–12 GiB removes the memory class for single huge pages, dense PDFs, 1M-row shared strings, 500 MB archives; native tools remove most of C10/C17 (formats, fonts, encodings, image types, encryption detection, legacy Office) with decades-tested code rather than this project's parsers.
- Costs: container awake ~$0.07/h (`standard-1`) – $0.40/h (`standard-4`), billed per 10 ms active; cold start 1–3 s documented (27 s seen for agent-runner's large image); images built and published by the existing GitHub workflow (agent-runner precedent) and installed by the installer (K3447); one more moving part to measure (P-M17).
- Removes C1, C2 (native tools stream), C5 (Workflows), C8 (one path per kind), C10/C17 (native tools), C14; C4/C6/C9/C11/C12 by the shared shape.
- Effort: highest new surface (container image, its DO, the Workflow), but it REPLACES the largest and most fragile code (pdf-reader's parser, pdf-pixels/image-codecs decoders, tesseract-wasm OCR, office/odf/ooxml readers for heavy files) instead of hardening it; the plane gets smaller (helps the 30.18 of 32 MiB bundle limit and the 50–87 MB idle).

## Comparison axes the writer must fill per option (table + prose)
Reliability (which classes C1–C17 it removes by design, which by discipline only; what depends on unknowns §5); efficiency (memory headroom, CPU per step, OCR throughput at 5,000 pages: hours estimate per option, member-request latency); the member's experience (2 s acts, progress N of M, time to whole estimates per kind and size, what happens on a kill or deploy); cost on Workers Paid (from PLATFORM §6.4: per 100 MB PDF, per 5,000-page scan, per 1M-row sheet, per 500 MB archive, monthly at a modest team load); effort to reach it from today (modules touched, what is retired, what is new, platform measurements first); how it is tested at the platform's shape (what can be local, what needs a probe).

## Recommendation (the lane's)
D, staged: (1) the shared shape S1–S9 first (it is needed under every option and removes C4, C6, C9, C11 at once); (2) the Workflow carrier with Workers readers for the light kinds (C) — this is also the fallback if containers disappoint; (3) the container reader for PDF/OCR/office/archives/images behind the same step interface, kind by kind, each proven on the generated corpus at the standard's sizes and on the live probe before it replaces the JavaScript reader. Before (2) and (3): the live measurements they rely on (P-M2, P-M4, P-M5, P-M14, P-M17, P-M1/P-M3 for uploads). Why not A: it keeps the record's isolate and thread as the reader's (C1, C14) and the self-built liveness whose stalls (G70) are still unexplained; the evidence shows four rounds of hardening it. Why not B over C: it rebuilds liveness that Workflows provide. Why D over C: the evidence's largest and most recurrent classes (C1, C10, C17) live in the hand-written parsers for heavy kinds; native, mature tools in a large container remove them rather than bound them. What would change the recommendation: P-M17 showing containers unreliable or slow to start under the team's load; P-M14 showing Workflow steps lost on deploy; Bob preferring no container cost (then C).
