## 4. The envelope (K4798 (4))

### 4.1 How to read the table

Each row is a bound a read of these kinds depends on. **Today** is the value in the code, with file:line (checked at the tip for the load-bearing ones; the rest from the shares). **Stated** names the requirement that states it, or **unstated** (also: "code constant only", "test bar only"). **Recommended** is the bound this plan proposes and what a file over it gets: always a named statement, `final`, nothing owed for ever, never silent (I3, I7). **Who:** "BOB's" is decided under P17, recorded in `rulings.md`, reported as done; "**Bob's**" changes what a member may do (K1881) and is put to him in §4.3. A bound that is only a protective limit against a hostile or broken file is BOB's.

Platform figures used (H16, H17, measured): the object has 128 MB; the plane idles at 85-86 MB as built (49 MB with the ASCII bundle, G39), leaving 42 MB (78-79 MB) to a read; a stored value is at most 2,098,176 B (record-core); `limits.cpu_ms` is unset so the platform default of 30 s of CPU a request governs; the door answers in 25 s (`ACQUIRE_BOUND_MS`); a proxy hop costs 1.0-4.0 s to first byte on a new connection and uploads run at about 8 MB/s through it (R-PROXY); an object call costs 110-160 ms; a large fetch runs at ~0.7 MB/s; workerd freezes `Date.now()` in synchronous work (R-CLOCK) and returns zeros from `process.memoryUsage()`; node timings are 1.2-1.5 × faster than the job's machine and are not workerd's. Bob's beta tiers (K4628): documents 100 MB or 5,000 pages, spreadsheets 50 MB, images 50 MB, archives 500 MB.

### 4.3 For Bob (K1881; each with an explanation, options and a recommendation, rendered when brought to him)

- **Q1 The largest document Civicsmith reads, by kind (A1, A4, A5).**
  - **Today:** a text or CSV is read in pieces up to 24 MiB (the first tick loads the file); a Word, PowerPoint or OpenDocument file is read whole up to 24 MiB; a web page up to 8 MiB; larger: kept and stated "too large", never read. Your beta tier is 100 MB.
  - **Options:** (a) keep 24 MiB and state it per kind; (b) reach 100 MB by reading in pieces from the store (text and web pages first: windows; Word and OpenDocument files by reading the zip's directory and only the parts needed); (c) raise the in-memory line (the object has 42-78 MB free after the plane's idle, so it cannot go much higher).
  - **Recommendation:** (a) now in the member's words, (b) built in this order: text, then Word/PowerPoint/OpenDocument by parts, then web pages with a streaming cleaner.
- **Q2 The most text one Word or OpenDocument file may hold (A2, A3).**
  - **Today:** 20 MiB of text whole, 32 MiB in pieces; a 40-69 KB file declaring 19 MiB already kills the object.
  - **Options:** (a) keep 32 MiB of text and add a count of paragraphs/cells/links (250,000) the file may hold; (b) 100 MB of text; (c) lower to 8 MiB.
  - **Recommendation:** (a). Over either, the file is kept and stated with the figure.
- **Q3 Pages and slides (A6, E3).**
  - **Today:** no limit except the safe view's 500 pages; a Word file has no page count.
  - **Options:** (a) 10,000 slides, no page limit for Word (bounded by Q2), the safe view stays a 500-page *view* and says so; (b) a 5,000-page safe view; (c) no limit.
  - **Recommendation:** (a). A safe view of 5,000 pages at 150 dpi is about 1.9 GB of pictures.
- **Q4 Which text encodings Civicsmith promises (C1).**
  - **Today:** UTF-8 only. A Latin-1, Windows-1252 or UTF-16 text or web page is read as damaged text and said to be complete, or refused with a wrong sentence.
  - **Options:** (a) UTF-8, UTF-16, and the page's or file's own declared charset (ISO-8859-x, Windows-125x); anything else stated; (b) UTF-8 and UTF-16 only; (c) UTF-8 only, stated.
  - **Recommendation:** (a). Old public-body pages and exports are in these.
- **Q5 Formats beyond today's (C3, B8).**
  - **Today:** RTF, legacy Word/Excel/PowerPoint (`.doc/.xls/.ppt`), email (`.eml`, `.msg`) and TAR/GZ/7z/RAR are not read, and the member is told "non-textual or too large". Your N1266 lists `.doc` and RTF as missing.
  - **Options:** (a) now tell the truth for each ("RTF is not read yet"), then add RTF and `.eml` (body decoded, attachments as members), then `.doc`, then GZ/TAR; (b) tell the truth only; (c) add them all before the beta.
  - **Recommendation:** (a).
- **Q6 What counts as the text of a document (C4, C5).**
  - **Today:** the body only. Footnotes, endnotes, headers, footers, comments, tracked deletions, text boxes, speaker notes are in the file and never searchable, and the reading says "whole". For a web page only the main region is read.
  - **Options:** (a) read every text part and mark each unit with its kind (a footnote, a comment, a deleted wording), so a member can include or exclude them in search and a citation names the kind; (b) read them but not offer them in search by default; (c) leave as is, but name what was not read.
  - **Recommendation:** (a). A footnote in a staff report is often where the dollar figure is.
- **Q7 What "as a visitor saw it" promises (A11, F4, F7).**
  - **Today:** the page's DOM at first load, not scrolled, without banners dismissed, without frames or shadow content; the daily render allowance (1,200 s) is charged the full 25 s for a failed render.
  - **Options:** (a) state exactly that in the copy's words now; bounded scroll and frames later; charge failures their real time and show the day's account; (b) add a bounded scroll now; (c) leave unstated.
  - **Recommendation:** (a).
- **Q8 The archive tier (A10, B3, B6, B7, E1).**
  - **Today:** ZIP only; 256 MiB for the file, for the expanded total and for a tree; 10,000 members; depth 3; 1 GiB and 40,000 entries a day when opened automatically. An uploaded ZIP is never opened. Your tier says "archives 500 MB".
  - **Options:** (a) a 500 MB archive *file*, expanded total ≤ 2 GiB at ratio ≤ 1,100, depth 3, 10,000 members, an uploaded ZIP opened like a fetched one, the daily budget lifted by a member act; (b) 500 MB expanded; (c) keep 256 MiB.
  - **Recommendation:** (a).
- **Q9 How long "being read" may last (D6).** As the PDF plan's B-3: 15 minutes for the largest text or Word file on a quiet copy, the member told when a large read starts. **Recommendation:** (a), shared with PDF.
- **Q10 Password-protected files (B9).**
  - **Today:** the scanner calls an encrypted ZIP, Word or PDF `clean`; Civicsmith then lets the original be opened.
  - **Options:** (a) "encrypted" is its own state: not clean, not opened without the scan's say, the reading stated "needs a password"; (b) also let the member type the password (a secret the record must never keep); (c) leave.
  - **Recommendation:** (a).
- **Q11 Read before or after the scan (E5).**
  - **Today:** a document is read, indexed and searchable whatever the scan says; only opening the original waits.
  - **Options:** (a) keep, and say it; (b) read only after a clean scan (a document waits "being scanned", but the scanner has never had signatures, FR-SCAN); (c) read at once but quarantine a found threat's text from search.
  - **Recommendation:** (a) for the alpha, (c) when the scanner has signatures.

### 4.4 The invariants (the "always", stated once; each corpus file is judged against all of them)

I1-I14 are the PDF plan's (every unit once; progress; never silent; memory; work per tick; pieces equal whole; a refusal is stated, final and finite; state loss is harmless; every path owes what it leaves; search finds the last line; the reading pages to its end; stored values bounded; contention; same bytes, same answer), read for *units* (paragraphs, slides, lines, members) instead of pages. The kinds here add:
- **I15 Declared is not allocated.** A bound is on what the read allocates and walks (objects, buffers, tokens), with ArrayBuffers counted; a file whose declared size passes and whose walk would not fit is refused *before* the walk, by name. Requirements: office-readers R12, ooxml R35 (rewritten), reading-pipeline R36.
- **I16 An encoding is named.** Every text read states the encoding it was decoded in; a decode that replaces more than the tolerance is partial with the count; nothing is `U+FFFD` and `failure: null`. Requirements: reading-pipeline R1, R32; docprofile R18.
- **I17 Every part is read or named.** A part of a file that is text is a unit; a part not read is named by kind and count in the reading and in the member's statement; the units of a document are never lost to a bound on something else (cells, links, comments). Requirements: office-readers R11; extraction R91, R97; K4571.
- **I18 Every member is accounted for.** An archive's members are each `filed`, `read`, `refused with a cause`, or `waiting for a named act`; none is `waiting` for ever; an uploaded archive is opened like a fetched one. Requirements: acquisition R38/R40.
- **I19 A copy says what it is.** A visitor view carries its fidelity ("first load, not scrolled"), a failed one carries its detail and its retry class, and a copy that did not render is not filed as the document. Requirements: capture-sources, acquisition R100.
- **I20 A deterministic failure is final, with its reason.** A throw on the file's own bytes, or a refusal by a bound, is never `transient`; a transient failure is retried under R77 until it ends or becomes final with its reason. Requirements: reading-pipeline R37; plane R77.
- **I21 A verdict that was not reached says so.** A file the scanner could not look inside (encrypted, over a limit, unrarred) is never `clean`; the member is told which. Requirements: file-scanner R3; file-safety R51.
- **I22 The statement names the cause.** The sentence for a refusal is the one for its cause, with the figure; "Read it" is offered only where it can succeed. Requirements: reading-pipeline R37; screens-inquiry; setup-words.

Corpus files are judged against I1-I22. Lines in §5 say which are particular to the file.
