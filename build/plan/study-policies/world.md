# Policies and standards in the world — research for BOB #122

Scope: how governments' and other organisations' policies and standards actually exist, so CivicOS can hold them as rich as it holds statutes and court decisions. Oakland/California first, then general. Researched 2026-10-06. Every claim carries its source; "observed" marks what I saw in a primary document myself.

## 1. The kinds

### Oakland / California, executive and departmental
- **City of Oakland Administrative Instructions (AIs).** Numbered city-wide instructions that "describe the City's policies, practices, standards, and procedures"; the City Administrator's office receives, analyses and edits AIs and uploads them to the City **intranet** (https://stories.opengov.com/oaklandca/published/Uc8f2o9W-q4). Examples: AI 123 Disability Access Policy (https://www.oaklandca.gov/files/assets/city/v/1/city-administrator/documents/ada/ada-policies/ai-123.pdf), AI 71/73 EEO and Gender Inclusion (https://www.oaklandca.gov/files/assets/city/v/1/employment-investigations-and-civil-rights-compliance/documents/working-for-oakland/workplace-amp-employment-standards/employment-investigations-and-civil-rights-compliance/city-of-oakland-gender-inclusion-policy-ai-73.pdf), AI 580 Race and Equity (https://cao-94612.s3.amazonaws.com/documents/Administrative-Instruction-580-Race-and-Equity_2023-03-22-211634_ykyr.pdf), AI 594 Telecommute (https://cao-94612.s3.amazonaws.com/documents/AI-594-Employee-Telecommute-Policy.rev_2021-06-17-191906_qnkw.pdf). Some AIs are also on OPD's public PowerDMS site (https://public.powerdms.com/OAKLAND/documents/579540). No public index of all AIs was found; copies are scattered across department pages and S3 buckets.
- **Oakland Police Department directives** form a family, not one document type:
  - *Departmental General Orders (DGOs)*, lettered/numbered by subject (e.g., M-03 Complaints, K-03 Use of Force, M-19 Bias-Based Policing, D-18 Social Media), published on PowerDMS (https://public.powerdms.com/OAKLAND/documents/1266222, https://public.powerdms.com/OAKLAND/documents/452).
  - *Special Orders*, numbered serially (SO 9196, SO 9212), which "immediately change and supersede sections of" DGOs and carry a **termination condition** — SO 9196: "TERMINATION: Upon revision of DGOs K-03 … and K-04" (observed; https://cao-94612.s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com/documents/1893442.pdf, https://public.powerdms.com/OAKLAND/documents/2953680).
  - *Training Bulletins*, which "constitute official policy" (https://public.powerdms.com/OAKLAND/documents/1266215); the *Manual of Rules (MOR)*, whose violations are the misconduct categories DGO M-03 refers to (observed in M-03); unit manuals such as the IAD Policy & Procedures Manual (https://cao-94612.s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com/resources/IAD-P-P-Manual-DRAFT-9Nov20-Final.pdf) and the Basic Academy Policy Manual (https://public.powerdms.com/OAKLAND/documents/1888097).
- **Lexipol manuals** (most California police agencies): a vendor template manual of state-specific policies, customised by each agency; ~95% of California agencies reportedly rely on it (https://texaslawreview.org/lexipol/; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lexipol; agency example https://www.santaclaraca.gov/our-city/departments-g-z/police-department/transparency/department-policy-manual). Oakland is the notable exception with its own DGO system (observed above).
- **Policies required by ordinance**: Oakland's Surveillance Technology Ordinance (OMC ch. 9.64) requires a **use policy** per surveillance technology, reviewed by the Privacy Advisory Commission and approved by Council, plus an **annual surveillance report** per technology (http://oakland-ca.elaws.us/code/coor_title9_ch9.64; https://www.oaklandca.gov/Government/Boards-Commissions/Privacy-Advisory-Commission).
- **School-district board policies**: the CSBA model — Board Policy (BP), Administrative Regulation (AR), Exhibit (E) and Bylaw (BB) — under a shared numbering scheme (e.g., BP 5144.1), with 650+ CSBA sample policies and their legal references maintained in **GAMUT** (https://www.csba.org/gamut). ARs are "detailed directions developed by the superintendent … telling how, by whom, where and when" (https://www.turlock.k12.ca.us/board-of-trustees/board-policies). OUSD publishes its BP/AR on its own portal (https://www.ousd.org/board-of-ed/board-policy; e.g., BP 5124 https://resources.finalsite.net/images/v1692101299/ousdorg/qrgisdwlmoqnybjdfyil/BP_5124_Translation_and_Interpretation_Services.pdf).
- **Utility tariffs and rules (CPUC)**: binding schedules and numbered Rules (e.g., PG&E Electric Rule 29), changed by **advice letters** under CPUC General Order 96-B, each with an effective date (https://www.pge.com/tariffs/assets/pdf/adviceletter/ELEC_6424-E.pdf; https://www.pge.com/tariffs/assets/pdf/adviceletter/ELEC_7516-E.pdf). Each tariff **sheet** is versioned individually: "Revised Cal. P.U.C. Sheet No. 61362-E, Cancelling Revised Cal. P.U.C. Sheet No. …" (https://www.pge.com/tariffs/assets/pdf/tariffbook/ELEC_SCHEDS_E-1.pdf).
- **Budget and fiscal policies**: Oakland's Consolidated Fiscal Policy is an **ordinance** (13487 C.M.S.) requiring a balanced budget and a 7.5% General Purpose Fund emergency reserve (https://stories.opengov.com/oaklandca/published/fw9ZPf3nM; https://stories.opengov.com/oaklandca/published/hgC-jO9uD). GFOA best practice recommends at least two months of operating revenue/expenditure as unrestricted general-fund balance and a formal reserve policy (https://www.gfoa.org/materials/fund-balance-guidelines-for-the-general-fund).
- **Service-level and performance standards**: often absent or informal. San Francisco Public Works states a 72-hour weekday pothole goal (https://sfpublicworks.org/services/potholes); for Oakland no published pothole-repair standard was found, and reporters measure 311 closure times against none (https://www.oaklandreport.org/p/oakland-potholes-311-requests-open; https://oaklandside.org/2022/03/10/oakland-streets-road-repairs-emergency-potholes/). State 9-1-1 standards (90% of calls answered in 15 s, 95% in 20 s) are an external standard the City Auditor applied to OPD (https://www.oaklandauditor.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/20251008_9-1-1-Emergency-Response-Times-Audit.pdf).
- **Contract service levels**: franchise and service contracts carry performance standards with **liquidated damages** per incident per day (Oakland solid-waste franchise reports: https://www.oaklandca.gov/files/assets/city/v/1/public-works/documents/sustainability/zero-waste-history/agenda-report-03-23-12.pdf; https://www.oaklandca.gov/files/assets/city/v/1/public-works/documents/sustainability/zero-waste-history/fourth-supplemental-report-04-12-12.pdf).

### General
- **Agency guidance documents** (federal): explicitly not legally binding; OMB's Good Guidance Bulletin 07-02 governs "significant guidance" so it is "not improperly treated as legally binding" (https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2007/01/25/E7-1066/final-bulletin-for-agency-good-guidance-practices). California analogue: a state agency's "guideline, criterion, bulletin, manual, instruction, order, standard of general application" that is really a regulation but was not adopted under the APA is an illegal **underground regulation** (Gov. Code §11340.5; OAL determinations: https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/california/1-CCR-260; https://oal.ca.gov/wp-content/uploads/sites/166/2024/11/RemediatedCTU2024-0206-01_DSH_-Determination-FINAL.pdf).
- **Accounting and audit standards**: GASB Statements are Category A authoritative GAAP for state and local governments; Technical Bulletins/Implementation Guides are Category B (GASB 76; https://www.journalofaccountancy.com/news/2015/jun/gasb-hierarchy-state-local-governments-201512566/). GAO's *Government Auditing Standards* (Yellow Book, 2024 revision) govern government audits (https://www.gao.gov/yellowbook).
- **Technical and safety standards**: NFPA (NFPA 70 NEC, 101, 13), free **read-only** online after registration, no print/download (https://www.iafc.org/topics-and-tools/resources/resource/free-access-to-nfpa-codes-and-standards); ICC model codes; ISO/IEC standards; ANSI-accredited standards. **ADA**: the 2010 ADA Standards (DOJ) = 28 CFR 35.151 + the Access Board's 2004 ADAAG (https://www.ada.gov/law-and-regs/design-standards/2010-stds/).
- **Accreditation standards**: CALEA — 440+ standards, agencies must meet all applicable *mandatory* standards and 80% of applicable *other-than-mandatory* ones; annual reports plus a full assessment on a 4-year cycle (https://www.publicsafety.upenn.edu/files/CALEA-2023-2024.pdf; https://www.calea.org/sites/default/files/4%20year%20process/4-year%20cycle%20communication82019.pdf).
- **Nonprofit and corporate policies**: IRS Form 990 Part VI asks whether the organisation has a written conflict-of-interest policy (12a), whistleblower policy (13) and document retention policy (14), and defines what each should contain (https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i990; https://www.councilofnonprofits.org/running-nonprofit/governance-leadership/good-governance-policies-nonprofits).

### Incorporation by reference (IBR)
- **Federal**: 1 CFR Part 51 — material may be incorporated only if "reasonably available to and usable by the class of persons affected"; the rule must state where copies can be examined; OFR holds the material until accessioned to NARA (https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-1/chapter-II/part-51; handbook https://www.archives.gov/files/federal-register/write/handbook/ibr.pdf).
- **California Building Standards Code (Title 24)**: by state law it adopts the ICC IBC/IRC/IFC/IEBC, NFPA's NEC and IAPMO's UPC/UMC with California amendments; republished every three years with interim supplements (2022 edition effective 2023-01-01; 2025 edition effective 2026-01-01) (https://www.dgs.ca.gov/BSC; https://www.dgs.ca.gov/BSC/About/History-of-the-California-Building-Standards-Code---Title-24). Part 12 is the Referenced Standards Code (https://codes.iccsafe.org/content/CARSC2022P1/preface). Cities then layer **local amendments** by ordinance: Oakland OMC ch. 15.04, re-adopted per edition with findings of local climatic/geologic/topographic conditions (http://oakland-ca.elaws.us/code/coor_title15_ch15.04; https://oakland.legistar.com/LegislationDetail.aspx?ID=5921543&GUID=727EDF0E-82CF-4242-8C1B-4084BDDB57BB).
- **Copyright/access**: *ASTM v. Public.Resource.Org* (D.C. Cir., Sept. 12, 2023) held non-commercial posting of standards incorporated into law is fair use (https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/cadc/22-7063/22-7063-2023-09-12.html). Standards bodies still sell them; "free access" is read-only reading rooms (NFPA above; ICC's codes.iccsafe.org).

## 2. Binding force

- **Graded force is explicit in some instruments.** ISO/IEC Directives Part 2: "shall" = requirement, "should" = recommendation, "may" = permission, "can" = possibility (https://www.iso.org/sites/directives/current/part2/index.xhtml). CALEA splits mandatory vs other-than-mandatory (above). Accounting splits Category A vs B (above). Federal guidance is non-binding by definition (OMB above). Many local policies mix binding rules with "command intent" or aspirational preambles in one document (observed: DGO D-18 opens with a "COMMAND INTENT" section; https://cao-94612.s3.amazonaws.com/documents/DGO-D-18-Member-Social-Media-Rev7-2.11.22.pdf).
- **How a policy gets force** (each seen in Oakland):
  - *Adopted by legislative act*: Consolidated Fiscal Policy is an ordinance (above); surveillance use policies are approved by Council resolution (OMC 9.64, above).
  - *Issued under delegated executive authority*: AIs signed by the City Administrator (observed: AI 594 signed "Edward D. Reiskin, City Administrator"); DGOs and Special Orders issued by the Chief of Police.
  - *Approved by an oversight body*: Charter §604 lets the Police Commission approve/reject OPD changes to policies on use of force, profiling, First Amendment assemblies and court-ordered elements, and propose its own changes, which go to Council; if Council does not act within 120 days of the Commission's vote they become final (https://cao-94612.s3.amazonaws.com/documents/Ordinance-13498-Police-Commission-Enabling-Ordinance.pdf; https://www.oaklandcityattorney.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/Public-Legal-Opinion-re-City-Council-and-Police-Commission-Authority-to-Revise-Police-Pursuit-Policy.pdf).
  - *By court order/settlement*: OPD's Negotiated Settlement Agreement tasks are audited for compliance with the DGOs that implement them (e.g., Tasks 28/29 against DGO M-4.1) (https://www.oaklandca.gov/files/assets/city/v/1/police/documents/opd-policies-and-resources/office-of-internal-accountability/tasks-28-29-investigation-of-criminal-misconduct-and-iad-investigation-priority.pdf).
  - *By contract*: franchise performance standards with liquidated damages (above).
  - *By incorporation in law*: Title 24, ADA Standards, 1 CFR 51 (above). *By regulator approval*: CPUC tariffs (above).
  - *Voluntarily*: accreditation (CALEA) and best practices (GFOA) bind only if the body adopts them.
- **Hierarchy with law.** Policies sit under statute and must not conflict (DGO M-03 defers to the MOR and state law; Lexipol markets itself as aligning policy to state and federal law: https://www.lexipol.com/about-us/). Inside one family, later/narrower instruments override earlier ones: Special Orders supersede DGO sections until the DGO is revised (SO 9196 above); departments may add requirements "consistent with the intent" of an AI (observed: AI 594 §II). A state agency's policy that acts as a rule without APA adoption is void as an underground regulation (above).
- **Waivers and exceptions.** Usually written into the instrument, with the approver named: Oakland Council may waive competitive bidding/RFP requirements by resolution "upon a finding that it is in the best interest of the City" (OMC 2.04.050/2.04.051; https://library.municode.com/ca/oakland/codes/code_of_ordinances?nodeId=TIT2ADPE_CH2.04PUSY_ARTIBICOPU; e.g., https://oakland.legistar.com/View.ashx?GUID=CE5AE6CB-4D9A-4660-927F-9D61F86FEEEB&ID=11052160&M=F). ADA allows departure only where "structurally impracticable" (28 CFR 35.151; https://www.ada.gov/law-and-regs/regulations/title-ii-2010-regulations/). DGO M-03 gives the IAD Commander discretion over administrative closures, recorded in the case log (observed). Supervisors may "deny, end, or modify" a telecommute agreement for non-arbitrary reasons (observed: AI 594). The waiver is therefore itself a record (a resolution, a log entry, an approval) separate from the policy.

## 3. Lifecycle and versioning in practice

- **Header metadata is the versioning.** AI 594 header: SUBJECT, NUMBER, REFERENCE, EFFECTIVE, SUPERSEDE (observed). DGO M-03 header: Effective Date (22 Dec 17), Evaluation Coordinator (IAD Commander), Evaluation Due Date (22 Dec 18), **Automatic Revision Cycle: 1 Year** (observed; https://public.powerdms.com/OAKLAND/documents/1266222). The version served publicly in 2026 still carries the 2017 effective date and a 2018 evaluation due date, i.e., the review cycle the document itself promises is visibly overdue.
- **Revision identifiers are inconsistent.** The D-18 file name says "Rev7-2.11.22" while the document body reads "Effective Date: XX XX 21" (a draft placeholder in a publicly posted copy; observed). The K-03 draft reads "Effective Date: XX MMM 20" (https://cao-94612.s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com/documents/OPD-Use-of-Force-Dept.-General-Order-K-03-DRAFT.pdf). Drafts, adopted versions and revisions circulate side by side with no reliable identifier.
- **Granular versioning exists where a regulator demands it.** CPUC tariffs version each sheet, with the cancelled sheet number, advice letter, decision and effective date on every sheet (above). Title 24 versions whole editions on a 3-year cycle with supplements (above). Lexipol manuals have a "Draft" manual where edits occur; each release creates a new "Issued" manual and archives the previous one, and vendor updates arrive as mark-ups the agency accepts, rejects or customises (https://helpcenter.lexipol.com/docs/editing-policy-general-notes; https://www.lexipol.com/platform/policies-and-updates/; update packets: https://information.auditor.ca.gov/reports/2017-131/documents/cathedral_city_police_department-March%202017%20Lexipol%20Updates.pdf).
- **Old versions are often not public.** California Penal Code §13650 (SB 978, 2020) requires police agencies to post *current* standards, policies, procedures and training materials; agencies remove and replace the old record when it changes (https://www.cityofmontclair.org/senate-bill-978-cpc-13650/). So the public record is "current only"; prior versions must be captured by the investigator or obtained under the CPRA.
- **Interim changes are silent in the base document.** A Special Order changes a DGO without the DGO text changing (SO 9196 above). CSBA districts revise BP/AR continuously ("policies are continually under review", https://www.ousd.org/board-of-ed/board-policy).
- **Publication**: mostly PDFs on department pages, cloud buckets (cao-94612.s3…), vendor portals (PowerDMS for OPD; GAMUT for districts; Lexipol-hosted manuals), or intranet-only (Oakland AIs). Where not posted, the route is a CPRA request: agency must determine within 10 days (extendable 14) and produce "promptly" (Gov. Code §7920.000 et seq.; https://firstamendmentcoalition.org/handbook/california-public-records-act/).

## 4. Structure

- **Typical anatomy** (observed across AI 594, DGO M-03, SO 9196): header block (type, number, title/"Index as", effective date, supersedes, reference/authority, coordinator/owner, review due); purpose or command intent; policy statement; definitions; applicability/eligibility (which units, roles, positions, contractors); procedures — AI 594 §XI is a two-column **"Responsible Party / Action"** step table; timeframes (e.g., 24-hour complaint line in M-03); roles with discretion (IAD Commander approvals); exceptions; related forms/exhibits; signature of issuing authority. Numbering is outline-style (I., A., 1., a., 1)) and pages "Page 1 of 28".
- **Metrics and targets** usually live outside the policy (state 9-1-1 standard, budget performance measures), or nowhere (Oakland potholes).
- **Machine-readable standards**:
  - *Akoma Ntoso / OASIS LegalDocML* covers acts, bills, amendments, debates, judgments, gazettes, plus a generic `doc` type and `statement`, usable for non-legislative documents (https://docs.oasis-open.org/legaldocml/akn-core/v1.0/akn-core-v1.0-part1-vocabulary.html); its FRBR work/expression/manifestation naming applies to any document type.
  - *ELI* ontology (FRBRoo-based work/expression/format, URI templates, controlled work-type vocabulary each publisher defines) (https://data.europa.eu/eli/ontology; https://op.europa.eu/documents/2050822/2138819/ELI+-+A+Technical+Implementation+Guide.pdf/).
  - *OASIS LegalRuleML* models "legal norms, guidelines, policies": obligations, permissions, prohibitions, bearers, defeasibility (exceptions), temporal validity and jurisdiction (https://docs.oasis-open.org/legalruleml/legalruleml-core-spec/v1.0/legalruleml-core-spec-v1.0.html).
  - *NISO STS* (ANSI/NISO Z39.102-2017), the XML format for standards' text and metadata (`std-meta`: designation, edition, ICS, etc.), derived from ISO STS and JATS (https://www.niso.org/standards/z39.102-2017; https://niso-sts.org/TagLibrary/niso-sts-TL-1-1d1-html/element/std-meta.html).
  - Policy-management products (PowerDMS, Lexipol KMS, GAMUT) expose HTML/PDF, not a shared export standard; I found no public interchange format.

## 5. How investigators and auditors use them

- **Policy as audit criteria.** Yellow Book 2024 defines criteria as "laws, regulations, contracts, grant agreements, standards, measures, expected performance, defined business practices, and benchmarks against which performance is compared"; criteria "identify the required or desired state" (para 8.124, glossary). Examples of criteria explicitly include "goals, policies, and procedures established by officials of the audited entity", "technically developed standards", "expert opinions", "prior periods' performance", "defined business practices", "contracts or grant agreements" and peer benchmarks (para 8.18). Findings have elements criteria, condition, cause, effect (paras 8.116–8.126) (https://www.gao.gov/assets/d24106786.pdf).
- **Oakland examples**:
  - City Auditor 9-1-1 audit (Oct 2025): criteria = state answer-time standards; condition = 54%/57% in 2024; cause = staffing and *outdated minimum staffing standards* not based on call volume (https://www.oaklandauditor.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/20251008_9-1-1-Emergency-Response-Times-Audit.pdf).
  - Illegal-dumping audit: *lack of policies and procedures* led to inconsistent enforcement (https://www.oaklandauditor.com/new-audit-report-assesses-the-city-of-oaklands-fight-against-illegal-dumping-offering-recommendations-for-improvement/).
  - Audit recommendation follow-up: 23% partially, 33% not implemented (https://www.oaklandauditor.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/20240321_Audit-Recommendation-Follow-Up-Report_FINAL.pdf).
  - OPD Office of Inspector General / Police Commission OIG audits compliance with DGOs and NSA tasks (Tasks 28/29 vs DGO M-4.1; Task 41 PAS audit: https://www.oaklandca.gov/files/assets/city/v/1/police/documents/opd-policies-and-resources/office-of-internal-accountability/task-41-performance-assessment-system-audit.pdf); the Commission's policy review of DGO M-19 (https://cao-94612.s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com/documents/Policy-Review-of-DG0-M-19-FINAL.pdf).
  - City Auditor's audit of the Police Commission's own CPRA handling (https://www.oaklandauditor.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/06/060120_Performance-Audit_Police-Commission-CPRA_FINAL-REPORT.pdf).
- **Common failure patterns** (each evidenced above):
  1. *No policy* (illegal dumping; Oakland pothole service standard).
  2. *Outdated policy / overdue review* (9-1-1 staffing standards; M-03's 1-year revision cycle lapsed).
  3. *Policy not followed* (OIG task audits; 9-1-1 below standard).
  4. *Policy silently amended* by interim orders (Special Orders) or vendor updates (Lexipol).
  5. *Waiver as routine* (recurring Council bid waivers under OMC 2.04).
  6. *Not public or only current version public* (AIs on intranet; PC 13650 "current only"; drafts with "XX" dates posted).
  7. *Underground rules* (state manuals enforced without APA adoption).
  8. *Recommendations not implemented* (follow-up report).

## Design implications

1. Model a **policy instrument family per issuer** (AI, DGO, Special Order, Training Bulletin, BP/AR/Exhibit, tariff Rule/Schedule, contract SLA), with an issuer-defined type vocabulary — as ELI does — rather than one fixed "policy" type.
2. Reuse the **work / version / portion** model for policies, but allow versioning at the **portion** level (CPUC sheets, DGO sections overridden by a Special Order), not only whole documents.
3. Record **force as data on each provision**: requirement / recommendation / permission / aspiration (ISO shall/should/may), plus mandatory vs other-than-mandatory (CALEA) and authoritative category (GASB A/B). A finding should say which grade of criterion was breached.
4. Record the **source of force** for each version: the enacting ordinance or resolution, the delegated issuer and their authority, oversight-body approval (Charter §604), court order, contract, incorporation by reference, or voluntary adoption. Link to the legal instruments CivicOS already holds.
5. Add **overrides with conditions**: an instrument can supersede portions of another until an event happens ("until DGO K-03 is revised"). The "in force on date X" view must apply these.
6. Make **waivers and exceptions first-class records** (who granted them, under which clause, the finding made, scope, duration, and the source document such as a Council resolution), so patterns like routine waivers can be counted.
7. Store the **declared review cycle and owner** (Evaluation Due Date, Automatic Revision Cycle, Coordinator) and flag overdue reviews automatically. That is a finding in itself.
8. Treat **capture and provenance** as core: most policies are PDFs on department pages, S3 buckets, PowerDMS/GAMUT/Lexipol portals or CPRA productions, and the public copy is "current only" (PC 13650). Snapshot every fetch with a hash and the date retrieved, so prior versions survive.
9. Tolerate **messy identity**: drafts posted as adopted, "XX" effective dates, file names whose revision labels disagree with the text. Keep the claimed version and the observed evidence separate, with confidence grades.
10. Support **incorporated standards** as referenced works with edition, adopting instrument, local amendments (Title 24 plus OMC 15.04) and an **access status** (free, read-only reading room, paywalled, reading room only). Never assume the full text can be republished, despite *ASTM v. PRO*.
11. Represent **structure**: definitions, applicability (units, roles, contractors), procedure steps with responsible party, timeframes, and metrics/targets, so a check can be posed against one step or one target.
12. Allow **criteria from outside the body's own instruments** (state 9-1-1 standard, GFOA best practice, peer benchmark, prior performance), as the Yellow Book does (para 8.18), and label them as external or benchmark rather than binding.
13. Encode the **finding schema** as criteria → condition → cause → effect → recommendation, with the criterion pointing at a specific policy portion and version, and track recommendation implementation status over time.
14. Provide failure-pattern **tags**: no policy, outdated, not followed, silently amended, waiver abuse, not public, underground rule, recommendation unimplemented. "No policy exists" must be recordable as a finding with evidence of the search made (portal checked, CPRA response).
15. For interchange, read and write **Akoma Ntoso `doc`/ELI-style keys** for the text, borrow **LegalRuleML** concepts (bearer, obligation, exception, temporal validity) for provisions, and accept **NISO STS** metadata for technical standards. Do not wait for an industry policy-export standard; none exists.
