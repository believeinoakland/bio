## 0. In short

**Why a document "works, then fails".** For these kinds the reason is one step earlier than for PDF: *they were almost never tried.* No walk, in nine walks of biosmoke8 and its predecessors, uploaded or captured a DOCX, DOC, ODT, RTF, PPTX, ODP, email or ZIP (H18, H19: the walks used PDFs, JPEG photographs, TXT, CSV/TSV, xlsx/ods safe views and web pages; the coverage map rates the office-format row "NEVER" and is a draft). No T51 cause (G5 to G90, C/E/F/L before them) names one of those kinds (H13, H14, H15). Every cause that touches them is a *shared mechanism* that a PDF, a text or a CSV walked into first (the reading tick, the pieces, the 24 MiB line, the ledger). So the code for these kinds has been fixed only where another kind forced it, and its tests feed it its own builders' kind of file: well-formed, small, UTF-8, text-heavy (H02, H20).

**What running them finds at once.** Probes through the real plane harness and the real readers, with no repository change (H20, H02, H12, H07, H11), are enough to show the state:
- a DOCX of 0.48 MB declaring 12 MiB of body text **kills the object** (heap out of memory in tick 1); 24 MiB does too; 2-6 MiB read whole in 22 ticks; an 8 MiB body takes 13.3 s for its first tick (H20, ran);
- a 69 KB DOCX of one-cell tables (19 MiB declared, under the 20 MiB whole bound) **runs out of memory in a 128 MB worker**; a 45 KB file of `<w:p/>` does the same at 29 MiB (H02, ran);
- a 60 KB DOCX with about 7,000 table cells **loses all its text**, paragraphs outside the table included, and the member sees "too large to keep whole" (H12 D1, H11 D11, ran);
- a 953-byte DOCX takes 8 s to read; a 1.5 KB PPTX 4.3 s; a 96 KB `content.xml` 9.7 s; an 800 KB part in doc-clean 21 s: one regular expression, scanned from every `<` (H02 D3, H03 D2-D3, ran);
- a Latin-1 or Windows-1252 page or text reads as `U+FFFD` text and is reported **complete**; a UTF-16 capture reads as NUL-interleaved text whose last line search cannot find; the same file *uploaded* is refused with "non-textual or too large" (H01, H08, H12, ran);
- an RTF is read as raw control words (`text/rtf`) or refused with a false sentence (`application/rtf`); an `.eml` is indexed as raw MIME with its base64 body; `.doc` is refused; each carries "non-textual or too large", and a "Read it" repair button that cannot succeed (H11 D6/D12, H08 D2, ran);
- **an uploaded ZIP is never opened**: `uploadCapture` profiles it and stops, no screen act opens it, and the archive screen shows every entry `waiting` for ever (H07 D1, H08 D5, code, probe: the upload answer has no `unpack` key);
- a ZIP whose central directory is in shuffled order costs 9,180 8-MiB part reads (73 GB, 106 s of node, before any R2 latency) where the same bytes in physical order cost 25 reads (H07 D2, ran).

**The failure classes** (§3) are the PDF plan's fourteen (MEM, WORK, ENC, PIECE, STATE, SHAPE, IDENT, SILENT, INTAKE, UI, FORMAT, STALEFAIL, LIVE, PATH) plus the ones these kinds forced: **NEST/ZIPBOMB** (archives and containers: member count, depth, ratio, names), **RENDER** (the visitor view's cost and fidelity), **DROP** (content the reader never emits, with no marker), **BIN** (binary taken for text), **STORE** (a stored value over its row bound), **CEIL** (a ceiling set by a constant with no requirement), **VOCAB** and **MEASURE** (the producer's vocabulary is not the consumer's; the instrument times the wrong interval), **SEC** (a safety verdict that does not gate, or says `clean` of what it could not read), **SUPPLY** (the scanner's image cannot be pulled) and **MISCLASS** (a profile that claims what the file is not). Three findings recur across almost every class, as for PDF: *no stated envelope* (in all but about five of some thirty round-7 causes the entry's invariant is "none stated", H15), *fixed on the path the test used* (G88 fixes plain text only; G68 text and CSV only; G41 sheets only; G2 the fetch arm only), and *measured outside the shape*.

**The envelope** (§4): 61 bounds in seven groups. Most are unstated, or stated only as a code constant or a test bar, and none of the stated ones is stated against the 128 MB object. The same file is judged at four unrelated sizes by four modules (acquisition 8 MiB, file-safety 20 MiB, reading-pipeline 24 MiB, capture and the scanner 256 MiB), and none is Bob's 100 MB tier. 27 rows are marked **Bob's**; they are put to him as eleven questions (§4.3).

**The corpus** (§5): 87 rows in twelve families (each row a ladder or a family of generated files, several hundred in all), plus 16 seeded variations a round, each with a truth file and a stated expected outcome (read whole, or a named refusal), through the real entry path (upload or capture, ticks to whole, the last line found by search) at the 128 MB shape, plus the faults. About a third are needed only to show that a refusal is *stated*, since no reader exists. The shape harness already feeds any file on disk unchanged (`harness.mjs:183`); the work is a seeded generator with a truth file under test-support, six ZIP writers made one, and four harness gaps (§5.1).

**Defects found by this review that are not yet causes** (§2.2, 36 items, X1-X36). The most urgent, all reproduced: **X1** a DOCX table of about 1,500 rows hides the whole document; **X2** a small DOCX kills the object; **X3** an uploaded ZIP is never opened; **X5** an office file over 8 MiB is stored `undetermined` and over 20 MiB is graded as an unopened archive and never shown; **X9** encodings; **X10** RTF, email and DOC; **X12** deterministic throws on a numeric reference are classed transient and retried for ever; **X16** the scanner says `clean` of an encrypted archive or document and file-safety then opens the original.
