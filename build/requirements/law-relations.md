## Public

### Purpose

How one held standard bears on another, as members record it from the text: amendments, repeals, renumberings and recodifications (temporal), references, definitions, exceptions, implementations and incorporations (referential), a court's links to the law it reads, and later decisions' treatment of a decision. It also answers which addresses a provision has been held under, whether a court citation is stated in a held capture, and the connection-owner read over these rows. It holds no standard: `standards` holds them and constructs this module over its reads (R13). Nothing here is written by a machine (R14), and nothing judges a law's merit (R18).

### Provides

Terms. An **end** is a standard id, or `{standard, portion?}`, a portion being a path the standard records (its `portion_path`). The **host** is the reads `standards` passes at construction (R13). A code named "the host's" below is minted by `standards` and answered here exactly as the host gives it. A row's id is `lrel-`, `clink-`, `ctreat-` or `lprop-` followed by 24 hex characters. These are rows of this module's tables, not record documents.

**LAW_RELATIONS, lawRelationsOf** (D192)
- **R1** (was `standards` R22) The relation types are two closed sets, kept apart and never mixed in one list or read: temporal `amends`, `repeals`, `renumbers`, `recodifies`; referential `refers_to`, `defines`, `excepts`, `implements`. `LAW_RELATIONS` exports both, frozen. *(not yet met: T35-80)*
- **R11** (new; the read R1 names) `lawRelationsOf({standard, viewer})` answers `{ok: true, standard, temporal, referential, truncated, says}`, the relations `standard` is an end of, in relation id order, temporal and referential in two lists and never one. Each item is R2's relation answer, with `direction` `out` or `in`. A relation whose citation passage the viewer may not read (`membership.inSight` over the passage's bundle) is neither answered nor counted. A withdrawn one is answered with its withdrawal. At most 2,000 relations are read, with `truncated` measured by reading one past. Refusals: the host's `STANDARD_NO_ID` (op `lawrelations`) for a blank id; the host's `NO_SUCH_STANDARD` (`standards` R17) for a standard the viewer may not read. It writes nothing. *(not yet met: T35-80)*

**lawRelate, lawPropose, lawWithdraw** (K1443: the machine only proposes them)
- **R2** (was `standards` R23) `lawRelate({type, from, to, citation, effective?, reason, author, viewer})` (`op=lawrelate`) records one relation from one standard or portion to another, by a member's act: refusals, in order, `MACHINE_CANNOT_RELATE` (a machine or empty author), `LAW_RELATION_UNKNOWN`, `NO_SUCH_STANDARD` (`standards` R17, the host's) naming the end, `PORTION_UNKNOWN` (the host's), `LAW_RELATION_NO_CITATION` (a temporal relation cites the amending instrument's extent, a referential one the referring passage, a content id of a held standard's text), `LAW_RELATION_NO_EFFECTIVE` (a temporal relation with no effective date or enactment event), `STANDARD_NO_REASON` (the host's). A relation that would change a `standards` R20 answer is recorded only this way, by the member's act; a machine's suggestion of one is a proposal (`standards` R9's pattern) and never moves an answer. Such a proposal (`lawPropose`: a law relation, a court link of R5 or a treatment of R6) is labelled through `record-grammar`'s `proposalLabel(proposer, "law_relation")` (its R49), never through the `standard` subject, which labels only `standards` R9's proposed standards. `lawWithdraw({relation, reason, author})` withdraws one, kept with who, when and why. *(not yet met: T35-80)*

**addressesOf** (K1446)
- **R3** (was `standards` R24) Recodification across addresses: `addressesOf({key, portion?})` follows the adopted `renumbers` and `recodifies` relations both ways and answers every key and portion the provision has been held under, each with the relation and effective date that moved it, bounded by `connection-grammar`'s default depth (`truncated` past it). `reevaluation`'s version notices across addresses read it (X73), through `standards`. It never throws. *(not yet met: T35-80)*

**resolveCourtCitation** (COURTS C1; K1449)
- **R4** (was `standards` R25) `resolveCourtCitation({citation, viewer, lookup?})` takes a court citation as `id-spaces` reads it (its R27) and answers `verified` only when a held capture states that citation and the viewer may see it: a `court` standard whose text, or a capture of the opinion it rests on, states the volume, reporter and page, naming the capture and extent. Otherwise it answers `not verified`, refusing nothing (D88). With `lookup: true` and the keyed lookup switched on (`acquisition.citationLookup`, its R37), it adds the service's matches, labelled as the service's and never as `verified`; switched off, it answers without them and says so. It writes nothing. *(not yet met: T35-80)*

**courtLink, courtTreat, stillStanding** (COURTS L4, links as data; D134)
- **R5** (was `standards` R26) `courtLink({type, from, to, citation, reason, author, viewer})` records, by a member's act, `interprets`, `applies` or `holds_invalid` from an extent of a `court` standard's text to a portion of a held statute, regulation or ordinance; refusals as R2's, and `NOT_A_COURT_STANDARD` when `from` is another kind. A machine's suggestion of one is a proposal. *(not yet met: T35-80)*
- **R6** (was `standards` R27) `courtTreat({decision, treatment, by_decision, citation, reason, author, viewer})` records a treatment row: `treatment` one of `reversed`, `vacated`, `depublished`, `overruled`, `affirmed`, citing the later decision's extent. `stillStanding({decision, date})` answers `standing`, `not_standing` (a held `reversed`, `vacated`, `depublished` or `overruled` effective on or before `date`) or `undetermined` where the later history is not read, saying so; never a default of standing. *(not yet met: T35-80)*

**neighbours, CONNECTION_KINDS** (B1a.7; `connection-grammar` R2, R6–R9)
- **R7** (was `standards` R28, its read; the registration at load stays `standards`', its R48) `CONNECTION_KINDS` holds this module's connection kinds: its law-relation kinds (R1, class `evidentiary`), its court links (R5, `evidentiary`) and the derived kind "in force at an event's date" (class `derived`), each with its members' word (K1486); `CONNECTION_OWNER` is `standards`, the owner name `standards` registers them under (unchanged by the split). `neighbours({node, kinds, at, page, viewer, scope})` answers, for a standard node, its relations and links valid at `at`, and for an event node the held standards in force at the event's `when` (`standards` R20, through the host), each derived item with its method (R20's) and `connection-grammar.derivedId`; an undetermined in-force answer is returned marked so. Its owner-conformance battery (`connection-grammar` R9) runs in this module's tests. *(not yet met: T35-80)*
- **R20** (new; what the code of R2–R7 already answers that their moved words do not name, stated so the split states it once; no new behaviour) `lawRelate` also refuses `STANDARD_FIELD_UNKNOWN` (the host's; a field outside the act's own, after `MACHINE_CANNOT_RELATE`) and `LAW_RELATION_SELF` (both ends the same standard and portion, after `PORTION_UNKNOWN`); its citation is a content id of the `from` standard's text that the viewer may read. `courtLink` also refuses the host's `STANDARD_FIELD_UNKNOWN` as `lawRelate` does, `LAW_RELATION_UNKNOWN` naming the link types and `COURT_LINK_TARGET_NOT_LAW` (`to` not a statute, regulation or ordinance). `courtTreat` refuses the host's `STANDARD_FIELD_UNKNOWN` as `lawRelate` does, then `TREATMENT_UNKNOWN` before reading its ends, `NOT_A_COURT_STANDARD` for either decision, naming which, and `LAW_RELATION_SELF` for one decision treating itself; a treatment is effective at the later decision's stated period start, and withdrawn treatments, or ones whose passage the viewer may not read, are not read by `stillStanding`, whose refusals are the host's `STANDARD_NO_ID`, `STANDARD_DATE_INVALID` and `NO_SUCH_STANDARD`, and `NOT_A_COURT_STANDARD`. `lawPropose` takes `what` (`relation`, `link`, `treatment`; else `LAW_RELATION_UNKNOWN`), a `why` of 1 to 240 characters (the host's `STANDARD_WHY_INVALID`) and a proposer (the host's `STANDARD_PROPOSER_UNNAMED`); a member's act naming `proposal` takes each field the member does not state from it and answers which, and a proposal is adopted at most once (the host's `STANDARD_PROPOSAL_ADOPTED`; an unknown one, `STANDARD_NO_SUCH_PROPOSAL`). `lawWithdraw` refuses `MACHINE_CANNOT_RELATE`, `NO_SUCH_LAW_ITEM` (no relation, link or treatment answers to the id) and the host's `STANDARD_NO_REASON`, and a second withdrawal answers `already: true` with the first. `addressesOf` refuses a blank key with the host's `STANDARD_NO_ID` and does not follow a relation whose passage the viewer may not read. `resolveCourtCitation` reads at most 2,000 court standards, saying so past it. `neighbours` refuses a missing viewer `VIEWER_MISSING` and answers an evidentiary item only to a viewer who may read its passage. Each refusal writes nothing. *(not yet met: T35-80)*

**Reads for standards**
- **R10** (new; the read `standards` R20 names: "an adopted temporal relation bounds the period of the version it amends") `boundsOn(standardId)` answers the temporal relations whose `to` is `standardId` and which are not withdrawn, in relation id order, each with its type and its effective date, or its enactment event and edge. **LawRecords (the host contract)**
- **R13** (new; `standards` R16's share) `new LawRecords(host)` constructs the module over its host's reads. The host supplies: `sql`, `record`, `membership`; `rows` and `one` (SQL over the same storage); `row(id)` (a standard's row: `kind`, `portion_path`, `instrument`, `period_from`, `period_to`, or null); `texts(id)` (its text content ids, in order); `readable(id, viewer)`; `content()`; `when()` (the clock, an instant to the second); `nonce()`; `inForceAt`, `periodOf`, `eventDay`, `eventWhen`, `gradeOf`, `zone`, `recognise` and `citationLookup`; `idsAtKey(key, portion)`, `idsOfKind(kind, limit)` and `idsCovering(day)` (the standards at an instrument key, of a kind, and whose stated period does not exclude a day); and the refusals named "the host's" above. Construction throws a `TypeError` naming every read missing from `host`, and writes nothing. Constructing it creates the five tables this module declares (R15), where absent, so every service here and `record-core`'s purge succeed with no caller migrating (N220, N267). This module never reads or writes a `standards` table itself: what it knows of a standard is what the host answers. *(not yet met: T35-80)*
