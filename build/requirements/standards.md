## Public

### Purpose

A standard is what a government act is measured against: a statute, regulation, ordinance, court decision or order, adopted policy, or public commitment (Functional Architecture, Layer 2 Function 1). This module holds each standard as content in the record, with its citation, its kind and issuer, where it comes from (a jurisdiction profile's `standard_sources`, or undetermined), and the period it was in force. It says which standards were in force at a date. It judges nothing about a government act (`conformance` does) and nothing about the merit of a standard (Operational Principle 1).

### Provides

Terms. A **period** is `{from, to}`, each a `YYYY-MM-DD` date or null; null is "not stated in the record", never "always". A **source** answer is `{state: "matched", source, kind, issuer, level, profile, basis}` or `{state: "undetermined", why}`; `level` is the source's level (`jurisdictions` R23, R31), or `undetermined` when the entry states none.

**standardDeclare({cite, kind, issuer, text, period, supersedes?, reason, author, viewer}) → `{ok: true, id, source, ...}` or refusal**
- **R1** Refusals in order: `MACHINE_CANNOT_DECLARE_STANDARD` (K251; an empty author or a machine identity: a machine proposes, R9); `STANDARD_NO_CITE` (an empty citation, or over 200 characters); `STANDARD_KIND_UNKNOWN` (outside `statute`, `regulation`, `ordinance`, `court`, `policy`, `commitment`, the set of `jurisdictions` R23); `STANDARD_NO_ISSUER`; `STANDARD_NO_REASON` (C-112.20, a new row, stamped by 1.53.0: the `reason`, the declarer's words on why the group holds its government to this standard, absent, not a string, blank or over 2,000 characters; DEC-88, K1025); `STANDARD_NO_TEXT` (no content id); `STANDARD_TEXT_UNRESOLVED` (a content id `content.contentRow` does not hold, naming it); `STANDARD_PERIOD_INVALID` (a date not `YYYY-MM-DD`, or `to` before `from`); `STANDARD_SUPERSEDES_UNKNOWN` (R6); then R18–R19's refusals for the fields they add, when given. The reason is read back with the declaration (R4, R5). (T35-31) `kind` is one of `jurisdictions`' `STANDARD_SOURCE_KINDS` (its R23: the six and `standard`, a documented standard set by a standards body, a profession, a regulator or an accreditor; K1902 (1)), refused `STANDARD_KIND_UNKNOWN` naming the seven. The declaration takes `held` (R34; `text` when absent): `STANDARD_NO_TEXT` and `STANDARD_TEXT_UNRESOLVED` apply only to `held: "text"`, and a `cited` or `absent` declaration is refused `STANDARD_TEXT_NOT_HELD_AS` when it names text. `issuer` may be a registered entity (R39). Then, when given, the refusals of R33 (`family`), R36 (`copy`, `copy_claimed`), R38 (`version_basis`, `overrides`, `force_source`), R39 (`designation`, `edition`), R40 (`adoption`), R41 (`access`), R42 (`target`) and R47 (a found extent, `question`), in that order, each writing nothing.
- **R2** `text` is one or more content ids: the standard's own words as captured. A standard is never held without a capture of its text (Intake Doctrine: material enters only with provenance). (T35; K1724) This holds for a standard used as a measure: a standard held `text` (R34). A `cited` or `absent` entry holds no text and is never a measure (R34); it is a fact a finding may state.
- **R3** The citation is matched against the `cite` pattern of every `standard_sources` entry in the active profiles' combined view (`jurisdictions.combine` over the list `record-core` holds). The first match gives `source: matched` with that entry's `source`, `kind`, `issuer`, `level`, `profile` and `basis`; a matched entry with no level answers `level: undetermined` beside the match (K108 (5), K171). No match, no active profile, or a withheld fact (`jurisdictions` R15) gives `source: undetermined` with why; the standard is still held (K102): the profile describes local sources and does not decide what law a group may hold its government to. A declared `kind` or `issuer` that differs from the matched entry's is kept as declared and the difference is stated beside it, never corrected.
- **R4** The answer and every later read carry who declared it and when (this module's clock), and the declaration is never edited: a correction is a new standard that supersedes it (R6).

**standardRead({id, viewer}) → answer or refusal**; **standardsIn({at?, kind?, source?, cite?, after?, limit?, viewer}) → `{items, cursor, truncated}`**
- **R5** `standardRead` answers R1's fields, R3's source, the declarer and time, what it supersedes and what supersedes it, and for each text content id its standing (`content.standings`) and whether a newer capture of its document holds the passage (`content.passageNotice`), so a changed statute text is seen and nothing is moved. `STANDARD_NO_ID` (its own row C-112.11; K369); an absent id, and any id for a viewer naming no member, is `NO_SUCH_STANDARD`, one answer (R17).
- **R6** `supersedes` names an earlier standard (an amendment, a renumbering, a later decision). The earlier one stays readable, and both reads name the link. A standard is superseded by at most one standard; a second is refused `STANDARD_ALREADY_SUPERSEDED`, naming the first.
- **R7** `inForce(id, date) → "in_force" | "not_in_force" | "undetermined"`, with why: `not_in_force` only when a stated bound excludes the date; `undetermined` when a bound needed to decide is null; never a default. It is kept as an alias of R20's `inForceAt({standard: id, date})`, answering exactly its state and why, `overridden` (R38) included, so its callers (`conformance`, `filings`, `action-plans`, `affordances`, `control-plane`, `plane`) need no change (Choices 18). Since T35 (K1973) R20's states are four: `IN_FORCE_STATES` names `overridden` too.
- **R8** `standardsIn` lists the standards the filters admit, in id order, at most 200 per page (a lower `limit` is honoured, a higher one is not), `truncated` measured by reading one past the page. With `at`, a standard is listed with R7's answer for that date, and `not_in_force` ones are left out.

**noSuchStandard(standardId, extra?) → refusal** (N309, K231, K275; a module-level function)
- **R17** The one answer to one condition: no standard the caller may read answers to `standardId` (absent, or any id for a viewer naming no member, answered alike; R5). It answers `{ok: false, reason: "NO_SUCH_STANDARD", code: "NO_SUCH_STANDARD", check, translation, standard, detail}`: `standard` the id as asked (null when none), `detail` one fixed sentence, the same for every caller, and `check` and `translation` its catalogue row's. `extra` adds a caller's own fields and never replaces these. R5 answers through it, and every act of a later module that answers this condition answers through it (`conformance` R1; `filings` R14 passes R5's answer through), so the code is minted at one site; its one catalogue row is this module's (C-112.10), its `where` naming this function, and conformance's C-113.9 gives way to it. It writes nothing and never throws.

**standardPropose({cite, kind?, issuer?, text?, why, act?, proposer, viewer}) → `{ok, proposal}`**; **standardAdopt({proposal, author, viewer, ...R1's fields})**
- **R9** A proposal (the Legal/Policy Lookup skill's work, or a member's suggestion) is stored apart from standards, labelled with who proposed it and whether it is machine work (`record-grammar`'s `proposalLabel(proposer, "standard")`: `lawProposalState`'s three states, one composer, K171), with a `why` of at most 240 characters. It is never a standard, never read by `standardsIn`, and is answered with a sentence saying so. A proposal may be stored with no captured text, and says so; it cannot be adopted until a member names its captured text: `standardAdopt` refuses it `STANDARD_NO_TEXT` (R1), whatever the proposer stated (ladders §6.4: the `legal_lookup` skill proposes standards with captured text). (T35; K1722, K1724) A member may instead adopt a proposal with no text as `held: "cited"` (R34), naming who cited it and where; it is then never a measure until a standard holding its text supersedes it (R6). A proposed force label (R35) is a proposal of the same kind, labelled through the same composer (subject `standard`), never a force until a member confirms it.
- **R10** `standardAdopt` is R1 by a member, naming the proposal; the new standard records the proposal it came from, and the proposal records its adoption. A proposal is adopted at most once. The adopting member's own `reason` is R1's (`STANDARD_NO_REASON`); the proposal's `why` (R9) is the proposer's and does not serve as it (DEC-88; K1025).

**standardsOf(host) → the module's instance**
- **R16** Constructing the instance creates every table this module declares to purge (R14), as the other factories do, so every service here and `record-core`'s purge succeed after construction with no caller calling `migrate()` (N220, N267, K267).

**T33: law structured and versioned** (T33-31; ladders §2 LAW, §6.4 L2–L3, §7.4 COURTS C1 and L4 links; K1438, K1442, K1446, K1447, K1449)

*The instrument, its portions and its copy*
- **R18** A declaration (R1) may name `instrument`, `portion`, `requires`, `copy`, `current_through` and `period_basis`. `instrument` is a work key shaped like ELI, composed by `instrumentKey({cite, view})` only from profile data: `jurisdictions` R50's `instrument_key.jurisdiction` segment, the matched `standard_sources` entry's `key` segment and the instrument's own number or section path as the cite states it; a source with no `key`, or no active profile, answers the key `undetermined` with why, never a key with a place in code. `portion` is a path within the instrument (the section path the `regulation` reader reads, `doctypes` R10) with the content id of its extent in the standard's text (`STANDARD_PORTION_NOT_IN_TEXT` when the extent is not one of the standard's text rows). Standards sharing an instrument key are its versions; a portion is answered as of a date by R20.
- **R19** `requires` is the list of passages of the portion that state what it requires, each a content id among the standard's text, quoted, never paraphrased (the comparison's "requires", ladders §6.3 L4). `copy` is `official`, `codifier` or `undetermined`, defaulting to the matched source's code `copy` (`jurisdictions` R6), and stated as declared beside it when a member declares otherwise. `current_through` is the date the copy states it is current through, with its basis (a codifier's banner, captured), or null for not stated. `period_basis` names what the period rests on: a cited passage, or an enactment event (`EVT-`, `events`) with the edge used (`start` or `end` of its `when`). Malformed values are refused `STANDARD_FIELD_INVALID`, naming the field.

*In force at a date*
- **R20** `inForceAt({key | standard, portion?, date, viewer?})` answers `{state, why, standard, version}`, `state` `in_force`, `not_in_force` or `undetermined`, through `civil-time.validAt` over each version's period: a period bound given as an event is read from that event's `when` (`events`), and an event with no `when` or an undetermined band answers `undetermined` with why. With a key, the version whose period covers `date` answers; two versions both covering it, or none deciding it, answer `undetermined` naming them. An adopted temporal relation (`law-relations` R2, read through its R10) bounds the period of the version it amends, repeals, renumbers or recodifies at its effective date. A date after a codifier copy's `current_through` with no later version held answers `undetermined` with "versions after <current_through> not held" (codifier lag, `measures-T33/time-law.md` §4), never `in_force`. It writes nothing and never throws.
- **R21** `standardsFor({target, limit?, viewer})` is the reverse index (LAW N4): for a held standard (or an instrument key, with an optional portion) it answers every document whose reading cites it (a reference `extraction` holds whose recognised key or cite matches the standard's instrument key or cite, by R3's patterns), and for a document (a bundle or capture) the held standards its readings cite. Each item carries how it matched. A document the viewer may not see is neither answered nor counted. `limit` is clamped to 1–500 (default 100), with `truncated` by reading one past.

**T35: policies and standards held** (T35-31; N643; Capability Ladders §6B.4, §6C.4; K1713, K1722–K1724, K1739, K1740; DEC-145)

*Family, held state and force*
- **R33** (PO1; `jurisdictions` R63) A declaration may name `family`: `{key, series, number}`, the policy series (or a standards body's family) the item belongs to, `key` and `series` an active profile's `standard_sources` entry's `key` and `series.key`, and `number` the item's number in that series' normal form. When the cite matches an entry with `series` (R3, and `id-spaces.recogniseSeries`, its R30, reading the same entries), `family` is taken from that match and a declared `family` that differs is kept as declared, the difference stated beside it (R3's rule). A `key`/`series` pair no active profile holds is refused `FAMILY_UNKNOWN`; no match and no declaration answer `family: undetermined` with why. `familyKey(family)` composes the family's key from `key` and `series` (`jurisdictions` R63) and is pure. `standardsIn` (R8) takes `family` as a filter. Every read of a standard answers its family and its owner (R45).
- **R34** (PO3; K1724) `held` is one of `text` (the standard's own words are held, R2), `cited` (known only because a held capture cites it: `cited_by`, `{captureSha, extent}` of the citing passage, required, `STANDARD_CITED_BY_MISSING`, refused as `content`'s extent refusals), or `absent` (looked for and not found: `search`, `{places, request?, answer?}`, required, `STANDARD_SEARCH_MISSING`: `places` a non-empty list of the portals, sites or offices searched, each a string of at most 500 characters or a held capture of the page searched, `request` an opaque reference to the records request made, `answer` a held capture extent of its reply). `isMeasure(id)` answers `true` only for a standard held `text` that the viewer may read; `inForce` and `inForceAt` (R7, R20) answer a `cited` or `absent` entry `undetermined` with "its text is not held, so it is not a measure", never `in_force`; and a later module that judges an act against a standard (`conformance`, `calculations` R6) asks `isMeasure` first. A `cited` or `absent` entry is read like any standard (R5) and is a fact a finding may state ("relies on a policy it has not produced"; "no written procedure exists"). It is superseded (R6) by a standard holding its text when the text is found.
- **R35** (PO2, ST3; K1722) `forceDeclare({standard, portion, force, holder?, criteria?, citation, reason, author, viewer})` (`op=standardforce`) records, by a member's act, the force of one provision or clause: `portion` a portion path of the standard (R18, R31; `PORTION_UNKNOWN`); `force` one of `requires`, `recommends`, `allows` (any kind) or, for a `policy`, `mandatory` or `discretionary` (`FORCE_UNKNOWN` otherwise, naming the list for the kind); for `discretionary`, `holder`, the office or body holding the discretion (a registered entity; `FORCE_NO_HOLDER`), and `criteria`, a content id of the standard's text stating the criteria, or the literal `"none"`, read "no criteria stated" (`FORCE_NO_CRITERIA` when absent); `citation`, the content id of the provision's own words that state the force (`FORCE_NO_CITATION`; `STANDARD_PORTION_NOT_IN_TEXT` when it is not among the standard's text rows). Refusals before these: `MACHINE_CANNOT_DECLARE_STANDARD` (R1's), `NO_SUCH_STANDARD` (R17), `FORCE_TEXT_NOT_HELD` (a standard not held `text`, R34), and after them `STANDARD_NO_REASON`. A provision holds at most one confirmed force; a second is refused `FORCE_ALREADY_CONFIRMED`, naming it, and a correction withdraws it with a reason (`forceWithdraw`, kept with who, when and why) before a new one. `forcePropose({standard, portion, force, holder?, criteria?, citation, why, proposer})` stores a proposed label apart, R9's way; `forceConfirm({proposal, reason, author, viewer})` is `forceDeclare` by a member naming the proposal, refused as it is. `forcesOf({standard, viewer})` answers each portion's confirmed force with its citation, holder and criteria, and its proposals apart, labelled. Nothing in this module writes a force without a citation of the provision's own words, and none is derived from another provision, a reader or a default (the machine never raises a force the text does not state).

*The copy, sight from the source, versions*
- **R36** (PO4; amends R19) `copy` is one of `official`, `codifier`, `in_force`, `draft`, `superseded`, `production` (produced in answer to a records request), `vendor_model` or `undetermined`, as the member declares it of the captured copy (`COPY_UNKNOWN` otherwise); R19's default from the matched source still applies to `official` and `codifier`. `copy_claimed`, optional, is what the copy says of itself (a header's "DRAFT", an effective date, a supersession line): `{says, extent}`, `says` one of the same values and `extent` a content id of its text stating it (`COPY_CLAIM_NOT_CITED`). The two are held apart, never merged: a read answers both, and their difference stated beside them, never resolved.
- **R37** (PO12; K1740, K1489) A `policy` whose text's capture is held in a bundle not every member may see (a hidden project, a source's material; `membership.viewerPredicate` over the capture's bundle) is held with that bundle's sight (`record-core`'s sight class `bundle`), not R14's `group`: every read of it, its forces, versions and relations (R5, R7, R8, R20, R21, R32, R35, R43) answers it only to a viewer who may see that bundle, and to any other exactly as an absent standard (R17), counted nowhere. `releaseStandard({standard, reason, author, viewer})` (`op=standardrelease`) by an owner of that bundle's project (`RELEASE_NOT_OWNER` otherwise; `NO_SUCH_STANDARD`; `STANDARD_NO_REASON`; `NOT_HELD_FROM_SOURCE` for a standard held at `group` sight) moves it to `group` sight from then on, recorded with who, when and why, and is never undone. A read admitted to such a policy answers `not_public: {release_by}`, naming whose it is to release (R45). Other kinds keep R14's `group` sight whatever their capture's bundle (C6; ladders §6C.8).
- **R38** (PO5, PO8; amends R18, R20) A declaration may carry: `version_basis: {captures: [earlier, later]}`, two held captures of one address whose texts differ, naming the version it supersedes (R6): its period's `from` is then the band after the earlier capture's retrieval instant and on or before the later's, never an enactment date, and its basis says it rests on the captures (`VERSION_BASIS_INVALID`: not two held captures of one address, or `supersedes` absent); `overrides: [{target, portion, until}]`, a portion of this standard that displaces `portion` of the held standard `target` until `until`, an event (`EVT-`, `events`) or a later revision of this standard's own key (`OVERRIDE_INVALID`, naming the field; `NO_SUCH_STANDARD`; `PORTION_UNKNOWN`); `force_source: {kind, citation}`, where its force comes from, `kind` one of `delegation`, `resolution`, `oversight_approval`, `court_order`, `contract`, `citation` a content id of the instrument stating it (`FORCE_SOURCE_INVALID`). R20 reads overrides: a target portion with an override in force on `date` answers `state: "overridden"` with the overriding standard and portion and why; an override whose `until` event has no `when`, or whose order against `date` is undetermined, answers `undetermined` with why, never `in_force`. `overridesOf({standard, viewer})` answers the overrides naming or made by a standard.

*Designation, edition, issuer, adoption, access*
- **R39** (ST1; K1453) A declaration may carry `designation` (the standard's designation as its issuer writes it, at most 200 characters) and `edition` (its edition or year as written, at most 50), read from the cite where `id-spaces.recogniseSeries` reads them (its R30's `number`, `edition`) and kept as declared otherwise, the difference stated (R3's rule); an edition is never defaulted (`id-spaces` R31). `issuer` may be a registered entity (`entities`; `NO_SUCH_ENTITY`) of any kind and sector (`entities` R42, K1453) or, as before, a string; a read answers the entity's label and sector beside it.
- **R40** (ST2, ST5; amends R20, R22) `adoptionRecord({standard, act, edition, from, amendments?, mode, citation, reason, author, viewer})` (`op=standardadopt` is R10's; this is `op=standardadoption`) records, by a member's act, that a body adopted an edition: `act` the held standard (an ordinance, resolution or policy) or event that adopted it, `edition` as R39, `from` a date or an event (as R19's `period_basis`), `amendments` portions of `act` amending the adopted edition (`law-relations` R1), `mode` one of `by_reference`, `voluntary`, `by_accreditation`, `citation` the act's passage stating the adoption; the adopting body is `act`'s issuer. Refusals, in order, each writing nothing: `MACHINE_CANNOT_RELATE`, `NO_SUCH_STANDARD` naming the end, `ADOPTION_MODE_UNKNOWN`, `ADOPTION_NO_EDITION`, `ADOPTION_NO_CITATION`, `STANDARD_NO_REASON`. `editionInForce({designation | standard, body, date, viewer})` answers the edition the body's adoptions put in force on `date` (`civil-time.validAt` over each adoption's `from` up to the next adoption's), with the adoption it rests on and the lag between the edition's own date, where held, and the adoption's `from`; none deciding it, two deciding it, or an undetermined `from` answer `undetermined` naming them. `incorporates`, carrying the `edition` incorporated, is a referential relation of `law-relations` (its R9), recorded by a member there; R43 reads it.
- **R41** (ST4; K1739, K1449) A declaration may carry `access`, one of `free`, `reading_room`, `paywalled` (`ACCESS_UNKNOWN`); a `standard` kind with no `access` reads `undetermined`. Edition, citation, adoption and access are held whatever is held of the text. A standard whose `access` is `reading_room` or `paywalled` takes text only from a capture a member made by their own act (the capture's author a member, never a machine credential; `TEXT_NOT_MEMBER_CAPTURED` otherwise), kept inside the group: no read of this module answers its text to a caller that is not a member viewer. Which passages a publication may quote is `case-checker`'s (K1739), not this module's.

*Targets and binding*
- **R42** (ST6; K1471) A declaration of kind `commitment`, `policy` or `standard` may carry `target`: `{metric, threshold, period, definition}`: `metric` the measured quantity in words with the content id stating it; `threshold` `{comparator, value, unit}`, `comparator` one of `at_least`, `at_most`, `within`, `value` an exact decimal (`calc-grammar`'s figure parser), `unit` as the text states it ("percent", "seconds", "days"); `period` the period it applies to (a `civil-time` period or a recurrence the text states); `definition` the content id of the body's own definition of the metric, or `"none"`, read "no definition stated". `TARGET_INVALID` names the field. A target's definition is adopted only by a member's act (the declaration's own). Whether a target was met is `calculations`' (a `CALC-` over the measure, K1471); this module holds the target and never computes it.
- **R43** (ST7; K1723) `bindsAt({standard, body, date, viewer})` answers whether a held standard binds a body on a date: `binds` when, on `date`, the body issued it (its issuer is `body`), or an adoption (R40) by `body`, or an adopted `incorporates` relation (`law-relations` R9) from a standard that binds `body`, or an imposition recorded by a member, puts it in force (R20, R40); otherwise, for a `policy`, `standard` or `commitment`, `benchmark`, labelled "not binding on" the body, and for a `statute`, `regulation`, `ordinance` or `court` standard `undetermined` ("whether this law binds the body is not recorded"), never `benchmark` by default; `undetermined` with why also where a date or an adoption needed to decide is undetermined. `impositionRecord({standard, body, law, citation, reason, author, viewer})` records, by a member's act, that the held law `law` imposes the standard on `body`, citing the passage of `law` that says so (refused as R23 refuses a relation: `MACHINE_CANNOT_RELATE`, `NO_SUCH_STANDARD` naming the end, `NO_SUCH_ENTITY` for `body`, `LAW_RELATION_NO_CITATION`, `STANDARD_NO_REASON`); it binds from the law's own in-force date (R20). Each answer names what it rests on. A member may declare a comparison a benchmark (`benchmarkDeclare({standard, body, reason, author, viewer})`, recorded with who, when and why); a declaration never makes a standard bind, and nothing in this module labels a benchmark binding. A later module that would call an act "nonconforming" or "violated" asks `bindsAt` and may say so only on `binds`; against a `benchmark` it says "below", "slower than" or another comparison (`conformance`, `calculations`). It writes nothing and never throws.
