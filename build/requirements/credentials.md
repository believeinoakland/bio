## Public

### Purpose

Holds the credentials a member or the instance acts by: the founder's password and claim, members' passwords and sessions, the signer keys whose signatures the record accepts, the credentials AI work runs under, each member's own Claude account reference and the short-lived grant an ask reads under, the group's own keys for keyed outside services, the group's setting that keeps its material away from every assistant, administrators' recovery codes, and the count-only tally of refused requests administrators read as the group's security level. There is no project-level Claude account, and the subscription token is each member's own, both by Bob's choice (K1502, K1547, K1755; `build/terms/anthropic.md`, Bob's choices), not by a rule of Anthropic's; the group may hold one Anthropic API key (K1755, R33–R36). Who the members are, and what each may do, is `membership`'s; this module reads it through membership's services and never decides it.

### Provides

Terms as `membership` states them (administrators, `by` the control plane's stamp). Every refusal names its reason; no service throws.

**Sign-in and sessions**
- **R1** (was `membership` R1) `claim({password, tokenFp, source, country})` sets the founder's password once: `SIGN_IN_PAUSED` while R38's window holds it (asked first; F3); `PASSWORD_TOO_SHORT` under 12 characters; `ALREADY_CLAIMED` once claimed, unless `tokenFp` differs from the one recorded at the claim (the bootstrap credential was replaced), which re-arms the claim. Each refused claim counts toward R38's window and R44's tally.
- **R2** (was `membership` R72) `bootstrapState(tokenFp)` answers `{claimed, rearmed, consumedAt}`: `claimed` once the bootstrap credential is spent, unless `tokenFp` differs from the one recorded at the claim (`rearmed`, R1); `consumedAt` is the instant the instance was claimed, `null` when re-armed. It names nobody and returns no secret.
- **R3** (was `membership` R73) `setPassword({role, password})` stores a salted, derived hash for `role`, replacing any earlier one, and never the password; it is an in-plane call reached by no route (`enroll`'s setter, R20; `recover`, R47). (T37; N776, DEC-182 (4)) `passwordChange({current, password, by, session, source, country})` (`op=setpassword`) is a signed-in member's or administrator's change of their own password. The role is the role of the live session `session` names (R5), `by` and `session` the control plane's stamps; a `role` in the body is never read. Refusals, in order, each writing nothing but its count: `MACHINE_CANNOT_SET_PASSWORD` with its row when `by` is a machine credential or an operator token; `NOT_SIGNED_IN` (R39's row) when `session` names no live session; R38's window (`SIGN_IN_PAUSED`); `PASSWORD_TOO_SHORT` under 12 characters; `CURRENT_PASSWORD_WRONG` with its row when `current` does not match that role's stored password (R41's comparison), counted toward R38's window and R44's tally (`signin`). On success, in one act, the password is set as `setPassword` sets it and every other session of that role ends, with the ask grants minted under them (R27, R39); the presenting session stays. It answers `{ok: true, role, ended}`, never either password. Neither password is logged, stored, echoed or carried in any answer. *(not yet met: T37)*
- **R4** (was `membership` R2) `login({role, password, ttlSeconds, source, country})` returns `{token, expires}` for a role holding a credential whose password matches (R41's comparison) and, for `member:<id>`, whose member is `active` (`membership.memberFacts`, its R68). Every other case (no credential, inactive member, wrong password) answers the one refusal `SIGN_IN_REFUSED` with one fixed detail, at the same cost, so a refusal never tells which roles exist. R38's window is asked first (`SIGN_IN_PAUSED`); each `SIGN_IN_REFUSED` counts toward it and toward R44's tally (`signin`); `source` and `country` are as R38 and R44 state.
- **R5** (was `membership` R3, its sessions half) `session(token)` returns `null` for an unknown or expired token, else `{role, expires, capabilities, administer, member, handle, rootOfTrust}`, the last five being `membership.sessionRights(role)` (its R92) read at that call, so a capability change or revocation takes effect on the next request.
- **R38** (F3; K1881) `claim` (R1), `login` (R4), `recover` (R47) and `passwordChange` (R3) are under one sign-in window, counted per source and per role. `source` is the caller's keyed fingerprint of the connecting address, made as `capture` R56 makes one (never the address; a call with none counts under one shared source of its own); the role is the `role` asked (`admin` for `claim`), whether or not any credential is held under it (the session's role for `passwordChange`). For each source and each role, the refused attempts in any 10 minutes are estimated by a two-bucket sliding window (`prev × (1 − elapsed/W) + cur`, W 10 minutes; `capture` R31's form), counted in the same transaction as the refusal; at 10 or more for the source, or 10 or more for the role, the attempt is refused `SIGN_IN_PAUSED` before any password, code or claim is judged. `SIGN_IN_PAUSED` is one answer for every arm (either bucket full; a role held or not; a member active or not): `{ok: false, reason, code, check, translation, detail, stated}`, `stated` the bound composed from the same constants, at the same cost as R4's refusal (one password derivation), so neither its words nor its time tell which bucket was full or whether the role exists. A paused attempt counts toward neither window and is written to R44's tally as `rate`. A success counts toward neither and empties neither. *(not yet met: T37)*
- **R39** (F14; K1881) `signOut({token})` ends the session `token` names (R5); `signOutEverywhere({token})` ends every session held under the role of the session `token` names, that one included. Each answers `{ok: true, ended}`, `ended` the number of sessions ended, and every ask grant minted under an ended session ends with it (R27). A token naming no live session (unknown, expired or already ended) is refused `NOT_SIGNED_IN` with its row, ending nothing, the same answer for each. From then on R5 answers `null` for every ended token. Neither ends another role's sessions or a standing question's grant (R32).

**Signing keys**
- **R6** (was `membership` R25) `signerAdd({keyB64, memberId, comment, by})`: `NOT_AN_ADMIN` as `membership` R12 (through `membership.notAnAdmin`, its R84); `BAD_KEY` unless the base64 field of an SSH public key; `NO_SUCH_MEMBER`; `SIGNER_MEMBER_NOT_ENROLLED` (no handle) or `SIGNER_MEMBER_NOT_ACTIVE`. Registering a known key rebinds it and makes it `active`, never a second row.
- **R7** (was `membership` R26) `signerSet({keyB64, status, by})`: `NOT_AN_ADMIN` (`membership` R84); `BAD_STATUS`; `NO_SUCH_KEY`; activating is refused as R6 for the owning member; revoking is never refused.
- **R8** (was `membership` R27) `signerList()` gives each key's `status`, `status_by`, `status_at` (R21), `member_status` and `attests`: true exactly when the key and its member are both `active`. When false, `attests_why` is `key_revoked`, `member_absent` or `member_<status>`. The set of keys the ratification gate accepts is exactly those with `attests` true, from this one predicate. Each key also states `origin` (`admin` for a key R6 registered, `self` for one R9 registered) and `registered_by`.
- **R9** (was `membership` R89) **signerRegisterOwn({keyB64, comment, by})** (`op=signerregister`). A member session registers its own browser-held key. `MACHINE_CANNOT_REGISTER_KEY` (C-96.17) for a machine credential or an operator token; `BAD_KEY` as in R6; `SIGNER_MEMBER_NOT_ENROLLED` or `SIGNER_MEMBER_NOT_ACTIVE` for `by`; `SIGNER_KEY_HELD_BY_ANOTHER` (C-96.15) when another member holds the key, which is never rebound. A key `by` already holds and that is `active` answers `existed: true`, unchanged (its `origin` and `registered_by` stay as first registered); one `by` holds that is revoked is refused `SIGNER_KEY_REVOKED` (C-96.16) and stays revoked, since only an administrator re-activates a key (R7). A new key is `active`, with `origin: "self"` and `registered_by: by`. Every administrator is notified: the answer's `notified` names `membership.activeAdmins()` (its R86) at that instant, and the self-registration is read from the key's row (`origin`, `registered_by`, the instant) for the notice the feed delivers (N375).
- **R10** (was `membership` R90) **signerRevokeOwn({keyB64, by})** (`op=signerrevoke`). A member revokes their own key, and this is never refused for a key they hold. Another member's key answers `NO_SUCH_KEY`, identically to a key no one holds. A replacement is a new R9. An administrator revokes any key by R7.
- **R21** (N505) Each key records `status_at`, the instant its `status` last changed: set when a key is first registered (R6, R9), and each time an act changes its status (R6's re-activation, R7, R10, R16). An act that leaves the status as it was (R6 on an `active` key, R7 setting the status it has, R9's `existed: true`) leaves `status_at` unchanged. A key registered before `status_at` was recorded holds null ("not recorded"; never back-filled, D-85), until an act changes its status.
- **R11** (was `membership` R70) `attestingKeys()` answers the signer keys that attest, the one predicate R8 states, for every reader that splices it today (`signerList`, the gate's facts, a case's document facts).

  Row C-96.15 (R9; N364): `SIGNER_KEY_HELD_BY_ANOTHER`, "This key is registered to another member, so it cannot be yours. Make a new key in this browser. Nothing was changed."
  Row C-96.16 (R9; K535): `SIGNER_KEY_REVOKED`, "This key was revoked, so it cannot be registered again. Make a new key in this browser, or ask an administrator. Nothing was changed."

**AI credentials**
- **R12** (was `membership` R28) `aiCredentialMint({who, tokenId, secretSha, principalKind, principalMember, taskScope, writes, note, confinedTo, expiresInDays})`: `AI_CREDENTIAL_MINT_NOT_A_MEMBER` when `who` is absent or a machine; `AI_CREDENTIAL_PRINCIPAL_UNSTATED` unless `principalKind` is `organisation` or `member`; `AI_CREDENTIAL_IDENTITY_TAKEN` for an empty or used `tokenId`. Records the credential with `minted_by` = `who`; never stores a secret, only its hash. `expiresInDays` is R42's.
- **R13** (was `membership` R62) An organisation-scoped credential (`principalKind: organisation`) is minted only by an active administrator (the founder included; `membership.isAdministrator`, its R64); anyone else is refused `NOT_AN_ADMIN` (`membership` R84), its `remedy` naming the member-scoped credential open to every member.
- **R14** (was `membership` R29) A member-scoped credential's principal is `who` itself; naming another member is refused.
- **R15** (was `membership` R30) `aiCredentialRevoke({who, tokenId})`: refuses a machine or absent `who` and an unknown id; revoking twice answers `already: true`. `aiCredentialLook({secretSha})` and `aiCredentials({limit})` never return the secret; the list is capped (default 200, at most 500) with a measured `truncated`. Every credential they answer carries `expiresAt` and `expired` (R42).

- **R42** (F15; K1881) An agent credential expires. `aiCredentialMint` (R12) takes an optional `expiresInDays`, a whole number from 1 to 365, 90 when not given; anything else is refused `AI_CREDENTIAL_BAD_EXPIRY` with its row, writing nothing. The credential records `expiresAt`, `expiresInDays` whole days after it was minted, and every answer showing a credential (R12, R15's `aiCredentialRevoke`, `aiCredentialLook` and `aiCredentials`) carries `expiresAt` and `expired`, true from `expiresAt` on. An expired credential is no longer live: `aiCredentialLook` answers it with `expired: true`, as it answers a revoked one with `revoked: true`, for the gate to refuse (`admission` R10); it is never renewed, and a new mint replaces it. A credential minted before expiry was recorded is given `expiresAt` 90 days after the migration that records it.
- **R53** (N761; K2129) This module's ops map takes `aicredentialmint`'s `secretSha` only from the internal request's body (the control plane sets it there after removing any a caller sent, `control-plane`'s N761 requirement), never from the request's query: a `secretSha` in the query is never read. R12 then refuses a `secretSha` that is not 64 lowercase hexadecimal characters `AI_CREDENTIAL_NO_SECRET` with its row (the next free C-29 number, C-29.33), writing nothing, so a mint that carries no digest never records a credential no lookup can find. *(not yet met: T37)*

**Each member's own Claude account (T33-20; K1502, K1503)** Terms: a **reference** is `{kind, secret}`, `kind` `subscription` (the member's own token, made through Anthropic's own sign-in, `claude setup-token`, AT-22 in `build/terms/anthropic.md`) or `apikey` (the member's own API key), the kinds `agent-model` reads. The member is the session's `member:<id>`, as the control plane stamps it.
- **R22** `accountReferenceSet({member, kind, secret, by})` holds one reference for `member`, replacing any earlier one, and only by that member's own act: `by` a machine credential or operator token is refused `MACHINE_CANNOT_HOLD_ACCOUNT`; `by` any other member, an administrator or the founder included, `NOT_YOUR_ACCOUNT`; `member` not `active` (`membership.memberFacts`) `ACCOUNT_MEMBER_NOT_ACTIVE`; a `kind` outside the two `UNKNOWN_ACCOUNT_KIND`; an empty `secret` `NO_SECRET`. Each refusal writes nothing. It answers `{ok, kind, set_at}` and never the secret. `accountReferenceRemove({member, by})`, by the same member's act only (the same refusals), removes it; a member with no reference is served by the group's API key while it is on (R35), else has no assistant (K1502, K1755).
- **R23** A reference is sealed at rest under its member: its secret is stored only encrypted, under a key no other member's act reaches, and is never shown or exported: no answer of any op, no list, log, error, export or audit carries it or a digest of it, and its table is declared `export: "never"` (R30). `accountReferenceState({member, viewer})` answers `{held, kind, set_at, suggestions, standing}` to that member alone; any other viewer, an administrator included, is answered `NOT_YOUR_ACCOUNT`, writing nothing.
- **R24** `accountReferenceFor({member, act})` unseals a member's reference only for that member's own asks, runs and standing questions: `act` is `{kind: "ask" | "run" | "standing", member}`, and it answers the reference only when `act.member` is `member` (a run the member started; a standing question the member authored, K1503). Any other `act` is refused `NOT_YOUR_ACCOUNT`; a member with none is refused `NO_ACCOUNT` by name (D-260 as K1503 reads it: a run with no member account is refused by name). The answer is used for the one call it serves and kept by no caller (`agent-model` R8).
- **R25** (K1479, K1500, K1502) Two switches belong to the member's reference, each off by default and set only by that member's own act, `accountSwitchSet({member, switch, on, by})`, `switch` `suggestions` or `standing` (R22's refusals; `UNKNOWN_SWITCH` for another name). `suggestions` on lets the assistant offer labelled suggestions for that member's asks (`skills`, `agent-worker`); `standing` on lets that member's standing questions call the model when their saved search finds something new (`answers`). Nothing here reads them for any other member, and removing the reference (R22) turns both off. These switches govern only acts served by the member's own reference; acts served by the group key follow the group's switches (R37).

**The ask grant (Q1-3; K1450, K1505 (14))**

- **R31** (N616, K1685) `aiGrantHeld({token})` answers whether `token` is a live grant, asking about no op: `{ok: true, member, viewer, expires}` exactly when R28 would admit a listed read under it (unexpired and, for an R27 grant, its session live; for an R32 grant, not ended by R32), else `GRANT_NOT_HELD` with its row (C-29.24), as R28 refuses a token that is not held. **The security tally and the level (F9, N703; DEC-165, DEC-166; K1874, K1875, K1882)**
- **R44** Counts only. `securityCount({kind, country})`, an in-plane write reached by no route, adds one to the count of `kind` for the current UTC hour and `country`. `kind` is one of `signin` (a refused sign-in, claim, recovery or password change's current password: R1, R4, R47, R3), `credential` (a refused key or link: an expired or unknown invitation, a wrong website key or join link, a revoked or expired agent credential), `rate` (a request turned away for coming too fast: R38's pause, the door's windows), `handover` (a refused hand-over at the doorbell) and `through` (a sign-in or recovery that succeeded under a role R38 paused in the hour before it). This module writes `signin`, `through` and R38's `rate` itself; `admission` and `capture` write theirs through this service, never a table of their own. `country` is Cloudflare's two-letter label for the request as the caller read it, or null; no address reaches this module, and it keeps and derives none. A count holds its kind, its hour and its country and nothing else: no address, fingerprint, handle, role, op, path, time within the hour, or content. An unknown `kind` is refused `SECURITY_KIND_UNKNOWN`, counting nothing; a count that cannot be written is dropped and never changes the answer of the act that refused. Counts older than 90 days are dropped. DEC-166's place rule: a count this module writes for a sign-in, claim or recovery (`signin`, and R38's `rate`) is held without a place at once, and its country is added only once an hour has passed with no successful sign-in or recovery under the same role, never when one follows within the hour; while it waits nothing names the role but a keyed digest of it, dropped when the hour ends; `through` is never placed. A successful sign-in is never counted, by place or otherwise. *(not yet met: T37)*
- **R45** `securityMap({from, to, by})` (`op=securitymap`) answers administrators only (`NOT_AN_ADMIN` through `membership.notAnAdmin`, its R84; a machine credential refused the same). `from` and `to` are instants, `from` before `to`, the period at most 90 days and inside the counts kept; anything else is refused `SECURITY_PERIOD_INVALID` with its row, naming what. It reads R44's counts alone, writes nothing, and answers `{ok: true, from, to, step, buckets, totals, countries, unplaced, note, level, levelAt}`, as follows.
  - `step` is `hour` for a period up to 48 hours, `six-hours` up to 14 days, else `day`; `buckets` covers the period in steps, each `{start, counts, usual}`, `counts` and `usual` keyed by R44's kinds and `total`.
  - The usual of an hour is the median of the counts for the same hour of the day on each of the 28 days before it, a day with none counting 0; a bucket's usual is the sum of its hours' usuals. `totals` is `{count, usual, busiest}`, `busiest` the bucket with the largest total, the earliest on a tie.
  - `countries` ranks the period's placed counts by country, most first, each `{country, count}`; `unplaced` is the count of the rest; `note` is exactly "A country is not proof of who is behind an attempt." (DEC-166 (1)).
  - `level` is `Ordinary`, `Raised` or `High`, at BOB's threshold (K1881): an hour is unusual when its total is at least 10 and more than five times its usual (five, when its usual is 0). The period is `High` when it holds any `through`, or when its last whole hour is unusual and `to` is within the hour before the call (still going on); else `Raised` when any hour of it is unusual; else `Ordinary`. `levelAt` is the start of the first unusual hour, or of the first `through` when that came first, or null.
  `securityLevel()`, an in-plane read reached by no route and answered to no viewer, answers `{level, levelAt}` for the 24 hours ending at the call, for `notice-producers`' one "Noticed" when the level becomes High (DEC-165 (7)). (N743; K2038) When the counts cannot be read, `securityLevel` answers the failure as a failure, `{level: null, levelAt: null}`, never `Ordinary`.
- **R49** (K1946 T3; `plan/draft-T36-virus-requirements.md` rev. 2 conflict (g)) `securityTotals({from, to})`, an in-plane read reached by no route and answered to no viewer, answers R44's counts for a period, for `file-safety`'s log forwarding (its R35): `{ok: true, from, to, counts}`, `counts` keyed by R44's five kinds, each the sum, over every country and over counts not placed, of that kind's counts for the hours that start at or after `from` and before `to`. `from` and `to` are instants, `from` before `to`; anything else is refused `SECURITY_PERIOD_INVALID` with its row (R45's), naming what. When the counts cannot be read it answers a refusal naming the failure, never zeros, so the forwarding sends them as absent. It writes nothing, names no country, and never throws.
- **R50** (N744; K2038) This module's ops map routes `securitycount`, R44's in-plane write, as a store-internal route that no caller reaches and that has no op spec (`op-declarations` R6), so `admission` R22 writes its tally through it from the Worker: the route takes `{kind, country}` only and answers as R44 does.

**Recovery codes (K1888)**

- **R47** `recover({role, code, password, source, country})`, reached without a credential, sets a new password for `role` and spends `code`.
