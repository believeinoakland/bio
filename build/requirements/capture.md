## Public

### Purpose

Brings material into the record with its provenance: fetches a document from its public address (directly, through a Google Drive export, from a web archive, or rendered as a visitor saw it), hashes and stores it as it arrives, records the fetch as a receipt, profiles what it is, captures a page's supporting files, and returns the provenance document a caller promotes. It also keeps what capture learns about sources and sites (reachability, site assets, links), and the doorbell by which anyone may hand the group material. It writes no bundle: no intake path writes live state.

### Provides

Terms. The **document address** is what the record identifies the document by; the **retrieval locator** is what was actually fetched (they differ for an archive replay and a Drive export). A **public locator** is `https:` on a named public host (`isPublicHttpsLocator`: no bare IP, no `localhost`, no credentials, a dotted host). The **caller class** and whether the caller is a member session are the control plane's stamps. Every refusal names a `reason`; one with a catalogue row carries its `check` and `translation`.

**acquire(body, {cls, member}) → answer** The one act that fetches and files. `body`: `locator`, optional `via` (`"archive.org"`), `address` (archive arm), `render`, `subresources`, `continue`, `authority`, `file`, `matchedSweep`. Success answers `{ok: true, existed, existed_undetermined?, document, parts?, subresources?, snapshot?, files?, subresources_skipped?, note}`.

**captureLimit(runtime) / recordCaptureLimit({runtime, observed})**
- **R23** The platform's outbound-request ceiling is learned by being refused, never declared: `recordCaptureLimit` with an observed ceiling records it (a different value replaces it and keeps the old one as `previous` with the instant it moved); with none, it counts one more run that was not refused and records nothing about where the ceiling is. `captureLimit` answers the ceiling, and `probeDue` when none is recorded or 25 runs have passed unrefused since the last observation, so a ceiling is re-probed rather than only ever learned downward.

**Site assets: siteAssets({host, addresses}), recordSiteAssets({host, primarySha, observations}), reusedParts(bundleId), recordReuseVerdicts({bundleId, verdicts}), reuseVerdicts({bundleId | sourceCapture})**

Through the op, `siteAssets` is bounded the same way (`limit`, `truncated`, `next`) unless `addresses` names the assets asked; the in-process walk (`acquisition` R19) reads it whole. *(bounded by N90, K175: `limit` defaults to 200, clamped 1…1000; each answer publishes `limit` and `truncated`, a paged read also `next`, the cursor passed back as `after`)*
- **R25** `recordSiteAssets` adds an unseen asset; for one fetched with bytes different from those held it updates the asset, counts the change, and appends a dated `posthoc` `changed` reuse verdict for every capture that reused the old bytes, naming them; a fetched asset's `last_fetched_by` names the capture whose fetch it was, and a reuse never moves it (CAP-14). A reused part's source is the reusing capture's own record, `undetermined` when it names none. `recordSiteAssets` answers every change counted in `changed` but lists at most `limit` of them in `changes`, each naming at most `limit` reusing captures (`reused_by`, with `reused_by_count`), and `truncated` when either list was cut; every posthoc verdict is still appended. *(bounded by N90, K175: `limit` defaults to 200, clamped 1…1000; each answer publishes `limit` and `truncated`, a paged read also `next`, the cursor passed back as `after`)*
- **R26** `recordReuseVerdicts` appends dated `ratify`-phase verdicts, never overwriting one; `reuseVerdicts` lists them newest first. `reuseVerdicts` lists at most `limit`, newest first, with `truncated`. `reusedParts(bundleId)` stays whole: ratification re-fetches every reused part, and the list is bounded by the bundle's own captures' walks (R23). *(bounded by N90, K175: `limit` defaults to 200, clamped 1…1000; each answer publishes `limit` and `truncated`, a paged read also `next`, the cursor passed back as `after`)*

**Links: recordLinks({sourceCapture, sourceBundle, capturedAt, links}), linksTo({address_norm}), resolveLinks({sourceCapture}), recordLinkVerdict(…)**
- **R27** `recordLinks` replaces a capture's outbound links, one row per (link, normalised citation), each with its raw and normalised address, fragment, partition and origin. `linksTo` answers every capture linking to an address and the elements cited. `recordLinkVerdict` appends a dated verdict for (source capture, address), never overwriting one, and answers the current verdict, the history and whether it changed. `resolveLinks` gives each `deferred` link, against the target's direct receipts only, a verdict: `contemporaneous` when one capture of the target was seen more than once on both sides of the source's retrieval; `superseded` when every capture of the target postdates it; otherwise `undetermined`, with the basis (bracketing captures differ, one capture at the source's own instant, all captures predate it, or a self-reference) and the target's capture and home. `offsite` when the record holds no capture of the target; `anchor`, `intra` and `refused` pass through. Undetermined is the resting state, stated as such. Every row passes the caller's viewer (membership R43, through the register's bundle) before it is listed or counted; a capture filed in no bundle is visible (D-701, K123). `linksTo` answers at most `limit` rows in (source, citation) order per answer, with `truncated` and `next` when more remain; `count` and `elements` are of the rows listed. The verdict history is the newest `limit` verdicts, oldest first, with `total` and `truncated`; `changed` is whether `total` exceeds one. Through the op, `resolveLinks` answers at most `limit` of the source's links per answer in link order, with `truncated` and `next`, the tally and verdict counts being of the links listed; an in-process caller that passes no `limit` is answered every link, and the answer says `limit: null`. *(bounded by N90, K175: `limit` defaults to 200, clamped 1…1000; each answer publishes `limit` and `truncated`, a paged read also `next`, the cursor passed back as `after`)*
- **R28** A link is classified as site chrome only when it is contained in a chrome region AND recurs across two or more distinct pages of the host; a link in a chrome region seen on one page only reads chrome `undetermined`, never lost; the classification is recorded with its basis and date and is always reclassifiable, never a deletion. `chromeOf({host, limit, after})` answers a host's classified links in address order, at most `limit`, with `truncated` and `next`. *(bounded by N90, K175: `limit` defaults to 200, clamped 1…1000; each answer publishes `limit` and `truncated`, a paged read also `next`, the cursor passed back as `after`)*
- **R29** Site chrome is a derived table, per host, regenerable by scan: each host's navigation observed with a fingerprint and date, and a per-host read of what navigation changed between captures. That read (`navChanges`, `op=navchanges`) answers the newest `limit` observations (default 200, at most 500), with `limit` and `truncated` and no `next`, since a comparison needs a contiguous sequence (K236).

**The render allowance: renderAdmit({allowanceMs, reserveMs, cap, at}) → `{state: "admitted", slot, …}` | `{state: "waiting", …}` | `{state: "deferred", …}`; renderSpend({ms, releaseMs, slot, at}) → day's account**
- **R39** Admission, in order: with `cap` set, slots past their expiry are reclaimed, and with `cap` or more running the answer is `waiting` with nothing reserved or counted; a reservation that is not positive is `deferred` (an unreserved admission is refused); when the day's spent plus reserved plus this reservation exceeds the allowance it is `deferred`, and the day's deferral count rises; otherwise the reservation is added to the day, a slot is taken that expires after the reservation, and the answer is `admitted` with its slot. Days are UTC dates.
- **R40** `renderSpend` frees the slot, adds the reported elapsed time (rounded up) to the day, and releases at most the reservation still held (never below zero); with no elapsed time reported it adds nothing and leaves the reservation charged.
- Errors: never throws.

**The doorbell: knock({contentB64 | contentText, note, contact}), inboxList(status, {sort, dir, limit, after}), inboxGet(knockId), inboxResolve({knockId, status, by, reason}), doorbellTally({viewer})**
- **R30** Anyone may knock, with no account. The envelope over the byte cap plus 4 KiB is `KNOCK_ENVELOPE_TOO_LARGE` (C-85.3); no content is a required-argument refusal; bad base64 likewise; empty content is `KNOCK_EMPTY` (C-85.5); content over 8 MiB (with an evidence store) or 64 KiB (without, stored inline) is `KNOCK_PAYLOAD_TOO_LARGE` (C-85.4).
- **R31** At most 5 knocks per source (a fingerprint of the connecting address, never the address) and 10 per instance in any 10 minutes, estimated by a two-bucket sliding window (`prev × (1 − elapsed/W) + cur`, refused at `≥ limit`) counted in the same transaction as the write: `RATE_IP` (C-85.1), `RATE_GLOBAL` (C-85.2), each with `stated`, the published bound composed from the same constants. A store that does not answer is reported as silence, never as a rate refusal. (DEC-108 (3); K1019)
- **R32** An accepted knock writes one inbox row (`KNOCK-<date>-<8 hex>`, digest, size, `note` to 2,000 characters, `contact` to 300, received time, status `new`) and, with an evidence store, the bytes under `bio/inbox/<digest>`; nothing else changes. Only a signed-in member lists, reads and resolves knocks (`pulled`, `discarded` or back to `new`, else `BAD_STATUS`), recorded with who and when; a knock id no knock answers to, read or resolved, is refused `NO_SUCH_KNOCK` (404, its own row in this module's table, K275; not R63's `EVIDENCE_NOT_HELD`), with nothing written. Every resolve, to any of the three statuses, takes the member's own `reason`: one absent, not a string, blank (white space only) or over 2,000 characters is refused `RESOLVE_NO_REASON` (C-118.7, 400), tried after `BAD_STATUS` and `NO_SUCH_KNOCK` and before anything is written, so the row, its status and its resolver stay as they were. An admitted resolve records the reason on the inbox row with who and when (DEC-88 (2); J1 (4), J2). A knock is not a capture and not a bundle. A knock becomes `pulled` only by R65: `inboxResolve` with `status: "pulled"` is R65's act for that knock and answers as R65 does, once its reason is admitted; the reason is recorded on the row with the pull. `pullKnock` called directly (`op=inboxpull`) is not `inboxResolve` and takes no reason (R65 unchanged). The inbox row also holds `knocker_digest` and `pseudonym` (R66), each null for a knock sent without a secret. `inboxList(status, {sort, dir, limit, after})` lists at most `limit` knocks, with `truncated` and `next`, sorted by `sort`: `received` (the default, newest first: today's order), `status`, `secret` (whether a knocker secret was presented: `knocker_digest` not null) or `project` (the project of the document a pulled knock was brought into, `provenance.homeOf`; a knock not yet pulled has no project and sorts last in either direction); `dir` is `asc` or `desc`; ties fall back to received time, newest first. An unknown `sort` or `dir` is the required-argument refusal naming it, with nothing read (DEC-108 (2)). No act of this module's clears a discarded knock's row or bytes while any litigation hold is in place (`actions` R52: an entry whose latest hold is `in_place`); this binds the discard archive's one-week clearing (DEC-108 (5)), which no requirement here yet provides. A later module states whether a hold is in place through a reader it registers once at start, as R44's listeners are registered; with none registered, or one that fails or does not answer, nothing discarded is cleared (DEC-108 (5)). *(bounded by N90, K175: `limit` defaults to 200, clamped 1…1000; each answer publishes `limit` and `truncated`, a paged read also `next`, the cursor passed back as `after`)* (DEC-88 (2); DEC-108 (2)(5); K1019)
- **R53** `knock` is a POST; any other method answers 405 and nothing else happens. The refusals are tried in this order and the first that applies answers: the envelope (R49); a body that is not JSON carrying `contentB64` or `contentText` as a string (the required-argument refusal, 400); `contentB64` that is not base64 (the same refusal naming `contentB64`, 400); empty content (R51); oversize content (R50); a weak knocker secret (R66's `KNOCKER_SECRET_WEAK`); the per-source rate (R47); the instance rate (R48). A refused knock writes no inbox row, stores no bytes and counts toward neither rate window; it is counted in R80's tally and nowhere else. A method other than POST is not a knock and is not counted. (DEC-108 (6); K1019)
- **R54** An accepted knock answers 200 `{ok: true, knockId, sha256, bytes, received}`: `sha256` the digest of the decoded bytes, `bytes` their length, `received` a sentence saying the material is in the group's inbox awaiting member review. With an evidence store, the answer is sent only after the bytes are held under `bio/inbox/<digest>`, and an inbox row never stands without its bytes: when they cannot be stored, the knock answers a failure and leaves no row. The answer also carries `pseudonym` (R66; null for a knock sent without a secret) and, when the doorbell generated the secret, `secret`: shown in that answer only and held nowhere.
- **R80** The doorbell keeps a count-only tally of the knocks it turns away: every knock R53 refuses (the envelope, a required argument, base64, empty, size, a weak secret, either rate limit) and every refusal R71 answers. For each UTC day it holds two counts: the knocks refused that day, and how many of them found the whole-doorbell limit reached (R48). It also holds the day the whole-doorbell limit was last reached, as a date only. It keeps the last 30 days and drops any older day. It holds no address, fingerprint, time of a knock, knocker digest, pseudonym, note, contact or content. `doorbellTally({viewer})` (`op=doorbelltally`) answers, to a member session only, `{days, last_limit_reached}`: `days` the kept days with at least one refusal, newest first, each `{day, refused, limit_reached}`, and `last_limit_reached` that date, or null when the limit has never been reached. It writes nothing. The tally is status, read where the inbox is read: no queue item and no notification is ever made from it. It never decides whether a knock is refused or admitted, and a count that cannot be written never changes the refusal's answer. (DEC-108 (6); K1019; BOB's privacy ruling on the tally)

**Bringing a knock in, and the knocker's pseudonym: pullKnock({knockId, by}), knocksOf({pseudonym, limit, after}), knockerDigestOf(secret)** (N364; DEC-78 items 1–4)
- **R65** **pullKnock({knockId, by})** (`op=inboxpull`). Refusals in order: the member-session fence (R32's); `NO_SUCH_KNOCK` (C-118.2); `KNOCK_DISCARDED` (C-118.4) for a knock at `discarded`; R63's `EVIDENCE_NOT_HELD` when its bytes are gone. A knock already pulled answers `existed: true` with the same capture. Otherwise, in one act: the bytes are held under their own digest; one acquisition receipt is written (`provenance.recordReceipt`) with `via: "doorbell"`, naming the knock as its address (`knock:<knockId>`); the knock becomes `pulled` with the capture's digest, `by` and the instant; and the answer carries a provenance document (`acquisition` R16) whose `source` is `{kind: "knocker", named: false, pseudonym, receipt: {knock_id, sha256, bytes, received}}` and whose `capture.actor` is `by`. The note travels as `knocker_note: {text, words_of: "the knocker", evidence_of_truth: false}`. The document never carries `contact`. Like `acquire`, it writes no bundle (`acquisition` R25); the control plane promotes the document at `collected` in the same act (`control-plane` R36). (N380, K580) `pullKnock` also takes `within`, a synchronous function (control-plane R36): it is called with the pulled document inside the pull's own transaction, after the receipt, the knock's `pulled` update and the actor are written. An answer `{ok: false, …}` rolls the whole pull back (no receipt, the knock unchanged, no actor) and is the pull's answer, with `knockId`; a throw, or an answer that is not synchronous, rolls it back likewise and answers `PULL_WITHIN_FAILED` (500, a programming fault, no row); its `detail` is one fixed sentence, never the thrown message (N409, K655). Any other answer is carried as `within` beside the pull's own. A knock already pulled does not call it. The bytes held under their own digest before the transaction stay (content-addressed).
- **R66** `knock` takes an optional `knockerSecret` (a string) or `generateSecret: true`. A supplied secret shorter than 20 characters is `KNOCKER_SECRET_WEAK` (C-118.3), and nothing is stored, and nothing counted but R80's tally. `generateSecret` makes one of at least 128 random bits. The row holds only `knocker_digest`, an HMAC-SHA-256 of the secret under an instance key held as R56's key is held, but a separate one (`KNOCKER_SECRET_KEY`), and `pseudonym`, a fixed readable derivation of the digest. The secret appears in no row, log or answer except R54's one-time `secret`. The same secret gives the same pseudonym at this instance, and a different one at another. `knockerDigestOf(secret)` answers `{knocker_digest, pseudonym}` for a presented secret at this instance (for `sources`' consent by secret), writes nothing and never throws. (DEC-108 (6); K1019)
- **R67** `knocksOf({pseudonym, limit, after})` answers, to a member session only, the knocks sharing that pseudonym, oldest first, with the continuity sentence "the same knocker secret was presented", never an identity. It answers at most `limit` (default 200, clamped 1…1000), with `truncated` and `next`, the cursor passed back as `after` (N90's bound).

**Late co-attestation and the capturing member's account: reattest({captureSha, locator?, by}), lateAttestationsOf(captureSha), recordCaptureAccount({captureSha, text, signature, by}), captureAccountsOf(captureSha)** (N364; DEC-81 item 3)

### Suggestions

- **The op handlers move here** (layers ruling 2); `control-plane` keeps routing, authentication and the envelope. `acquire` becomes a service the handler calls, which is also what the capture-request arm needs.
- **What stays out.** `projectLinks` writes `refs` (connections, K23). `driveShells`, the monitor tick, `#fireArchiveFallback` and the `#conditions*` readers are monitoring's and the scheduler's; they call `acquire`. The tasks inbox (`taskDrain`, `taskList`, `taskForward`, `taskResolve`, `#routeTask`) routes by project ownership and `refs`, so it goes to `queue` (K49's later module, named by K91 (3)), which drains this module's event queue through R45; this module keeps only the event queue R15 writes, and the render allowance (R39–R40).
- **Observation rows.** `recordReuseVerdicts` writes observation-log rows (§4.1 row 3); a listener like provenance's `onReceipt` (its R47) serves it.
- **The compute measurement** (`recordruntime`, `runtime_observations`) is `instance-setup`'s (K98), recorded by the listener it registers through R55. Until `instance-setup` is extracted, `legacy-store` registers the recording, as it registers the scheduler's arming for R44.
- **The doorbell's rate account.** `knock_rate` keeps only the current and the previous window's buckets (the prune after each accepted knock); the previous bucket is read by R31's estimate, so the prune must keep it. The source fingerprint is today the first 8 bytes of an unkeyed SHA-256 of the connecting address, which can be reversed by enumerating addresses; a keyed digest would keep R31's "never the address" against that.
- **Held captures (DEC-97).** The page's other two acts on ticked rows are existing ones: a batch release is `op=release` over the selection (`ratification` R20–R26), and "link them to a question" is `op=cite` over the selection onto the inquiry, with one stance for the whole batch (`citation` R1–R3; `retrieval` R18's selection) (K1019). A set-aside and its undo can share one table of appended acts, the latest per document deciding.
- **The tally (R80).** One row per UTC day with two counters, and one stored date, suffice; the count can be added in the refusal's own path without touching `knock_rate`. Its precision (whole days, 30 kept) is BOB's privacy ruling: a finer "when last", beside the inbox's received times, could single out a knocker.
