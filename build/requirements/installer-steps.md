## Public

### Purpose

Does what the install and update do to a group's Cloudflare account and to the release's fleet. Given a session on the account, the release chosen and the copy's names, it reaches the management API patiently, reads what the account holds and names a copy's parts, probes the plan, the buckets and the address, uploads the plane and each member under the release's own limits and bindings, places the container members, reads the upload back, verifies that every part serves the release, and checks that no Logpush job logs who opens which file. It chooses no release, draws no page and no cookie, takes no OAuth step, keeps nothing between calls, and stores no credential; the lines it draws on the progress page are drawn through the sink its caller hands it.

### Provides

Terms. A **session** is `new Session(token, onWait)`: the run's connection to the management API, holding the access token privately. **`onWait(waited, bound)`** is the caller's way to draw a wait (nominal seconds waited, nominal bound), or `null`. **`emit`** is the progress sink: this module calls only `emit.step(id, label)`, `emit.ok(id, label?)` and `emit.no(id, label?)`. A **release** is `{version, blob, sha?, capable, from, man, base?, limits}` as the installer's choice of release answers it: `blob` the plane's exact bytes as one `Blob`, `man` its signed manifest or null, `base` where its members are kept (absent: `RELEASE_LATEST`), `limits` the plane's stated limits (R10). A **member** is an entry of the manifest's `fleet`. A **step** is one emitted line, which ends ok or not. A **lag** is a sentence naming a part that is not on the release. **Names** are `namesFor(slug, scheme)`'s answer (R2). **`CF_API`**, **`RELEASE_LATEST`** and **`RELEASE_TAGGED(version)`** are the management API's base and the places a release is kept (the installer's `CFG` reads them back).

**The call to Cloudflare, and what the account holds**
- **R1** (split from `installer` R66, R71, R19; F4, F6) `cfCall(session, path, init, base)`, and `cf(session, path, init)` which is the same bound to `CF_API`, make one call to the management API with the session's bearer and answer the reply's `result`. A reply that is not ok or states `success: false` throws an Error carrying the API's first message (else `HTTP <status>`), its `code` and `status`. A 401 throws `ApproveAgain` (`approveAgain: true`) and is never retried. A failure waiting can mend (429, 5xx, no answer) is retried with back-off, 2 s doubling to 30 s, until `WAIT_BOUND_MINUTES` (5) of nominal waiting have passed, each wait announced by `session.onWait(waited, bound)` before it, then thrown as `Lasting` (`lasting: true`, the cause's message, status and code); the waits are the delays asked for, never read from a clock. A POST is repeated only when `init.idem` marks it safe, or after a 429 (the platform said it did nothing). `special(e)` is true of an `approveAgain` or a `lasting` error and `approve(e)` of an `approveAgain` alone; `apiOf(session, account)(path, init)` is the call with `/accounts/<id>` in front; `patiently(fn, {onWait, minutes})` and `sleep` are the same wait for any other call. The token is read only where a request is made; `String(session)` is `[session]`, and no error or answer carries the token. Test: a 429 then a 200 completes after one announced wait; a 401 throws `approveAgain` after one request; a 503 for the whole bound throws `lasting` having announced each wait; a POST not marked `idem` answering 500 is not repeated, one answering 429 is; control: with every timer immediate the same waits are counted (no clock is read).
- **R2** (split from `installer` R64) `namesFor(slug, scheme)` answers a frozen `{slug, scheme, captures, published, member(m), app(m, class, multi), role(r)}`. In the `slug` scheme: the buckets `bio-captures-<slug>` and `bio-published-<slug>`, a member's Worker `<member>-<slug>`, a container application `<member>-<slug>` (one class) or `<member>-<class lowercased>-<slug>` (several). In the `legacy` scheme: the fixed `bio-captures` and `bio-published`, the member's own name, and `<member>` or `<member>-<class lowercased>`. `role("captures")` is the captures bucket, any other role the published one. `schemeOf(bindings, slug)` reads a plane's `CAPTURES` r2 binding: `legacy` for `bio-captures`, `slug` for `bio-captures-<slug>`, `null` when it names neither or is absent. Pure; never throws. Test: both schemes for a one-class and a two-class member; `schemeOf` of each binding and of none. Control: two slugs share no name in the `slug` scheme.
- **R3** (split from `installer` R62, R69, R70) `inventory(api, memberNames)` answers `{scripts, buckets, planes, orphans}`: `scripts` a Map of the Workers by name (with their migration tag where stated), `buckets` the names, `planes` one `{slug, scheme, bindings, finished}` for each Worker that holds the Durable Object class `Store` (`scheme` from R2's `schemeOf`, else `slug`; `finished` true for the `legacy` scheme and for a plane holding the binding `INSTALL_COMPLETE`, R6), and `orphans` `{legacy, slugged}`, the buckets and members no plane of that copy holds: the fixed names in `legacy`, the others in `slugged` (a Map from slug to names). A lookup that fails throws, and so does a plane whose settings cannot be read: an absence not established is not established. `allCopies(inv)` lists every copy, a plane and what it holds, or what is left of one without a plane (the fixed-name remains under the slug `bio-captures`). `freeName(slug, taken, validate)` answers the first `<slug>-<n>` (n from 2 to 99, within 40 characters) that is not taken and valid, else `null`. `listScripts`, `listBuckets`, `listNamespaces`, `listApps` answer lists (empty for a reply that is not one); `bindingsOf(api, name)` a script's bindings `{name, type, …}` or `null` when they cannot be read (unknown, never none). Test: a fake account with a finished copy, an unfinished one under its slug, one under the fixed names, and stray buckets: each in its place; a failing lookup throws; control: a secret's value is never read.
- **R4** (split from `installer` R63) `readCopyFacts(address)` makes one `op=bootstrap` request, no credential, to the copy's address and answers, for each of `team`, `installed`, `used`, `release`, `projects`, `documents`, `published`, `members`, `{known: true, value}` when the flat wire field (`team_name`, `installed_at`, `last_used_at`, `version`, `count_projects`, `count_documents`, `count_published`, `count_members`) is a non-empty string or a non-negative integer, else `{known: false, why}`, `why` `silent` when there was no usable answer (no address, a network failure, not ok, not an object) and `older` when it answered a version but not this field. `countsViaApi(api, copy, memberNames, scripts, spaces, apps)` answers `{workers, containers, captured, published}`: the copy's Workers present, the container applications on their Durable Object namespaces, and the objects in each of its two buckets, `null` for a count that cannot be read. Never throws on a copy that is silent or refuses. Test: a stub copy answering name, release and counts; control: a silent one answers every fact unknown with `silent`, and one that answers a version but no counts, `older`.
- **R5** (split from `installer` R65; DEC-282) `resourcesOf(api, copy, memberNames)` lists everything one copy holds as `{kind, name, id?}` in the order it is deleted: its container applications (those on its Workers' namespaces), its members' Workers, its plane, then its two buckets. `deleteResources(api, resources, done)` deletes in that order (a Worker with `?force=true`; a bucket emptied page by page, then deleted), calls `done(resource)` after each, and at the first refusal throws an Error carrying `remaining` (the names not yet deleted, the refused one first), `cause` and `status`; an `approveAgain` is rethrown unchanged. It deletes nothing that is not in the list. Test: a copy beside another: after the deletion the account holds none of the first's resources and all of the second's; a refused bucket names itself and what follows; control: `resourcesOf` alone deletes nothing.
- **R6** (split from `installer` R69; F9, K3747) One run at a time, and the mark. The lock is the throwaway Worker `bio-install-lock` whose plain-text bindings `T` (the time it was last touched) and `ID` (the run's id) are its only content. `takeLock(api, lock, now)` answers false when that Worker exists and was touched within `LOCK_LIVE_MS` (3 minutes) by a run other than the one `lock.again` names (a revoked permission cannot drop its lock, and the run that continues it is not stopped by it), and otherwise touches it and answers true. `touchLock(api, id, now)` rewrites it; `dropLock(api)` deletes it and never throws (a lock goes stale by itself). `MARK` is `INSTALL_COMPLETE`, the name of the secret binding an install writes last. Test: a lock touched a moment ago stops a second run; one older than the bound does not; the continuing run is not stopped; control: `dropLock` on a refused delete throws nothing.

**The probes**
- **R7** (split from `installer` R4, R6; DIST-3, DEC-42) `establishPlan(session, account)` finds the plan by provoking the platform, never by reading a plan field. It uploads the throwaway Worker `bio-plan-probe` (`PLAN_PROBE`) carrying `limits.cpu_ms`. Code 100328 answers `{plan: "free"}`; any other failure `{plan: "unknown", detail}` with the platform's message; an accepted upload is deleted at once (`?force=true`) and answers `{plan: "paid", leftover}`, `leftover` true when that deletion failed, which the caller says. Test: 100328 is free; a 500 is unknown with its message; an accepted upload answers paid and the probe is gone; a refused deletion answers paid with `leftover`. Control: no plan field of any reply is read.
- **R8** (split from `installer` R5, R7) `scriptExists(session, account, name)` answers true when the script's settings answer, false only on a 404, and throws on any other failure: a lookup that fails is not a lookup that found nothing. `ensureBuckets(session, account, names)` creates the captures bucket and then the published one; "already exists" (any case) counts as created, and it answers the names that were already there; any other failure throws. Test: an existing bucket is named in the answer; a refused creation throws with the platform's message; a 500 on a lookup throws, a 404 is false.
- **R9** (split from `installer` R14) `ensureSubdomain(session, account, slug)` reads the account's workers.dev prefix (a 404 is none). With none it registers `<slug>` and, only when that answer says the name is taken, exists or is unavailable, `<slug>-` and four lower-case alphanumerics; when both are taken it throws "no workers.dev prefix is set on this account and the names tried were taken", and any other error is thrown as it is. It then enables the script's address, and answers `{sub, registered}`, `registered` null when the account already had a prefix. Test: a prefix held is used; none is registered from the slug; the slug taken registers the suffixed name; both taken throws; control: a refused enabling throws.

**The plane's bytes, bindings and uploads**
- **R10** (split from `installer` R20, R15; DIST-15, N336) `planeLimits(source)` reads the plane's stated limits from the release's bytes: `source` is text or bytes (a verified download, never decoded to text, R14), with the same answer for both. The statement is exactly one distinct quoted string `bio-plane-limits/1 <key>=<positive integer> …` (one pair per key, keys sorted, single spaces), found as a whole quoted string, so a bundler's rewriting of numbers, names and quotes does not change it and `bio-member-limits/1` is never read as it. It answers `{ok: true, limits}` (frozen); `{ok: false, why: "none"}` when there is none; `{ok: false, why: "unreadable", detail}` for two different statements, a malformed one or unsorted keys. `LIMITS_NOT_STATED` holds the sentence for each `why`. `reportsBuilds(source)` is true when the text or bytes hold both `storeVersion` and `memberVersions` as whole words (a name running into word characters does not count), false for anything else; never throws. Test: text and bytes of the same module answer alike; none, two, malformed, unsorted; a bundler-quoted statement reads; `reportsBuilds` of a release that carries the fields and one from before them.
- **R11** (split from `installer` R9, R10, R21, R47, R30) The bindings the uploads share, each a pure function: `selfBinding(slug)` (the service `SELF` to the slug), `BROWSER_BINDING`, `instanceAiBinding(v)` (the secret `INSTANCE_AI_TOKEN` only for a string of 16–512 printable characters without spaces, `instanceAiOk(v)`; anything else `[]`; **never a generated value**), `sealBinding(v)` (the secret `ACCOUNT_SEAL_SECRET` for a non-empty string, else `[]`), `profilesBinding(ids)` (the plain text `JURISDICTION_PROFILES`, the ids comma-joined in order, for a non-empty list, else `[]`), `ownHostsBinding(hosts)` (the plain text `OWN_HOSTS`, comma-joined, for a non-empty list, else `[]`), `memberBindings(members, names)` (one service binding for each listed member the plane binds, named by `instance-setup`'s `FLEET_BINDINGS` (`BINDING_OF`) and targeting `names.member(member)`, in that table's order), `hostOf(base)` (the host of an address, else null). `membersPresent(session, account, names)` lists the bindable members whose Worker the account holds; a lookup that fails for any reason but "not found" counts as not present (binding a Worker whose existence is unestablished is refused by Cloudflare, code 10143), and an `approveAgain` is rethrown. `heldBindings(session, account, slug)` answers a Map from binding name to type, with `.list` the bindings, or `null` when the settings cannot be read or state no bindings (unknown, never none); an `approveAgain` or `lasting` error is rethrown. Test: each function over its good, absent and malformed inputs; a table of members that omits one binds it not; control: no generated value for `INSTANCE_AI_TOKEN`.
- **R12** (split from `installer` R10, R9; D-102, DIST-2, DIST-11) `uploadInstall(session, account, slug, secrets, release, {names, members, profiles, noSelf})` PUTs the plane to `/workers/scripts/<slug>` as a form whose `metadata` and `index.mjs` parts state: main module `index.mjs`; compatibility date `2026-07-01` with `nodejs_compat`; `limits` exactly `release.limits`, the installer holding no value of its own; the bindings the Durable Object `STORE` (class `Store`), the plain texts `VERSION` (the release's) and `INSTANCE_NAME` (the slug), the secrets `ADMIN_TOKEN` (`secrets.boot`), `PROBE_TOKEN` (`secrets.probe`) and `DAEMON_TOKEN` (`secrets.daemon`), `INSTANCE_AI_TOKEN` and `ACCOUNT_SEAL_SECRET` as R11 says, `JURISDICTION_PROFILES` when chosen, the r2 buckets `CAPTURES` and `PUBLISHED` of `names`, `SELF` unless `noSelf`, `BROWSER`, and the member services R11 lists for `members`; and the migration tag `v1` creating `Store` with the SQLite backend. The `index.mjs` part is the release's `blob` as it is. It writes none of R34's names. A refusal throws. Test: the upload's metadata equals this list exactly for a release stating `{subrequests: 10000}`; a member present is bound, one absent is not; `noSelf` omits `SELF` and nothing else; control: no value for `INSTANCE_AI_TOKEN` is invented.
- **R13** (split from `installer` R17, R36, R37; K1541, K1678) `uploadUpdate(session, account, slug, withR2, release, opts)` PUTs the same module in the update's shape: `limits` restated; `VERSION` and `INSTANCE_NAME`; `CAPTURES` and `PUBLISHED` only when `withR2`; `SELF` unless `opts.noSelf`; the services of `opts.members`; `BROWSER`; a fresh secret `DAEMON_TOKEN` (`opts.daemon`, else `opts.secrets.daemon`, else a new random value); `ADMIN_TOKEN`, `PROBE_TOKEN` and `ACCOUNT_SEAL_SECRET` only from `opts.secrets` (a resumed install states the credentials it has just generated, which replace the kept ones); `INSTANCE_AI_TOKEN` only when `opts.instanceAi` is valid; `ACCOUNT_SEAL_SECRET` from `opts.seal` only when it is given (a held one is never restated, a new value would orphan every sealed reference); `JURISDICTION_PROFILES` and `OWN_HOSTS` when given. `keep_bindings` is `secret_text`, `durable_object_namespace` and, unless `withR2`, `r2_bucket`; `service` is deliberately not kept, since the explicit service bindings are what heal an older copy and one dropped from them would be dropped from a working copy. No `migrations`. It writes none of R34's names. Test: the upload's metadata equals this list; with `withR2` false the buckets are kept, not restated; a held seal secret is not restated and none held gets one; control: `service` is absent from `keep_bindings`.
- **R14** (split from `installer` R33; K3441, K3442) The read-back. `scriptHash(session, account, slug)` answers the SHA-256 (hex) of the script as the account now holds it, taken over the bytes as downloaded: the whole body, or, for a multipart answer, the `index.mjs` part found as the chunks arrive and hashed incrementally (R38). The body is never turned into a string and back, which alters bytes 0x80–0x9F and costs several copies of a 21 MB module; `null` when nothing readable came back (a network failure, not ok, no boundary, no such part). `releaseSha(release)` answers `release.sha`, taken from `release.blob` once and kept on the release. `readBack(session, account, slug, release)` reads up to three times, 1.5 s apart, stopping at the first that equals the release's hash, and answers `null` when it does, otherwise the lag to name: that the script could not be read back, or that it is not the release's bytes, giving the first 16 hexadecimal digits of both hashes. Test: a module holding bytes 0x80–0x9F read back as itself and as a multipart part equals the release; a changed byte is named; a read that fails is named; a read that differs once and then matches is `null`; at the real size (21 MB) the same, shown in `installer` R33's test.
- **R15** (split from `installer` R67, R69; S7) `planeInSync(session, account, slug, release, held, {names, members, profiles})` answers true only when the account already holds the whole deployed state R12 would send, each binding with the kind it would send (`STORE`; `VERSION` equal to the release's; `INSTANCE_NAME`; the three credential secrets and the seal secret by name, a secret not being readable; `CAPTURES` and `PUBLISHED` on `names`' buckets; `SELF`; `BROWSER`; each member service on its target; `JURISDICTION_PROFILES` when chosen) and the script's bytes hashing to the release's; any one missing or different answers false, and identical bytes alone are never "already done". `putSecret(session, account, slug, name, text)` sets one secret without an upload; `rotateSecrets(session, account, slug, secrets, held)` restates `ADMIN_TOKEN`, `PROBE_TOKEN` and `DAEMON_TOKEN` and, only when `held` lacks `ACCOUNT_SEAL_SECRET`, that. Test: a held plane equal to R12's upload answers true; with the bytes the same and one binding dropped, false, for each binding in turn; a different version, false; control: `rotateSecrets` on a held seal does not restate it.

**The fleet**
- **R16** (split from `installer` R71, R61; F5) `fetchVerified(url, wantSha, what, onWait)` GETs one file of a release (redirects followed, the installer's user agent) and answers its bytes only when their SHA-256 is `wantSha`. A source that is slow or unavailable (5xx, 429, no answer) is waited on as R1 waits and then thrown as `SourceUnavailable` (`source: true`, naming `what`); any other answer that is not ok (404) is thrown as it is, the member's own refusal; bytes that do not hash to `wantSha` throw "`what` failed its integrity check" and are never returned. Test: 503 three times then 200 answers the bytes; 503 for the whole bound throws `SourceUnavailable`; a 404 throws as a 404; a changed byte throws the integrity error.
- **R17** (moved from `installer` R11; IC-82, D-297; R61) `loadFleet(release, onWait)` and `installFleet(emit, session, account, slug, release, loaded, ctx)`: members install only when all of these hold: the repository was reachable (the release has a manifest), the release names a fleet and a `fleetSig`, signers are armed (`ARMED_SIGNERS`, R33), the statement rebuilt from the manifest by `fleetStatement` verifies in the fleet namespace, and the statement's plane is the plane just installed (the release's hash and version equal the manifest's). Each failing condition makes `loadFleet` answer `{skip: sentence}` saying which, and no member is installed, nor is the install failed. Each member's bundle and parts are fetched (R16) and hashed against the statement. A part whose module type is not one of the four known (`CompiledWasm`, `Data`, `Text`, `ESModule`) and not a description part refuses that member. A `bio-plane` service is bound to the slug, and another member a service names to its Worker under the copy's names. A member the statement marks as a container (`agent-runner`) is installed as R20 says, and container members first, because a member bound to a container's class is refused while the class is not there; `sheet-worker` installs as any member and stays inactive until the release that activates it (K1506). Every member left out is named with why, and the install never fails over a member: `installFleet` throws only for an `approveAgain` or a `lasting` error, and answers `{done, left, imageLags, completed}`. Test: a signed fleet installs every member; each failing condition (no manifest, none named, no signer, a manifest whose statement cannot be rebuilt, a tampered statement, another release's plane) installs none and says which; an unknown part type leaves that member out alone; a failing member degrades alone and is named; control: a fleet statement with a member dropped does not verify.
- **R18** (new; N1240, K3782; R61, R71 of `installer`; amends R17) **The fleet is verified in full before anything is created, and held as hashes, not as bytes.** `loadFleet` fetches and verifies every member's bundle and parts (R16, R17), reads from the verified bytes what the later steps need (the member's stated limits, R21; its `Worker` description; its container classes, R20; its part list and the statement's hashes), and returns for each member those results and `bytes()`, and **no bundle and no part's bytes**. `bytes()` fetches the member's bundle and its parts that are not descriptions again (R16), checks them against the same hashes, and answers `{bundle, partBytes}`. `installFleet` calls it once per member, immediately before that member's first upload, and keeps nothing of it past the member, so at most one member's bytes are alive at a time. A second read that cannot be completed (the source down for the whole bound, an answer that is not ok) or that does not hash to the statement leaves that member out, named, with nothing of it uploaded, and `installFleet` throws nothing; the next update retries it. A first read that cannot be completed still ends the run with `SourceUnavailable` before anything is created. Why: at the release's real size the live memory of an install peaks at 120 MB of a Worker's 128 with the 11.2 MB fleet held, and 109 without it. Test: after `loadFleet` the result holds no `Uint8Array`, `ArrayBuffer` or `Blob` of more than 1 KB anywhere in its graph; the source is asked twice for each bundle and each non-description part and once for each description; the bytes uploaded are the second read's; control: a bundle that changes between the two reads leaves that member out (`failed its integrity check`) and uploads nothing of it while the others install; a source that fails at the second read leaves that member out, named; one that fails at the first still throws `SourceUnavailable` with nothing created.
