## Public

### Purpose

The obligation inbox: tasks routed from captures whose authority is undetermined, drained from capture's queue under the task grammar (C-19.1), listed to the members who may see their subjects, and forwarded or resolved by their assignee, anyone when unassigned, or an administrator.

### Provides

**The obligation inbox: taskDrain, taskList, taskForward, taskResolve** (`op=taskdrain`, `op=tasks`, `op=taskforward`, `op=taskresolve`)
- **R1** `taskDrain({limit, actor, now})` takes up to `limit` (1–500, default 50) queued capture events in order. An event whose capture is filed in no bundle is kept with its attempts counted (`waiting`). One with a live task (open or forwarded) of its kind on that bundle adds a `folded` entry to the task's history. Otherwise a task is created, routed to an active owner of the bundle when it is a project, else to an active owner of the first project (by id) with a live `cites` edge to it, else to the earliest active administrator (the first member of `membership`'s `activeAdmins` after the founder, its R86; `group-admin`), else `unassigned`; an ungrammatical task (C-19.1) is dropped and reported under `refused`; an exhausted id space keeps the event, its `waiting` entry carrying `record-core.mintExhausted("TASK")`'s `code`, `check` and `detail` (its R62; N322). Answers `drained`, `created` (with each route's basis), `folded`, `waiting`, `refused`, `remaining`, `limit`. A consumer registered with `scheduler` runs it while events wait. (T35; K1951, K1974) The drain asks capture's queue only for events of kind `authority-undetermined` (`taskEvents({limit, kind: "authority-undetermined"})`, capture R45's optional `kind`), and its `remaining` and the consumer's wait count only that kind (`taskEventCount({kind: "authority-undetermined"})`), so an event of any other kind, an `archive-unpack` among them, is never taken, routed as a task, folded, refused or counted here. (T36) The drain reads capture's queue in pages, oldest first: an event kept `waiting` (its capture filed in no bundle, or the id space exhausted) does not use up `limit`, and while fewer than `limit` events have been created, folded or refused and a page came back full (as many events as it asked for), it reads the next page, passing `after`, the `cursor` of the page's last event. So `limit` or more waiting events at the head never hide a filed event behind them. No event is read twice in one drain, and `waiting` still names every waiting event the drain read.
- **R18** (K2038; scheduler R15) The drain's wake is bounded. After a `task-drain` tick that drained nothing, the consumer's `wake` is the earliest instant any waiting event R1 reads is next due: an event tried `a` times (capture R45's `attempts`, `lastTry`) is due at `lastTry` + `TASK_DRAIN_BACKSTOP_MS` × 2^(a−1), and one tried `TASK_DRAIN_RETRY_LIMIT` times or more wants no wake; with none due, no wake, so an otherwise idle instance holds no timer. Such an event is kept (R1), retried on any drain that runs for another reason, and the drain is re-armed at R1's short delay when capture enqueues an event and when a promotion commits (`promotion.onCommitted`, its R45; only a promotion files a capture). A tick that drained something keeps the short delay. `TASK_DRAIN_BACKSTOP_MS` (60,000) and `TASK_DRAIN_RETRY_LIMIT` (8) are exported by name. (T36) The wake's reading of the waiting events (`#backoffWake`) pages with `after` as R1 does, while a page comes back full, so the earliest due instant is taken over every waiting event of R1's kind, not over the first page alone.
- **R2** `taskList({assignee, status, refersTo, limit, viewer})`: tasks on subjects the viewer may see, newest first, filtered as asked, at most `limit` (1–1,000, default 200), `truncated` measured one past the cap, and per-status counts over the visible set plus the queued-event count.
- **R3** `taskForward({id, to, actor, now})`: `NO_ACTOR`; `MACHINE_CANNOT_FORWARD` (C-32.10); `NO_SUCH_TASK`; `ALREADY_RESOLVED`; `TASK_NOT_YOURS` (C-76.1; its own code, apart from intent's C-111.15, N382) unless the actor is the assignee, the task is `unassigned`, or the actor is an administrator; `NO_SUCH_MEMBER` (an active member), answered through `membership`'s `noSuchMember` (its R121, C-96.47; K231: one code, one site) with `member` the `to` asked; `ALREADY_THEIRS`. Reassigns with role `member`, status `forwarded`, and appends `forwarded` with the actor to the history. `taskResolve({id, actor, now})`: `NO_ACTOR`; `MACHINE_CANNOT_RESOLVE` (C-32.11); `NO_SUCH_TASK`; an already resolved task answers `already: true` and writes nothing; `TASK_NOT_YOURS` as above; then status `resolved`, `resolved_at`, and `resolved` with the actor appended. With `items`, both run per item through `record-core.perItem` (its R50–R52). On a task of kind `check-requested` (R14), `taskForward` is refused `CHECK_NOT_FORWARDED`: a check request is addressed by expertise and sight, or to a member its requester names (R13), never reassigned (DEC-135 (2), (6)); `taskResolve` by an addressee who has not taken it closes that addressee's own To do only, the request staying open to the others, and the taker's To do closes only by R15's record.

**The task grammar** (a check registered with `promotion`, its R39, and with `record-core`, its R59)
- **R4** A non-replay promotion carrying `data/inbox.json` whose C-19.1 grammar finds an error is refused `INBOX_REFUSED` with `findings: [{check, detail}]`; a replay is exempt; a bundle without the file is not asked. The audit (record-core R59) runs the same grammar over each bundle's `data/inbox.json` and reports each error as C-19.1, as `checkBundle` did. R1's drain runs the same function over each candidate task. It is one function at all three, never a copy (promotion R38).

**Its share of the store's counts and the id ledger** (N342, K435; `record-core` R63, R40)
- **R5** At start this module registers with `record-core` (its R63) the figure of its table: `tasks` (a task naming a bundle in `hid` is left out, by `refers_to`). These are the keys and the subtraction `op=stats` and purge's proof answer today. It seeds `record-core`'s id ledger (its R40) with `["TASK", "tasks", "id"]` at start and again before its first mint; on a store whose ledger table is not yet created it learns nothing and never throws.

**Queue's reads** (N363; for `queue` R8, R19, R39)
- **R6** Three reads, each answering what `queue`'s feed reads today, so the feed does not change: `recentTasks({viewer, limit, statuses, assignees})` answers the tasks on subjects the viewer may see, of any status or, when `statuses` is given, only of those (N373, K566), and of any assignee or, when `assignees` is given (an array of member ids, `"unassigned"` among them as a value), only of those (N410; an empty `assignees` answers none), newest first (`created` descending, then `id`), at most `limit` (the cap taken over the tasks so filtered), each as `taskList` gives a task (R2); `resolvedTasks({viewer, since, limit})` answers the resolved tasks with `resolved_at` at or after `since` on subjects the viewer may see, `resolved_at` descending, then `id`, at most `limit`; `taskExists({id, viewer})` answers whether a task has that id on a subject the viewer may see (R2's gate; R9), so a task the viewer may not see answers as no task (N374, K565). Each never throws; a store without the table answers empty or false.

**Ask for a check** (N557; DEC-135, Bob's; K1745) (`op=checkrequest`, `op=checktake`, `op=checkrecord`, `op=checkrequests`, `op=checksof`)

Terms. A **check request** is R13's record; its **target** a bundle id (a finding, or a calculation, a passage or a determination it rests on); its **label** an expertise label normalised as `membership` R21 normalises it. A **check** is R15's record. An **addressee** is a member R13 addressed. `by` and `viewer` are the control plane's stamps (R10).

- **R13** `checkRequest({target, label?, member?, note?, by, viewer})` asks for a check, by a project owner (DEC-135 (1)). Refusals in order, each writing nothing: an empty or machine `by` `MACHINE_CANNOT_CHECK` (only a member asks, takes or checks); `target` not held or not in `by`'s sight (`membership` R80) `NO_SUCH_CHECK_TARGET` (one answer for both); `by` not an owner (`membership` R54) of the project the target belongs to (`record-core` R34's `project`; for a project, itself) `CHECK_NOT_AN_OWNER`; neither or both of `label` and `member` `CHECK_ADDRESS_ONE`; an empty `label` `EXPERTISE_NO_LABEL` (`membership` R106's answer, as it gives it); a `member` who is not `active` or whom `membership` R80 does not admit to the target `CHECK_MEMBER_REFUSED` (DEC-135 (6): the owner may ask a named member instead); `note` over 1,000 characters once trimmed `CHECK_NOTE_TOO_LONG` (empty reads as null). Otherwise it records `{request, target, label, member, note, by, at}` under an opaque id and addresses, at that instant, every member `membership.checkAddressees({target, label})` answers (its R106: declared or confirmed for `label`, and admitted to the target), or with `member` that one member; it answers `{ok, request, at, addressed}`, `addressed` the number of addressees, never their names. No one who cannot see the target is ever addressed (DEC-135 (2)).
- **R14** While a request is untaken, each addressee holds a To do for it: a task of kind `check-requested` on the target, assigned to that addressee (role `member`), `open`, which R2 and R6 answer to that addressee as they answer any task, and to no one R9 withholds it from (DEC-135 (2)). `checkTake({request, by})` takes the request: refused, writing nothing, `MACHINE_CANNOT_CHECK`; `NO_SUCH_CHECK_REQUEST` for a request absent or whose target `by` may not see (one answer); `CHECK_ALREADY_TAKEN`, naming the taker's handle and when, once another member has taken it; a second take by the taker answers `already: true` and writes nothing. Any `active` member who may see the target may take it, addressed or not: expertise gates nothing and the request only addresses (DEC-135 (5); `membership` R24). Exactly one take succeeds, however many arrive together. On success the taker's To do is theirs (open; created for a taker who held none), and every other addressee's To do for the request is resolved in the same act, its history naming the taker (`taken`, with the taker's handle and the instant), so `resolvedTasks` (R6) and `queue` R39 answer it as closed by the member who took it (DEC-135 (3)).
- **R15** `checkRecord({request, verdict, reason?, by})` records the check or a reasoned concern (DEC-135 (4)). Refusals in order, each writing nothing: `MACHINE_CANNOT_CHECK`; `NO_SUCH_CHECK_REQUEST` (as R14); `CHECK_NOT_YOURS` unless `by` took the request; `CHECK_VERDICT_UNKNOWN` unless `verdict` is `check` or `concern`; `CHECK_NO_REASON` for a `concern` whose `reason` is absent or blank once trimmed; `CHECK_REASON_TOO_LONG` for a `reason` over 4,000 characters; `CHECK_ALREADY_RECORDED`, answered with the first record (one record per request). Otherwise it appends `{check, request, target, checker, handle, label, expertise, verdict, reason, at}`: `handle` held by value; `expertise` the checker's current state for `label` as `membership` R24 answers it at that instant, `confirmed` (an administrator confirmed it) or `self-declared` (declared, not confirmed), or null when the checker has not declared it. It resolves the taker's To do. The check gates nothing: no capability, sight, grade or act depends on it or on its `expertise` (DEC-135 (5)).
- **R16** `checkRequests({viewer, after?, limit?})` answers the requests the viewer made, newest first, at most `limit` (1–500, default 200) with `truncated` and `next`: each `{request, target, label, member, note, at, addressed, taken, check}`, `taken` null while no one has taken it (so the requester sees that no one has, DEC-135 (6)) or `{handle, at}`, and `check` null or R15's record. A request whose target the viewer may no longer see is left out and not counted (R9). `checksOf({target, viewer})` answers every check recorded on `target` (R15's records, oldest first), so a check shows wherever its target shows (DEC-135 (4)); a target the viewer may not see answers `[]`, as one with none. Each writes nothing and never throws.
- **R17** Requests, takes and checks are append-only in this module's own tables, declared to the whole-store purge (R8; K23); nothing is overwritten or deleted. A machine never requests, takes or records a check (Design Requirement 12). Each refusal R3, R13–R15 add carries its row in this module's own check family (`checks.mjs`). No place is named in their behaviour or outward text.
