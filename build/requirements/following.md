## Public

### Purpose

The group's following of what happens at a body and in a register, on the scheduler's one alarm: a body a member follows is read from its Legistar records so that `events` can write what it did; a document watched per meeting is captured before each meeting by the body's notice period; a public register is re-read or re-rendered on the tick, and one behind a member's account only at that member's act; a member may follow a register query naming a person; a portal's dataset is snapshotted with its changes keyed field by field. Like `monitoring`, it watches and captures; what a change means is never its to say.

### Provides

Terms. A **follow** is a member's standing act naming what is followed and for which period. A **tick** is one scheduled read of one followed subject. A **subject** is `{kind, id}`, `kind` one of `body`, `meeting`, `register`, `person-query`, `portal`. (T35) `policy` is a further kind, a held policy's published copy (R20). The **host** is `monitoring.sweepHost()`'s services (its R65: pause, claim, land, gate). Every refusal names `reason`; one with a catalogue row carries its `check`, `code` and `translation`. (T45; v0.2 rule 17; K2781 (2)) A **watch** is one of four things a plan may set running: a **follow** (R1, R7, R9, R10, R20), a watched **address** (`monitoring` R15), a **duty** adopted from a promise (`duties` R2), or a member's **standing question** (`answers` R15, R28). A **link** records that a plan set a watch running (R23).

#### Following a body through Legistar (§5B.4; K1443, K1468)

- **R1** `followBody({body, from, until, author, viewer})` records a member's follow of a body for a period. Refusals, in order, each writing nothing: a machine author `MACHINE_CANNOT_FOLLOW`; a body absent or unseen by the viewer `NO_SUCH_BODY` (alike); a body without a Legistar body identifier (an `entities` identifier whose scheme the profile names for Legistar) `NO_LEGISTAR_ID`; `from` absent or after `until` `BAD_PERIOD`. `unfollow({follow, author})` ends it at its author's act. Only a body and period a member follows is read (K1468).
- **R2** A followed body's tick reads its Legistar events, event items, votes and matters for the followed period, each read a capture through `acquisition`, attributed to the follow as its authority, and hands each captured read to `legistar-reader` and to `events.followedImport({captureSha, body, period, by})` (the sibling `events` draft), which write the machine-attributed events, participants and votes and the agenda and minutes posting times (K1443). A read already captured with the same bytes lands nothing new.
- **R3** (§6.4; Choices 17) A matter's enactment record (`MatterEnactmentNumber`, `MatterEnactmentDate`, `MatterStatusName`) whose bytes differ from its last capture is captured as a new version of its Legistar address, so a version notice follows from that capture through the instrument keys (`reevaluation`, X73); this module raises no notice itself and never reads a rendered codifier page.

#### Per meeting (§4.4 TIME L3; K1444, K1445)

- **R4** For each monitored address whose governing frequency is `per_meeting` (`monitoring` R14) and whose watch names its body, one capture is due at each of the body's meetings, at the meeting's start minus the body's notice period. The meetings are the profile's recurrences for that body (expanded by `civil-time`) and its observed meeting events (`events`), an observed meeting governing over the recurrence instance it matches; a meeting whose status is cancelled earns no capture. Times are in the meeting's zone.
- **R5** A `per_meeting` watch with no body named, a body with no recurrence and no observed meeting in the next 24 months, or a notice period the profile does not hold with a primary source (K1445), is `unscheduled` with its reason, stated on R13's answer, never given a guessed time.
- **R6** Each `per_meeting` capture states the alarm's lateness: the instant it was taken less the instant it was due, so a capture taken after the meeting began says so.

#### Following a register (§7.4; K1449)

- **R7** `followRegister({address, render, author, viewer})` records a member switching a watch on for a public register (R-2 C-E1). On the tick, a static register is re-read; one that needs rendering or session cookies is re-rendered through `capture`'s render path when `render` is set; otherwise only its static form is followed and every answer about it says so. Refusals as R1's, with `NO_LOCATOR` for an address that is not a public https locator.
- **R8** (K1449) A register behind an account, or fee-bearing, is never read on the tick: it is refreshed only by `refreshRegister({follow, author})`, the act of the member whose own credential it uses, with the price shown first when fee-bearing, and what it brings is marked not reproducible by the public. A tick that reaches such a register records `member_act_required` and fetches nothing.

#### A register query for a person (K1484, C2 row 11)

- **R9** `followPersonQuery({register, scheme, value, person, author, viewer})` follows a register's own query for one identifier a member names (a filer id, a licence number), the `scheme` one the profile lists for that register. A query by name alone, a query across registers, or a scheme the profile does not list for that register is refused `PERSON_QUERY_NOT_NAMED`, writing nothing. Each tick reads only that query's answer; it follows no link out of it and never searches for the person elsewhere. A fee-bearing or account-gated register falls under R8.

#### Portal snapshots (§8.5)

- **R10** `followPortal({address, query, key, author, viewer})` follows a portal's dataset, keyed to its query (the address with its query parameters, normalised), with a declared key field. Each tick captures the dataset's answer as a snapshot, a capture like any other, and records it as a vintage whose validity is the capture instant (`civil-time` `validAt`).
- **R11** `snapshotDiff({follow, from, to})` answers, between two snapshots of one portal follow, the rows `added` and `removed` by key and, for a key in both, each `changed` field `{key, field, before, after}`. A key missing or repeated in either snapshot makes that row `undetermined` with its reason, never matched by position. It writes nothing; a difference is never a finding.

#### For `scheduler`: followDue(now), followWake(now), followTick(now, rank?), onFollowed(module, fn)

- **R12** Due while any follow (R1, R7, R9, R10) is due by its cadence or any `per_meeting` capture (R4) is due; wake is the earliest instant one falls due, or null when none is. A body, register, person query or portal follow is due daily from its last read unless the follow names a longer cadence.
- **R13** A tick reads at most 50 due subjects, oldest due first (or in the rank's order when `scheduler` passes one, as `monitoring` R19), each claimed under the host's epoch so a retry never reads a subject twice (`monitoring` R21), and answers `{configured, at, epoch, read, captured, unscheduled, member_act_required, failed, paused}`. When the host is paused (`monitoring` R30) it reads nothing and says so.
- **R19** (K1666) `onFollowed(module, fn)`: one registration per module; after a follow is recorded, ended, or its next due instant changes (R1, R4, R7, R9, R10), `fn({follow, due})` is called once, after the act's transaction, with that follow's id and its next due instant (null when none), so `scheduler` re-arms its wake. A throwing `fn` never undoes the act.
- **R14** `follows({viewer})` answers every follow the viewer may see with its subject, author, period, cadence, last read, next due, and its `unscheduled` reason if any, so a follow that is not being read is visible without waiting for a tick. (T45; N855; Actions D6d; v0.2 rule 17) Each follow also answers `ends` (R22) or `ends: null` with `ends_state: "none_set"`, and, when ended, `ended_reason` (`author`, `ends`, `answer` (R25) or R20's).

#### Watching the policies a group holds (§6B.4 L5; K1727)

- **R20** (K1727, K1740) Every policy the group holds is watched with no member act: for each standard `standards` holds as a policy (its `family` a policy family, T35-31) whose text is held from a capture of a public https address, this module holds one watch of that address (subject kind `policy`), made when the text is first held and ended when the policy is superseded or withdrawn. A watch is due every 7 days from its last read (R12), read on the tick (R13) under the host as any follow, and each read is a capture through `acquisition` attributed to the watch. A read whose bytes equal the last capture of that address lands nothing new; one whose bytes differ is kept as a new capture of the address, beside every earlier one and never replacing it, so every version seen is kept, and is handed to `standards` as a version of the policy from captures (T35-31), whose text it never edits. Each capture takes the sight of the policy it watches, so a policy kept from the public keeps its watch's captures there too (K1740). An address behind an account or a fee is never read on the tick (R8, R15): the watch records `member_act_required`. An address that does not answer is recorded `failed` with its reason and stays watched; R14 lists each watch with its last read and next due, its author stated as none (the group's standing watch, K1727), and R19's listeners are told when a watch is made, ended or its next due changes, as for any follow.
- **R21** (K1727; DEC-145 (5); T36, N741, K2038) `policyChanges({after?, since?, limit?, viewer})` answers `{changes: [{watch, standard, address, before: {capture, at}, after: {capture, at}, amendment_held}], cursor}`: one entry per kept capture of R20 whose bytes differ from the capture before it, in order of the later capture's instant after `after`, at most `limit` (default 200, clamped to 1–200), `cursor` the last answered when more follow, else null. With `since` (an instant), only changes whose later capture's instant is at or after `since` are answered, the order, `after`, `limit` and `cursor` unchanged, so a reader reads a window from its start rather than from the oldest change kept; a `since` that is not an instant answers no change and says so (`since_invalid: true`, as `bias` R44's). `amendment_held` is `true` when `standards` holds an amendment or a superseding version of that policy with an effective date between the two captures, else `false`. A change in a policy the viewer may not see is left out whole. It writes nothing; a change is never a finding and never says what changed means (R16). It is the read `notice-producers` (T35-67) shows as a "Noticed" item for a member to review, in DEC-145 (5)'s words.

#### Watches, their end dates, and the once-only question (T45; N855 (6d), N881; Actions D6d, D14; v0.2 rules 1, 17, 18; K2781 (2))

- **R22** (T45; N855; Actions D6d; v0.2 rule 17) **A follow's end date.** `followBody`, `followRegister`, `followPersonQuery` and `followPortal` (R1, R7, R9, R10) also take `ends`, a calendar day after today's local day (instance zone), or none. It is not the read period of R1 (`until`, the days of a body's records that are read). Refused `WATCH_END_REFUSED` when given and not such a day, before any write. `followEnd({follow, ends, by, viewer})` (`op=followend`) changes it later, by the follow's author or by R25; `ends: null` removes it. After its end day a follow is ended as `unfollow` ends it, by itself, recorded once with `ended_by: null` and `ended_reason: "ends"`; it is then never due (R12) and R19's listeners are told. A `policy` watch (R20) takes no `ends`: it is the group's standing watch (K1727) and ends only as R20 says.
  - Tests: a follow with `ends` yesterday is ended at the next tick and read no more (control: `ends` tomorrow is read); R19 told once at that end; `until` and `ends` independent (a body's past period with no `ends` is still due); a policy watch refuses `ends`.
- **R23** (T45; N855; Actions D6d; v0.2 rules 1, 17) **Linking a watch to the plan that set it running.** `watchLink({plan, matters?, watch: {kind, id}, author, viewer})` (`op=watchlink`) records, by a member's act, that the plan set the watch running: `kind` is `follow`, `address`, `duty` or `question`; `matters`, when given, names matters of that plan the watch serves. Refusals, in order, each writing nothing: an empty or machine author `MACHINE_CANNOT_LINK_WATCH`; a plan absent or not visible, `action-plans`' `NO_SUCH_PLAN` (one answer); a closed plan `PLAN_CLOSED`; a `kind` outside the four, or a watch absent or not visible to the author (a follow, R14; an address, `monitoring` R32; a duty, `duties`' read; a standing question, which only its author sees, `answers` R16), `NO_SUCH_WATCH` (one answer); a matter not of that plan `MATTER_NOT_OF_PLAN`; an ended watch `WATCH_ENDED`. A second link of the same watch to the same plan answers `existed: true` with the first. Links are append-only and keep who and when. A link to a standing question is seen only by that question's author, as the question is (`answers` R16), in every read here.
  - Tests: each refusal with a control; a second member cannot link another member's standing question (`NO_SUCH_WATCH`, the same answer as an absent one); that link is absent from every read for another member (control: its author sees it).
- **R24** (T45; N855; Actions D6d; v0.2 rule 17; DESIGN §10 "every promise met") **The once-only question.** `watchQuestions({after?, limit?, viewer})` answers one entry per link whose question is due and not yet answered (R25), in (due instant, link) order after `after`, at most `limit` (1–200, default 200), with `cursor`:
  - **Due** at the first of: (a) the plan closed (`action-plans`' plan read, the closing instant); (b) for the link's matters (all the plan's matters when it names none), each matter holding at least one duty adopted from its responses' promises, every such duty reading met, at the instant the last one did: read through `plan-work.matterPromiseDuties` (its R13, `all_met`; the plan's reconciliation R18), as this module reads it with no member's sight, so whether a question is due never depends on who asks; a matter it answers with `partial: true` is not met. The cause is stated, `closed` or `promises_met`. A link whose watch has already ended is never due.
  - **Each entry** is `{link, plan, matters, watch: {kind, id, ends}, due_at, cause, relied_on, offered, recipients}`. `ends` is the watch's present end date (R22; `monitoring` R71; R25's for a duty; `answers`' `ends` for a question).
  - **Relied on** is true when another link of the same watch names a plan that is not closed; for an address, also when `monitoring` R72 names a project other than the plan's; and for a `policy` follow, always. A relied-on watch is listed with `offered: []`; otherwise `offered: ["keep", "stop"]`. `relied_on` names no other plan or project.
  - **Recipients**: for a standing question, its author alone. Otherwise the link's author, while she is an active member (`membership.memberFacts`) who may see the plan; else the plan's project's active owners (`membership` R65); else the administrators (`membership` R86).
  - An entry the viewer may not see (the plan, or a standing question not hers) is left out whole and counted nowhere (R17). It writes nothing; asking once is R25's record and `action-producers` R10's item.

  - Tests: a closed plan's link listed once with `cause: closed`; a link listed on `promises_met` when the second of two adopted duties reads met (control: one met, one pending, not listed); a link whose other plan is open is `relied_on` with `offered: []` (control: that plan closed, both offered); an address another project's document asks for is relied on, naming no project; a standing question's entry seen by its author only.
- **R25** (T45; N855; Actions D6d; v0.2 rules 1, 17) **The answer.** `watchAnswer({link, keep?: {until}, stop?: true, author, viewer})` (`op=watchanswer`) records the one answer to R24's question, by one of its recipients: `keep` with `until`, a calendar day after today's local day, or `stop`. Refusals, in order, each writing nothing: an empty or machine author `MACHINE_CANNOT_LINK_WATCH`; no such link visible to the author `NO_SUCH_WATCH`; no due question for that link, or an author who is not one of R24's recipients for it, `WATCH_NOT_ASKED` (one answer); a relied-on watch `WATCH_RELIED_ON`; neither or both of `keep` and `stop`, or `until` not such a day, `WATCH_END_REFUSED`. An answered link answers `existed: true` with the first answer. The answer sets the watch's end in its owner's terms, in the same act:
  - a follow: `keep` sets R22's `ends`; `stop` ends it now, `ended_reason: "answer"`, by this author (whether or not she made the follow);
  - an address: `monitoring.addressWatchEnd` (its R71) with `until`, or today;
  - a duty: the end day is held on the link here. The duty itself stays held and its occurrences are still derived and readable (`duties` R10); only telling of it stops after that day (`notice-producers` R5). `dutyWatchEnd(duty)` (in process, for `notice-producers` R5) answers the day after which the duty is no longer told: null while the duty has no link, or any link of it is unanswered; otherwise the latest end day its answered links hold (a `keep`'s `until`, or a `stop`'s day). It writes nothing and never throws;
  - a standing question: `keep` sets its `ends` through `answers.standingQuestionEndSet` (its R34, the author's own act); `stop` is `answers.standingQuestionEnd` (its R16).

  A refusal by the owner's act undoes nothing here and is answered as given. Once answered, R24 never lists the link again.
  - Tests: each kind kept and stopped, its end shown afterwards by its owner's read; a second answer `existed: true`; a non-recipient refused `WATCH_NOT_ASKED`, alike with an unasked link (control: a recipient lands); a relied-on watch refused `WATCH_RELIED_ON`; a duty stopped is still read as held with its occurrences, and `dutyWatchEnd` answers that day (control: a duty with one answered and one unanswered link answers null).
- **R26** (T45; N881; Actions D14; v0.2 rule 18; the plan's reconciliation R7, R13) **For the plan's "where it stands" view.** This module registers once at start, through `plan-work`'s watch-reader door (`registerWatchReader`, its R9; the only module that registers there, its links covering addresses), the read `watchesOfPlan({plan, viewer})`: each watch linked to that plan the viewer may see, with its kind, its owner's words for it (a follow's subject, an address, a duty's performance, a standing question's words to its author only), its end date or `none_set`, whether it has ended, and `relied_on` (R24's rule). It writes nothing and never throws; `plan-work` reads it (its R7, the closing summary included) and imports nothing of this module.
  - Tests: the registered read answers a plan's three linked watches with end dates (control: a watch linked to another plan is absent); a second registration is refused by the door.

**When the promises were met, from one source** (T46)
- **R27** (T46; N921; amends R24 (b); `plan-work` R15) R24's `promises_met` is read from `plan-work.matterPromiseDuties` alone (its R13, R15), as this module reads it with no member's sight: a link's question is due on `promises_met` when, for each of the link's matters (all the plan's matters when it names none), the read answers `all_met` and not `partial`, and its `due_at` is the latest of those matters' `met_at`. This module no longer derives that instant from `duties.occurrencesOf` or an occurrence's evidence; a matter whose read answers no `met_at` makes the question not due by this cause. R24's other terms (the `closed` cause, the first of the two, an ended watch never due) are unchanged. Control: a matter whose two duties were read met on different days makes the question due at the later instant; with one of them unmet, the link is not listed on `promises_met`.

### Suggestions

- **Open for BOB.**
  1. **Uses** beyond Rule 3 (above).
  2. **`per_meeting` naming its body.** `monitoring`'s watch (the gathering grammar or the document's frequency) has no body field today; R4 needs one. Either the gathering grammar gains `body` (monitoring's job, a grammar change) or this module holds the link per watched address (`perMeetingBody({address, body, author})`). The draft assumes the second keeps monitoring untouched (P6).
  3. **Who owns `per_meeting`'s captures.** R4 takes them off `monitoring`'s plan; `monitoring` R16 keeps listing them `unscheduled` (no interval). A one-line `monitoring` rewording ("scheduled by `following`") avoids two answers.
  4. **`events`' Legistar write.** R2 uses `followedImport` as the sibling `events` draft names it (T33-26); its `by` is the follow.
  5. **Cadence words.** R12's daily default and "a longer cadence" follow `monitoring` R14's list; BOB may prefer one shared word list.
  6. **The court register path**: a court register is a register under R7–R8; a row becoming a `filing`/`order` event is `events`' (T33-26).
  7. Refusal codes are this draft's.
- **T35-65 (open technical details, BOB's).** R20's 7-day cadence is BOB's (a protective choice under K1881: a policy page changes seldom and the fleet's fetch volume stays low); a member may not shorten it in T35. The watch may be a row of the follows table with subject kind `policy` and the `per_meeting` columns null. Which standards are policies is `standards`' family read (T35-31); if its name differs, the job follows it.
- **The host.** Running every tick under `monitoring.sweepHost()` (claim, land, pause, gate) is `link-sweep`'s precedent (N506) and gives one pause and one idempotence key.
- **Tests against captured JSON.** Fixtures of Legistar `Events`, `EventItems`, `Votes`, `Matters` for a fictional body under the test profile; a zone-less `EventDate` normalised from site data; a `- CANCELLED` meeting earning no `per_meeting` capture.
