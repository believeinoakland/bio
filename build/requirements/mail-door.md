**Status** · New module for T53 (layer 3, directly after `doorbell`; K5530 G: BOB's packaging of K5504–K5506's capability, K2008), written by BOB #325 from CAPABILITIES' READY draft `build/plan/beta/capabilities/N824-email-capture.md` §4.1 (draft ids R1–R14, kept) with `N824-container-member-job.md` (the layer arrangement, §C.2), as K5527 (1), (2), K5530 and K5534 (C31) amend it: no member's own email address is stored; a forwarded message is kept whole, headers included, as its source; unconfirmed mail is removed after 14 days and the member told (by a `notice-producers` line reading the counts `clearWaitingMail` answers); a published copy shows the visible headers without addresses (R7); the nesting refusal is `mail-reader` ML10's, relayed. Every requirement is not yet met (T53). Not yet approved by Bob. `modules.json` and `layers.md` gain the module (BOB's). The plane's `email()` export waits on the measurement M1 (the probe on account `20b5…7f72`; R12).

**Size (P6).** New. Expected well under 4,000 lines: a waiting list, a rate account, a tally and their reads.

## Public

### Purpose

A member's private address brings mail into her private waiting list; she reads it and brings it into the record with her statement. Anyone else's mail to the team's domain is refused. It is the mail twin of the doorbell and the upload, and files nothing itself: `capture` files. Nothing here is AI, and the product sends no mail.

### Provides

Terms. A **private address** is a member's own forwarding address under the team's mail domain; a **waiting row** is a received message held privately until its owner brings it into the record or it is removed. `by` and `viewer` are the control plane's stamps, never a body's. Every refusal names its `reason` and `code` and carries its row's `check` and `translation`.

- **R1** (T53; N824; K2425 (5), K2434; B2) **A member's private address.** `mailAddressOf({member, viewer})`, `mailAddressMake({by, within})`, `mailAddressOff({by, within})`. A signed-in member with `contribute` (member-session fence as `capture` R86: `MEMBER_SESSION_REQUIRED` for a machine identity or no stamp; `contribute` is `member-roster` R1's, checked at the door by the op's class as `captureupload` is, `control-plane` R72) has at most one live address: a local part `m-` followed by 12 characters of at least 60 bits drawn from a cryptographic source, joined to the team's mail domain (set by instance-setup R114, read through `deps.settings()`; distinct from the website domain claim of `groupDomainSet`, instance-setup R7-R9). `mailAddressMake` makes a new one and turns the old one off in the same act; `mailAddressOff` turns it off. Only the member reads her own address; no administrator, no other member, no read for the record's sake reads it, and it is never written to a log, a receipt, a document or a published copy. The address's digest, not the address, is what the door matches. With no mail domain set, `mailAddressMake` is refused `MAIL_NOT_SET_UP`. Test: two members get two addresses; a new address turns the old off; a second member's read of the first's address answers nothing; a machine identity is refused `MEMBER_SESSION_REQUIRED`; no table row, answer or log line holds the clear address, and none holds any member's own email address (K5505, K5527 (1): the product never asks for, stores as a field or uses one; only the digest of the private address is kept). Control: a member with no `contribute` is refused as the `captureupload` door refuses her.
- **R2** (T53; N824; K2425 (5); B2, B5) **`receiveMail({raw, envelope_to, envelope_from, size, now})` takes mail at the door.** Refusals in order, each refused during the mail conversation and writing nothing but R9's tally: the door off (`MAIL_OFF`); `envelope_to` not the digest of a live member address (`MAIL_NO_SUCH_ADDRESS`, one answer for an unknown address and a switched-off one); the member's hourly or the team's daily bound reached (R8); a declared `size` over the mail bound (`MAIL_TOO_LARGE`, with size and bound, and judged before a byte is read); a read stream that passes the bound (stream cancelled); zero bytes (`MAIL_EMPTY`). Otherwise one waiting row (R3) is written and the message is accepted; `envelope_from` is read for nothing and kept nowhere, so no member's own address is stored. No reply, bounce text or notification is sent by the product. Test: a message to a live address is accepted and waits; to an unknown local part, to a switched-off address and to the right address on a closed door are each refused with the stated code and nothing written; a declared size of bound plus 1 is refused before reading; the stream cancel is asserted by a counting source. A member's own address as `envelope_from` is held by no row, log, tally key or answer, and changes no decision. Control: a message exactly at the bound is accepted.
- **R3** (T53; N824; B3, B5; `doorbell` R3's pattern) **The waiting list.** An accepted message writes one row `MAIL-<date>-<8 hex>`: owner (the member whose private address it was sent to: the address, not any header, says who forwarded it, K5505), the raw bytes' SHA-256, size, received instant, status `waiting`, `days_left`, and, from `mail-reader`, the headers as stated (R4); the raw bytes under a private evidence key (`bio/mail/<sha256>`), outside the record (K5506): no bundle, no receipt, no document, no grade, seen by its owner alone, until she brings it in with her statement (R5) or R6 deletes it. `mailWaiting({viewer, limit, after})` lists the viewer's own rows only, newest first, at most 200 per page with `next`; `mailRead({id, viewer})` answers one row's headers, text and attachment list (name and size as stated, never opened) to its owner only, `NO_SUCH_MAIL` for anyone else (one answer for absent and not hers). Test: a member lists only her own mail; another member's id answers `NO_SUCH_MAIL`; the bytes are under the private key and not in the evidence of any bundle. Control: after bring-in (R5) the row reads `brought_in` and the list still shows it, with its capture.
- **R4** (T53; N824; B7) **Headers are the message's claims.** `from`, `to`, `cc`, `date`, `subject`, `message_id`, `received` and `authentication_results` are recorded and shown as the message states them, each labelled "as the message states it" by key; the sender's name or address is never recorded as the capturing member and never as the source in the sense of `sources` R12; no check is made against any directory; a header that cannot be read is `null` with the reason, never repaired. Nothing here is graded by a machine. Test: a message whose From names a member shows that name as stated and records the receiving member as the actor; a malformed Date answers `null` with its reason. Control: a well-formed message answers every field.
- **R5** (T53; N824; K2425 (4), K2434; B3) **`mailBringIn({id, statement, by, within})` files the message.** `by` must be the row's owner (else `NO_SUCH_MAIL`). Refusals in order, each writing nothing: the member-session fence; `NO_SUCH_MAIL`; `MAIL_ALREADY_IN` (answered with the capture, `existed: true` and no second receipt); `statement` absent, not a string, blank or over 2,000 characters, `MAIL_NO_STATEMENT`. Otherwise in one act inside `within`: it calls `capture` R116 with the raw bytes (the whole message as forwarded, headers included, kept as its source, K5527 (1)), the statement, `by` and the headers, then the row reads `brought_in` with the capture's sha. The answer is `capture`'s answer. The row's bytes stay until the capture's job rows are registered (`capture` R109's registrar), then are deleted. Test: a bring-in files one capture whose digest equals the raw bytes' (headers included) and whose receipt carries the statement; a blank statement is refused `MAIL_NO_STATEMENT` and the row still waits; a second bring-in answers `MAIL_ALREADY_IN`. Control: the bytes are gone from the private key after a successful bring-in and still there after a refused one.
- **R6** (T53; N824; B5; C9 (3); K5527 (2)) **Unconfirmed mail is removed after 14 days, and the member is told.** `clearWaitingMail({now, limit})`, with `mailClearDue(now)` and `mailClearWake(now)`, deletes each `waiting` row older than `MAIL_WAITING_DAYS` (14, final; runtime-limits R37) with its bytes, at most `limit` per call (default 200, clamped 1 to 1000), oldest first, answering `{ok, cleared, truncated}`. It is run by the `scheduler` consumer `mail-clear` (`scheduler` R35; `scheduler` uses `mail-door`, which the layers allow, section 4.1), never at read time; `days_left` is counted from the received instant at the instance clock. The doorbell's own discard clearing is `doorbell` R29, a separate consumer. A member may delete her own waiting message at once (`mailDelete`, with no reason, no record). `clearWaitingMail` answers, beside the counts, `owners`: for each owner whose mail it removed, the count and the received dates, never a sender, subject or text, and calls no notice module (it cannot: `notice-producers` is layer 11); a notice-producers line reads those counts and tells each owner once, privately, 14 days on (K5527 (2), final; notice kind `mail_cleared`, a proposed name). Removal writes no record and no receipt. Test: a row received 14 days and 1 minute before the instance clock is gone after the tick, with its bytes; one received 13 days ago stays; `mailDelete` removes the row and bytes; the answer names the owner of each removed row with its count and received dates and no header, an owner with nothing removed is not named; `limit: 1` over three due rows clears one and states `truncated`. Control: a brought-in message is never deleted by this clearing.
- **R7** (T53; N824; K1485, DEC-408; B4; reworded K5692) **No mail address of a person is published, and none is bulk-copied.** The published copy of a captured message is built elsewhere (`doc-clean` and `case-disclosures`, T53-275: From, To, Cc, Date and Subject shown, never a technical header nor an email address, a name only where marked material, K5534); mail-door's part is that it gives them the raw message whole (K5527 (1)) and holds and answers no address of a person but its own rows' owner: no row, answer or log line of this module carries R1's private address or a sender's or recipient's address outside the stored message bytes, and the product never extracts addresses from mail into a contact list, a people entry or an entity. Test: after a message with addresses in From, To, Cc and its Received headers is received and read, no answer of `mailWaiting` or `mailRead` but the owner's own view of the message, and no table row but the stored bytes, holds those addresses or R1's private address. Control: the member's own view shows all headers.
- **R8** (T53; N824; K1485; B5) **Rate bounds, stated in words.** At most `MAIL_PER_MEMBER_HOUR` (proposed 20) messages to one member address in any hour and `MAIL_PER_TEAM_DAY` (proposed 200) to the team in any day, counted by a two-bucket sliding window as `doorbell` R2 does and estimated the same way, over keys that never hold an address in clear (`doorbell` R12's keyed digest). A refusal answers the sentence of its key with the figures only in `stated`, as `doorbell` R9 does. The figures are read from `runtime-limits` (R37), never written here. Test: the 21st message in an hour to one address is refused, the 20th accepted; a second member's address is counted apart; at the team bound every address is refused. Control: after the window passes, acceptance resumes.
- **R9** (T53; N824; `doorbell` R19) **A count-only tally of mail turned away.** For each UTC day, two counters (refused for address, refused for size or rate) and one stored date, 30 days kept; no sender, no address, no digest, no message. It is read by a signed-in administrator. Test: three refusals of two kinds are counted; nothing else is stored. Control: an accepted message changes no counter.
- **R10** (T53; N824; B7) **Loops, lists and automatic mail.** A message that states it is automatic (`Auto-Submitted` other than `no`, `Precedence: bulk` or `junk`, a `List-Id`, an empty return path) is accepted like any other and flagged `automatic: true` in its row, the four facts read from `mail-reader`'s answer (`auto_submitted`, `precedence`, `list_id`, `return_path`; section 3; owed by FILES' addition A2), never refused, never dropped and never acted on; the product sends no mail and so cannot create or join a loop. A message that already has a waiting row or a capture under the same raw digest is accepted as a second sighting: one row, `existed` stated (R5's idempotence). A message `mail-reader` refuses for nesting deeper than 3 levels (its ML10, the one count shared with archives, the refusal carrying the depth reached) is refused by relaying that refusal unchanged; this module mints no `MAIL_TOO_NESTED` and reads no nesting figure of its own. Test: a bulk-flagged message is listed with the flag; the same bytes sent twice leave one row; a message nested 4 deep is refused with `mail-reader`'s refusal naming the depth, unchanged. Control: a plain message has `automatic: false`.
- **R11** (T53; N824; B7) **Never AI.** No module, prompt or assistant is called in receiving, listing, filtering, deleting or filing a message; the code path imports none (`ai-runs`, `agent-*`); a message's text is never sent to any provider by this module. Reading after bring-in is the pipeline's, under the AI rules that already bind a capture. Test: a static check finds no import of an AI module in this module's files; a fake provider records zero calls across all of R2-R10. Control: the reading of a brought-in message runs as any capture's.
- **R12** (T53; N824) **The door is off by default and is declared.** With no mail domain set (instance-setup R114) or the switch off, `receiveMail` refuses `MAIL_OFF` and `mailAddressMake` refuses `MAIL_NOT_SET_UP`; the member acts (`mailaddress`, `mailwaiting`, `mailread`, `mailbringin`, `maildelete`) are declared by the module's own exported `DECLARED_OPS` as `doorbell` R26 does (`op-declarations` R80-R81, `op-grades` R49-R50, `test-support` R15's `declaredOpHolds`), and routed by `control-plane`, which gains `mail-door` in its `uses`; the mail entry is the plane's `email()` export (the measurement M1, `N824-container-member-job.md` section D, decides it can be carried), calling `receiveMail`, and is not an op and not a public route. Test: a fresh copy refuses all and lists nothing. Control: with the switch on and a domain set, R2 accepts; with the door off or no domain, uploading a saved `.eml` or `.msg` (`captureupload`) still works (K5504). *(not yet met: T53)*
- **R13** (T53; N824; C9 (4)) **Every refusal has its catalogue row and its words by key.** Each code above has a C-number row and its translation read by key through `setup-words` (the translation names no figure; `doorbell` R9). One code is defined in one module's table (`doorbell` R21): **`MAIL_NO_STATEMENT` is `capture`'s row, the new check C-118.12** (`capture/checks.mjs:21`: "C-118's next free number stays this module's to mint: C-118.12"; its `where` is `emailCapture`'s statement test, as C-118.10's is `uploadCapture`'s), and mail-door relays it; `MAIL_WITHIN_FAILED` carries no C row, as `UPLOAD_WITHIN_FAILED` carries none (K2452). The codes mail-door itself raises (`MAIL_OFF`, `MAIL_NOT_SET_UP`, `MAIL_NO_SUCH_ADDRESS`, `MAIL_TOO_LARGE`, `MAIL_EMPTY`, `MAIL_ALREADY_IN`, `NO_SUCH_MAIL`, the two rate codes) are rows of the new family C-157 (BOB, K5692; C-156 is `ai-catalogue`'s), in `mail-door`'s table alone. Test: each code answers its row and a missing row fails loud; C-118.12 is raised at `emailCapture` and nowhere else. Control: a code with a row in `words.json` reads its word.
- **R14** (T53; N824; `capture` R115) **One clock, no side channel.** Every instant read here follows `capture` R115's clock; the module keeps no copy of a sender, address or body outside R3's row and key. Test: with `BIO_NOW_MS` pinned, `days_left` and the clearing follow it. Control: unpinned, the wall clock.
- **R15** *(not yet met: T53)* (T53; N824, K5527 (2); amends R6; owed line drafted by a worker for BOB, who reviews it) **The clearing's owners are kept as counts until the producer has read them.** Because `clearWaitingMail` is run by the `scheduler` (layer 10) and `notice-producers` (layer 11) can read but not be called by it, each clearing that removed mail also keeps, per owner, `{owner, count, received_dates, cleared_at}` (R6's `owners`, nothing else: no sender, subject, header or text, no address of a person, R7), and `clearedOwners({after, limit, viewer})` answers those entries in order of `cleared_at`, `after` a cursor, `limit` clamped 1 to 1000, with `cursor` and `truncated`, to a viewer who is the producer's stamp and to no member. An entry is dropped 30 days after it is made. It writes nothing and never throws. Test: a clearing of two owners' mail keeps two entries; `clearedOwners` answers them by `cleared_at` with a cursor; an entry 31 days old is gone; a viewer who is a member is answered nothing. Control: a clearing that removed nothing keeps no entry; no entry holds a header or text.

## Private

### Uses

- `record-grammar`, `record-core` (`transact`, the evidence store under the private key `bio/mail/<sha256>`, `declareTable`), `test-support`, `setup-words` (the words of every code, by key).
- `membership`: the member-session fence and the viewer's identity class (R1, R3, R5), and `notAnAdmin` (R9).
- `member-roster`: the capability vocabulary `contribute` (its R1; R1's fence).
- `credentials` (as the draft's Uses lists it; used by no requirement above by name).
- `provenance`, `acquisition`, `capture`: `capture.emailCapture` (its R116) files a message; the receipt route `email` is `provenance`'s (its R82).
- `runtime-limits`: the `mail` rows of `READ_BOUNDS` and the per-level figures (R8; `boundOf("mail", name)`), no literal here.
- `mail-reader` (layer 1, new): the headers as stated, `received[]`, `authentication_results`, the four automatic-mail facts, `nesting_depth` and its refusal (ML10), the attachment listing (R3, R4, R10).
- The mail domain and the on/off switch are handed in by the composition as `mailDoorOf(ctx, deps)`'s `deps.settings()`; this module does not use `instance-setup` or `scheduler` (layers 11 and 10). `scheduler` uses this module (its `mail-clear` consumer, R6); `control-plane` and `plane` use it (the op arms and the `email()` export).

- R11 (never AI) and R14 (one clock, no side channel) are this module's invariants, stated with its services above.
