## Public

### Purpose

The plane's periodic work runs on one reconciling Durable Object alarm, never on a cron (SCHEDULER.md). This module holds the registry of consumers, each of which says when it next wants to wake, whether it is due, and what to do; it runs the due ones when the alarm fires, keeps the alarm at the earliest wake any consumer still wants, and deletes it when none does, so an idle instance holds no timer. The work itself is each consumer's owning module's.

### Provides

Terms. A **consumer** is `{name, key, due(now), wake(now), tick(now)}`: `wake` is the instant it next wants the alarm or null; `due` the instant it is due or null; `tick` does the work and answers what it did. The **grace** is 250 ms.

**onAlarm(now) → answer**; `alarm()` (the runtime's handler) is `onAlarm(Date.now())`
- **R1** It runs the `tick` of every consumer whose `due(now)` is not null and is at most `now` + grace, in registry order from the consumer R30 names, awaiting each for at most what is left of the firing's budget (R30, R31); then it reconciles authoritatively over the whole registry: the alarm is set to the smallest non-null `wake(now)`, or deleted when there is none.
- **R2** The answer is `{swept, drained, created, folded, refused, waiting, remaining, rearmed, nextAt, probes}` (the drain's counts, zero when it did not tick; `rearmed` whether an alarm is set; `nextAt` its instant or null; `probes` the test probes that ticked), plus each other consumer that ticked, its answer under its key: `monitor`, `connderive`, `overduescan`, `queuerenotify`, `monitorcadence`, `airunreap`, `capturerequests`, `airunwake`, `calibration`, `groupdomain`, `biasdebt`, `deadlinerecheck`, `gatheringsweep`, `workingonseal`, `workingonattest` (K1160), `follow`, `dutytransitions`, `interestchecks`, `moneydetectors`, `standingquestions`, `datedwaits` (T33-80), `scheduledpublish` (R22;), `filescan`, `filerender`, `filedeeper`, `fileforward` and `filereputation` (R24; K2153), `doccopy` (R25; T39), and a registered consumer's own. A consumer that did not tick is absent. A real consumer never appears in `probes`.
- **R3** A consumer whose `due`, `wake` or `tick` throws is answered under its key as `{error}` with the message; every other consumer still ticks and the reconcile still runs.

**arm(now) → instant | null** (the producers' door; `#armScheduler` today)
- **R4** Reconciles without firing: the alarm is set when none is set or when the earliest wake is sooner than it, never pushed later; deleted when no consumer wants one. It answers the alarm as it stands. It runs no tick and writes no work. (T51, K4561, K4574) An alarm already past by more than a minute, when no firing is running, is spent, not armed (the platform stops running an alarm it killed too often and goes on reporting its time): `arm` sets it afresh to the later of the earliest wake and now. A live alarm is never pushed later.

**Ordering by intent** (`layers.md`, layer 7)
- **R10** Where a consumer's due work exceeds its batch, the scheduler ranks it by what `intent`'s `servesOf` (its R28) answers each item serves: work serving an objective's open gap (intent R6), then work serving an aspiration in force, then longest-waiting (no aspiration ranks above another, intent R12; K228); but any work that has waited longer than one whole cadence of its own goes first, so priority orders the work and never starves it. Each batch-bounded tick (`monitor-cadence`, `archive-monitor`, `gathering-sweep` (`link-sweep`'s `sweepTick(now, rank)`, its R4; monitoring R56 before N506's split; K1122), `capture-request-drain`, `bias-debt`) receives the rank with its `now`, as `tick(now, rank)` (N224); a sweep item (`<bundle>#<id>`) serves what its bundle serves, so the rank asks `servesOf` about that bundle (K1160): `monitoring`'s `cadenceTick(now, rank)` and `archiveTick(now, rank)` (its R19, R20), `capture-requests`' tick (its R11, R12) and `bias`'s `biasDebtSweep` (its R33) as each takes it.

**Recovery** (Technical Architecture v10 §10.7's rule)
- **R11** When the instance starts, it reconciles as `arm` does, so an alarm lost to a failed firing or a reset is re-derived from durable state rather than waiting for the next producer. (T51, K4561, K4574) The same rule holds at start, so every deploy and restart of the object restarts the owed work.

**The suspended run** (D-583)
- **R12** A run waiting on a request that reaches `expired` is woken on the alarm that expires it, the expiry told to it as a completion, exactly once.

**The test seam: `SCHED_PROBE`; `schedProbeArm(now)`, `schedProbeLog()`, `schedAlarmAt()`**

- **R28** (T51; K4563, K4565, K4574) `heal(now)` is the request paths' door: with no alarm set or a spent one it arms as `arm` (R4); otherwise it asks no consumer anything; at most one look in five seconds per instance, none inside a firing. Every firing records its heartbeat, the storage value `sched_beat` `{last_run, running_since, watchdog_seen, watchdog_ran}`: `running_since` at its start, cleared when it ends however it ends, and `last_run` when it completes. `health(now)` reads and answers `{last_run, running_since, watchdog_seen, watchdog_ran, alarm_at, oldest_overdue, stale}`: `oldest_overdue` the earliest wake already come (null if none), `stale` (fix round 9, G95; K4807 (2), K4811 (5)) true when work is owed and either the oldest wake already come is at least 300 s old and no firing has completed in 300 s, or one firing has run longer than 300 s (`running_since` at least 300 s old); a firing counts as completed at its end instant, `ended` below, and a firing in progress does not reset the clock. It is false for work owed under 300 s, an owed row in back-off, a healthy backlog with a firing every 10 s, one row owed 400 s with the last firing 20 s ago, and nothing owed. A process signal only, never shown on a screen. It re-arms nothing. (fix round 7, G70; K4428) Every firing also records in the heartbeat `{scheduled_for, started, ended, next_set_for, gap_ms}` (what the alarm was set for, the firing's start and end, what the firing set next or null, and the time since the previous firing ended or null) and prints them as one structured line, `{"scheduler_alarm": {...}}`; it changes no pace. - **R29** (T51; K4566, K4570, K4574) `watch(at)` is the watchdog's one call, made by the plane Worker's cron once a cycle. It records `watchdog_seen` (the cron's slot, never backwards) and `watchdog_ran` (the instant the call ran, read from its own clock; fix round 9, G94) and re-arms outright, to the later of the earliest wake and now, only when no alarm is set and no firing is in progress (`running_since` within a cycle), or the alarm is spent, or work is owed, no firing is in progress, `last_run` is older than two cycles and the alarm is still ahead. - **R30** (fix round 9, G91; K4807 (2), K4811 (4)) **A firing has a budget and rotates.** The consumers of one firing share a budget of about 10 s (`FIRING_BUDGET_MS`, an injected constant; the tests inject 50 ms). When the budget is spent before every due consumer has ticked, the firing starts no further consumer, reconciles (R1) and ends; the next firing begins at the consumer after the last one that ran, so no consumer is always last and a slow consumer ahead of `pending-work` (registered through R8, after R5's list) never holds its due tick for ever; `pending-work` takes its turn in the rotation like any other. A consumer already past its turn at a firing's end is not run twice in it. Every consumer a firing runs is recorded as one structured line `{"scheduler_consumer": {name, started, ended, outcome}}`, written before and after the await when the call runs past a threshold the test injects (R-LOG), and the heartbeat (R28) carries the last firing's slowest consumer. Test: `r8-budget.test.mjs` over `alarmDouble` with the budget 50 ms and consumers `scanlike` gated before `reading`: `reading` ticks before the gate opens; `scanlike` is not started twice; the per-consumer line exists and was written before the await. Counts and order only (R-CLOCK). Control: a firing whose consumers all end inside the budget runs them all, in registry order. (K4905) The heartbeat holds `rotate_from` (the consumer the next firing begins at, null when the last reached every consumer), `inflight` (`{name: started}`) and `slowest` (`{name, ms}`). (job 62, G91; K4922) While a firing runs, the alarm is never read in the past: an `arm` made inside a firing weighs no wake and sets nothing past; it leaves the alarm as it stands or, when the platform answers none, sets the instant the firing can no longer outlast (its start, the budget and the grace). The reconcile at the firing's end weighs every wake, the work such an arm announced included, and sets the next alarm outright; a firing in which no arm is made leaves `getAlarm()` none, as the platform does. Test: `r9-visible.test.mjs`; through the door `plane/r8-headofline.test.mjs` (R80).
- **R31** (fix round 9, G92; K4807 (2), K4811 (4)) **A call that outlasts the budget is neither abandoned nor started twice.** A consumer whose call is still running when the budget is spent stays in an in-flight set: a later firing does not ask its `due` or start it again while the call is in flight, and takes its result when the call settles; a rejection after the firing is that consumer's `{error}` (R3) at the next firing.
