## Public

### Purpose

The one read across every owner of a relationship (K1469): a bounded, as-of exploration from a node over the owners registered with `connection-grammar`, each hop cited and graded in the one connection shape, the weakest hop governing, with presets (organisation chains, flows, event links, overlaps between people, the path between two nodes) as kind sets. It writes nothing, asserts no connection, ranks nothing and logs no one's exploring.

### Provides

Terms. A **hop** is one connection in `connection-grammar`'s shape `{id, from, to, kind, owner, valid, evidence, grade: {assertion, ends}, derived}`, as its owner's `neighbours` answers it. A **path** is a sequence of hops; its **grade** is its weakest hop's. A **class** is the kind's registry class: evidentiary, derived, declared or hunch. The **bounds** are `connection-grammar`'s: depth 8 by default and at most 10; a fan-out of 1,000 items per page of an owner's answer; a hub bound per kind (`connection-grammar.hubBoundOf(kind)`: 4,000 for `event_voted`, 1,000 for every other kind; its R10); 5,000 visited nodes; a time budget. A **viewer** is the control plane's stamp; it fails closed when absent. A **scope** is an optional `{inquiry}`: the working inquiry whose hunch hops may be walked.

**explore({from, to?, kinds?, at, depth?, sortBy?, scope?, viewer})** (`op=explore`)
- **R1** Refusals, in order: `NO_NODE` (no `from`), `BAD_NODE` (`from` or `to` not an id `record-grammar` knows), `NO_DATE` (no `at`; an exploration is always as of a stated date and never of "now"), `BAD_DATE` (`civil-time`'s refusal), `UNKNOWN_KIND` (a kind no owner registered, naming it), `DEPTH_OVER_MAX` (above 10, `connection-grammar.depthOf`), `UNKNOWN_QUANTITY` (a `sortBy` no kind in the walk states).
- **R2** It walks breadth-first from `from` over every owner registered for the asked kinds (all registered kinds when `kinds` is absent), calling each owner's `neighbours({node, kinds, at, page, viewer, scope})` with at most 100 nodes per call. Without `to` it answers every path found up to `depth`; with `to`, only the paths that end at `to`.
- **R3** Each path is answered with its hops in order, each hop as its owner answered it, and with the path's grade, the weakest hop's on each axis (`connection-grammar.chainGrade`). Paths are ordered by `connection-grammar.orderPaths`: hop count, then the earliest validity, then the hops' ids; with `sortBy`, by that stated quantity, the quantity named in the answer. No score, rank or measure across mixed kinds of link is computed or answered (K1471).
- **R4** Only hops valid at `at` (`civil-time.validAt`) are walked. A hop whose validity does not settle `at` is walked and marked `undetermined` at that date, and a path through it is `undetermined`, never shown as holding.
- **R5** Bounds: an owner's answer for a node is read page by page (`next`) to its end, so a kind's set within that kind's hub bound (`connection-grammar.hubBoundOf`) is walked whole, never cut at one page's fan-out. Items of one kind that an owner pages beyond that kind's bound are not walked, and `fanout_truncated` names the node and the kind. A node its owner answers as a hub (`connection-grammar`'s `neighbours` contract, R6 there) is named in `hubs` as "too common to walk", with the kind (R20), that kind's bound, its set size and why, and is not expanded for that kind. When 5,000 nodes are visited or the time budget runs out, the walk stops and answers `connection-grammar.exhausted` (`truncated: true`, `undetermined: true`, the reason), with the paths complete so far marked complete and none partial shown as complete. The answer always states `visited`, `depth`, `budget_ms` and `elapsed_ms`. (N566; K1566, K2063)
- **R6** Labels (K1487): a hop of class `declared` is marked "declared, not evidenced" at the lowest grade; a hop of class `hunch` is walked only when `scope` names the inquiry holding it and the viewer may see that inquiry, and is marked a hunch with no grade; any path with either hop is labelled a lead (`connection-grammar.chainLabel`), with the sentence that no finding rests on it until each such hop is replaced by evidence. A derived hop carries its derivation.
- **R7** Sight: a hop the owner withholds from the viewer is not walked, and nothing in the answer counts or reveals it, `visited` included.
- **R8** An answer with no path states its level in `observation-log`'s vocabulary (the record's held facts searched as of `at`, and to what depth) and lists what each owner reports as "held as a table, not read" for the nodes visited, so an absence is never stated beyond the record's reach (D59).
- **R9** It writes nothing: no table of any module, `observation-log` included, gains or changes a row through an exploration, and nothing records who explored what (D68).

**Presets: presets(), chain({from, at, viewer}), flowsFrom({from, at, period?, viewer}), relationsOf({event, at, viewer}), pathBetween({from, to, at, depth?, viewer}), overlaps({a, b, at | period, kinds?, viewer})** (`op=explorepreset`)
- **R10** `presets()` answers each preset by name with its kind set; each preset is R2–R9's walk over that set, never a walker of its own: `chain` the structure kinds of `lines` (`part_of`, `reports_to`, `oversees`, `appoints`, `funds`) and `duties`' powers; `flowsFrom` the money kinds; `relationsOf` the event relations; `pathBetween` every registered kind with `to` given. A kind an owner registers later joins the preset that names its class and family with no change here.
- **R11** `overlaps` answers, for two persons, each node both reach in one hop of the same kind (a shared post, board, school, employer, event or stated tie) whose two hops' validities intersect at `at` or within `period` (three-valued through `civil-time`), each with both hops, the intersection, and the size of the shared set: how many other persons hold a hop of that kind to that node in that span ("with 5,000 others held"). A hub is named as R5 says. An overlap is a cited fact and is never stated as the two knowing each other.

**rederive({kind, from, to, as_of, method, id, scope?, viewer})** (`op=exploreverify`)
- **R12** It recomputes a derived connection from its parameters through its owner and answers whether its deterministic id (`connection-grammar.derivedId`) equals `id`, with the hops it rests on and whether any is declared or a hunch. A derivation that cannot be recomputed answers `matches: false` with the reason, never a guess. It writes nothing.
- **R19** (N582; K1607) `scope` is R2's optional `{inquiry}`: the inquiry the leg naming the derived connection belongs to. `rederive` reads the derivation's two ends and the hops its inputs name with that `scope` passed to every owner, so a hunch hop of that inquiry the viewer may see is read and reported as a hunch in what the derivation rests on (R6), and the connection is seen as resting on a lead (`hypotheses` R6). An input it cannot read (no owner answers it, it is withheld from the viewer, or it is a hunch outside `scope`) is answered `undetermined` with the reason, naming nothing the viewer may not see (R7); while any input is undetermined the answer is `matches: false` with `undetermined: true` and never `matches: true`, so a caller refuses it as a derivation that cannot be re-derived. Without `scope`, no hunch hop is read (R6). An input **names a hop** only when it is an object carrying `connection` or `id`, or a string that is a record id (`record-grammar`) or a derived connection id (64 hex); one of the derivation's own two ends is the end, not a hop; any other object (`{entity}`, `{end, capture, ref}`, `{source}`) is a cited record and any other string (an occurrence key) a value the method names, neither read as a hop (K1787). A malformed `scope` (not `{inquiry}`) is refused `BAD_SCOPE`.

**timelineOver({set, from?, to?, viewer})** (`op=exploretimeline`)
- **R13** For a set of entity ids a caller passes (an inquiry's or project's subjects), it composes `events.timeline` over the set with the payers and payees of the money facts concerning those events (`money`), each item cited, in `events`' order, the lanes "what they did" and "what we did" kept apart as `events` answers them (R-3 I-6). It reads no project or inquiry itself.

**Hubs judged per kind** (T36; N566; K1566, K1726, K2063)
- **R20** (N566; K1566, K1726, K2063; connection-grammar R6, R10 as amended by T36-40) A hub is judged per kind in every walk, the presets' period walks included (R10, R11). A hub answer names no kind. So when an owner answers `hub` to a call that names more than one of its kinds, the walk asks that node again for each of those kinds alone (R2's call, with the same `at`, `viewer` and `scope`). A kind its owner then answers in items is walked (R5), and only a kind answered as a hub when asked alone is named in `hubs`. A hub answer to a call naming one kind names that kind. These calls count toward the time budget. No call is made for a kind the walk did not ask, and the calls reveal nothing R7 hides. **Proven by** M-X1a re-measured at the desk (K1726's measure). It uses the chain fixture (Bob's seven-hop chain, explore's Suggestions) in which one council member holds 4,000 `event_voted` connections valid in the walk's window, read through `events`' registered owner as T36-14 leaves it (merged before this module in L5). The walk answers within `connection-grammar`'s `time_budget_ms` (`elapsed_ms` below `budget_ms`, no `time` exhaustion), with the 4,000 votes walked, the member not in `hubs` and no `fanout_truncated` for `event_voted`, and no owner call binding more than 100 nodes (R2). Where the 5,000-node bound is met, the walk answers R5's `nodes` exhaustion, never `time`. With 4,001 votes, the member is named in `hubs` with kind `event_voted`, bound 4,000 and set size 4,001, and the walk's other kinds from that member are still walked. The job records the measured `elapsed_ms` beside K1726's 266–283 ms (chain).

**The connection graph: neighbourhood({centre, at, rings?, kinds?, scope?, viewer})** (`op=exploregraph`; T48; N1027; DEC-215 (2), K3138; K1469, K1487)
- **R21** (T48; N1027; DEC-215 (2); K3138, K1469, K1487) The read the connection graph draws on Explore connections, a person's "Connections" and the money trail: what the record joins a centre to, two rings out. Refusals as R1's, `centre` in `from`'s place (`NO_NODE`, `BAD_NODE`, `NO_DATE`, `BAD_DATE`, `UNKNOWN_KIND`), then `BAD_RINGS` for a `rings` other than 1 or 2 (default 2). It is R2's walk from `centre` to depth `rings`, with R4–R7's validity, bounds, labels and sight and R20's per-kind hubs, never a walker of its own (R15). It answers `{centre, at, nodes, steps, stopped, visited, budget_ms, elapsed_ms}`:
  - `nodes`: each node reached, `{id, ring, id_kind}`, `ring` 0 for the centre, `id_kind` the kind `record-grammar`'s id grammar reads from the id;
  - `steps`: each hop walked, once even when reached from both ends, `{id, from, to, kind, word, class, grade, evidence, valid, label?, undetermined?}`: `word` the kind's members' word as registered (`connection-grammar.kindOf`, K1486), `grade` the hop's `{assertion, ends}`, `evidence` its citation as its owner answered it, a declared hop labelled "declared, not evidenced" at the lowest grade and a hunch hop labelled a hunch with no grade (R6);
  - `stopped`: every place the walk stopped short, each `{node, why, kind?, bound?, set_size?}`, `why` one of `ring` (a node on the outer ring, not walked further), `hub` (R5, R20, with its kind, bound and set size), `fanout` (R5), `nodes` or `time` (R5's exhaustion), with one sentence: what lies beyond a stop is undetermined, not absent (K1469).

  Nodes and steps are in R3's order of the paths that reach them, never ranked or scored (R16); nothing hidden from the viewer is answered or counted (R7). It writes nothing (R9). Test: a centre with a person one ring out and an organisation two rings out answers three nodes, two steps with their words and grades, and the organisation in `stopped` as `ring`; control: a hop withheld from the viewer leaves `nodes`, `steps` and `visited` as if absent.
- **R22** (T48; N1030; K3145; amends R5) A walk stopped at its depth says so, beside `truncated`: `explore`, its presets and R21 answer `depth_stopped: true` with `at_depth`, the nodes reached at `depth` that the walk did not expand (at most 500, `at_depth_truncated` past that), whenever a path reached `depth`; else `depth_stopped: false`. It never states that such a node has further hops: it was not asked, and the sentence says what lies beyond is undetermined. Test: a chain of three nodes (two hops) walked to depth 2 answers `depth_stopped: true` naming the far node; control: the same chain walked to depth 3 answers `false`, no node lying at depth 3 (K3243).
- **R23** (T48; N1030; K3145; amends R12, R19) `rederive` (`op=exploreverify`) also takes `chain`, a path as R3 answers it: its hops in order (1–10), each either a derived connection's parameters (`{kind, from, to, as_of, method, id}`, R12's) or a hop of another class (`{id, kind, owner}`). It refuses `BAD_CHAIN` (empty, over 10, or a hop of neither form) and `CHAIN_BROKEN` (two consecutive hops sharing no node), naming the hop. It answers `{matches, lead, hops}`: each derived hop answered as R12 and R19 answer it (with `scope` passed to every owner), each other hop `{rederived: false, class}` (an evidentiary hop is cited, not recomputed); `lead` true when any hop is declared or a hunch (`connection-grammar.chainLabel`); `matches` true only when every derived hop matches and none is undetermined, else `false` with `undetermined: true` where one is. A call with no `chain` answers exactly as before. It writes nothing. Test: a two-hop chain of a derived and an evidentiary hop answers `matches: true`, the evidentiary hop `rederived: false`; control: a tampered id on the derived hop answers `matches: false`.
- **R24** (T48; N1030; K1487 (3); `connections` R69; amends R6) A declared hop backed by a standing attached source (a `step_source` hop, `connections` R69, whose `evidence[0].relation` is the declared hop's `id`) that the viewer may see is not walked as a declared hop: the walk takes the `step_source` hop in its place, so a path through it is not a lead on that hop's account, and the declared hop is named on it as `backs: <relation id>`. Without such a source, or with one the viewer may not see, the declared hop is walked as R6 says. It reads only what the registry's owners answer; this module reads no table of `connections`. Test: a path through a declared `member_of` with a source attached answers the `step_source` hop and no lead label; control: after the source is withdrawn the same path is a lead.
- **R25** (T50; N1086, N1090; K3302; DEC-236 (B204 (3)); `connections` R69; amends R21) `exploregraph`'s `steps` keep what a hop carries beyond R21's fields as the walk's hops (R3) do: a `step_source` hop keeps `attached_by` (who attached the source) and `at` (when); a hop without them answers neither key. Each of `nodes` carries `label`: the canonical label of the registered entity for an `ENT-` id (`entities`), else `null`; `null` means the record holds no name for it (the screen draws `connection.node.unnamed`), and a node's label is never its id. The centre carries one too. An `ENT-` node also carries `entity_kind`, the entity's kind (`null` for any other node), so the screen draws DEC-236's "{kind}". Names and kinds are read for the nodes the answer lists, in one bounded read (`entities` R56), and sight is unchanged (R7). Test: a centre with a person one ring out joined by a `step_source` hop answers the person's label, the hop's `attached_by` and `at`, and the organisation two rings out `label: null` when unnamed. Control: a hop of another kind carries no `attached_by`; a hidden hop leaves `nodes`, `steps` and `visited` as if absent.
- **R26** (T51; N1233; K3774; DEC-329 (3); K3665; `entities` R56; amends R3, R10, R21) Every hop an answer lists carries its two ends' names: each hop of a path (`explore`, R3; the presets, R10) and each of `exploregraph`'s `steps` (R21) has `from_label` and `to_label`, the canonical label of the registered entity for an `ENT-` end (`entities` R56), else `null`; `null` means the record holds no name for that end (and for a non-entity end, such as an event), and a label is never the id. So `screens-investigate` draws `screen.explore.hop` with `{from}` and `{to}`, and `screen.explore.hop.unnamed` for an end that is `null`, in place of ids; the hop's kind word stays the `connection_kinds` vocabulary's. Names are read once for each distinct end of the paths the answer lists, in reads of at most 500 ids (R56), and sight is unchanged (R7): a withheld hop names nothing and a hop's ends are named for no hop that is not answered. This module answers data only (K3665); the unnamed node's count `{n}` is `entities` R57's, and R25's `label: null` on a node is what asks it. Test: a chain from a registered person through an unnamed body answers the person's label as hop 1's `from_label`, `null` as hop 1's `to_label` and hop 2's `from_label`; control: a hop withheld from the viewer leaves every label and `visited` as if absent; an event end answers `null`.

**Every meaningful chain, the most direct, shorter ones beside a member's own, and keep looking (T53; N1260)**

- **R30** (T53; N1260; DEC-409 "every meaningful connection", DEC-410; K1471, K1487; R2-R9, R15, R20, R22) **`connectionsBetween({from, to, kinds?, at, depth?, scope?, limit?, budget_ms?, viewer})` (`op=exploreall`): every meaningful chain between two ends.** Refusals, in R1's order, then `EXPLORE_NO_END` (no `to`), `EXPLORE_SAME_END`, `CHAIN_LIMIT_OVER_MAX` / `CHAIN_LIMIT_INVALID` (`limit`, `chainLimitOf`), `BUDGET_OUT_OF_RANGE` (R34's check of `budget_ms`). It answers every meaningful chain, fewest steps first, ordered by `orderPaths` (connection-grammar R23), at most `limit` (default 50, at most 200). A chain through a hub node is listed, its step at the hub marked `hub: {node, kind, bound, set_size}`, exactly when both of its steps at the hub were returned by the answers of the chain's other nodes (the hub itself is never asked), and the hub is named in `hubs` (R5; entries `{node, owner, kind, bound, set_size, why, words}` as `reader.mjs:41` has them); a chain that would need the hub's own list is not listed. Answer `{ok, from, to, at, chains, listed, found, cut_by_limit, more_may_exist, complete_through_steps, stopped?, resume?, resumable?, hubs, unread, fanout_truncated, depth_stopped, at_depth, visited, depth, budget_ms, elapsed_ms}`. Each chain `{steps, step_count, grade (chainGrade), strength (chainStrength), label (chainLabel), undetermined?, level_complete}`; each step a link whose members are the hops as their owners answered, with `from_label`/`to_label` (R26) and R6's marks. `complete_through_steps` is the largest L such that every meaningful chain of at most L steps is counted in `found`; `listed` is the number in `chains`; `cut_by_limit` is `found > listed`; longer chains found in the time follow with `level_complete: false`, not claimed to be the first of their length. `more_may_exist` is false only when the walk finished to `depth` with no bound cut and `cut_by_limit` is false; otherwise true, with `stopped` the R15 shape when a bound of R15 stopped the walk (reason `time`, `nodes`, `fanout`, `depth` or `hub`), and no `stopped` when only `limit` cut the list. With no chain, R8's statement of level; a stop by time as R33. Writes nothing, logs no one (R9). Test: a graph with a 2-step, a 3-step and a 4-step route lists all three in that order (`explore` with `to` still lists only the 2-step); 40 parallel payments between two nodes are one step with `count` 40; no chain repeats a node; `limit: 2` of 3 answers `listed` 2, `found` 3, `cut_by_limit` true, `more_may_exist` true and no `stopped`; a hub H with A-H and H-B answers the chain A-H-B marked `hub` and a chain needing H's own list is absent with H in `hubs`; over 200 random graphs with no bound hit, the chains equal a reference one-direction enumerator kept in the test; a 6-step pair whose one-direction walk stops at 5,000 nodes is answered whole; a hop hidden from the viewer leaves `chains`, `found` and `visited` as if absent; a declared hop is marked a lead. Control: the same graph asked with `kinds` excluding the 3-step's kind lists two; `explore` R2's answer is byte-identical to before.

- **R31** (T53; N1260; DEC-409 "the most direct path", DEC-410 "Most direct path"; K1471, K1487) **`mostDirect({from, to, kinds?, at, depth?, scope?, budget_ms?, viewer})` (`op=explorepath`).** Refusals as R30 (no `limit`). It answers `{ok, from, to, at, most_direct, equally_short, most_direct_evidenced?, ...R30's bound fields}`. `most_direct` is the first chain under `orderPaths` among the chains with the fewest steps, a chain as R30 shapes it (hub, lead and `undetermined` marks kept), or null when the walk stopped before the level at which `to` is first met was complete (then `undetermined: true` with the R15 `stopped`) or no chain exists (with R8's statement of level). `equally_short` is the number of other chains with the same step count and the same `strongerChain` result (decided only by date or ids). When `most_direct` is a lead, `most_direct_evidenced` is the first chain with no declared or hunch hop within the same bounds, or null with `searched: {depth, complete}`; a finding never rests on a lead (K1487). Writes nothing. Test: two 2-step chains with weakest hops B and C answer the B one with `equally_short` 0; two with B and B on different dates answer the earlier with `equally_short` 1; a 2-step chain through a declared hop and a 3-step all-evidenced chain answer the lead as `most_direct` and the 3-step as `most_direct_evidenced`; a fake clock that expires before the first meeting level answers null with `stopped.reason` `time`; no answer carries `score`, `rank` or `central`. Control: with an evidenced 2-step chain, `most_direct_evidenced` is absent.

- **R32** (T53; N1260; DEC-409 "simplified", DEC-410 "A chain you built", DEC-411; K1467, K1487, K4403 (8); extends R23's `chain` idea, not its refusals) **`shorterThan({chain, kinds?, at, depth?, scope?, limit?, budget_ms?, viewer})` (`op=exploreshorter`): shorter chains beside a member's own.** `chain` is a path as the caller holds it, 1 to 10 hops, each `{id, kind, from, to}` (`owner` optional). **Why not R23's hop shape, and where `from` and `to` come from.** R23's non-derived hop is `{id, kind, owner}` with no ends, and `rederive` skips the adjacency test for a hop that states none (`explore/rederive.mjs:145`); this op needs the ends to find the chain's two ends and to test adjacency. The caller does not look them up: the trail the screen keeps (screens-investigate R69) is the `exploregraph` steps as answered, and R21's steps carry `from` and `to` (and R3's path hops are connections with them); a caller holding R23-form hops adds them from the answer it took the hops from. Nothing here reads a hop's ends from its id. The chain's two ends are its first and last nodes in walking order (a one-hop chain: `from` and `to`). Refusals (new codes, none shared with R23's `BAD_CHAIN` / `CHAIN_BROKEN`, which keep their meaning and shape): `OWN_CHAIN_INVALID` (empty, over 10, a hop without id or kind, or the ends not told apart), `OWN_CHAIN_NO_ENDS` (a hop without `from` or `to`, naming the hop), `OWN_CHAIN_GAPPED` (two consecutive hops share no node, naming the hop), then the walk refusals of R30. It finds meaningful chains (R30's meaning) between the two ends with fewer steps than the member's chain (`kinds` default: every registered kind; the member's chain is not edited, replaced, graded or marked). Answer `{ok, chain_steps, shorter: {evidenced, leads}, fewest_steps, searched: {kinds, depth, visited, hubs}, complete, ...bound fields}`: `evidenced` the shorter chains with no declared or hunch hop, `leads` those with one (each labelled a lead, R6), each ordered by `orderPaths`, at most `limit`; `fewest_steps` the least step count among them, or null; with none, `shorter` lists are empty and `searched` states kinds, depth and the hubs not walked, never that none exists. A hidden hop never shortens anything. No key or text says better, worse, wrong or roundabout (R36). Writes nothing (R9).
