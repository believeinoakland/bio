## 1. What the construct is, and why it is major

Publication is the point where the record's trustworthiness becomes public and irreversible. Everything below it is the group's own — captures with provenance, content, meaning, inquiries and findings held to a bar — and publication is the act by which the group STANDS BEHIND a production and lets a stranger, with no credential and without this instance's cooperation, verify that the group said what it claims and rested it on what it says it did. It is the last rung of the authored ladder (release → stand behind → ground → conclude → accept → ratify → publish) and the only one that cannot be walked back: *"Publishing IS an irreversible act! It's (one of?) the only irreversible acts"* (Bob, 2026-08-03, DEC-19).

Three consequences give the construct its shape. The record must be able to prove later what it published — so the published projection is content-addressed, signed, and readable by tooling that is not BIO. What is published must not claim more than the record supports — so the fence sits on the provenance chain, the bar on the project, and the bias travels with the case. And a correction moves forward, never back — so a published case is revised as a new EDITION, and the history is what may never change.

## 2. The objects

| object | what it is | ruled |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **a case** | a production of a PROJECT, composed of one or more findings, some load-bearing; *"a case is a different object, not just a different phase of a finding"* | DEC-72, DEC-44 |
| **the bar** | the standard of evidence a production is held to — a property of the project (`required_strength`, strictest wins), never of an inquiry or claim; every load-bearing finding meets it; non-load-bearing findings may appear and are not presented as load-bearing | DEC-72 |
| **an edition** | a published case is revised as a new edition, incremented, treated as a separate document; a citation names an edition; supersession is surfaced, not followed; what may change between editions is everything and what may not is the history | DEC-12 |
| **the exclusion statement** | the authored statement of what was left out — authored fresh per edition, never byte-identical to the last (C-21.1); a case cannot be signed before it is authored | DEC-12; M10 |
| **the bias acknowledgement** | the declared bias the case was produced under, public, accompanying every published case; hunches are the one bias that must be cleared first | DEC-20 |
| **the case document** | the signed artefact, format `bio-case-document/2` (`/1` accepted for documents authored before §3 rule 12): id, edition, project, scope, findings roster with the load-bearing partition, the version hash per member and — since rule 12 — each member's own edition, frozen strength pair and grounds (`case_strength`, `case_strength_grounds`), the completeness block, the exclusion list, `required_strength` (C-41.1 … C-41.12; C-2.8 and C-3.1 per member) | CASE-5b; D-442 |
| **the published projection** | the `published_*` tables and the `PUBLISHED` R2 bucket — a second, structurally separate store that the public read path answers from and nothing else does | §4 |

## 3. The rules, stated once

1. **One-way.** Publication cannot be undone; below it, acts *"cannot be undone SILENTLY"* and a correction always moves forward (DEC-19). A claim can be removed from a finding, rescinding it to an inquiry; the published edition stays.
2. **Only a project publishes, and only its manager.** *"Only findings that are part of a project can be published"*; the publisher is a manager of the project, by default its owner; a case requires at least one load-bearing member (DEC-72).
   **Applied 2026-09-18 by BOB #15 to D-429 (REC-137 found it):** a PROJECT's own document is not a finding, so
   `op=ratify` refuses a project bundle outright; a project publishes through its cases. Wherever `op=ratify` ratifies a
   finding it takes case ratification's rules (Membership v2 §7): an OWNER among the signers, and a deliverer who is the
   founder or a JOINED member. A caller who cannot see the project is answered exactly as for one that does not exist,
   never with a statement about its contents (REC-138's class). The builder verifies at the code that `op=ratify`
   publishes nothing outside a case.
   **BUILT 2026-09-18 by REC-140 (IC-157).** `op=ratify` refuses a project bundle by type (C-58.1
   `RATIFY_PROJECT_BUNDLE`, its owner included); a finding a RATIFIED case pins is published only through
   `Store#caseAuthority` — case ratification's two questions, moved there from `ratifyCaseDocument` so both acts ask one
   rule (an owner signs, C-57.1; the founder or a joined member delivers, C-56.1); the gate facts are read at the
   ratifier's viewer, so a hidden bundle answers byte-for-byte as a never-minted one. **The verification the note asked
   for found that `op=ratify` DOES publish outside a case** — an information bundle, an inquiry in no case, and a finding
   prepared into a case whose document is not yet ratified (published loose, so the ceremony's order is not enforced).
   Pinned as measured and NOT closed: the ruling does not say how (D-431, with Bob).

   signs a finding (`awaiting` = pinned minus `published_bundles`), so *case document first* is the ceremony's own order.
   (a) **A FINDING** is ratified only at a `bundle_sha` a RATIFIED case pins (`published_case_members.version_sha`), under

3. **The fence is on the provenance chain, not the content axis.** A bundle at or past `verified` carries a chain per captured document; every hop names WHO; content authority may be undetermined but must be *"STATED, dated, and carried into what the public reads. Silence is refused"* (C-18.9; Bob, 2026-07-31). What a published hash claims is *"these bytes, this address, this date, this chain of custody"* — and no more. The fence moved here from the content axis so that a gate never pressures anyone into inventing an attribution.
4. **Bias is public; hunches are cleared first.** *"Not all bias needs to be cleared before a piece is published. The only bias type that must be clear before publication is hunches"* (DEC-20); hunch debt is publication-disqualifying (`schema.mjs`), and since PL-8 it is enumerable (`leg:hunch`).
5. **Inheritance is per axis.** A leg on a published case says `grade_source: inherited`, names an edition, and cannot be stronger than the case beneath it on either axis (C-21.2).
6. **The subject's right of reply is a declaration, not a gate**: the publisher's determination and justification are an element of the group's recorded bias (DEC-13).
7. **Attribution is the attesting member's choice.** A member's material contribution to a published case — an observation or an opinion — is attributed at one of four levels, chosen by that member: the group, the project, the member's cover, or the member by name; a source who spoke off the record to preserve anonymity is valid (Bob, 2026-09-14, amending the same day's naming rule). The record carries the chosen level with the act. Cover and handle (Membership v2 §3) resolve in favour of member choice, the cover being one of the four.
8. **The standard of proof attaches to the production, set by the project's bar and the audience's needs** — not to a claim object, which is not needed for it to vary, and not to an individual member's role (Bob, 2026-09-14, confirming the record's shape with one correction accepted: *"Okay"*).
9. **Anything leaving the instance addressed to someone carries hash, date, author and both threshold floors in-band** (DEC-31's one binding rule, ahead of the deferred design).
10. **The public read path is credential-free and pinned to the record namespace**: `op=verify` and `op=publishedmanifest` answer from the published projection only, need no credential, and let *"any member or any stranger rebuild and independently verify the published record without this instance's cooperation, permission, or continued existence"*; a scratch namespace is deliberately unreadable there.
11. **The exclusion statement is checked by a SECOND PERSON, and the check is DISCLOSED, never enforced** (BOB #27, 2026-09-22, ruling D-150 on SCHEDULER #13's LED-7 question, at the code: case ratification needs one owner's signature, Membership v2 §7). The row's external evidence is that the catch was a second person with standing, and that an authored field is necessary and not sufficient. So the statement carries ACKNOWLEDGEMENTS — each an authored, attributed, dated act by a joined participant of the publishing project other than its author, or by a review-copy recipient through their grant (§6A) — and the signed case document's completeness block lists them, or states that nobody but its author acknowledged it. It never refuses publication for want of one: a group may be one person (Design Requirement 2), and a gate that pushes a member to recruit a signature is a bug in the gate. A reviewer is shown the statement FIRST, as the thing to attack (§6A.4). Whether an unresolved objection to the statement travels with the published case is doctrine, and NOT ruled here.
    **As built 2026-09-23 (D-150; IC-227, I3 + I5): the PLANE HALF.** `op=statementack` is the act, UNGATED through the review copy's two doors (a recipient by the grant's secret, `#liveReviewGrant`; a member by session, of a draft or of an authored and unsigned case document), and the POSITION is `#isJoinedParticipant` — so an invited member who has not joined and an administrator are refused `STATEMENT_ACK_NOT_A_PARTICIPANT`, and the statement's author (the draft's last editor, or the document's `completeness.author`) `STATEMENT_ACK_BY_ITS_AUTHOR`. An acknowledgement is a row of `statement_acknowledgements`, keyed on the SHA-256 of the statement as the document prints it, the project and the case identity it stood at, so an edited statement is a different sentence and starts with none. `op=publish` lists every acknowledgement of the statement it publishes in the case document — `completeness.statement_sha`, `completeness.acknowledged` (the count, so zero is a statement) and `completeness_acknowledgements[]` (kind, by, recipient, at), and in the prose beside the statement *Who else read this statement*, or *Nobody but its author acknowledged it* — leaving out the publisher's own, who becomes the author at that act, and counting it in the answer. An acknowledgement landing on an authored and UNSIGNED document RE-AUTHORS it (only the two runs of the list move; its hash moves, so a signature over the old bytes is `CASE_RATIFY_STALE`), because `op=publish` cannot run twice over one prepared edition; a SIGNED one refuses `STATEMENT_ACK_ALREADY_SIGNED`. `op=caseratify` commits the list from the signed bytes into `published_cases.completeness.acknowledgements` (NULL — never an empty list — for a document authored before this landing, which says nothing about acknowledgements), and `op=publishedcase` serves it; `op=reviewcopy` shows the list for the statement as it stands. C-41.10 refuses bytes listing the statement's author as its own acknowledger, or a count that disagrees with the list, and requires no list. NOTHING refuses publication for want of one (`bio-plane/test/d150-statement-acknowledgement.test.mjs`, whose control arms a publish gate on an acknowledgement and fails the one-member arm by name). NOT BUILT: the SURFACE — the review copy leading with the statement and an acknowledge act in the member UI (UI-68's, DELEGATED to UI); and the format token did not move (`bio-case-document/2`), so the gate cannot require the list of a new document without refusing a /2 document authored before it.
12. **PUBLISHING WRITES NOTHING ON A MEMBER FINDING — RULED 2026-09-22 by BOB #28 on D-442** (CONDUCT #14's route; measured by the REC-166 worker, `MEASUREMENTS.md` M-100), at the code, from `INVESTIGATIVE-SESSION.md` §7 (a project's act writes nothing on the shared question) and DEC-72 (publication is the case relation). A finding is shared: several projects cite it and several cases pin it, each by its `bundle_sha`. `publishCase()` still PROMOTES every member — it writes the case's completeness block and exclusions, the *What This Excludes* section, the frozen strength pair and grounds, the member's own edition and a Session Log receipt naming the case into the finding's bytes — so one project's PREPARE moves the finding off another project's ratified pin and flags that case. CASE-5b moved the case's eight stamps out; this finishes it. (a) **`op=publish` promotes no member**: the case document pins each member at the `bundle_sha` it has when prepared, and the finding's bytes stay what its project concluded. (b) **Everything the promotion wrote is stated ONCE, in the case document**: the completeness block and exclusion list (already the case's, C-41), and per member its role, its pinned sha, its own published edition, and the FROZEN STRENGTH PAIR AND GROUNDS as read at the act — a fact about this case's reading of that finding at that sha, so a case still has no strength of its own (DEC-72) — and the publish receipt (who, when, which project) as the case document's history, never the finding's. (c) **What a member signs**: the case-document signature now covers the completeness, the exclusions and the frozen pairs, which the member-bytes signature covered before, and the member-bytes signature covers the finding as its project concluded it. ## 6A. The review copy — an addressed act that never leaves the instance

**Ruled by Bob on 2026-09-17, twice in one day, and the second ruling overturned the first design.** DEC-31 had been deferred since 2026-08-03 on a named trigger — *the first group that asks to send a case to a named recipient without publishing it* — and the trigger fired the way a good trigger does: Bob named the need rather than the abstraction. *"A member will sometimes want to hand a case to one person or internal group. It seems to me that that action should stand beside the publish act… it's important that the product of that act clearly indicate that it's a pre-publish version."*

**The doctrine is his and is settled: the act stands BESIDE publish, the product is MARKED as what it is, it is as complete as a full publication can be, and it NAMES WHAT IS MISSING.** The naming is his too — *advance copy* or *review copy*, both valid; **not *pre-publish***, which asserts a future that may not happen, and a great many review copies will never be published at all.

### 6A.1 The two determinations Bob overruled, kept because the correction is the design

BOB #12 determined that a review copy must be **published-but-unlisted** and therefore **immutable and versioned**. Bob reversed both, and his branch is the better one for a reason worth stating rather than merely recording.

**The fence was taken as fixed and the artifact was moved.** The reasoning ran: the two-bucket fence is structural, the private bucket never leaves the Durable Object, there is no third bucket that is both outside the instance and private — therefore a review copy that reaches an outside recipient must be published at an unguessable address. **Bob took the artifact as fixed and kept it behind the fence instead:** *"Agreed that a review copy never leaves the instance."* Same fence, opposite move, and it gives up only REACH while the other gave up the fence's meaning.

**And the second reversal FOLLOWS from the first rather than contradicting it, which is what makes his design coherent where the other was merely consistent.** Immutability was reasoned from exposure: an unlisted copy in someone's hands cannot be recalled, so versioning was the only honest answer to *when does a rendering someone acted on become a record*. **If it never leaves, there is no loose copy to go stale — so mutability costs nothing and editing is exactly right.** Bob: *"An 'advance copy' or 'review copy' (both valid names) is mutable. Only a real publish is not. An editor must be able to edit, right? (Mind you, the editor must have project permissions to do so.)"*

**The cleaner rule falls out of it: ONE irreversible act, not two.** Publication remains the only act that cannot be walked back (DEC-19), and the review copy is emphatically not a small publication. That is the single sentence to carry out of this section.

### 6A.2 How a recipient reaches something that never leaves

**THE PRECONDITION, decided 2026-09-18 by BOB #14 from REC-126's stop (gap 1) — without it a grant scopes nothing and
revokes nothing.** REC-126 found at the code that `op=casedocument` answered with NO login and returned an UNSIGNED case
document in full — scope, findings, completeness statement, exclusions, bias acknowledgement — and that case ids are
sequential, so a stranger could walk every group's unpublished working case. No ruling of Bob's stood behind that; it was a
mechanism choice (CASE-5b). **The fence already rules the answer:** unratified working material never crosses to the
public, and the ops table's own reasoning is that even a title *names what the group is looking into*. So: **an UNSIGNED
case document answers only to members with standing in the owning project and to a live grant holder for that case;
everyone else receives exactly the answer a case that does not exist receives**, so enumeration learns nothing. **A SIGNED
case document stays public** — a stranger needs it to verify. Built as a correction to landed substrate, ahead of the
review copy itself.

**This is the mechanism question Bob's ruling opened, and it is the architect's rather than his.** A copy that stays inside the instance must be READ inside the instance, so the recipient needs a way in. **That way in is nearer the existing gate-and-capability vocabulary than it is to publication, and it must not be built as a small publish.**

The shape: **a scoped, revocable, read-and-comment GRANT against one production.** Its properties, each chosen against a failure this record has already met:

| property | what it means | why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **scoped to one production** | the grant names one case, not a project, a member or the instance | a grant that widens silently is the publication fence loosened by a side door (IC-61 / D-280(a)) |
| **revocable, and revocation is real** | withdrawing it ends access; nothing was copied out, so there is nothing to chase | this is the whole value of never leaving the instance, and it is lost the moment a rendering is exported |
| **read and comment, not edit** | the recipient is a reader; editing needs project permissions and is the editor's act, per Bob | comment is what a reviewer is FOR; edit-by-recipient would make authorship unattributable |
| **attributed** | the grant names who issued it, to whom, and when; comments carry their author | an unattributed act on a production the group will stand behind is the record claiming more than it can support |
| **never a bucket** | the grant is a capability over the private bucket, NOT a third bucket | there is no third bucket, and inventing one in implementation is how the fence dies quietly |

**WHO MAY AUTHOR, ISSUE AND REVOKE — decided 2026-09-18 by BOB #15 from REC-126's build, where it ran provisionally at
the project owner.** Each answer follows a ruling already made, so none is returned to Bob:
- **AUTHOR the draft:** anyone with the project's existing EDIT permission — Bob's own words above, *editing needs
  project permissions and is the editor's act*. The draft is the production, and editing it is editing.
- **ISSUE a grant:** the project OWNER only, and NO administrator bypass. Sending unratified material to someone outside
  the group is the same kind of act as publishing, and DEC-72 makes publishing the owner's.
- **REVOKE a grant:** the project OWNER. **CORRECTED the same day by BOB #15 — the first version said *the owner or any
  administrator*, and that contradicted Bob's doctrine** in `BIO_Membership_Architecture_v2.md` §4: administrators
  *"audit everything and direct nothing"* on a project, with ONE exception, §7.13 (*"that is the whole of the
  exception"*). The case it was meant for — a grant outliving an owner who has left — is §7.13's own: an administrator
  adds an owner when every owner is inactive, and that owner revokes.
- **No two-person rule.** No ruling asks for one, and inventing one would be a fence tighter than its rule.

**What the grant is NOT, stated because the near-miss is easy:** it is not an account, not membership, and not a weaker class of member. A recipient holding a grant has no standing in the project beyond reading one production and saying something about it.

**HOW THE RECIPIENT GETS IN, decided 2026-09-18 (REC-126's gap 2).** The only credentialless path today, the invite link,
makes its holder a member, which this section forbids. So a grant carries its own **bearer SECRET**, and its properties are
the grant's own table applied to a credential: **a READ credential only** — it can never deliver an attested act (AI Roles
rule 4 is untouched); **stored hashed**, never recoverable from the record; **bound to one case edition**; **killed on
revocation**; and **a dead secret is indistinguishable from one that never existed**, so a revoked recipient learns nothing
by trying. No members-only interim: it would ship a review copy that cannot reach the people it exists for.

### 6A.3 The original question survives in a narrower form, and is NOT dissolved

DEC-31 asked *when does a persistent rendering someone acted on become a record?* Under the immutable-published design that question dissolved. **Under Bob's design it does not — it narrows, and the narrower form must be stated rather than quietly dropped.**

**The moment a member exports a PDF of a review copy and emails it, a rendering someone may act on is loose again**, and it is loose by the member's own act, outside the fence, where the record can only say what it holds. Two consequences bind now:

1. **DEC-31's BOUND RULE STILL BINDS AND ITS SCOPE IS UNCHANGED.** Any rendering that leaves the instance addressed to someone carries its hash, its date, its author and both threshold floors **in-band** (H4's rule, extended 2026-08-03 and enacted at `5318b53`). A review copy rendered to a file is exactly such a rendering. **The rule costs nothing at the moment of rendering and cannot be applied afterwards**, which is why the deferral bound it while deferring everything else.
   **Measured 2026-09-19 by BOB #16: `op=reviewcopy` does not yet carry it.** Its answer has the date (`updated_at`)
   and the author (`updated_by`) and no hash over what is rendered and no threshold floors — *"both threshold floors of the
   stance rendered"*, the two strength axes the published container's running header prints. So **no surface offers to
   export, download or print a review copy to a file until the plane's answer carries all four in-band** — a SHA-256 over
   the canonical bytes of what it answers, its date, its author, and the floors the case would be held to on both axes
   (the project's `required_strength`), proved to be the same quantity the published container renders. Reading the copy
   inside the instance needs none of this, and is rowable now.
   **BUILT on the plane 2026-09-23 (REC-148, IC-229).** `op=reviewcopy` answers `inband`: a SHA-256 over the
   answer as served minus `inband` (`JSON.stringify(rest, null, 1)`, UTF-8), the draft's `updated_at` and `updated_by`,
   and both floors read from the project's `required_strength` by the `#projectBar` call `op=publish` freezes. All four
   come from ONE function, `inbandQuartet` (`bio-plane/src/inband.mjs`), which also takes the case container's manifest
   hash and puts the same quartet on the container's assembly answer; `test/reviewcopy-inband.test.mjs` proves one case
   edition's two quartets agree field for field and that no second hasher exists. The surface offering export, and
   point 2 below, are NOT built.
2. **THE SURFACE SAYS SO AT THE ACT.** A member exporting a review copy is told, in the same breath, that what leaves cannot be revoked and that the grant they hold is the thing that can. This is the *never pressure someone into inventing an attribution* rule pointed at privacy: a member who needs genuine confidentiality must be told what this act does and does not give them, rather than sold a revocable grant as control over a file they have already emailed.

### 6A.4 Completeness is already expressible and is REUSED, not invented

*As complete as possible, with what is missing clearly indicated* is vocabulary this record already has, and inventing a second vocabulary for review copies would be the defect this construct exists to refuse. A review copy states its own gaps **in the same words a published case does**: the `searched` section (IC-112, which now refuses a case document that omits it), undetermined-is-first-class, the strength walk naming its weakest legs, and the frontier's absence vocabulary.

**And the gate rule applies unchanged: a gate that pressured a member into filling a gap to get a review copy out would be a bug in the gate** (DEC-69, and `CLAUDE.md`'s rule that the publication fence moved off the content axis for exactly this reason). A review copy with honest gaps is the normal case, not a degraded one — it is very often the REASON for sending one.

**AND SO THE PRODUCTION IS A DRAFT CASE, decided 2026-09-18 (REC-126's gap 3).** `publishCase` refuses an incomplete case
(`BELOW_PROJECT_STRENGTH`, `NOT_CONCLUDED`, …), and a review copy must not be refused for exactly the gaps it is sent to show.
So a review copy is taken from a **DRAFT case, identified BEFORE the publish gates run**, and **the gates' results become the
draft's list of what is missing** — the same refusals, in the same words, shown to the recipient as the gaps rather than
applied as a wall. Nothing about the draft is published, and publishing it later runs every gate as it does today. **The exclusion statement LEADS the review copy** (§3 rule 11, D-150): the recipient is shown what the case leaves out first, asked to attack it, and may acknowledge it or comment on it through the grant.

## 7. Attribution, and the catalogue owed

The catalogue is what lets a project set its bar against what its audience actually needs; without it `required_strength` is a number a project picks.

**THE PUBLISHING GROUP'S PUBLIC IDENTITY — DESIGNED 2026-09-21 by BOB #24 (SCHEDULER #9's Q4; REC-163, UI-77).** Since
D-436 a group's identity in the record is ONE slug, written once (State Rules §3.1). What a reader of the public surface
may be shown, and on whose word:

1. **The slug is PUBLIC.** It already travels in every published bundle's signed `group`, and it names the worker (D-102),
   so the instance's default address carries it. A public read discloses nothing the group has not already published or
   served. `op=instancegroup` therefore admits the public class, and the setup page and the public header show the
   recorded slug, or say that none is recorded. A group that wants to stay unnamed chooses an unrevealing slug at
   install, because the slug is written once. **ONLY THE SLUG** (BOB #27, 2026-09-22, ruling REC-163's design gap at the
   code, `store.mjs` `instanceGroupPublic`): the public answer is the slug, or `group: null` with the stated absence,
   and the store that answered. The rest of the recorded row — when (`recorded_at`), by which act (`source`: the
   install's first boot or the root of trust's seed) and by whom (`recorded_by`, a person when seeded) — is in no
   signed bytes, tells a stranger about the install, and can name a member, so it stays with the credentialed readers,
   who are answered the whole row. A probe naming `bio`, and a caller with no credential naming `store=scratch`, get
   the public answer: a public fact is public to every class, and both stores record one slug. The page reads `bio`.
2. **A DISPLAY NAME: the group's own words, set by an administrator, never in signed bytes.** It is one bounded text value
   in the store's durable state. An administrator's session sets and revises it, with `by` stamped by the server as for
   the Membership v2 §4 governance acts (never a bearer, never an attribution the caller supplies), and each change is
   dated in its history. It is presentation only: signed bytes carry the slug, the identity that cannot move. It asserts
   only what the group calls itself, so it needs no verification, and for that same reason every public surface shows it
   WITH the slug and never instead of it. A name that imitates another body cannot stand alone as an identity.
3. **A DOMAIN: recorded as a CLAIM, and shown publicly only while VERIFIED.** An administrator's act records it. The plane
   verifies it by fetching a well-known file on that domain, through the per-host governor, that names this instance's
   address and slug. It records a dated verdict (`verified`, `absent` or `mismatched`) and re-checks it on the reconciling
   alarm. Public surfaces show a domain only while its latest verdict is `verified`; members see the claim and its state.
   Showing an unverified domain would be the record claiming an association it cannot support: a group could claim a
   city's domain. That is the defect class this construct exists to refuse.

   **RULED 2026-09-23 by BOB #31 (REC-164).** The well-known file is `/.well-known/civicos-group.json`. The verdict set
   has a FOURTH value, `undetermined`: the fetch did not complete (governor held, network, a non-JSON answer), so the
   record cannot say whether the domain names this instance. The domain is NOT shown while its verdict is
   `undetermined`, and members see the undetermined state stated as such, never as `absent`.

REC-163 and UI-77 build as placed, except that the public surfaces show the slug rather than a neutral label, which means
the op gains the public class (I3, additive; the integrator mints the IC). The display name and the verified domain are
new items, RECORD and then UI, placed after them. Not built.

**POINT 1 BUILT ON THE PLANE, 2026-09-22 (REC-163, IC-174).** `op=instancegroup` admits the public class: a caller holding
no credential is answered the slug, or that none is recorded, and nothing else. The slug is read through the one reader
every stamp uses, so the page, the op and the bytes of every document the instance creates name one group. A credential
the admission gate admits keeps the whole row — when the slug was recorded, by which act and by whom — because point 1's
reason covers the slug alone: those facts are published and served nowhere, and this section does not rule them public.
The setup page served at `/` carries the slug in its served bytes, signed in or out. It says in words when none is
recorded, and when the record does not answer it says it could not read the group, never that none is recorded. NOT

**POINT 1 BUILT IN THE MEMBER UI, 2026-09-22 (UI-77).** `civicos-ui/app.html` no longer declares a group. Its member fence

**POINTS 2 AND 3 BUILT ON THE PLANE, 2026-09-23 (REC-164; IC-223, minted and resolved at integration).** Two durable values, each
with a dated history that nothing updates or deletes. `op=groupnameset` and `op=groupdomainset` reach an administrator's
own signed-in session: a bearer is refused by name (C-64.4), `by` is stamped by the server after the caller's parameters
are copied, and the store asks the live roster for an active administrator (C-64.5), so a member naming an administrator
is still themselves. The display name is one line of at most 120 characters (C-64.6) and is written into no signed
bytes. A domain is a bare host name (C-64.7). The act records it with the address the administrator's session reached,
then fetches `https://<domain>/.well-known/civicos-group.json` through the per-host governor, and reads whether its
`{ instance, group }` names that address and the recorded slug. The verdict is dated: `verified`, `absent` (no file, or
a redirect away from the domain), `mismatched` (another instance, another group, or not the format) or `undetermined`.
The reconciling alarm re-checks the current claim once a day (`group-domain-recheck`), because a check made once at set
time certifies a file the domain can change. `op=groupidentity` answers a stranger the slug, the display name only when
a slug is recorded beside it, and a domain only while the latest verdict on the current claim is `verified`; a
credentialed reader also gets the claim, its latest verdict and both histories. NOT BUILT: any surface that shows
either value (UI, next). Not ruled, and chosen provisionally: the fourth verdict, the file's path and format, and which
address the file must name.

## 8. Risk tiers, and what is dishonest today

Evidence packages are classified in three tiers — file freely; file with caution; do not file without counsel, publishing the evidence and withholding the filing templates — because *"a poorly filed Tier 3 case could create adverse precedent that forecloses future, properly constructed challenges"* (`BIO_Communications_Platforms.md`; Roadmap §8). The field is `risk_tier` on the action schema (State Rules v1.5). The placeholders that satisfied the check are gone: the counterparty's with D-130, and `risk_tier: 1` with D-182 (2026-09-23), which writes UNDETERMINED wherever no member stated a tier and publishes the three words (`node tools/status.mjs 8.risk-tier`). NOT BUILT: a member-facing tier chooser, and actions written at the old default still read 1 because their bytes say so.
