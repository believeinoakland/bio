1. Respect the member

Members are volunteers and professionals (journalists, auditors, lawyers, accountants and others) doing serious work. The product informs them once, at the moment it matters, and then trusts them.

  1. Never nag, never second-guessIn your words: the workflow must not be nagging or second-guessing users… Anything short of that is a flaw. Informing at the act, once, is respect; repeating it is not.The grade note appears once, when a document is captured. Nothing reminds the member about it later.DEC-69 (2026-08-10); DEC-39, DEC-51, DEC-49
  2. Nothing chosen for a member unseenAny default is visible and changeable at the moment it applies. Nothing is preselected behind the member's back.When an action is chosen, its suggested deadline reminder shows in the form, ready to change or remove.DEC-77; DEC-94 (1)
  3. Reminders are the member's ownA reminder exists only because a member asked for it. "Overdue" is the one thing said unasked, once. Nothing reaches a member outside CivicOS: no email, no push.A records request's due date changes the item's position and colour as it nears; only when it passes does the queue say "Overdue".DEC-94 (1)–(4); DEC-10
  4. Absent, not greyedWhat a member cannot do is not on their screen. The system still refuses it underneath, but the screen should make the refusal unnecessary.A member without the capability to invite people never sees an "Invite" button.UI-KICKOFF "Constraints that are law"; Membership v2 §5
  5. You are who you signed in asNo screen asks who you are, and no one can act as anyone else.Every act records its member from the session, never from a field the member fills in.UI-KICKOFF "Identity is server-stamped"
  6. The member's words stay the member'sThe machine may shape and propose. Every reason, conclusion and signature is a member's own.A drafted "What changed" statement is labelled machine work until a member adopts or rewrites it.Interaction Constructs §P; DEC-24; DEC-60; DEC-101

2. Simple on top, depth beneath

The domain is complex; the screen should not be. Detail is always one layer away, never on top.

  1. Minimum visible complexitySimple interfaces even when the domain and the system are complex, with layers: simplicity at the top, more detail as a member drills down.A question shows two grade badges and one phrase ("Short on connection"); a hover names the weak link; a click opens the full chain.Bob's UX principles, 2026-07-28
  2. Show what matters at this levelWhat carries weight at one level may be irrelevant at another. Relevant state always shows, named so a newcomer can learn it, signalled with colour, with its consequences: what it allows and what it forbids.On the project page a question's state is shown; on the document page it is not, because it does not bear on the document.Refinements, 2026-07-28
  3. See connections at the right levelDon't be myopic: let members see how things connect, at the level where the connection matters.A finding shows which other projects' questions cite it, without opening them.Bob's UX principles, 2026-07-28
  4. Organised the way members thinkData, layouts and journeys follow how members think about the task, not how the system is built. The most common actions are the easiest to reach.Members find "my questions" and "what is waiting on me", not "inquiries" and "queue items".Bob's UX principles, 2026-07-28; Roadmap §12
  5. Density is signals, not crammingIn your words: Information density is not less whitespace; it is signals. Colour, chips, type, hovers, menus, collapsibles and space, each drawn from deliberately.A deadline is a coloured chip with a date, not a sentence.Refinements, 2026-07-28
  6. Zoom out as easily as inA member focused on a detail can, once it is sorted out, step back to the big picture in one move, and land where that detail sits in it.After fixing one weak link, one click returns to the project's questions with that question highlighted and its new grades showing.Bob, comment on this page, 2026-10-02

3. Tell the truth about the record

The record's honesty is its value. The screen never hides a gap, guesses past one, or makes evidence look stronger than it is.

  1. What we don't know is shown"Undetermined" is an answer, never an error. It always comes with its reason."Undetermined, because the page could not be read as text."Interaction Constructs §U; DEC-86
  2. Five gaps, five namesUndetermined, Withheld, Unrated, Nobody looked and Refused each have their own look and one sentence pattern, never borrowed from another."Nobody looked" (no one searched there) never looks like "Undetermined" (someone looked and could not tell).DEC-86; DEC-82
  3. One vocabulary for ordinary states"Nothing here", "Still loading", "Could not read this, because…". Every wait names what, from whom, and by when."Waiting on the City Clerk's reply, due 14 October."DEC-98
  4. Never one scoreStrength is two grades side by side, how well the documents are held and how firmly they are linked, never combined."Capture B · connection C", never "B-".DEC-44; DEC-82
  5. Each kind of measure has its own formLetters grade evidence, bars show progress, weights mark acts; no form is borrowed across them. Colour marks which scale, never how good the value is.A capture grade of D is the same colour as an A: the capture scale's colour. Its letter says how strong it is.DEC-82 (measures map); DEC-79
  6. A grade is about checking, not truthA grade says how easily someone else could check the thing for themselves, never whether it is true.A hover on "capture B" reads: "Can we trust this copy? B: the group fetched it itself and fingerprinted it on receipt."DEC-82
  7. History is the recordCorrections are added, never erased. Nothing on screen implies deletion where the system corrects by adding.A withdrawn docket entry stays visible, followed by the entry that takes it back and why.UI-KICKOFF "Append-only is the ethos, visibly"; DEC-116
  8. Change weighs as much as stateWhat changed, is changing, or is about to change gets the same weight as what is.A finding whose source document changed carries a mark saying so, until a member deals with it.Refinements, 2026-07-28
  9. Trust signals are always visibleWhere something came from and how far it is trusted show without asking.Another group's finding carries its origin mark wherever it appears.Roadmap §12; DEC-92; DEC-96
  10. What you may not see is withheld wholeNo title, no count, no hint of what is hidden; at most, that something was withheld."Part of what this rests on is not yours to see."DEC-36; K903 (4)

4. Weight is felt

The tool fades into the background for ordinary work, and slows a member down, even for a moment, before a heavier act.

  1. The tool fades; the work stays in focusIn your words: the tool should fade into the distance so that users' focus is where it belongs — on the work, while members still appreciate the weight of the work they're doing.Tagging a document is one click, inline, with no dialog.DEC-87
  2. Friction matches weightReversible acts happen inline. Reasoned acts ask for a reason in place. Terminal and attested acts open a full dialog saying what ends and who signs. Irreversible acts only go through the publication ceremony.Declining an option asks for a reason in a field under it; signing a case opens the ceremony.DEC-87; DEC-80
  3. Weight is read before the clickEvery act's button carries its weight's name and mark."Decline · reasoned", with its weight mark, before the member presses it.DEC-87; DEC-82
  4. Consequence in the world raises frictionAn act whose effect cannot be taken back outside the record opens the full dialog stating that effect, whatever its weight.Naming a person in the registry opens a dialog, though the act itself can be undone in the record.DEC-88
  5. Outward acts say so at the actAnything that becomes visible outside the group says so at the moment it is taken."This tells the public, including anyone you are investigating, that your group is working on this."UI-KICKOFF "DR-13, the tell discipline"; Design Requirement 13; DEC-111
  6. Refusals teachA refusal names what is wrong and why, in the member's own terms. Never "something went wrong"."Cannot sign: finding 2 rests on testimony with no independent corroboration."UI-KICKOFF "Refusals teach"; DEC-49

5. Always know where you are

A member always knows whether they are looking at the group's private work, at what the world can see, or at another group's work brought in.

  1. Two spaces, two framesThe working record and the published record each have their own distinct frame. Publishing is the only crossing.The working frame and the published frame differ at a glance: ground, masthead and band.DEC-106; UI-KICKOFF "The privacy fence is architectural"
  2. In-between material is labelledReview copies and outgoing drafts sit in the working frame, with a band saying what they are and who can see them."Shared for review: 2 people outside the group can read this."DEC-106
  3. Every case shows its pathEach document and case carries a small marker of where it stands on the way to publication."working → shared for review → published".DEC-106
  4. Another group's work is marked and read-onlyA case imported from another group arrives in its own read-only project inside the working space. It carries the other group's name and its origin mark wherever it appears, and nothing in it can be edited. Its look always tells confirmed findings from unconfirmed ones: the system recreates each finding from the case file, and a finding that has not been recreated never looks like one that has. Using it in the group's own work is a further act: the group may accept what was recreated.A band reads "Another group's case: West Oakland Neighbors · Edition 2 · read-only · 3 of 4 findings recreated". Each finding carries one of "Not yet checked", "Recreated", "Recreated in part" or "Did not recreate", each with its own mark and words, so the difference shows without colour; an accepted finding adds "Accepted by our group".DEC-112 (import and recreation); DEC-92 (origin mark); DEC-96 (acceptance); DEC-46 (3); Bob, comments on this page, 2026-10-02

6. The machine looks; the member concludes

The assistant does the looking. Every judgement and every act that commits the group is a person's.

  1. The assistant proposes, never concludesIt finds, pursues, extracts and checks. It never concludes, signs, sends, or chooses the question.It suggests three passages that may support a question; a member accepts one.DEC-24; DEC-55; DEC-60
  2. Machine work looks like machine workEverything the machine produced carries one shared treatment, with who asked for it."The assistant did this, at Rosa's request."DEC-90
  3. Docked beside what it servesThe assistant is a panel docked beside the screen it serves, which a member can widen; some of the work always stays visible. It never floats over the work.It docks at the side on wide screens and at the bottom on phones, and shrinks back by itself when a guided step needs the member to press something.DEC-90; DEC-99
  4. A guide points; the member pressesA guided step highlights the real control and never presses it. It may place a labelled draft in a field for the member to edit and adopt; the act itself is always the member's."Press 'Start the action' when you're ready", with the real button outlined and the drafted request, labelled "Draft · edit before sending", already in the field.DEC-90; DEC-120 (G5)
  5. No shortcut past an act's safeguardsAnything the assistant starts still goes through the act's own checks, reason and receipt.An assistant-started records request still opens the outward-act warning.Interaction Constructs §P
  6. Any multi-step journey can be walked as a guided flowA guided flow takes the member through the real screens, one step at a time: it opens each screen, says what to do there and why, checks the step is done, and moves on. It runs from a wizard script for every group, with no AI needed; the assistant can also plan one on the fly. A step may place a labelled draft in a field for the member to adopt; only the member presses the act's button. Abandoning a flow is a non-event."Guided: first records request · step 2 of 6 · Choose the office. Why: the request goes to the office that holds the records." The real form is open beside it.DEC-120 (G4, G5); DEC-27; DEC-90; ASSISTANT-PILOT §3
  7. Guided flows are found where they start, and kept workingWherever flows begin, a standard mark sits in a standard place: hover shows the flows that start there and how often members finish them; a click starts one, or records a new one from there. Members author by recording a walk-through or asking the assistant; the flows the product depends on are required and must always work; a broken optional flow is withdrawn until fixed, never shown broken. A script says what to do, never what to conclude.On a project's page, the mark beside the title shows "2 guided flows start here: Start a records request (finished by 14 members) · Open a first question".DEC-121

7. Alive, not noisy

The screen keeps up with the record, without shouting.

  1. Alive means liveWhen something visible changes, the screen shows it promptly, as long as the update doesn't disrupt what the member is doing.A colleague's new document appears in the open list.Refinements, 2026-07-28
  2. Motion only when action is neededMovement is used sparingly, only when a member needs to act.Nothing animates on load or while idle; movement only draws the eye to something waiting on the member.Refinements, 2026-07-28
  3. Urgency changes the display, not the volumeAs a deadline nears, an item's position, colour or wording may change. That is display, not a notification."Due Friday" moves up the queue and takes the attention colour.DEC-94 (2)

8. For everyone

A first-time volunteer, a screen-reader user, a reporter on a phone and an official reading a printout all get the whole story.

  1. Accessible to the international standardEvery member screen and every published form (page, print and file) meets WCAG 2.2 at level AA, checked as each screen is accepted.Every chart and badge has a text equivalent a screen reader can speak.DEC-99
  2. Colour is never the only signalEvery colour has a word, icon or texture beside it.Each grade scale has its own icon (document, link, speech, person); each stage bar its name.DEC-82; DEC-79; DEC-99
  3. Every hover also opens on focus or tapAnything explained on mouse-over is also reachable by keyboard and by touch.Tapping a grade badge on a phone opens the same explanation a hover shows.DEC-82; DEC-99
  4. Usable in one sessionSomeone with no civic experience can understand and use it within one session.A newcomer meets only two things first, strength against the bar and "Undetermined"; everything else is explained where it is used.Design Requirement 11; DEC-82; DEC-91 (onboarding waits for the new design)
  5. Words in one place, English firstThe interface's words live in one place so other languages can be added. A translated case is never presented as the signed case.A Spanish translation of a case says "Unofficial translation" and links the signed original.DEC-99
  6. Every device, honestlyPhones, tablets, laptops and desktops each have their own possibilities and limits; a feature may be absent where it does not fit. On a phone a member reads everything and does everyday acts, including capturing a document; signing, publishing and sending happen on a larger screen for now, and the phone says where to finish.At a council meeting a member photographs a handout and captures it from their phone; that evening they sign the case at their laptop.Refinements, 2026-07-28; DEC-122 (G1)
  7. The public reads least, so explain mostLayered explanation matters most on the published case. The complete edition carries every layer, so anyone can review or recreate the case without CivicOS.Each finding opens "Relied on · meets this project's bar (capture B, connection C)".Refinements, 2026-07-28; DEC-112
  8. Light and darkMember screens come in light and dark, following the device's setting, with a choice in settings. The public case follows the reader's setting on screen; print and the complete edition are always light.A member working at 10pm sees the dark appearance; the printed case is always on white.DEC-122 (G2)

9. One product, many groups

Every group runs its own copy. The product looks and behaves the same for all of them, and belongs to none.

  1. One design language, one source of meaningOne visual language throughout. What each state means is defined once, so it looks and reads the same wherever it appears."Overdue" looks identical on the queue, the action page and the plan.Bob's UX principles, 2026-07-28; refinements (the semantics table)
  2. No city in the productOutward text names the group and CivicOS. Local facts (holidays, offices, laws) come from the group's own profile.A deadline counts Oakland's holidays because the group's profile lists them, not because the product knows Oakland.K1 ("No jurisdiction in the product")
  3. The group leadsOn every public face the group is the publisher and signer. CivicOS is credited quietly. Believe in Cities appears only as links."Lakeshore Tenants · The Coliseum lease · Edition 2" … "Made with CivicOS".DEC-118
  4. The same for one group or a thousandNothing in the design assumes a single group or a central host.A group alone and a group in a busy network see the same screens.Roadmap §12; Design Requirements 1, 14
  5. Built to evolveThe workflow will change as experience and requirements grow; the design leaves room for it.New act kinds take their weight and look from the shared rules, without new screens.Bob's UX principles, 2026-07-28
  6. Nothing loaded from outside the group's copyEverything the interface needs (typefaces, icons, scripts) ships inside the group's own copy. No outside fonts, analytics or trackers, on member screens or the public case.Opening a screen tells no outside company which group is working, from where, or when.DEC-122 (G3); DEC-31; Design Requirements 9, 14
  7. Your standardClean, self-evident, functional and consistent with the brand. Extra effort and outside-the-box thinking on every call; minimal-effort solutions are unacceptable.Every screen in steps 2 to 5 is checked against this page before it comes to you.UI-KICKOFF "Bob's UX standard"

Gaps, now ruled

All five gaps are ruled. Nothing on this page waits on you except your overall approval and any principle you want changed.

G1. What a phone can do: A

A phone reads everything and does everyday acts, including capturing a document. Signing, publishing and sending happen on a larger screen for now, and the phone says where to finish. (3 October; now principle 8.6)

G2. Light and dark: yes

Member screens in both, following the device; print and the complete edition always light. (3 October; now principle 8.8)

G3. Nothing loaded from outside the group's copy: yes

Typefaces, icons and scripts ship inside the group's copy; no outside fonts, analytics or trackers. (3 October; now principle 9.6)

G4. Guided flows without the AI: yes

Wizard scripts run for every group with no AI and no key; the assistant adds free-form planning where a key is set. (2 October; principle 6.6)

G5. Labelled drafts in fields: yes

A step may place a labelled draft in a field for the member to edit and adopt; only the member presses the act's button. (2 October; principles 6.4, 6.6)
