S5. A group whose place Civicsmith doesn't know yet

What you are asked: may a group outside the places Civicsmith ships rules for add its own place's rules?

Why it is yours: it is something Civicsmith would newly do, and it decides who may write the rules a group's due dates rest on.

How it works today

At setup a group chooses its places from a list. Each place is a rule set researched with its sources: records and open-meeting laws and their deadlines, holidays, office hours and time zone, the offices, and where their records are published. Today the list holds one place: Oakland, with Alameda County and California. A group may choose none, and everything else works, but no due date is worked out from law and offices are added by hand. A place can't be typed in, because a name alone carries none of its rules.

An example

A tenants' group in Fresno installs Civicsmith. Fresno isn't listed, so it chooses “None of these”. When it requests records from the Fresno city clerk, Civicsmith can't say the reply is due in 10 days, or skip the city's holidays. A member types the due date in themselves, and it is marked as the group's own date, not the law's.

The choices

A. Only the places Civicsmith shipsA group elsewhere chooses none until its place arrives in a release.Gains: every rule comes from research with its source.Costs: groups outside Oakland get no help with deadlines, perhaps for a long time.
B. A group may write its own place's rulesInside its own Civicsmith, an administrator records the laws, deadlines, holidays and offices, each with its source. These rules are marked as the group's own research, never as Civicsmith's, and they serve only that group. They can later be offered for a release.Gains: any place can start now.Costs: a new editor for rules, and a group's mistakes in its rules move its due dates. A new kind of origin to show (“the group's own rules”).
C. A group asks, and the place joins a releaseThe setup screen has “Ask for your place”, which tells the people who make Civicsmith which place is wanted and by how many groups. Until it arrives, the group runs with none.Gains: research stays in one place; demand decides what is researched next.Costs: it sends something outside the group (which place it works in), so it is an outward act; slow for the asking group.

Recommendation: A for this release, with B as the next requirement once a group outside Oakland asks. The design already works with no place, and it says so honestly. B is the lasting answer, but it should be built for a real group's place rather than guessed. Risk: early groups elsewhere meet a thinner Civicsmith. The setup screen says so, and nothing pretends.
