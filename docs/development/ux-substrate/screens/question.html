S7. Who may translate the interface, what stops abuse, and local words

What you are asked: the limits and process for a group translating Civicsmith's words, now built around your suggestion (6 October) that the assistant does the first cut, and how local words are handled.

Why it is yours: it decides who may change what other members read, which is policy.

How it works today (DEC-127)

Administrators, and members an administrator gives the grant to, translate. The assistant can already draft missing words as labelled drafts, and a member who knows the language checks and adopts each one. An adopted word shows at once to every member reading that language. One person can therefore change what protects other members, such as the warning before an outward act, or "Undetermined", by mistake or on purpose.

Your suggestion: the assistant does the first cut

Where the person translating can reach the assistant, it drafts every missing word, and the speaker's task is to find its errors. That also answers the abuse question for small groups. The assistant's draft becomes the second opinion, so a second human is needed only where a speaker changes a protected word away from it.

Recommended process

  1. The grant. An administrator gives it to named members, for a named language.
  2. First cut. The assistant drafts every missing word, each labelled “Draft · the assistant's”. Without the assistant, the granted member types them.
  3. Review. A granted member who speaks the language reads each draft against the English and keeps it or corrects it.
  4. Ordinary words show once kept.
  5. Protected words: the fixed terms, the weights, every warning and dialog before an outward, signed or irreversible act, the court notice, and every "who can see this" notice.
    • Kept as the assistant drafted it: shows once one speaker keeps it.
    • Changed from the assistant's draft, or typed without one: shows only after a second granted speaker confirms it, or an administrator confirms it after reading the assistant's translation of it back into English. Until then, members see the English.
  6. Records. Every adopted word records who adopted it, when and what it replaced. An administrator can undo it in one act. The English is one hover away for every word, and any member can mark a word "this translation looks wrong".

Local words

Local words are the names of offices, laws, programs and places ("City Clerk", "Immediate Disclosure Request", "California Public Records Act", "Fruitvale"), and the group's own names for its projects and neighbourhoods. Translating them like ordinary words breaks things: a member can't find the office, and a request names a law that doesn't exist.

  1. Official names stay as they are. A name an office or law uses is shown in the original, with a short explanation in the member's language beside it on first use and on hover. For example: “City Clerk · la oficina que guarda los registros de la ciudad”.
  2. Official translations. Where the place publishes its own official translation of a name, Civicsmith uses it and shows its source. These are held with the place's researched rules and arrive with releases, like the rules themselves.
  3. What goes to an office (a records request, a letter, a filing) goes in the office's own language. The member sees a translation beside it, labelled as such, to check what is being sent.
  4. The group's own local words (its projects, its neighbourhood names) are the group's to translate, as ordinary words.

The choices

A. As ruled (DEC-127)The grant, one person adopts; local words handled like any other word.
B. The process and the local-words rules aboveGains: the assistant does most of the work; no one person can change what protects members; nothing is lost for good; offices and laws keep their real names.Costs: a group with one speaker and no assistant sees protected words in English until a second speaker or the assistant is available.
C. Administrators onlyCosts: a group whose administrators don't speak the language can't translate.

Recommendation: B. Risk: the assistant's back-translation can itself be wrong. That is why a second speaker is preferred, and why the record and one-act undo stay in place.

Reply, for example: “S7: B” · or a change.
