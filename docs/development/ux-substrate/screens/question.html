S7. Who may translate the interface, and what stops abuse

What you are asked: the limits and process for a group translating Civicsmith's words for its members.

Why it is yours: it decides who may change what other members read, which is policy.

How it works today (your ruling of 4 October, DEC-127)

Not every member can translate. Administrators can, and so can members an administrator gives the grant to. Missing words are typed in, or drafted by the assistant as labelled drafts. Then a member who knows the language checks and adopts each word, and an adopted word shows at once to every member reading that language. Signed and published records are never translated in place; a translation of a case is labelled unofficial and links the signed original. Fixed terms (the grades, "Undetermined", the acts' names) each carry a note on what they mean.

Where it could be abused

One person with the grant can change what every member reading that language sees, and those members may not be able to check it against the English. Take a member who translates the warning before an outward act ("this tells the office what you are looking at") as something harmless, or "Undetermined" as "Proven". Spanish-reading members are then misled about exactly the things that protect them. A mistake can do the same damage as malice.

The choices

A. As ruledThe grant, and one person adopts.Costs: one person, by mistake or on purpose, can change what protects other members.
B. A second person for the words that protect members, and everything undoable (1) An administrator gives the grant to named members, for a named language.
(2) Ordinary words show once one granted member adopts them.
(3) Protected words show only after a second granted member confirms them; until then members see the English. Protected words are the fixed terms, the weights and every warning and dialog before an outward, signed or irreversible act, the court notice, and every "who can see this" notice.
(4) Every adopted word records who adopted it, when, and what it replaced. An administrator can undo it in one act.
(5) For every translated word, the English is one hover away.
(6) Any member can mark a word "this translation looks wrong". It is listed for the granted members and the administrators.Gains: no one person can change what protects members, and nothing is lost for good.Costs: a group with only one speaker of a language sees protected words in English until a second speaker joins.
C. Administrators onlyCosts: a group whose administrators don't speak the language can't translate at all.

Recommendation: B. Risk: slower for small groups. The English fallback is never wrong, only less welcoming.
