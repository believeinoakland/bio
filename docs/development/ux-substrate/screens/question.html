S8. Information levels: how much explaining a member sees

What you are asked: whether explanations come in levels a member can choose, and how many levels there are.

Why it is yours: it decides how Civicsmith teaches, which is UX, and it adds a member setting.

How it works today

Every mark explains itself, and about 425 names carry a card (what it is, and why it matters here). New today, at your suggestion: each screen's heading says what the screen is and what you can do there, and each entry in the rail says what it holds. Nothing shows until the pointer rests for half a second, so explanations cost nothing visually. But an experienced member resting on a heading or the rail would still get guidance they no longer need.

An example

On her first day, Ana rests on "Your queue" and learns what a queue is and its three kinds. A month later she rests there only while thinking, and the same paragraph appears every time.

The choices

A. No levelsEverything always explains itself.Costs: guidance keeps appearing for members who no longer need it.
B. Two levels Marks and names, always on: every mark's meaning and every reference's card.
Guidance: adds what each screen, section and rail entry is and what you can do there.
A new member starts with guidance on. After their first month, Civicsmith asks once whether to keep it.
To switch: a small control in the masthead showing the current level (one click), Alt+Shift+I, or Settings › Your account.Gains: newcomers are taught, experienced members aren't interrupted, and either can switch in a moment.Costs: one more setting; every piece of guidance must be written and translated.
C. Three levelsAdds Teach me: explanations shown in place, without hovering, for members who want them in view (on a phone, or while learning).Costs: screens lose density at that level; more to write and keep accurate.

Recommendation: B, with C added later if newcomers ask for it. You can try B now: the viewer's toolbar switches between "Marks and names" and "With guidance", and Alt+Shift+I does the same. Risk: a member who switches guidance off may miss a new screen's explanation; the switch is always one click away.

Reply, for example: “S8: B” · “S8: C” · or a change.

S7. Who may translate, what stops abuse, and local words: as described, recorded

Your answer, 6 October: “S7: as you've just described”, after your suggestion that the assistant does the first cut. Recorded as DEC-157.

  • An administrator gives the grant to named members, for a named language.
  • The assistant drafts and a speaker finds its errors.
  • Ordinary words show once kept.
  • A protected word kept as the assistant drafted it shows once kept. Changed, or typed without a draft, it waits for a second granted speaker, or an administrator reading the assistant's back-translation. Until then members see the English.
  • Every change is recorded and can be undone in one act. The English is one hover away, and any member can mark a word as wrong.
  • Official names of offices, laws, programs and places stay as they are, with an explanation beside them. An official translation is used with its source.
  • What goes to an office goes in the office's language, with a labelled translation for the member.
  • The group's own local words are its to translate.

Drawn in the Translations screen and its wizard.
