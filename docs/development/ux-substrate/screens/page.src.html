Folded in, not separate audiences:

  • Residents addressed by an action: the people an awareness action speaks to; they meet the team's words, not its screens.

Newsrooms, a core audience (your comment, October 5). Journalists are not only outsiders who receive a case: a newsroom may run its own Civicsmith. What that means for the journeys:

  • Its cases back its own stories: the editor is the project owner who signs (journey 15), and legal review before publishing is a review copy for the newsroom's lawyer (journey 21).
  • Protecting sources matters most here (journey 20), and its administrator decides what members are told about court orders, since newsrooms may rely on protections such as shield laws (DEC-136).
  • It works to deadlines across many documents, so the wide path's eighth rule, fast for those who know, is written for it.
  • Newsrooms collaborate on stories: exchanging cases and sharing packs (journey 25); sharing packs waits until a real team asks, and a newsroom is the likely first.
  • A case can be signed now and published at a set time, such as the moment its story runs (DEC-147; journey 15).

Across every ring: members who read in another language, screen-reader and keyboard users, and people working from a phone. These are not separate audiences; every journey must work for them.

The wide path

Your words, October 3: the path to success must be "wide enough and inclusive enough for all audiences". Eight rules every journey and screen is checked against, as you approved (DEC-129).

  1. A front door in every journeyEach journey has a first step a newcomer can take alone, with no prior knowledge, and the screen shows where it starts.
  2. Wizards on the main roads, never gatesThe main journeys can be walked with a wizard. Nothing is locked behind one, and anyone can leave it at any step: stopping is a non-event.
  3. You can always see where you areEvery journey shows its stage and what comes next, so a newcomer never wonders whether they are done.
  4. Explained where it appears, and findable againA term is explained the first time a member meets it, at that spot, in plain words. After that it is never pushed again, but the explanation is always one hover, focus or tap away on the term itself, and in one list of every term, for when a member forgets.
  5. Heavier steps slow you downFriction rises with an act's weight, so no one stumbles into anything permanent, signed or outward.
  6. Any order, and pick up where you left offSteps can be taken in whatever order the work allows; only a step that truly needs another first waits for it. A member can leave a journey for other work at any point, and come back at any time to find it exactly as they left it, with their unfinished work listed where they will see it.
  7. Many front doorsA member can start from whatever drew them in: most often a problem they live with, or else a code section, a court case, a news story, a budget, a meeting, something they experienced, a tip. Civicsmith offers a sensible first step from each, and no journey assumes everyone starts from a question.
  8. Fast for those who knowProfessionals and experienced members move quickly: search, keyboard, acting on many items at once, no repeated teaching.

Where members start

Most people come to this work because something in their own lives isn't working: the street outside, the block they live on, the bill they pay, the services they were promised. Others come through something they read: a code chapter, a court case, a news story. Each can be a member's first motivating interest, and each needs a sensible first step. No journey should assume everyone starts from a question.

A problem they live with

The most common way in. At the city auditor's open forum you attended (October 4), these were the reasons people gave most often for coming. Each is a frustration; Civicsmith's job is to help turn it into a question about the city's own standards that the record can answer.

What this means for the design:

  • Frustration is welcome and the record stays disciplined: a member's own words can say how angry they are; the question, the evidence and the case say what the documents show.
  • Civicsmith holds the city to its own laws, standards and statements. It takes no side on what policy should be, including on a ballot measure, while still checking the city's claims about one.
  • Every kind of document is captured, pointed at and cited the same way: a code section, a court filing, an article, a budget, minutes, a contract, a dataset.
  • Home asks "What happened?" and offers "Start from…", with a problem the member lives with first and these after; none is the "right" way in.
  • The assistant can suggest first questions from whatever the member starts with, such as one question per requirement in a code section, each labeled as a suggestion, for a member who has connected their own Claude account and switched suggestions on.
  • Professionals bring their own angles: lawyers start from filings, accountants from financial reports, policy analysts from codes and rates. Their journey (16) routes questions to them.

2. The assistant panel

Drawn early, because every other screen leaves room for it. It docks beside the screen it serves, on the member's own Claude account or the team's API key where the team offers one, labeled as machine work in everything it produces, with the member's daily limit always in view. For a member reached by no account (none of their own, and no team key), it does not exist: no button, no empty panel, no reminder. Open any screen with “With the assistant” to see it; the question screen below opens with it.

Everything else a member does is the same either way. Where the assistant would have helped, the member's own way takes its place: “Ask” becomes “Find and count”, drafts come from the team's own templates, and standing questions still run, their new matches arriving as a list. Section 4 lists all six rules, and every journey in section 3 can be walked both ways with the switch at the top.

With the assistant

Docked right on a desktop, as a sheet on a phone. It answers only from what the team holds and says where it looked; about laws it gives legal information, never advice. Its drafts go into fields, labeled, until the member makes them theirs.

Without it

“Ask” becomes “Find and count”: search, and counts that show their method. Drafts come from the team's own templates. Standing questions still run; their new matches arrive as a list.

Help with writing (your rulings, DEC-153 and, through the development process, K1841). Wherever a member describes something in their own words (a note, an observation, a request, a case's scope or what changed), “Help me write this” sits under the field when the assistant is reachable. Never in a field giving a member's reason for an act: a reason is always the member's own. With the member's suggestions switched off it works only from what they typed; with them on it may also draw on what the team holds. It never adds a fact. For an observation it only helps word what the member saw. Its words arrive in the field labeled “Draft · the assistant's, asked by <handle>”, and nothing is saved until the member edits and keeps them. It is never offered where the assistant is refused: vouching, concluding, signing, publishing, an irreversible act. Without the assistant, nothing appears.

Making content, and finding it in what you hold

Your question of October 6: where does a member create content, or ask for content to be found in a document, a set of documents or a project? Creating stays where each kind of thing lives, always from a passage the team holds. Finding now has one control, “Find in this”, the same on a document, on the documents a member is holding together, and on a project (DEC-164).

Where a member creates content

  • Add: a document from an address or a file, a photo, or what they saw themselves.
  • Document: cite a passage.
  • Person: add a fact from a document. Timeline: record an event or a dated fact. Money trail: read a figure into a money fact.
  • Standard: hold a requirement as a standard. Proceeding: hold an order as obligations. Calculation: work out a figure, its method shown.
  • Questions, hunches, notes: in a project and in Your notes.

Where a member asks for content to be found

  • Find in this (new): pick what to look for (people and offices the team follows, money figures, dates and deadlines, requirements, events in minutes and agendas, a name or term). Every result shows its passage, labeled “Found by search”. A result becomes a fact only when a member records it, with the act that already makes that kind of fact, from that passage.
  • Find: search everything, hold a set together, see where nobody looked.
  • Ask (with the assistant) or Find and count (without).
  • Keep finding this: new matches arrive in the queue as a list.

What search can and cannot find

It matches words as written, figures, dates, the words of a requirement (“shall”, “must”, “within”) and the names of people and offices the team already follows. A table’s dates and amounts are counted through a calculation, never recorded one by one. A name the team doesn’t follow yet is found only as a word; the member adds the person from the passage.

The assistant’s proposals: later

Once measured, and only at a member’s request for a question they name, the assistant will also propose people, events and money figures that search can’t match. Each is labeled machine work, graded no higher than its method earns, and becomes a fact only by the same acts, by a member. Drawn on the screen, marked “Not in the first release”.

4. Civicsmith without the assistant

Your rulings of October 5 and 6: a team may run without the assistant, and a member reached by no account (none of their own, and no team key) uses Civicsmith without it. These are the rules every screen follows for that member, and they are drawn in every journey above.

Absent, not grayed

No assistant button, panel, tray or “suggested” section appears, and nothing invites them to connect again. Their own settings say “not connected” in one line, where they can change it.

Ask becomes find and count

The Ask screen is a search with counts that show their method. Everything the assistant would cite, the member can find and cite themselves.

Drafts come from the team's templates

A records request, a communication or a filing starts from the team's own template, labeled as a draft, exactly where the assistant's draft would go. Translations are typed by members.

Standing questions still run

The search re-runs on schedule and new matches reach the queue once, as a list, unread by any machine. (A member with the assistant gets them read and summarized.)

Wizards are the same

Every wizard runs from its script with no account. Only the assistant planning a new wizard on the fly needs one.

What they cannot do

Ask in plain words; receive suggestions; have documents read and summarized; have translations drafted; have “what changed” drafted. Each of these has a member's own way to do the same work.
