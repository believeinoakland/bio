1. Who we design for

The original brief named five audiences in order of how often they use the product; the requirements gathered twenty. Here they are reconciled into three rings. The newcomer member stays the centre of gravity.

Inside the group: members

Everyone who belongs to the group and works in its copy. They hold different roles at different moments; one person may be several of these.

Newcomer memberA community activist or volunteer, not a technologist, often with no civic-records experience. The interface is built around them.UI-KICKOFF audience 1; ux-experience audiences
Experienced investigatorWorks questions, cites evidence, runs checks and prepares cases; includes the member who vouches for a document at the release moment.UI-KICKOFF audience 2
Professional memberA journalist, lawyer, accountant, auditor or engineer who joins a group. Declares expertise so the group knows whom to ask; an administrator may confirm it, which gates nothing.Bob's comment on the principles page, 2 October; Membership v2 §1.3
Project ownerRuns a project: its objective, its bar, its participants, and publishing its cases.UI-KICKOFF audience 3
AdministratorKeeps the roster, capabilities, keys and the group's identity. The person who installs the copy and the founder who claims it are administrators at their first moment.UI-KICKOFF audience 4; ux-experience: operator, founder, administrator
The group's future membersWhoever picks up this record years later. Dead ends and reasons are kept for them.ux-experience audiences

Invited in

Outside reviewerReads a review copy of a case before it is published, comments, and acknowledges what it leaves out.Publication §6A

People who meet the group's work without an account.

Someone handing over materialAnyone, possibly a whistleblower, who gives the group documents through its doorbell, with no account.Intake Doctrine §2a

JournalistReceives, checks and cites the group's cases.Case Making audience table
Partner groupAnother group, often running its own copy. Work flows both ways: this group imports and builds on its cases, and it imports and builds on this group's.Roadmap §11; DEC-112; Bob's comment on this page, 4 October

Oversight bodyA grand jury, controller or auditor receiving referrals.Design Requirement 8

Folded in, not separate audiences:

  • Group operator, founder: moments of the administrator's role, kept as steps in journey 1.
  • Project participant, member outside a project: states any member can be in, not separate audiences.
  • Residents addressed by an action: the people an awareness action speaks to; they meet the group's words, not its screens.
  • The assistant: an actor that works for members, not an audience; it appears in the journeys where it acts.

Across every ring: members who read in another language, screen-reader and keyboard users, and people working from a phone. These are not separate audiences; every journey must work for them.

2. The wide path

Your words, 3 October: the path to success must be "wide enough and inclusive enough for all audiences". Proposed as seven rules every journey is checked against.

  1. A front door in every journeyEach journey has a first step a newcomer can take alone, with no prior knowledge, and the screen shows where it starts.
  2. Wizards on the main roads, never gatesThe main journeys can be walked with a wizard. Nothing is locked behind one, and anyone can leave it at any step: stopping is a non-event.
  3. You can always see where you areEvery journey shows its stage and what comes next, so a newcomer never wonders whether they are done.
  4. Explained where it appears, and findable againA term is explained the first time a member meets it, at that spot, in plain words. After that it is never pushed again, but the explanation is always one hover, focus or tap away on the term itself, and in one list of every term, for when a member forgets.
  5. Heavier steps slow you downFriction rises with an act's weight, so no one stumbles into anything permanent, signed or outward.
  6. Any order, and pick up where you left offSteps can be taken in whatever order the work allows; only a step that truly needs another first waits for it. A member can leave a journey for other work at any point, and come back at any time to find it exactly as they left it, with their unfinished work listed where they will see it.
  7. Fast for those who knowProfessionals and experienced members move quickly: search, keyboard, acting on many items at once, no repeated teaching.

3. Fourteen journeys

Each card says who takes the journey, its newcomer's front door, its wizard, and what changed from the list gathered earlier. Open "Steps" for the path: steps are listed in their usual order, but members may take them in any order the work allows, leave, and come back later. Marked steps are the heavy ones: permanent, signed, outward (the world sees it), reasoned, terminal or irreversible.

4. Wizards

Outlines only: what each wizard covers, where it starts, and its steps. Each step says what to do and why; a wizard points at the real control and never presses it; what it puts in a field is a labelled draft until the member keeps it; stopping is fine. The three required wizards must always work, or the release is held.

Required

Set up and claim

Starts at: The installer page, then the setup page
  1. Before you start: a cloud account with a payment method, about twenty minutes.
  2. Choose the short name. Why: it appears in addresses and signatures, and can never change; a group that wants to stay unnamed picks one that reveals nothing.
  3. Install and let it test itself.
  4. Claim it with the one-time password; choose your password.
  5. Name the group, add a logo if you have one, verify your web address, choose your places and languages.
  6. Add a second administrator and record who holds the hosting account.
Required · outline only for now

Welcome a new member

Starts at: The invitation link
  1. Choose a handle. Why: the record shows it on your work. It needn't be your legal name. A name people know lends your work credibility and lets reporters reach you, but puts it on public cases; a pen name shields you from pressure, and your administrators still know who you are. You can also choose, act by act, to be credited only as the group.
  2. Choose your language.
  3. This is your group's home: what it is working on, and what is waiting on you.
  4. Two things to know first: a question's strength against its project's bar, and "Undetermined".
  5. Your first act: join a project, capture a document, or record what you saw.
Required

Publication ceremony

Starts at: A case ready to publish
  1. What you are publishing: the case, its edition, its scope.
  2. Check: each finding's strength against the bar; anything still open.
  3. What the case leaves out, and who acknowledged it.
  4. The group's declared bias.
  5. Preview the public page, as a stranger will see it.
  6. Sign with your key. Why: publishing is permanent; corrections come as a new edition.
Optional · offer first

First records request

Starts at: Any office, or a question that needs records
  1. Which office holds the records.
  2. What records, in plain words.
  3. The law it goes under, from your group's profile.
  4. Your draft, labelled as a draft: edit it until it is yours.
  5. Send it. Why the warning: this tells the office what you are looking at.
  6. The due date is set and shows in your queue.
Optional · offer first

Your first question

Starts at: A project page
  1. What do you want to know? Say it as a question.
  2. Find or capture a document that bears on it.
  3. Point at the passage that matters.
  4. Say whether it supports the question or cuts against it.
  5. See the question's strength against the project's bar, and what would raise it.
Optional

Invite a member

Starts at: The roster
  1. Who: their handle, and the cover the group knows them by (not a legal name).
  2. What they may do: contribute, by default.
  3. Send the link; it waits for them.
Optional

Use another group's case

Starts at: A link to a published case, or the directory
  1. Import the case into a read-only project.
  2. Let Civicsmith recreate each finding.
  3. Accept what recreated, with a reason.
  4. Watch the publisher's docket for new editions.
Optional

Translate the interface

Starts at: Settings, for administrators or members given the grant
  1. Choose the language.
  2. See the words still untranslated.
  3. Ask the assistant to draft them; each is marked "Draft".
  4. Check and adopt each one, or type it yourself.
Optional

Start and send

Starts at: An option on a plan
  1. The option you chose and why.
  2. Prepare what is sent; the assistant may draft it.
  3. Approve it and send it, after the outward-act warning.
  4. The clock and the next checkpoint go into the queue.
