1. Who speaks

Three names appear in front of people, and two voices. Keeping them apart is what lets a reader trust what they are reading.

  1. The group leadsEverything public is the group's: it publishes, it signs, it is accountable. Its name heads every public page and every member's workspace.The example below: the group's name heads the page, and CivicOS is a small credit at its foot.DEC-118
  2. CivicOS is the tool, credited quietlyCivicOS appears as a small credit, never as the publisher, and never speaks for the group."Made with CivicOS" at the foot of the public page, in the complete edition and in the case file.DEC-118; K1
  3. Believe in Cities is the networkThe network has its own sites. Inside CivicOS it appears only as links to its directory and forum, and in the "working on" notices sent there."Find related work in the network directory ↗".DEC-111; DEC-118
  4. Two voicesThe group's voice is the words the group writes: its cases, findings, notices and docket entries, in its own "we". CivicOS's voice is the interface's words: labels, explanations, refusals and the assistant. CivicOS never says "we" on the group's behalf, and the group's words are never rewritten by CivicOS.A finding reads "We found that…" because a member wrote it; the line above it, "Relied on · meets this project's bar", is CivicOS's.Principle 1.6; Roadmap §4–§5 (values in "our" voice, principles in "we" voice)

2. How the names are written

  1. CivicOSOne word, capital C, O and S. Never "Civic OS", "CIVICOS" or "the CivicOS". It is software, not an organisation: "made with CivicOS", never "CivicOS says" or "CivicOS found"."Your group's copy of CivicOS".Old interface wordmark; K1; DEC-118
  2. Your group's copyWhat the code calls an instance is, for members, "your group's copy of CivicOS", or just "your group's copy". Never "instance", "plane" or "server"."Who controls this copy" (the administrators' card).DEC-109; principle 2.4
  3. The group's two namesThe group's display name leads wherever people read; its short name (the slug) appears where an address or a signature is shown. A group that wants to stay unnamed chooses an unrevealing short name at install."Lakeshore Tenants" in the header; "lakeshore-tenants" beside the signature and in the address.Publication §7 (public identity)
  4. No city in the productCivicOS's own words never name a city, county or state. Places come from the group's profile and the group's own writing."Counting your profile's holidays", never "Counting Oakland holidays".K1

3. CivicOS's voice

Seven traits, each from a value or principle you have already set. Together they describe a calm, exact colleague who respects the reader's time and judgement.

  1. PlainEveryday words and short sentences, naming things as members know them. A term of art is used only when it is the right word, and then explained once, where it appears.Publishing makes this edition permanent and public. not Commit will finalize the immutable artifact.Design Requirement 11; principles 2.4, 8.4
  2. ExactDates, numbers, names and sources, not adjectives. "Show your work. Always."Waiting on the City Clerk's reply, due 14 October. not Response pending soon.Operational principle 2; DEC-98
  3. Calm, especially under pressureWeight is carried by clarity, never by alarm. No exclamation marks, no capitals for emphasis, no red-alert words. You chose "pressure" over "attacks" for the same reason.This reply is marked as a legal threat. Record whether a litigation hold is in place. not WARNING: You may be sued!Operational principle 8 and its wording note; principle 7.2
  4. Honest about limitsSays what is not known, and why, in the five fixed terms. Never guesses past a gap or softens it.Undetermined, because the page could not be read as text.Principles 3.1–3.3
  5. RespectfulInforms once and trusts the member. No praise, no scolding, no "Are you sure?": instead, it states what the act does, and lets the member decide.Declining this option records your reason with it. You can reconsider it later. not Are you sure? Great job!DEC-69; principles 1.1, 4.2
  6. Neutral on policyCivicOS's words take no position on what policy should be and never characterise a person or office. Words like "breach" appear only where a member's determination put them.Noncompliant with the two-thirds requirement, as determined by Rosa on 3 March. never The city illegally diverted funds.Operational principle 1; Action §1 rule 3
  7. Disciplined output, human membersMembers may be angry, and their own notes may say so. What CivicOS writes, and what it helps the group publish, stays disciplined.A member's private note can say "this is outrageous"; the drafted finding the assistant offers says what the documents show.Roadmap §5 ("civic OS OUTPUT must be disciplined, but humans can be human")

5. Words

The words members see, held in one place (principle 8.5), with the ones CivicOS avoids. Terms you have ruled are fixed.

Use

  • group, member, administrator, owner
  • project, question, finding, case, edition
  • document, capture, record (not "bundle")
  • matter (on a plan), action, determination
  • To do, Noticed, Signal (the queue)
  • Undetermined, Withheld, Unrated, Nobody looked, Refused
  • wizard, wizard script, the assistant
  • your group's copy (of CivicOS)
  • Stated, Reported, Proven ("working on" notices)

Avoid

  • "user", "content", "data" for people and their work
  • "instance", "plane", "op", "token" in member text
  • "claim" on public pages
  • "Submit": name the act ("Send", "Publish", "Record")
  • "Oops", "Something went wrong", "Are you sure?"
  • "AI" as a label: say "the assistant", or "machine work"
  • Characterising words ("corrupt", "illegal", "fraud") in CivicOS's own text
  • Exclamation marks and capitals for emphasis

Addressing people

  • The member is "you"; the group is "your group".
  • Other members by their handle, as the record shows them.
  • Offices by their role ("the City Clerk"), never a private person.
  • Dates written out: "14 October 2026"; times with their zone.

Questions for you

Four choices no ruling covers yet.

V1. Does CivicOS have a symbol, or only a wordmark?

The credit line, the browser tab and the app icon on a phone all need something. The old interface used the typed name with "OS" in the signature colour.

A. Wordmark only (recommended)CivicOS is always the typed name; the tab and app icon use a simple initial derived from it. Quiet, consistent with "credited quietly", nothing to explain.
B. A small symbolA simple mark beside the name, designed in step 4. More recognisable on a phone's home screen; one more thing to carry meaning.

Answer: "V1: A" or "V1: B".

V2. May a group show its own logo?

Today a group's public identity is its name, short name and verified web address. Many groups have a logo they'd want on their cases.

Recommendation: yes, optional. An administrator may add the group's logo; it appears beside the group's name on its public face and workspace, stored inside the group's copy (nothing loaded from outside). The name always appears in words too, so the logo is never the only signal. Never required.

Answer: "V2: yes" or "V2: no".

V3. Does the assistant say "I"?

Your ruling labels its work "the assistant did this, at Rosa's request". In conversation it needs a way to speak.

Recommendation: yes, in conversation only. It says "I" in its replies ("I found three passages…"), and appears as "the assistant" in every label and record. It never says "we", which is the group's word.

Answer: "V3: yes" or "V3: never 'I'".

V4. Who may change the interface's words?

The words live in one place (principle 8.5), so they can be translated. A group might also want its own terms (a tenants' group calling a project a "campaign").

A. CivicOS's words only (recommended for now)Every group sees the same words; translations come with releases. Fixed terms keep cases readable across groups.
B. Groups may rename some labelsFriendlier locally, but the same thing reads differently from group to group, and imported cases get confusing.

Answer: "V4: A" or "V4: B".
