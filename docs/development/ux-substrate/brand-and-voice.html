1. Who speaks

Three names appear in front of people, and two voices. Keeping them apart is what lets a reader trust what they are reading.

  1. The group leadsEverything public is the group's: it publishes, it signs, it is accountable. Its name heads every public page and every member's workspace.The example below: the group's name heads the page, and Civicsmith is a small credit at its foot.DEC-118
  2. Civicsmith is the tool, credited quietlyCivicsmith appears as a small credit, never as the publisher, and never speaks for the group."Made with Civicsmith" at the foot of the public page, in the complete edition and in the case file.DEC-118; K1
  3. Believe in Cities is the networkThe network has its own sites. Inside Civicsmith it appears only as links to its directory and forum, and in the "working on" notices sent there."Find related work in the network directory ↗".DEC-111; DEC-118
  4. Two voicesThe group's voice is the words the group writes: its cases, findings, notices and docket entries, in its own "we". Civicsmith's voice is the interface's words: labels, explanations, refusals and the assistant. Civicsmith never says "we" on the group's behalf, and the group's words are never rewritten by Civicsmith.A finding reads "We found that…" because a member wrote it; the line above it, "Relied on · meets this project's bar", is Civicsmith's.Principle 1.6; Roadmap §4–§5 (values in "our" voice, principles in "we" voice)

2. How the names are written

  1. CivicsmithOne word, only its first letter capital. Never "CivicSmith", "Civic Smith", "CIVICSMITH" or "the Civicsmith". In lower case only in addresses and identifiers (civicsmith.org). It is software, never an organisation, a firm or a person: "made with Civicsmith", never "Civicsmith says" or "Civicsmith found"."Your group's copy of Civicsmith". The credit "Made with Civicsmith" links to a short page saying Civicsmith is free software for civic groups, so no reader takes it for a firm or a co-author.DEC-124; K1; DEC-118
  2. Your group's copyWhat the code calls an instance is, for members, "your group's copy of Civicsmith", or just "your group's copy". Never "instance", "plane" or "server"."Who controls this copy" (the administrators' card).DEC-109; principle 2.4
  3. The group's two namesThe group's display name leads wherever people read; its short name (the slug) appears where an address or a signature is shown. A group that wants to stay unnamed chooses an unrevealing short name at install."Lakeshore Tenants" in the header; "lakeshore-tenants" beside the signature and in the address.Publication §7 (public identity)
  4. No city in the productCivicsmith's own words never name a city, county or state. Places come from the group's profile and the group's own writing."Counting your profile's holidays", never "Counting Oakland holidays".K1

3. Civicsmith's voice

Seven traits, each from a value or principle you have already set. Together they describe a calm, exact colleague who respects the reader's time and judgement. The name points at the destination, skilled civic accountability work; the voice keeps the way there wide, so a newcomer never feels the tool is only for experts (your words, 3 October: the path to success must be "wide enough and inclusive enough for all audiences").

  1. PlainEveryday words and short sentences, naming things as members know them. A term of art is used only when it is the right word, and then explained once, where it appears.Publishing makes this edition permanent and public. not Commit will finalize the immutable artifact.Design Requirement 11; principles 2.4, 8.4
  2. ExactDates, numbers, names and sources, not adjectives. "Show your work. Always."Waiting on the City Clerk's reply, due 14 October. not Response pending soon.Operational principle 2; DEC-98
  3. Calm, especially under pressureWeight is carried by clarity, never by alarm. No exclamation marks, no capitals for emphasis, no red-alert words. You chose "pressure" over "attacks" for the same reason.This reply is marked as a legal threat. Record whether a litigation hold is in place. not WARNING: You may be sued!Operational principle 8 and its wording note; principle 7.2
  4. Honest about limitsSays what is not known, and why, in the five fixed terms. Never guesses past a gap or softens it.Undetermined, because the page could not be read as text.Principles 3.1–3.3
  5. RespectfulInforms once and trusts the member. No praise, no scolding, no "Are you sure?": instead, it states what the act does, and lets the member decide.Declining this option records your reason with it. You can reconsider it later. not Are you sure? Great job!DEC-69; principles 1.1, 4.2
  6. Neutral on policyCivicsmith's words take no position on what policy should be and never characterise a person or office. Words like "breach" appear only where a member's determination put them.Noncompliant with the two-thirds requirement, as determined by Rosa on 3 March. never The city illegally diverted funds.Operational principle 1; Action §1 rule 3
  7. Disciplined output, human membersMembers may be angry, and their own notes may say so. What Civicsmith writes, and what it helps the group publish, stays disciplined.A member's private note can say "this is outrageous"; the drafted finding the assistant offers says what the documents show.Roadmap §5 ("civic OS OUTPUT must be disciplined, but humans can be human")

5. Words

The words members see, held in one place (principle 8.5), with the ones Civicsmith avoids. Terms you have ruled are fixed.

Use

  • group, member, administrator, owner
  • project, question, finding, case, edition
  • document, capture, record (not "bundle")
  • matter (on a plan), action, determination
  • To do, Noticed, Signal (the queue)
  • Undetermined, Withheld, Unrated, Nobody looked, Refused
  • wizard, wizard script, the assistant
  • your group's copy (of Civicsmith)
  • Stated, Reported, Proven ("working on" notices)

Avoid

  • "user", "content", "data" for people and their work
  • "instance", "plane", "op", "token" in member text
  • "claim" on public pages
  • "Submit": name the act ("Send", "Publish", "Record")
  • "Oops", "Something went wrong", "Are you sure?"
  • "AI" as a label: say "the assistant", or "machine work"
  • Characterising words ("corrupt", "illegal", "fraud") in Civicsmith's own text
  • Exclamation marks and capitals for emphasis

Addressing people

  • The member is "you"; the group is "your group".
  • Other members by their handle, as the record shows them.
  • Offices by their role ("the City Clerk"), never a private person.
  • Dates written out: "14 October 2026"; times with their zone.

V1. A wordmark alone, or a wordmark with a symbol?

A wordmark is the name itself, always set the same way: "Civicsmith" in one typeface and weight. A symbol is a small picture that stands for the name and can appear without it, wherever there is no room for the word: a browser tab, a phone's home screen, a stamp in print. Either way the name in words appears wherever it fits. Below, each option is shown in four places. These are sketches in this page's placeholder type and colour; step 4 draws the final mark.

The name alone. Where there is no room for it, a square with the first letter, "C", taken from the wordmark rather than drawn as a symbol.

V2. A group's own logo: yes, optional

Your answer, 4 October: "Yes, optional". An administrator may add the group's logo. It appears beside the group's name on its public face and in its workspace, stored inside the group's copy (nothing loaded from outside). The name always appears in words too, so the logo is never the only signal. Never required.

V3. The assistant says "I": yes

Your answer, 4 October: "Yes". In conversation the assistant says "I" ("I found three passages…"). In every label and record it appears as "the assistant", with who asked ("the assistant did this, at Rosa's request"). It never says "we", which is the group's word.

V4. Translation: a draft ruling from your direction

Your direction, 4 October: A group should be able to translate their words too - and thus support multi-lingual usages across their membership. But a surface or feature of the assistant may be needed to support the group in this way.

Draft ruling for you to confirm:

  1. Each member chooses their language. Screens show in it wherever a translation exists, and in English otherwise, word by word, never blank.
  2. A group may translate the interface for its members. Civicsmith's own translations come with releases. A group may add a language its members need, or improve a translation, in its own copy. The fixed terms (the grades, "Undetermined", the queue's kinds, the acts' names) each carry a note on what they mean, so every language says the same thing. Renaming words within a language stays out (option A), so cases still read the same from group to group.
  3. The group's own writing can be read in another language. On any note, question, finding or notice, a member can ask to read it in their language. The assistant drafts the translation, labelled "Machine translation · original in English", with the original one tap away. A translation never replaces the original, and a member's own words stay theirs.
  4. Signed and published records stay in the language they were signed in. A translation of a published case is labelled unofficial and links to the signed original (principle 8.5).
  5. A translation workspace, with the assistant. Administrators, or members given the grant, get a page listing every interface word in English beside the group's language, with gaps marked. The assistant drafts the missing words as labelled drafts, and a member who knows the language checks and adopts each one. Without the assistant, members type them in.
  6. Later: a group may offer its translation to Civicsmith's library, reviewed before it reaches every group, the way wizard scripts are shared.

Example. Barrio Unido's members speak Spanish and English. Their administrator opens Translations and chooses Spanish. Most words already come translated with the release; the assistant drafts the few left, each marked "Draft", and Lucía, who is bilingual, checks and adopts them. Later a member reading an English finding taps "Leer en español" and sees the machine translation, marked as such, with the English original a tap away.

Answer: "V4: as drafted", or your edits to any point.
