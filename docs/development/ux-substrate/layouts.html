2. The assistant panel

Drawn early, because every other screen leaves room for it. It docks beside the screen it serves, on the member's own Claude account, labelled as machine work in everything it produces, with the member's daily limit always in view. For a member who has not connected an account, it does not exist: no button, no empty panel, no reminder. Open any screen with “With the assistant” to see it; the question screen below opens with it.

With the assistant

Docked right on a desktop, as a sheet on a phone. It answers only from what the group holds and says where it looked; about laws it gives legal information, never advice. Its drafts go into fields, labelled, until the member makes them theirs.

Without it

“Ask” becomes “Find and count”: search, and counts that show their method. Drafts come from the group's own templates. Standing questions still run; their new matches arrive as a list.

4. Civicsmith without the assistant

Your ruling of 5 October: a member who connects no Claude account uses a version without the assistant. These are the rules every screen follows for that member, and they are drawn in every journey above.

Absent, not greyed

No assistant button, panel, tray or “suggested” section appears, and nothing invites them to connect again. Their own settings say “not connected” in one line, where they can change it.

Ask becomes find and count

The Ask screen is a search with counts that show their method. Everything the assistant would cite, the member can find and cite themselves.

Drafts come from the group's templates

A records request, a communication or a filing starts from the group's own template, labelled as a draft, exactly where the assistant's draft would go. Translations are typed by members.

Standing questions still run

The search re-runs on schedule and new matches reach the queue once, as a list, unread by any machine. (A member with the assistant gets them read and summarised.)

Wizards are the same

Every wizard runs from its script with no account. Only the assistant planning a new wizard on the fly needs one.

What they cannot do

Ask in plain words; receive suggestions; have documents read and summarised; have translations drafted; have “what changed” drafted. Each of these has a member's own way to do the same work.

7. Two things for you

S1. Releasing a case at the moment its story runs

What you are asked: may a group sign a case now and have it published later, at a time it sets?

Why it is yours: it is something Civicsmith would newly do, and it changes when publishing happens, which today is the moment an owner signs.

How it works today

The publication ceremony ends with the owner signing, and the case is public at that moment. A newsroom can show a reporter an embargoed advance copy, but that is a review copy that never leaves the group's copy. Signing needs a laptop or desktop: a phone says where to finish (your ruling on phones).

An example

The Bay Courier will run Teo's story, “Rents rose without a vote”, at 6:00 on Thursday 19 November, linking to Lakeshore Tenants' case. Dev, the project owner, finishes the ceremony on Wednesday evening. If he signs then, the case is public twelve hours before the story, and another outlet can run it first. If he waits, someone must be at a laptop at 6:00 on Thursday, or the story links to a page that is not there yet.

The choices

A. Keep it as it isPublished when signed.Gains: nothing new; a case is never public without a person acting at that moment.Costs: a newsroom must be at a laptop at the hour, or publish early. Newsrooms are a core audience.
B. Sign now, publish at a set timeThe last step offers “Publish now” or “Publish at…”. The signed edition waits, not public, in the group's copy. At the hour Civicsmith checks again; if anything changed since signing (a source, a member's tie confirmation, a hold), it does not publish and tells the owner once. The owner can cancel or move the time until then.Gains: newsrooms' own practice; nobody up at 6:00.Costs: a new state to show (“Signed · publishes Thursday 19 November, 6:00”); the case shows when it was signed and when it was published.
C. Sign beforehand, release from a phoneThe owner signs on a laptop; at the hour, one tap on a phone publishes it.Gains: a person still acts at the moment.Costs: someone must still be up; widens what a phone may do beyond your ruling.

Recommendation: B. It serves the newsrooms you made a core audience without anyone acting at the hour, and the check at release keeps a case from going out on something that changed overnight. Risk: a case goes public while nobody is watching; the check, the cancel and the queue item telling the owner it went live answer that.

S2. Approving the Civicsmith wizard library

What you are asked: do you approve the seventeen wizard scripts in section 3 (the Wizards tab) as the first Civicsmith library?

Why it is yours: your ruling on wizards says the library Civicsmith ships with every release is approved by you; groups can add their own, but cannot change these. The three required ones (set up, welcome, publication ceremony) must always work, or a release is held.

What approval covers

The words of each step (what to do, and why), the screen and control each step points at, and the drafts a step places (always from the group's own templates, labelled). It does not settle the final words of the welcome wizard, which you deferred until the new screens are in use; that one is approved as a first version and revised then.

Recommendation: approve, walking two or three in the viewer first (Get a record, Publication ceremony, Connect your Claude account show the most). Anything you comment on is changed before it goes to the development process.

Reply, for example: “S2: approved” · “S2: approved, except…” · or a comment on any step.
