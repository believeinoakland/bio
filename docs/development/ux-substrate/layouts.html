2. The assistant panel

Drawn early, because every other screen leaves room for it. It docks beside the screen it serves, on the member's own Claude account or the group's API key where the group offers one, labelled as machine work in everything it produces, with the member's daily limit always in view. For a member reached by no account (none of their own, and no group key), it does not exist: no button, no empty panel, no reminder. Open any screen with “With the assistant” to see it; the question screen below opens with it.

Everything else a member does is the same either way. Where the assistant would have helped, the member's own way takes its place: “Ask” becomes “Find and count”, drafts come from the group's own templates, and standing questions still run, their new matches arriving as a list. Section 4 lists all six rules, and every journey in section 3 can be walked both ways with the switch at the top.

With the assistant

Docked right on a desktop, as a sheet on a phone. It answers only from what the group holds and says where it looked; about laws it gives legal information, never advice. Its drafts go into fields, labelled, until the member makes them theirs.

Without it

“Ask” becomes “Find and count”: search, and counts that show their method. Drafts come from the group's own templates. Standing questions still run; their new matches arrive as a list.

Help with writing (your rulings, DEC-153 and, through the development process, K1841). Wherever a member describes something in their own words (a note, an observation, a request, a case's scope or what changed), “Help me write this” sits under the field when the assistant is reachable. Never in a field giving a member's reason for an act: a reason is always the member's own. With the member's suggestions switched off it works only from what they typed; with them on it may also draw on what the group holds. It never adds a fact. For an observation it only helps word what the member saw. Its words arrive in the field labelled “Draft · the assistant's, asked by <handle>”, and nothing is saved until the member edits and keeps them. It is never offered where the assistant is refused: vouching, concluding, signing, publishing, an irreversible act. Without the assistant, nothing appears.

Making content, and finding it in what you hold

Your question of 6 October: where does a member create content, or ask for content to be found in a document, a set of documents or a project? Creating stays where each kind of thing lives, always from a passage the group holds. Finding now has one control, “Find in this”, the same on a document, on the documents a member is holding together, and on a project (DEC-164).

Where a member creates content

  • Add: a document from an address or a file, a photo, or what they saw themselves.
  • Document: cite a passage.
  • Person: add a fact from a document. Timeline: record an event or a dated fact. Money trail: read a figure into a money fact.
  • Standard: hold a requirement as a standard. Proceeding: hold an order as obligations. Calculation: work out a figure, its method shown.
  • Questions, hunches, notes: in a project and in Your notes.

Where a member asks for content to be found

  • Find in this (new): pick what to look for (people and offices the group follows, money figures, dates and deadlines, requirements, events in minutes and agendas, a name or term). Every result shows its passage, labelled “Found by search”. A result becomes a fact only when a member records it, with the act that already makes that kind of fact, from that passage.
  • Find: search everything, hold a set together, see where nobody looked.
  • Ask (with the assistant) or Find and count (without).
  • Keep finding this: new matches arrive in the queue as a list.

What search can and cannot find

It matches words as written, figures, dates, the words of a requirement (“shall”, “must”, “within”) and the names of people and offices the group already follows. A table’s dates and amounts are counted through a calculation, never recorded one by one. A name the group doesn’t follow yet is found only as a word; the member adds the person from the passage.

The assistant’s proposals: later

Once measured, and only at a member’s request for a question they name, the assistant will also propose people, events and money figures that search can’t match. Each is labelled machine work, graded no higher than its method earns, and becomes a fact only by the same acts, by a member. Drawn on the screen, marked “Not in the first release”.

4. Civicsmith without the assistant

Your rulings of 5 and 6 October: a group may run without the assistant, and a member reached by no account (none of their own, and no group key) uses Civicsmith without it. These are the rules every screen follows for that member, and they are drawn in every journey above.

Absent, not greyed

No assistant button, panel, tray or “suggested” section appears, and nothing invites them to connect again. Their own settings say “not connected” in one line, where they can change it.

Ask becomes find and count

The Ask screen is a search with counts that show their method. Everything the assistant would cite, the member can find and cite themselves.

Drafts come from the group's templates

A records request, a communication or a filing starts from the group's own template, labelled as a draft, exactly where the assistant's draft would go. Translations are typed by members.

Standing questions still run

The search re-runs on schedule and new matches reach the queue once, as a list, unread by any machine. (A member with the assistant gets them read and summarised.)

Wizards are the same

Every wizard runs from its script with no account. Only the assistant planning a new wizard on the fly needs one.

What they cannot do

Ask in plain words; receive suggestions; have documents read and summarised; have translations drafted; have “what changed” drafted. Each of these has a member's own way to do the same work.

7. For you

S16. The lighter level waits longer instead of hiding: as recommended, recorded

Your answer, 7 October: “S16: as recommended”. Recorded as DEC-175, changing DEC-162:

  • Explain promptly: every explanation opens after the pointer rests half a second.
  • On a longer pause: marks and names still after half a second; guidance (screens, sections, rail entries, acts) after a second and a half. Nothing is hidden.
  • In both, keyboard focus and a tap show everything at once; Escape closes it.
  • The switch is where it was: the masthead, Alt+Shift+I, Settings › Your account › Explanations, and the switch at the top of this page.

S15. Opening a high-risk file without a deeper check: B, recorded

Your answer, 7 October: “S15: as recommended”. Recorded as DEC-173 and drawn in “Opening a file”:

  • For a high-risk file no scanner has flagged, checked by the built-in scanner within the week, a member may open the original without a deeper check.
  • First they read why it is high risk and what opening it risks, and confirm: “I will open it on my own device, not a shared one” and “I will not enable macros or editing”.
  • Never for a file under a scan hold: that still needs its release.
  • Who opened it is not recorded. A deeper check still opens the original for every member for 24 hours.

S14. Members' own accounts: B, recorded

Your answer, 7 October: “I agree that a group should be able to keep their information away from AIs. But in those situations when administrators have asked for that, that should be explained to members.” Recorded as DEC-172 and drawn in setup, Members and Settings › The assistant:

  • A member may always connect their own Claude account, whatever the group pays for.
  • Two separate group settings: whether the group pays (its API key), and “keep our material away from AI”, off by default.
  • An administrator turns it on only with a reason. While it is on, no assistant can be used in the group, the group's or a member's own.
  • Every member sees it on Settings › The assistant: who turned it on, when, and the reason in their words, with “if you think this should change, ask an administrator”.
  • “The group's key only” no longer exists.

S10 to S13. Security tools: as recommended, recorded

Your answer, 7 October: “S10: as recommended; S11: as recommended; S12: as recommended; S13: as recommended”. Recorded as DEC-168 and drawn in Settings › Security and “Opening a file”:

  • S10. A service that keeps the files it judges malicious for its own research (Sophos, Palo Alto WildFire) may be added, with that keeping stated plainly; the administrator confirms it before turning the service on.
  • S11. A service that shares by default is offered only in its private mode, which Civicsmith turns on and checks on every call; if it cannot confirm private mode, nothing is sent. Not offered where the vendor's terms say submissions are not confidential unless stated (ANY.RUN).
  • S12. The no-sharing rule covers web addresses too: an address check never sends the full address to anyone new; only checks that send a scrambled fragment, or Cloudflare's own.
  • S13. A site known to be bad: the document is still captured, as high risk; it opens only in the safe view, the check's answer is noted on it, and the member is told.

S9. Where attempts come from: B, recorded

Your answer, 6 October: “S9: B - but never the member's own address”. Recorded as DEC-166 and drawn in the security screen's “Where from”:

  • Refused and blocked requests are counted by the country Cloudflare reports, for the period chosen, as a ranked list.
  • No address is ever kept or shown, and a member's least of all: nothing derived from it (country, network) appears either.
  • A member who mistypes a password and then signs in within the hour is counted without a country; a member's sign-ins are never placed.
  • The screen says a country is not proof of who is behind an attempt.

I read “never the member's own address” as covering both the member's network address and the whereabouts it reveals. Tell me if you meant more or less.

S8. Information levels: B, recorded

Your answer, 6 October: “S8: B”. Recorded as DEC-162. There are two levels:

  • Marks and names, always on: every mark's meaning and every reference's card.
  • Guidance: adds what each screen, section and rail entry is and what you can do there.

New members start with guidance on, and after a month Civicsmith asks once whether to keep it. A member switches with the masthead control, Alt+Shift+I, or Settings › Your account › Explanations, all now drawn.

S7. Who may translate, what stops abuse, and local words: as described, recorded

Your answer, 6 October: “S7: as you've just described”, after your suggestion that the assistant does the first cut. Recorded as DEC-157.

  • An administrator gives the grant to named members, for a named language.
  • The assistant drafts and a speaker finds its errors.
  • Ordinary words show once kept.
  • A protected word kept as the assistant drafted it shows once kept. Changed, or typed without a draft, it waits for a second granted speaker, or an administrator reading the assistant's back-translation. Until then members see the English.
  • Every change is recorded and can be undone in one act. The English is one hover away, and any member can mark a word as wrong.
  • Official names of offices, laws, programs and places stay as they are, with an explanation beside them. An official translation is used with its source.
  • What goes to an office goes in the office's language, with a labelled translation for the member.
  • The group's own local words are its to translate.

Drawn in the Translations screen and its wizard.
