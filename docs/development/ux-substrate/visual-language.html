Civicsmith's visual language

One look for every screen a member uses and every case a group publishes, built on the plumb bob. It replaces the three looks that had grown up separately, and it is drawn here in itself: every mark, button and band on this page is the real thing, in light and dark.

1. The idea

A plumb bob hangs on a line and shows what is truly vertical. Builders use it to check work against a standard that does not bend. That is what a group does with its government's own rules, and it is how this look behaves: plain, upright, measured, and quiet until something needs the member.

Its colour is verdigris, the green patina a brass plumb bob takes on with use. It marks Civicsmith itself and everything a member can do. Every other colour carries a meaning, and only that meaning.

Five rules hold it together. Every screen in step 5 is checked against them.

  1. Colour always means something.Verdigris for acting and for Civicsmith; rust only for attention (a clock, a change, a refusal, something visible outside the group); one colour per evidence scale. The assistant's work and another group's work carry no colour of their own: an edge, a mark and a word tell them apart.
  2. Three voices in type.A serif for words people wrote (the group's questions, findings and cases, and passages quoted from documents); a plain sans for Civicsmith's own words; a monospaced face only for identifiers a machine checks, such as a fingerprint.
  3. Lines before shadows, and square corners.Like a printed form: thin rules separate things; only something lifted above the work (a dialog, the hold strip) casts a shadow. No rounded pills.
  4. Still until the member is needed.Nothing moves on load or while idle. The one movement is a wizard drawing a ring onto the control the member should press: once by itself, and again whenever the member asks (“Show me where” in the guide, or Alt+Shift+W).
  5. Never colour alone.Every colour has a word or an icon beside it; every pairing meets the international accessibility standard (WCAG 2.2 AA) in light and dark.

2. Type: three voices

Brand and voice says two voices speak on every screen: the group's and Civicsmith's. The type makes that visible. Three typefaces from one open-licensed family (Source Serif, Source Sans and Source Code), shipped inside the group's copy so no outside company learns who is reading.

Why these

Grounds: paper for working, a clean sheet for publishing

Members work on a faintly green paper; a published case sits on a near-white sheet. The change of ground is the first thing that says which space you are in.

The four evidence scales

Blue for a document copy, plum for a connection, ochre for testimony, olive for a subject match: four hues far enough apart to tell at a glance, each with its own icon, so a member who sees colour differently still reads them. Colour names the scale, never how good the grade is: a capture D is the same blue as an A.

Rust is attention, and only attention

A deadline that has passed, a source that changed, a refusal that teaches, something about to be seen outside the group. Because it appears rarely, it is seen. It is never used to decorate, and there is no alarm red.

No colour for machine work or another group's work

Both are about who made something, not how good it is, so neither takes a colour from the evidence scales. Machine work has a dotted edge; another group's work a double rule. The words always say it too.

4. Shape, space and motion

Corners

Two pixels on buttons, fields and marks; square on sheets and panels. A printed form has corners. There are no rounded pills anywhere.

Lines before shadows

Hairline rules separate. A shadow means “lifted above your work”: only a dialog, a menu and the hold strip have one.

Space

Eight steps on a four-pixel base. Density comes from signals, not cramming (principle 2.5): whitespace is kept, and marks carry the information.

Targets

Every button is at least 40 pixels tall, every phone target 44, well above the standard's 24-pixel minimum. A focused control always shows a two-pixel verdigris ring.

Motion

Changes of state take a tenth to a fifth of a second, with no bounce. Nothing animates on load or while idle. A wizard's ring draws onto its control once, and again each time the member asks for it. With the device set to reduce motion, nothing moves at all.

Light and dark

Member screens follow the device and can be set in settings. The dark theme is drawn, not inverted: every colour was chosen again to keep its meaning and its contrast. A printed case and the complete edition are always light.

6. The marks

The marks are the product's vocabulary of signals: each answers one question, has one form, and looks the same on every screen (principle 9.1). Hover over, focus or tap any of them in the product to see what it means; here the explanation is written beside it.

Evidence: letters grade it, two strengths, never one

Full form, where there is room. Where space is short, the scale's name drops and the icon carries it:

What we don't know: five gaps, five looks

Undetermined, because the page could not be read as text. Withheld. Part of what this rests on is not yours to see. Unrated · rests on nothing yet Nobody looked in the 2021 council minutes yet. Refused: Can't sign yet. Finding 2 rests on testimony with no independent corroboration. Add a corroborating source, or move finding 2 to supporting.

Each has its own mark, outline and sentence, and none looks like an error: “Undetermined” is half known (a half-filled circle, a dashed outline, always with its “because”); “Withheld” is a redaction bar, saying only that something is missing; “Unrated” is an empty grade; “Nobody looked” is a dotted lens; “Refused” teaches, in rust, what to do next.

10. What it replaces

The old lookWhat it gave this oneWhat was let go, and why
The old interface's design languageIts best ideas: paper for working and a clean sheet for publishing; serif for judgement, sans for plain speech, mono for fact; verdigris as the signature; rust for attention only; square corners and no pills; calm motion; faces shipped inside the copy.Its document (BIO_Design_Language_v0_2) is lost, so this page now is the source. Mono for every fact is narrowed to identifiers: dates and numbers read better in the sans with aligned figures. It had no dark theme, no marks for the five gaps, origins, the queue or wizards.
The action plan sketchesThe plan page's structure and words, which you approved and which bind (step 5 draws them in this look); purple for machine work showed it must be told apart.A cool blue-grey look with a second family of typefaces loaded from outside. Purple for machine work is replaced by a hue-less dotted edge, so it never competes with the evidence colours.
The measures mapIts rules, all kept: letters, bars and pips; colour names the scale, never the value; an icon per scale; a texture per ladder.Its placeholder colours and typefaces. Connection moves from teal (too close to verdigris) to plum; the scales were re-chosen to be told apart by people who see colour differently.

The approved measures map keeps its rules; its specimens will be redrawn in this look when it is next republished.

Beyond colour

Every mark carries a word or an icon; the three ladders differ in texture as well as colour; dates and grades never rely on colour to be read.

Keyboard, focus and touch

Every control shows a focus ring; every explanation that opens on hover also opens on focus and tap; targets are 40 to 44 pixels.

Reading

Text sizes start at 16 pixels for work and 18 for reading, at most about 68 characters to a line; nothing is set in capitals except short labels, which screen readers read as words.

Nothing from outside

Typefaces and icons ship inside the group's copy, about 0.8 MB of open-licensed type covering Latin, extended Latin and Vietnamese. Other scripts use the device's own faces until a group adds a language that needs them.
