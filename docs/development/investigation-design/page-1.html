The Investigation Plan

Your plan for an investigation (steps, dependencies, resources, deadlines and milestones, teams and subgoals, status reports), set against what the product has, what the study proposed, and the rules already in force. Then the decisions, in the order that lets work start.

Yes, the project is the investigation. You ruled that on 6 October (K1627). Your plan is the member-visible part of it. The study's design already had a plan of steps; yours is larger.

Most of your plan fits. Steps that come and go, dependencies, deadlines, milestones, and the information an investigation needs all build on things that exist or were designed.

Four parts reverse earlier lines, so they are yours to decide: assigning work to members (the study said "nobody assigns"), teams with leaders (today a project has only owners and participants), money and other resources (your "no budgets" of 29 September, made for action plans), and status reports written by leaders.

Nine questions start the first two stages, which use no AI: four from the earlier page (D1, D17, D18, D23) and five new ones (D25–D28, plus D23 revised). The rest can wait.

One line of your doctrine your plan touches

The Capability Ladders say: "no drift into BI, case management or dashboards" (§10; DEC-48, D224, D231). The study cited it to rule out "tasks, assignees, roles and cadence reminders". Teams, assignments, resources and reports sit close to that line.

My reading, for you to confirm when you rule D25–D30: a plan that serves the investigation is part of the path, so it is within the line. What stays out is a general project-management tool: timesheets, Gantt charts, workload dashboards, scores on members.

Lines that hold whatever you decide

These come from your own rulings. They shape every option below; none of them is asked again.

  • Who did what is recorded; nobody is measured. Every act carries the member's handle, never their cover name. Nothing counts, scores or ranks a member's work or attention (DEC-68), and there is no record of who opened which file (K1892). A report may say who completed a step; it never totals a person's hours or compares members.
  • No nagging. A reminder is one the member asked for. A nearing date changes how an item looks; an overdue date is told once; nothing goes by email or push (DEC-69, DEC-94). A leader cannot set reminders on someone else.
  • The project is the visibility boundary. No role changes what a member may see (K1484). Every joined participant sees the whole project, including every team's work. Administrators see and direct nothing inside a project; authority belongs to owners.
  • Working material stays inside. The plan, its resources, assignments and reports are never published (D15).
  • The group's own dates are never findings about government. A missed milestone of ours is our business, not evidence.
  • The machine proposes; a member accepts (K1627). The engine may propose steps, dependencies and estimates; it never assigns anyone.

Settled as details (no ruling needed)

  • Dependencies. A step may wait on other steps, on a document arriving, or on a dated event. The engine keeps the order: a step whose prerequisites are open shows what it waits on, and opens when they are done. A loop of steps waiting on each other is refused.
  • Where it lives. The plan's new parts go into the planned plan module and the investigation view, not into a new part, unless size forces a split. That is a packaging matter for BOB.
  • Expertise as a resource reuses the declared and confirmed expertise members already have, and the existing "ask for a check".
  • Information as a resource is the study's "why we hold it": a step that needs a document makes a sought document.

Your decisions

To start the first two stages (no AI)

D25 · teams, subgoals and leaders

You asked for a team with a shared goal and subgoals, with project and subgoal leaders. Today a project has one objective and two roles. The question is what a subgoal team is and what its leader may do.

  1. A. No new structure: owners lead, and subgoals are the project's questions or lines of enquiry.
  2. B. A subgoal is a named part of the investigation with its own steps and a team of joined participants. An owner names its leader. The leader may assign steps within the subgoal and issue its status reports. The project's leaders are its owners. Teams organise work and hide nothing: every joined participant sees every subgoal.
  3. C. As B, and each team also has its own private space and its own authority, apart from the owners.

Recommended: B. It gives you real teams and leaders without a second kind of authority over the project, and without splitting what members can see.

D26 · assignments

You said some members will be given assignments. The study said only that a member may claim a step for herself.

  1. A. Claiming only.
  2. B. A member may claim a step, and an owner or the subgoal's leader may assign a step to a joined participant, who accepts or declines with a reason. An assignment may carry a due date. A reminder is the assignee's own choice, offered when she accepts; an overdue step is told once.
  3. C. Assignment binds without the member accepting.

Recommended: B. Real groups do hand out work; accepting or declining keeps the respect for members your rulings require.

D27 · deadlines and milestones

Steps can carry deadlines, each with its basis (a law's clock, a promise, another step, or the group's own choice). The question is milestones.

  1. A. Members may set named, dated milestones on the plan ("contract and change orders in hand by 1 December"), each gathering the steps it needs; it is met when they are done. Our own target, never a finding.
  2. B. No milestones; the project's computed stage is the only one.

Recommended: A.

D28 · removing steps

You said steps are deleted when no longer needed. The record's rule is that a decision keeps its reason, so a later member can see what was dropped and why.

  1. A. Every step is retired with a reason and stays readable.
  2. B. A step anyone acted on (adopted, assigned, worked) is retired with a reason: it leaves the working plan and stays in "what we did". A step a member typed and nobody acted on can be deleted outright.
  3. C. Any step can be deleted outright.

Recommended: B. The plan stays realistic, as you asked, and the history of real decisions is kept.

D23 · the words members see (revised)

From the earlier page, with your direction folded in: the line "nobody assigns steps; no hours or costs" is removed, since D26 and D29 now decide it. One naming point remains: you call it "the plan", and "plan" already names the action plan (what to do about a finding).

Resources and reports (no AI; can join stage 2 or follow it)

D29 · resources

You said the engine estimates and tracks people, money and other material resources, information, connections and expertise. Money reverses your "no budgets" of 29 September, which was written for action plans.

  1. A. A free-form "what this needs" list on a step, with no figures.
  2. B. Each step may state what it needs, by kind, with an amount where the member has one: people (how many, which skills, how much effort), money, materials, information, connections, expertise. What was actually used is recorded on the step. The plan totals the stated figures per subgoal and for the project. Effort is about the step, never summed per person. Money is the group's own spending, kept apart from the government money the record studies (K1463). A connection (someone the group can call on) is held as the member who knows them chooses to share it, and never published.
  3. C. As B, plus a budget the plan is checked against, with warnings when an estimate exceeds it.

Recommended: B, for the investigation's plan only. Your "no budgets" for action plans stays unless you say otherwise.

D30 · status reports

You said project and subgoal leaders generate status reports on their own work, their team's and the project's.

  1. A. No reports: "where it stands" and "what we did" are always available to read.
  2. B. A leader starts a report; the system drafts it from the record since the last one (steps done and started, what is waiting, deadlines met and missed, milestones, resources used against estimates, new findings). The leader edits it, adds her own words and issues it, dated, to the project. It may say who completed what; it carries no per-member figures. Never published. A team may set its own report schedule as a reminder its leader asked for; nothing is required.
  3. C. As B, required on a fixed schedule.

Recommended: B. A required schedule would be the nagging your rulings forbid.

With the AI stages

D31 · an AI budget for the investigation
  1. A. None; each member's own daily ceiling, as built.
  2. B. Optional: an owner may set an allowance (a limit, not a new account) for the investigation over use of the group's key. Estimates are shown before a run; when the allowance is reached the member is told once and further group-key runs wait for an owner to raise it. A member's own subscription stays hers and is not counted against it.
  3. C. A hard cap that also covers members' own accounts.

Recommended: B, decided together with D12 (showing cost to whoever pays).
