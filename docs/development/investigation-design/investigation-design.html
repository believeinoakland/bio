2 · Your direction so far

In your words, newest first. Where a later word changes an earlier one, the later governs.

"It's important to clarify that actions that affect the answering of a question rests with members - with the support and guidance of the system. Members can have hunches, hypotheses, and follow leads - as can the system. The system (including AIs) can proactively explore a question (that is, look for answers). They have the capabilities to gauge to what extent the results that they find might contribute to the question - both in support and as contradicting other gathered information. The system can present potentially useful information to members working on a question. But it's the act of members that decide whether information is dismissed or added to the body of evidence (based on its grade and the rules of evidence that apply."

On steps: "Yes, work is done (steps are taken) that don't relate to a specific question. This work may be done to provide context for members, in the development of a question, to understand how to respond to findings, and so on." What a step keeps when it ends: "agreed". Tying a chance find to a step afterwards: "Yes".

D33 and D32's answers · 8 October

"A project has an objective it's trying to establish. It does so by posing questions that could be answered (or not) What's you're proposing is that a step is the work done in pursuit of an answer to a question. Some steps might contribute to answering the question, others may be dead-ends - though we should recognize that even some dead-ends may still contribute to our overall understanding of the world. This understanding is recorded in the form of documents, content, connections, and constructs created while pursuing that step. It's also important to recognize that the same question may be of interest to more than one objective. So a step taken in pursuit of an answer isn't always just for a single objective or project. So we should recognize the value of tracking the work being done to answer a question. A step may be defined to indicate that a member or AI are doing specific work (a step), but sometimes the information that contributes to an answer may be found through happenstance."

3 · The model as it stands

Who does what

Members

  • pose questions under the project's objective
  • take steps on a question, or for the project
  • hold hunches and hypotheses; follow leads
  • close a step: its outcome, what it produced, what was learned
  • tie a chance find to the step it helped

The system, including the AI

  • takes steps on a question: searching, capturing, reading
  • has its own hunches, hypotheses and leads, labelled as its own
  • judges how a find bears on the question: supporting it, or contradicting what is gathered
  • offers what it found to the members working on the question
A member's act decidesdismiss the information, or add it to the evidence on the question, by its grade and the rules of evidence

The chain

Everything except the step is built. The step is placed where your words put it.

4 · Questions

Open now

D34 · when the system explores on its own

Your rule today: an AI run starts only at a member's act, with one exception, a standing question a member writes, re-run on a schedule (K1481). "Proactively explore" goes further.

  1. A. A member asks the system to explore a question; within that bounded run it decides for itself where and how to look.
  2. B. As A, and a member may also set a question to be explored over time, with a cadence and an end date, within the paying account's limits.
  3. C. The system explores any open question unasked, whenever it sees fit.

Recommended: B. Proactive, while a member still decides that it happens and who pays. C would reverse K1481 and leave cost unbounded.

D35 · steps that serve no question
  1. A. They belong to the project. A step that helps form a question is linked to it once it exists. Deciding how to respond to findings stays with the action plan.
  2. B. They belong to the group, visible from every project.

Recommended: A.

D36 · who "the members working on a question" are
  1. A. The joined members of every project that draws on the question.
  2. B. Only members who took a step on it or follow it.
  3. C. A, with each member free to follow or stop following.

Recommended: C.

Paused until the model settles

From your plan of 7 October, to be restated around steps on questions: D27 deadlines and milestones; D28 removing steps (a step that was worked on keeps its record; an untouched one can go); D29 resources (people, money, information, connections, expertise); D30 status reports (their "leaders" fell with D25); D31 an AI budget for an investigation. Dependencies between steps need no ruling: a step may wait on other steps, a document or a date, and loops are refused.

5 · Research

What the study set out (6 October)

Fourteen realistic investigations, from a school rebuild (your Grandview example) to a sewer-fee diversion, five best-practice papers, six capability studies and three reviews. The proposal: four new parts (a library of reading guides, a record of why each document is held, the plan, the investigation view), one AI planner that fetches nothing, and one AI flow that reads one held document for one purpose. Everything else adds to existing parts. Eight stages, the first three with no AI. Cost per investigation, as an order of magnitude only: about $15–40 small, $100–300 for a Grandview-sized case, $300–1,000 or more over years.

What the product has today

  • Questions with evidence links (legs), alternative accounts (basis versions), claims, conclusions with a falsifier, strength against the project's bar, contradictions, hypotheses, dated waits.
  • Documents captured with provenance and grade, read by a tiered reader with OCR; ZIP records productions unpacked; search inside a document.
  • Objectives with computed progress and gaps; promises and deadlines with their basis; a timeline of what happened and what we did; watching of sources.
  • Cases published by a project's owner; determinations against standards; action plans and actions with legal clocks.
  • No step of investigative work anywhere. The nearest: actions (outward only), the action plan's steps (after a finding), to-dos (routed by the system), recheck triggers on a question, objective gaps, and your deferred "backward question" ("what else would have to be true?").

Words that mean more than one thing

basis

  1. a question's legs
  2. one named version of them
  3. why a date is due
  4. what an action rests on
  5. what a proposal rests on
  6. a member's grounds for her testimony

finding

  1. a concluded question
  2. something the machine noticed
  3. a check's result
  4. a recorded absence
  5. a missing document in a sequence

claim

  1. the statement on a basis version
  2. what a published case asserts
  3. a case's completeness statement
  4. a legal claim

plan and step

  1. the action plan and its steps
  2. the "Plan" inside one action
  3. "an action: a step someone takes"
  4. wizard and progress steps

The design should give members one meaning each for "finding" and "basis", with other words for the rest (owed to the design stream).

Rules your plan of 7 October touched

  • Your "no budgets" of 29 September (for action plans: no costs, assignees or hours).
  • The Capability Ladders' "no drift into BI, case management or dashboards". My reading: a plan serving the investigation is within it; timesheets, workload dashboards and scores on members are not.
  • No measuring members (DEC-68), no nagging (DEC-69, DEC-94), no record of who opened which file (K1892).
