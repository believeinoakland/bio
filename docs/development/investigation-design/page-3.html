The Work on a Question

Your answers of 8 October, put together into one picture: what a step is, what the system may do, and where the line sits that only members cross. Then three questions it leaves.

What you have said, in short (recorded in your words as D32 and D33):

  • A project pursues an objective by posing questions, which may or may not be answered.
  • A step is work done in pursuit of an answer. Some steps help; some are dead ends, which can still add to our understanding.
  • That understanding is kept as the documents, content, connections and other records created during the step.
  • One question can serve several objectives, so the work on a question is worth tracking for its own sake.
  • Some steps serve no particular question: they give members context, help form a question, or help decide how to respond to findings.
  • A step names its outcome, what it produced and, optionally, what was learned. A find by chance may later be tied to the step it helped.
  • Members and the system can both have hunches and hypotheses and follow leads. The system may explore a question on its own, gauge how its finds bear on it, and offer them.
  • Only a member's act dismisses information or adds it to the evidence, by its grade and the rules of evidence.

The picture

Members

  • pose questions under the project's objective
  • take steps on a question, or for the project itself
  • hold hunches and hypotheses; follow leads
  • close a step: its outcome, what it produced, what was learned
  • tie a chance find to the step it helped

The system, including the AI

  • takes steps on a question: searching, capturing, reading
  • has its own hunches, hypotheses and leads, labelled as its own
  • gauges how a find bears on the question: supporting it, or contradicting what is gathered
  • offers what it found to the members working on the question

Three questions

D34 · when the system explores on its own

Your rule today is that an AI run starts only at a member's act, with one exception: a standing question a member writes, re-run on a schedule (K1481). "Proactively explore" goes further, so I need to know how far.

  1. A. A member asks the system to explore a question. Within that bounded run, the system decides for itself where and how to look.
  2. B. As A, and a member may also set a question to be explored on its own over time, with a cadence and an end date, inside the limits of the account that pays. This widens the standing question from reading to exploring.
  3. C. The system explores any open question unasked, whenever it sees fit.

Recommended: B. It gives you proactive exploration while a member still decides that it happens and who pays. C would reverse K1481 and leave cost unbounded.

D35 · steps that serve no question

You said some steps give context, help form a question, or help decide how to respond to findings. Where they sit decides who sees them.

  1. A. They belong to the project. A step that helps form a question is linked to that question once it exists. Work on how to respond to findings belongs to the action plan, which already exists for that.
  2. B. They belong to the group, visible from every project.

Recommended: A.

D36 · who are "the members working on a question"

The system offers finds to the members working on a question. Since a question can serve several projects, someone has to be on that list.

  1. A. The joined members of every project that draws on the question.
  2. B. Only members who have taken a step on the question, or said they follow it.
  3. C. A, but each member can choose to follow or stop following a question.

Recommended: C. Everyone who might care sees it once, and nobody is held to a question they have left.
