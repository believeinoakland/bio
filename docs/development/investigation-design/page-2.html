Steps, Basis, Claims, Findings

How the parts of an investigation relate in what is built today, where a step would fit, and the words that mean more than one thing. Drawn from the canon and the product's requirements, read in full.

The product already has a careful chain from a question to a published case: evidence links, alternative accounts, a claim, a conclusion, a finding, a case.

It has no step. Nothing holds the work an investigation does to get evidence: find a document, ask someone, file a request, visit a site. The only "steps" in the canon belong to the action plan, which decides what to do about a finding, after the investigation.

Several key words carry two to seven meanings, "basis" and "finding" most of all. Settling them is part of designing the plan.

Words with more than one meaning

basis

  1. a question's legs (its evidence links)
  2. one named version of those legs, with its claim
  3. why a date is due: a law, a promise, another step, or the group's own choice
  4. what an action rests on
  5. what a machine proposal rests on
  6. a member's stated grounds for her own testimony

finding

  1. a concluded question
  2. something the machine noticed, waiting for a member to take it up
  3. a check's result
  4. a recorded absence ("the negative result is a finding")
  5. a missing document in an expected sequence

claim

  1. the statement on a basis version
  2. what a published case asserts
  3. a case's statement that its material is complete
  4. a legal claim

plan and step

  1. the action plan and its steps (what to do about a finding)
  2. the "Plan" section inside one action
  3. "an action: a step someone takes"
  4. wizard steps, and progress steps on a bar

What this suggests for the investigation's plan

These are my readings from the study, offered for you to react to, not yet questions to rule. Your wording of the plan comes first.

1 · a step is the investigation's own work

A step is work the group does to get evidence or understanding. When a step goes outside the group (a records request, a request for comment), it starts an action, and "action" keeps its present meaning.

2 · a step serves a question

Most steps serve a question. Some serve an objective's gap, testing a possible explanation, or settling a contradiction. A step that serves nothing has no place in the plan.

3 · a step's result is not yet evidence

A step ends with a document held, a look recorded (including "looked, absent") or a reply. Turning that into a leg on a question stays a member's act, because a leg is what a member stands behind.

4 · one meaning each for "finding" and "basis"

Members would read "finding" only as a concluded question, and "basis" only as what a question rests on. The other senses get other words, drawn by the design stream.

5 · the backward question

"What else would have to be true?" is the natural way to propose steps from a question or a hoped-for action. You deferred it; it may be worth reviving for the plan.
