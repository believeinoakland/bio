Part 0

Orientation

What the system is, who uses it, the rules it will not break, and the words the rest of this document uses without explaining them again.

Start hereWhat CivicOS is

CivicOS is a tool for community accountability groups. It helps a small group of ordinary people capture what a public body published, work out what it means, build a case they can stand behind, and act on it — while never claiming more than the evidence supports.

A concrete example, and it is the one the design is tested against throughout: a neighbourhood group notices that money keeps moving out of the city's sewer fund. They want to know whether that is lawful, who authorised it, and whether anyone is accountable. Answering that means finding budget documents, council agendas, staff reports and contracts across a dozen city web pages; working out which ones relate to each other; deciding what they collectively show; and eventually publishing a case that a journalist, a lawyer or a city administrator can check without taking the group's word for anything.

Today that work is done in shared folders, spreadsheets and email threads. It fails in predictable ways: the source page changes and the screenshot has no proof of date, the person who found the crucial document leaves, and the finished claim rests on a chain nobody can retrace.

The whole product, in one sentence

The trustworthiness of the record. A group captures what a public body published and can prove later that it said what they claim. Everything else — the screens, the search, the workflow — is substrate for that. It follows that a defect which lets the record claim more than it can support is worse than a missing feature, and much worse than an ugly one — because a case built on a record that overclaims is worse than no case at all.

The stance, which is doctrine and not a mission statement

The objective is better government, pursued through greater understanding, less narrative, and accountability. The working assumption is that at the highest level all stakeholders want better outcomes in the public interest — while bad actors genuinely exist, and are identified by evidence rather than assumed by role. There is no structural prior against any class of actor anywhere in the system.

“Less narrative” is a constraint on us, not only on them, and this is the part that shapes the most design decisions. Threats to the record run in both directions: someone may damage a publisher, and someone may make claims reaching far beyond what the evidence supports. Nearly every rule in the next section defends against the second. The primary threat model here has always been self-directed.

How it is deployed, which explains several constraints

Each group runs its own instance, installed into the group's own Cloudflare account — not a tenant on our servers. An installer puts a sovereign instance into a group's account; that is the distribution model, not a demo.

The server side is called the plane, and it is built from three Cloudflare parts. A Worker is a small program that runs in Cloudflare's network rather than on a server you rent — it starts when a request arrives, handles it, and stops, so there is nothing to keep running and nothing to patch. The trade is strict ceilings on time and memory per request, which is why several design questions below resolve to measure it first. A Durable Object is the exception to a Worker's forgetfulness: a single long-lived instance that owns some state, handles its requests one at a time, and here carries a SQLite database — the record itself. R2 is bulk file storage, and holds the captured bytes.

Two consequences show up repeatedly. The group owns and can leave with their record, so the record must survive without us. And because the runtime is a Worker, capability that would be routine on an ordinary server has to be shown to fit before it can be promised.

Two different populations, deliberately kept apart — because they need opposite things, and because the same person is often in both.

And the rule that separates them, which is doctrine

The level of proof legitimately varies — and it is declared, not assumed. A lawyer building toward litigation may need the record to establish something; a reporter may need it to be convincing; a group deciding what deserves a closer look needs less than either. So each project declares the evidentiary strength its work requires — the default for a new project is set by the group — and every published case carries that declaration prominently, beside the strength the case actually reached. A reader building on someone else's case sees the standard it was held to and the declared bias it was produced under, and may need to strengthen it to meet their own standard before relying on it.

What never varies is the machinery of publishing itself. Publishing — the group signing a case as its own, defined properly in the vocabulary below — has one set of requirements for everyone: the conclusion is authored, what would disprove it is named, what was left out is stated, and no unearned grade survives. And nobody's standard is ever set by who they are: the group authors it, about its own work, before the work. A standard imposed by role — easier for officials, harder for activists — would build a role-based prior into the record, the same defect as automatically treating documents from some office as suspect. The record refuses that in both directions.

The same person is often both, and that is why the split is about relationships rather than populations. A lawyer may run an instance to build a case and also read someone else's published case. Which sense applies depends on the case, not on the person — so the two lists are not two groups of people to be told apart, and no part of the system ever needs to decide which kind someone “is”.

The non-technical archetype is load-bearing on almost every design choice below. Three examples, each explained fully later. When a member brings a worry to the system, they write it in their own words — they are never asked to first sort it into a category like “question”, “problem” or “project”, because choosing the category correctly would require the analysis they came here to do. When something technical fails, the failure is explained in their terms, never shown as a raw error. And when the system eventually asks the hardest question in the product — what would prove your conclusion wrong? — it first offers their own evidence to pick from, and only then a blank box, because a blank box on a hard question invites a filler answer.

Start hereThe seven rules that decide arguments

These are not style preferences. Each was written after something went wrong, and each is enforced structurally where it can be. Nearly every design decision in Parts 1–4 is one of these being applied.

Undetermined is first-class
Never invent an answer to get past a required field. If the system does not know something — who authored a document, when a page was published — it records that it does not know, in a form rendered identically everywhere. A check that pressures someone into inventing an answer in order to proceed is a bug in the check, not in the person.
An equality that costs nothing is not evidence
Two empty documents having the same digital fingerprint agree on nothing. Our own rate-limiter declining to fetch a page is not evidence the source failed. An account of where bytes came from that the sender supplies is an account the sender can invent. All three are refused structurally, not by convention.
Grade tracks how it was established, never how credible it is
A grade says what would be needed to check this, not how much to believe it. A member who was in the room may produce the most valuable fact in a case at the lowest grade, and it is labelled by its author rather than discounted.
A claim is worth its weakest link
A conclusion resting on four pieces of support — its legs, defined properly later — is worth the weakest one, and the screen names which one that is: never a score, never an average, never a single composite letter. Twice over, in fact, because two different things are measured — the weakest document the conclusion reaches, and the weakest relationship it relies on.
Evidence that cuts against your conclusion travels the same path as evidence that supports it
Called invariant 7. Its enforcement point is that leaving something out is an authored act with a name on it, rather than an absence nobody can see.
Measure, do not assume
Numbers carry their date and instrument. A vendor's documentation is a claim, not a measurement. Three claims the archive design rested on turned out to be wrong when finally measured, after weeks in the design document.
Run the negative control
Break the thing you just tested and confirm the test fails. A suite that does not fail when you break its subject is not a suite. One check here passed 67 assertions while testing nothing, because every input the code produced was well-formed by construction.

One structural mechanism deserves naming here because it is referenced throughout: the two-bucket fence. Working material and published material live in separate tables, and the code path that answers the public can only reach the published set. So answering an anonymous query structurally cannot leak working material — it is not a permission check that could be forgotten, it is a different set of tables.

Start hereThe four objects

Everything in the record is one of four things. Each is stored as a bundle — a text document with structured fields plus attached files, with append-only history, so what changed and who changed it is always recoverable.

What sits between the objects, and is not one of them

A connection is an edge — a stated relationship between two pieces of information, running through the thing they have in common. It is not a fifth object and it is not part of either document; it is the link itself, and it is recorded, graded and attributable like everything else. Most of the value of a record is in its connections rather than its documents, because a document nobody has related to anything answers no question. Connections are what make the four objects into a record instead of a filing cabinet.

Why a project holds no claims, which is easy to get wrong

A project is a container with membership and access control; an inquiry carries a question, its evidence and its conclusion. Merging them would put access control on every question — so the project workspace shows who is working on something and shows no strength, no grade and no conclusion.

Start hereThe question, the claim, and the three kinds of link

This is the middle of the system and the part most easily got wrong. Three words that sound interchangeable are not, and three kinds of link that all look like arrows do different jobs.

An inquiry asks; a claim answers

An inquiry is a question. “Was the $2.1m transfer authorised?” A question has no truth value — it cannot be right or wrong, only open or answered.

A claim is a proposition. “The transfer was not authorised by any resolution.” It can be true or false, supported or contradicted. It is the kind of thing evidence bears on.

An inquiry becomes a finding when a claim is added to it — together with what the claim rests on, and with a statement of what would prove it wrong (the falsifier, and it is required). That act is called concluding, and it is not a third kind of object: it is the inquiry adopting a claim as its answer. This is why the evidence attaches to the claim rather than to the act of adopting it.

Working from an assumption — the hunch, and the objectives it creates

The journey rarely starts with evidence in hand. It starts with an assumption — which CivicOS calls a hunch — and the system is built to support exactly that: create an inquiry, and immediately add one or more claims to it that nothing yet supports. An unsupported claim is not a defect to be hidden; it is the honest record of what the member believes and has not yet shown.

An unsupported claim is also a standing objective. The system recognises it and adds it to the list of things it can pursue on the member's behalf: as material arrives — from the member's own searching, and from the system pursuing the objective — legs are created and added to the claim, some supporting it, some cutting against it, and both are kept. The search behind this covers both content and claims: the documents the record holds, and the concluded inquiries whose findings could themselves bear on the question. A claim that gathers real support earns its strength; one that gathers evidence against it is doing its job too — and either way, no unsupported assertion survives to publication, because a hunch must clear before a case can be signed.

What “strength” is a property of, since three different things carry a grade

Grades are applied to a capture (a document read in), a connection (a stated relationship between two pieces of information), and a leg (a piece of a claim's support). A claim itself is never graded directly — its strength is derived from what it rests on, which is what stops anyone from simply declaring a claim strong.

The three kinds of link, which are not the same link

LinkWhat it assertsGraded?
Citation A reference from one piece of content to another. “This points at that.” An act of pointing, and nothing more — you can cite something you believe is wrong. No. Pointing makes no claim about the world, so there is nothing to grade.
Connection That two pieces of information concern the same thing — running through the entity they share. A claim about the world, and improvable as evidence firms up. Yes. Built from how well each end was established.
Leg That a piece of evidence bears on a claim — either supporting it or cutting against it. A claim's basis is its legs. Yes.

Cuts against is deliberately weaker than disproves. A leg pointing the other way does not kill a conclusion; it is weighed with the rest, and it must travel the same path as a supporting one. That is invariant 7, and it only works if the two can coexist inside one claim.

Structure and lifecycle are different kinds of model and get different notations. A single flowchart cannot carry both: it gives every relationship the same arrow, so contains, refers to and becomes would render identically. Can one finding hold several claims? Open — and there is a real example on the table

As modelled today, a finding holds one claim, and several propositions compose as separate inquiries that a larger one rests on — a leg pointing at a concluded inquiry is a leg pointing at that inquiry's claim.

The open question is whether some findings genuinely want several parallel claims — not sub-claims nested inside claims, but two independent answers to one question. The example: an inquiry asks “Can a parallel municipal power utility develop distribution lines separate from those of another utility in overlapping service territory?” One claim's legs cite utility regulations that conflict with parallel distribution. A second claim's legs cite rights enshrined in the state constitution, and the court decisions that clarify them. Two claims, two independent bodies of support, one question — and forcing them into two inquiries would split an answer a reader needs whole.

The alternative reading is that these are two inquiries under one parent, and the published rendering reassembles them. Nothing is built yet, so both shapes remain cheap — the diagram's 0..1 records the modelled answer, not the settled one.

The record and how things get into it

The plane
The server side of one group's instance. A Worker plus a database plus object storage. Everything the record knows lives here.
Capture
Fetching a document a public body published and storing the exact bytes, with a record of what address served them, when, and what came back.
Provenance chain
The account of how bytes reached us. A direct fetch from the publisher is one hop; recovering a page that has gone dark from a web archive is two. Longer chains are honest, not hidden.
Op
One operation the plane offers — the API surface. 108 exist. “An op with no caller” means functioning capability no screen can reach.
The gate / checks
A catalogue of numbered rules a bundle must satisfy. The gate runs them and names which ones failed and on what.
Promote
The single write authority. Nothing enters the record except through it, which is why one place can enforce every rule.

How strongly something is held — and what the “something” is

Three things carry a grade: a capture, a connection and a leg. A claim is never graded directly; its strength is derived from its legs, which is what stops anyone declaring a claim strong.

Identifier
A string the source itself mints to name one thing uniquely — an ordinance number, a contract number, a parcel number, a case number. It matters for grading because an identifier is designed not to collide, so finding the same one at both ends is strong evidence the two documents mean the same thing.
Correspondence
A match on attributes that line up rather than on a shared identifier — the same name, the same date, the same amount. Weaker than an identifier for one reason: two unrelated things can correspond by coincidence. Two contractors can share a name; two payments can share a sum.
Grade A–D
How something was established. A the source itself says so, in its own words. B an identifier the source uses, found in captured content at both ends. C correspondence rather than identity. D asserted with no captured basis — recorded as testimony, with an author and a date, and not discounted for being D. Grade is improvable, and raising it is the clearest thing the system can offer a case.
Connection
An edge between two pieces of information, running through the entity they share. The central construct, and the one most of a record's value sits in: documents answer nothing until something relates them. A connection carries its own grade, its own basis, and the name of whoever asserted it. Its grade is built from how each end was established — both ends measured the same way, so combining those two is legitimate. Two limits as built, both real: a connection runs between whole documents, though the evidence underneath it is anchored to specific passages — the anchor is discarded when the connection is formed, so following one lands you on a 300-page PDF rather than on the line. And it runs through a named thing, so two documents about the same idea that share no named entity cannot yet be connected.
Citation
A reference from one piece of content to another — an act of pointing, carrying no claim about the world and therefore no grade. Distinct from a connection, and easy to confuse with one. As built it runs document to document; a reference within one document has nowhere to live yet.
Claim
A proposition — something that can be true or false. What an inquiry adopts as its answer, and the thing legs bear on. Never graded directly.
The two graded things
Grade is applied to two different kinds of thing, and they are never combined. Capture grade belongs to a document — how its bytes were read in. Connection grade belongs to an edge — how a stated relationship was established. A document can be captured perfectly and connected weakly, and the reader needs to be told which.
Strength
Two independent measurements, reported side by side and never composed. The capture measurement is the weakest capture grade among every document the conclusion reaches; the connection measurement is the weakest connection grade among every edge it rests on. They range over different populations and answer different questions — are these the bytes the body published? and are these things really related? — so neither can stand in for the other, and no rendering reduces them to one letter.
Basis / leg
What a claim rests on. Each leg points at a document or at another claim, and carries a role — supports or cuts against. A leg that cuts against the conclusion counts toward strength exactly as a supporting one does; that is invariant 7 made storable.
Not load-bearing
A leg with no established grade contributes nothing and costs nothing. It is not weighed, not averaged, does not drag the conclusion down, and does not stop it being graded. It stays in the basis, named, as something present but not yet carrying weight. The conclusion is graded on the legs that do carry weight, which is honest because those are the only legs it rests on. Every ungraded leg is named, however many there are.
Unrated
The boundary case: when no leg has an established grade, the conclusion rests on nothing established and has no computed strength at all — naming every leg that is why. It may still be published, as unrated. Punishing honesty and laundering weakness are both refused, which is why an ungraded leg is neither ranked below the worst grade nor quietly ignored.
Hunch
An assumption held ahead of its evidence — the working form of “I believe this and have not yet shown it”. It takes two shapes: a connection asserted before the evidence exists, graded temporarily high enough to make disconnected material traversable; and an unsupported claim added to an inquiry, which the system treats as a standing objective it can pursue on the member's behalf. Either way it is temporary declared bias — declared bias has its own entry under “Meaning”, below — and it is the one kind of bias that must be cleared before publication, because a hunch asserts what it has not earned.

Acts and their weight

Act
Anything a member does that goes on the record. Always the same four-beat motion (Part 1).
Rung
How heavy an act is, visible before it runs: reversible · reasoned (a justification is required and never prefilled) · weighty (undoing it is itself an act, not a back button) · attested (public, requires a key — the one irreversible rung: what it publishes stays published, and correction happens forward, by another attested act; see the note below).
Weight
A separate question: how an act applies to a set. refuse all-or-nothing · report proceeds and says what moved · per-item each succeeds or is retained with its reason.
Conclude
Turning a question into a finding. Requires an authored conclusion, at least one leg of basis, and a falsifier.
Falsifier
What would prove the conclusion wrong. Required, and there is no way around it. Three reasons, and the first is the one that matters: a claim nothing could falsify is not a finding, it is a position, and this project's stated threat is its own overclaiming. Naming one also forces the claim to be stated precisely enough to be checkable — a vague claim has no falsifier. And it leaves the record a trigger: if the falsifying thing later turns up, everyone can see the conclusion is owed a second look.
Publish / ratify / attest
Signing a case with a key so a stranger can verify it without trusting the group — or us. The one irreversible act in the system: what it publishes stays published. Correction happens forward — a new edition, a signed withdrawal — never by unwinding: see below.
Completeness statement
What the group left out of the case, and why — authored, never prefilled, and checked byte-for-byte against the previous version so it cannot be carried forward unchanged. This is the gate that makes a published case more than a published finding.
Edition
A published case can be revised. Republishing increments an edition, and the case is treated as a separate document — earlier editions stay published, stay verifiable, and still say what they said, so a reader who relied on one is never left holding a hash that has quietly changed underneath them.
Publishing is the one irreversible act — and correction is always possible

Publishing is irreversible: a published edition can never be unpublished. It answers forever, to anyone, without the group's cooperation — that permanence is the whole value of the act, and it is why the ceremony around it is the heaviest in the system. Nothing else in the record is irreversible in this sense.

What is always possible is correction — and it moves forward, never by unwinding. People make mistakes and misread documents, so every path forward exists: a published case is revised as a new edition, a separate document, while every earlier edition stays published and still says what it said. An attestation is withdrawn by another attested act — dated, attributed, signed — with both standing in the record. A finding is rescinded to an open inquiry by removing its claim.

So a reader who relied on edition one is never left holding a hash that changed underneath them, and a group that got something wrong is never trapped by it. The record can always change its position; it can never silently unsay one.

Meaning, and the machinery that finds it

Entity / subject registry
The record's list of the things it talks about, so that a thing keeps one identity across every document that mentions it. Each entry is a person, body, office, ordinance, parcel, contract or fund, and carries: its aliases, because the same fund is “Sewer Enterprise Fund” in the budget and “Fund 6100” in the ledger; and its declared relationships to other entries, each justified and citable. It is what makes “everything concerning Ordinance 13579” answerable rather than a text search, and it is the far end of every connection — an edge runs between two documents through the entry they share. A relationship the group declares here is constitutive, not evidence: it defines how the record uses a word, so it is not graded as though it were a weak claim about the world.
Reading
What a machine extracted from a document's text: which entities it names, what facts it states.
Progression
A model of how something is supposed to flow — a meeting has an agenda before it and minutes within N days after. Declaring it is what makes a gap detectable: an award with no solicitation is only a finding if somebody said a solicitation was expected.
Declared bias
The group states what it already believes, so a reader can discount it. A statement may raise scrutiny, constrain inference and assert evidenced patterns; it may never issue a verdict. The construct that fights undeclared distortion is held to a higher standard than the distortion. Bias is public: it accompanies every published case produced under it, in the artifact rather than by reference — a declared position a reader cannot see is not a declaration.
Bias debt
When a bias is added or changed, existing analysis is marked as owing a re-run. Debt is disclosed; it does not block publication — the reader is told what the analysis has not yet been re-run under, and can weigh it. The single exception is a hunch, which must be cleared before publishing. The line between them: a hunch inflates a grade, so publishing over one states a strength that is not true; ordinary bias only frames interpretation, which disclosure fully answers.

One structural decision, four resolutions from an adversarial review, and five standing rulings. Most of Parts 1–5 is one of these being worked out.

The collapse — three concepts are one object

An earlier design had a focus (an open question), a finding (a conclusion), and a case (something published) as three separate object types. They are one recursive object, inquiry, with phases: open → concluded → published. The member-facing words inquiry, finding and case remain, but they name phases and are never stored separately.

The reason is recursion — and it is recursion rather than nesting, which is not a quibble. A line of inquiry can rest on other lines of inquiry. “Was the sewer fund misused?” rests on “Was the $2.1m transfer authorised?”, which rests on documents. Three separate types could not express that; one recursive type does it with no new mechanism. This is the single largest change in the design, and most of Parts 2 and 3 trace back to it.

Nesting would mean each inquiry sits inside one parent and is part of it. That is not the shape. An inquiry that supports another is independent — it can support several at once, it has its own conclusion, and it can be published on its own. So the structure is a graph, not a tree of boxes inside boxes. The proof is that the design needs a rule (R3, below) forbidding an inquiry to rest on itself: under nesting that could not happen in the first place, and no such rule would be necessary.

Note what the sentence above deliberately does not say: not a claim rests on a claim. The thing that rests on things is an inquiry — and since inquiry, finding and case are three phases of one object, everything here is equally true of all three. A published case can be the basis of a fresh question, which is what a working record looks like.

Four resolutions, forced by an adversarial pass

R1 · An ungraded leg carries no weight, and costs none
Ignoring an ungraded leg launders weakness; ranking it below the bottom grade punishes honesty. So it does neither: it contributes nothing and it drags nothing down, staying in the basis as a named leg that is not yet load-bearing, while the conclusion is graded on the legs that are. Every ungraded leg is named, however many there are. Only when no leg is graded does the conclusion have no strength at all — then it is unrated, and may still be published as such.
R2 · Capture and connection are measured separately
They grade different kinds of thing — a capture grade belongs to a document, a connection grade to an edge — so nothing averages, mixes or collapses them, and no rendering may reduce them to one letter. A conclusion reports two measurements over two different populations: the weakest document it reaches, and the weakest relationship it relies on. A document captured directly is never the top capture grade — that claim is forbidden and enforced.
R3 · The basis graph is acyclic
An inquiry may not rest on itself, even transitively. Enforced at write, and the refusal names the cycle it found. Without it, two inquiries each resting on the other would make every strength computation run forever.
R4 · Division costs what severance costs
Because a conclusion is worth its weakest leg, a question mixing one strong claim with one thin one is worth the thin one — so the design lets a member divide such an inquiry into separate ones. This resolution prices it: removing a single leg (severance) requires an authored reason, so splitting the whole inquiry must too. Division records where every leg went, including the ones that cut against, and a published child names its parent and its siblings — a reader who can see one half can see the other exists.
Why R2 was the expensive one

Three of the sixteen files had drawn a case publishing at a single letter, with capture facts and connection facts under one unlabelled grade. Under R2, every one of those frames is wrong, and eleven build items changed. It also exposed a comment in the shipping code that named the two axes and treated their shared ranking as a feature — a design intent the resolution reverses.

Five standing rulings

  • A published case can be revised, as a numbered edition; earlier editions stay published and verifiable.
  • A case is shown to the body it is about, before it is published, so they can respond and the response is carried inside the published artifact — the practice professional auditors and journalists treat as mandatory, and which this design had listed as a hazard. But the requirement is the group's declared position, not the contact itself. A group that sought comment says so and prints what came back. A group that deliberately did not — because giving a hostile body advance notice can be a real risk — says so, and says why, inside the case. A mandate would be either broken quietly or would suppress the case; a declaration cannot be, and a reader can weigh the justification for themselves.
  • Claims about our own impact are held to the same standard as findings about a body. Absent outside evidence, an impact claim is unproven.
  • The hunch becomes a first-class construct: temporary declared bias, and the one kind of bias that must be cleared before publishing. Other bias is disclosed and travels with the case rather than blocking it.
  • A project declares the evidentiary strength its work requires, and the published case carries that declaration beside the strength it actually reached.

Sixteen files, about 1.1 MB, from thirteen research passes over two days. This page reports the reconciled design, not the raw passes.

Three of the passes — the core, evidence and output storyboards — were written in parallel by sessions that could not read each other, and they contradict each other. A reconciliation pass resolved 38 contradictions, quoting both sides and giving a reason for each, and found that 17 of 35 build items were changed by the resolutions while 18 were not. It added zero new items — every correction landed inside work that already existed, which is itself a finding: the resolutions changed what the items do, not what work exists.

The honest caveat, and it is not decorative

Every research pass in this study made at least one sharp claim that did not survive checking. Counts on this page were verified against the repository rather than taken from a pass. Where a pass could have written a plausible answer and instead stopped, the reconciliation records where it stopped and why — five such places are named, and they are in Part 4's open questions rather than quietly closed.

Part 1

The path

What the system asks a person to do, from the moment something bothers them to the moment a stranger can check their case.

The pathThe journey, end to end

Six stages, ten surfaces. The distinction that matters is carried by the nodes, not the edges: a dark teal node is an act — the member does it and it goes on the record — and a light node is a surface being read. The arrows only order the work. Follow them top to bottom through the sewer-fund example.

Two properties that are easy to lose, and both are load-bearing

  • The journey re-enters itself twice. A concluded finding becomes a leg of a larger question (S3c → S3a), and a published case becomes a leg of a later, larger inquiry (S9 → S3a). That is what the collapse bought. If either edge is missing, the record becomes a series of dead ends instead of something cumulative — each case starting from nothing, which is exactly how this work fails in shared folders today.
  • The consequence of an action re-enters as evidence (A6 → S3b). What the city says back — or does not say — is new information about how the system actually responds, as against how it is supposed to. This is the loop that turns a group from a publisher into an observer of the body's behaviour, and it is entirely unbuilt.

The pathThe ACT — one motion, not seven dialogs

A non-technical member cannot learn seven different dialogs. Every act in the system is therefore the same four beats, wherever it appears — it is not a screen, it instantiates inside S1, S3, S5, S8 and S10.

The same four beats, walked through once

The member is on the transfer inquiry and has just read the fund ledger. Beat one: they choose cite this document onto the inquiry — an option the surface did not invent; the plane publishes which acts are available here, so a button that exists is a button that can work. Beat two: before anything runs, the pre-flight shows what would be refused, in the plane's own words — if the ledger's bytes no longer match what was captured, the member learns it now, not from a failure after committing. Beat three: they author the reason — “the ledger shows the transfer leaving Fund 6100” — into a field that arrives empty and is never prefilled, because the reason is testimony and testimony is not suggested. Beat four: the receipt, with their name on it.

And then the record moves, visibly. One act, several honest side effects: a new leg now sits under the claim, graded by how the ledger connects; the claim's strength recomputes, and if this leg is now the weakest, the case says so by name; an event lands in the queue of every member watching this inquiry and its ancestors; and the act itself is on the record — who, what, when, and the authored reason — where it can be read for as long as the inquiry exists. Nothing happened silently, which is the entire design.

Why beat three matters more than it looks

The alternative — try it and read the error — trains a member to treat refusals as obstacles to get around. Showing the refusal first, in the plane's own words with the offending items named, makes the rule legible instead of adversarial. It is also why the commit control is absent rather than greyed out while an act would refuse: a disabled button invites a member to hunt for the trick that enables it.

Ruled 1 August · DEC-8

The pre-flight is plane-sourced always. A surface may render a refusal it received from the plane; it may never compute one. Two mechanisms: the plane publishes the refusal contract by default, and runs a dry-run op when a refusal turns on state the surface cannot see. The obvious test — “is this refusal knowable by the browser?” — is wrong, because a refusal is knowable by the browser exactly when the browser holds a copy of the rule, and a copy is drift waiting to happen.

The pathThe four hardest moments

Where the design earns its keep or fails. Each is a place a non-technical member meets something the system genuinely cannot simplify away — so the only question is how honestly it is presented.

MomentWhy it is hardWhat the surface does
Getting the bother in A member arrives with a worry, not a typed object. Asking them to choose between “question”, “problem” and “project” asks them to have already done the analysis they came here to do. They write the question in their own words. The type is inquiry and the phase is derived. Nothing asks them to classify.
Concluding, and naming the falsifier Stating what would prove you wrong is the least natural act in the product, and it is precisely what separates a finding from an opinion. The falsifier is offered as a selection first — pick the leg that would do it — and free text second. The conclusion is authored, empty, and unavoidable.
The weakest link, and division Weakest-link scoring means a question mixing one strong claim with one thin one is worth the thin one. Without a way to split it, the member's only options are to overclaim or stay silent. The strength consequence renders live as legs are selected — a weak leg visibly drags the chain down before anything is committed. Division is authored, apportions every leg including the ones that cut against, and discloses its siblings.
The exclusion statement “What did you leave out, and why” is the gate that makes a case more than a published finding — and it is trivially satisfiable by a checkbox or a copy-paste. The field ships empty, and the gate compares it byte-for-byte against the previous version so nothing can be carried forward unchanged. A gate that only checks presence is a checkbox.
Part 2

Underneath

What has to exist for Part 1 to work — the layers, the processes, and the states a member can actually land in.

UnderneathThe layer stack, and the rung with no owner

The system is built in layers, each allowed to know only about the ones below it. Nine layers; eight have an owner, a home and a defined interface. One does not exist.

The structural finding, stated plainly

The meaning layer produces grades and nothing consumes them. The record layer can certify a document and cannot certify a claim. The search layer cannot see meaning at all. Those are three views of one absence: there is no claim layer. A member can capture a document, extract what it says, connect it to an entity and search it — and cannot state a conclusion that rests on it.

The project's capability ladder — its ordered list of what the system is being built to be able to do, a different ladder from the act rungs in Part 1 — had no entry for it either. Two were added on 1 August, and the ladder now says plainly that they are not appended after the foundations: they are what the other eight entries serve.

What this means for scheduling

The genuinely serial chain is short. Eight of the thirty-six build items depend on nothing and could start today, which means the constraint is people, not dependencies — the reverse of the situation when capture was the bottleneck.

The empty cells, which are the findings

  • Documenting has no machine driver of any kind — data, event and clock are all zero. This is the sharpest single number in the study. Documenting is the middle of the path, and the record does nothing about it unattended: nothing ages a question that has stalled, nothing notices when the evidence under a conclusion has changed, nothing recomputes a strength — because there is no object for any of that to happen to. The empty row and the missing layer are the same finding seen twice.
  • Exploring has no machine driver either, and that is a design position, not a gap. A machine that decides what a member should look into is a recommendation engine, and a recommendation engine is the natural enemy of “less narrative”. The line to hold: the objectives engine (Part 0) does not cross it, because pursuing an unsupported claim the member authored is directed by the member's own words — that work lands under discovering, driven by the claim, not here. What stays refused is the machine choosing the member's questions for them.
  • Once something is published, the system stops thinking about it. Nothing ages a published case and nothing re-examines one whose evidence has moved. That is in direct tension with the ruling that a published case is an input, not a terminus — and it means a case can quietly become wrong with nobody notified.
  • Impacting has one machine process and it does not exist. An action carries a deadline that a check validates the shape of and nothing ever compares against the calendar. So the record can hold a statutory deadline and never notice it passing.

Three live defects worth seeing on their own

These are not gaps in an unbuilt design. They are in the shipping system now, and each was verified in the source during this study.

  • An uninvited member can see every project's identifier, title and state. The visibility check is applied on the search path only, so four other ways in bypass it — and the interface then rebuilds the same leak on the client by walking every project. Two defects that compound rather than duplicate.
  • Search silently degrades to a title-substring scan, and looks identical when it does. The fallback bypasses the visibility check and answers a much weaker question in the same interface, with nothing to tell the member which one they got.
  • The record asserts a counterparty it does not have. The intake screen writes the literal text “to be named” into every action's counterparty field, which satisfies the check that the field is non-empty — in the one object that reaches outside the system.

Settled, and still open

Seventeen decisions closed, and the six questions nobody should close by guessing.

SettledSeventeen decisions, none open

Decisions reach this register when they are genuinely the architect's: doctrine about what the record may claim, risk carrying his name, or effects on people outside the project. Mechanism, sequencing and scoping are decided by the session and recorded, not asked. Every entry carries what was running provisionally while it was open, so an open question never stops work.

DEC-4

Should the record extract text from scanned, image-only PDFs?

Yes — build it. The recommendation to accept the limit was overruled, correctly: coverage is not a volume question, and scanned-only is what a signed order, an exhibit or a deliberately-imaged release looks like. Verification against the rendered image is the grade ceiling, reachable from both machine reading and a publisher's text layer — because a text layer is itself an unverified transcription.

architect
DEC-12

May a published case be revised, or is publication final?

Revisable, as numbered editions, each a separate document; earlier editions stay published and verifiable. This turned a defect into a missing feature — the system was already keeping every published hash while overwriting the signature that attested it.

architect
DEC-13

Must a case be put to its subject before publication?

Yes — and the gate is the declaration, not the act. The group's position on contacting the subject is declared, justified, and carried inside the case as declared bias. A group with cause not to give notice says so and says why, and a reader weighs that. Backed by professional standards: government audit practice gives a body 7–30 days on a draft and prints its disagreement alongside the finding.

architect
DEC-14

Are claims about our own impact held to the same standard as findings about a body?

The same standard. Absent outside supporting evidence, an impact claim is unproven. Outcomes — a hearing convened, a study commissioned — are dated first-party facts and stay fully claimable. The evidence forced this: studies of information-and-accountability interventions pool to roughly zero effect.

architect
DEC-15

Where does a connection's grade come from — earned by the system, or asserted by the member?

Both, and the fork was false. A hunch is temporary declared bias: asserted high enough during investigation to make disconnected material traversable, and it is bias debt that must clear before publication. The gate already existed in the bias doctrine, written for something else entirely.

architect
DEC-16

With questions resting on other questions, what does a member's queue group by?

Every ancestor, with one shared resolution. The unit of state is the event — one state, several homes — which dissolves the flooding objection without a second mechanism.

architect
DEC-17

What makes an unsupported case harder to state than a supported one?

The project declares the strength its work requires, and the case publishes it beside the strength actually reached. Not a system-wide floor, which would refuse honest weak findings, and not a reader-based gate, which would be a prior by role.

architect
DEC-6

May a declared-bias statement name a person, or only an institution?

Any registry kind is legal. A narrow list admits office — the kind most prone to becoming a prior against whoever holds it — and refuses the specific, citable ones. Refusing the subject does not remove the bias; it removes the disclosure of it.

DEC-7

Who may act on a task, and who may claim an unassigned one?

Both halves kept, and a hole closed: an automated credential could resolve an unassigned task. A daemon cannot close somebody's work — but it could close nobody's work, and closing is the act.

Plane-sourced always. A surface renders refusals; it never computes them. The existing copy's one defence turned out to be a guard that does not read the authority it claims to check.

Yes — decidable without escalating, because an earlier ruling supplies the principle: the expectation was authored by the group, so acting on it executes their own decision rather than substituting for one.

What is queued to be built, in what order, and what I want from this review.

OrderThirty-six items, and the chain that decides the calendar

Every item names its milestone, what it sits behind, what it depends on, and an acceptance condition that is a command that either passes or fails rather than a judgement — plus the deliberate break that must make the test fail, so a test that proves nothing is caught.

The first item, and the one that must not queue behind it

The inquiry type is the single highest-leverage item. It unblocks 21 of the other 34 transitively, three surfaces that cannot exist without it, and the first consumer the A–D grades have ever had. It depends on nothing.

Publishing what-may-be-done takes the parallel slot, for a compounding reason rather than a preference: every act screen built before it accrues its own copy of the plane's rules, which must later be deleted. Delaying the type costs existence — three surfaces simply cannot be built. Delaying the other costs rework, which is bounded and countable. Rework beats absence; and here the order takes both, because no act surface appears until after it exists.

In the order I would take them. Everything here is cheap to change now and expensive once the surfaces exist.
