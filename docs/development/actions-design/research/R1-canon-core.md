### (b) Constructs, terms and rules

**§1 What the layer is for (L5–9).** Where "a group turns what it has learned into what it does". It runs "from the first suspicion that something is wrong or working, through planning, preparing, deciding and sending, to tracking the response and recording how it ended". Functional Architecture v3 calls it "Turn findings into outputs: work products, publications, escalations, communications, and ongoing tracking." Groups act differently on the same record: "a reporter reports, an activist works to fix, a lawyer supports or changes a claim, an oversight body refers, an administrator responds". A group may take many actions or none, and "may act on a success as well as on a breach" (Bob, 2026-09-29).

**§2 The contract (L11–13).** "An action rests on the record, and one asserting a breach rests on a published finding and a standard held in the record. The group plans and decides every act; the AI proposes and prepares, and never files or sends. Compliance is recorded as carefully as noncompliance. Every deadline names its basis."

**§3 The constructs (L15–27).** Seven, each with its home module:

| construct | definition (abridged, key words verbatim) | module |
|---|---|---|
| Standard | what a government act is measured against: statute, regulation, ordinance, court decision or order, adopted policy or public commitment, "held as captured text with its citation and period in force" | `standards` |
| Determination | "a member's determination, resting on published findings, that a named government act is compliant, noncompliant or unclear against named standards, per standard" | `conformance` |
| Consequence | "what a breach did and to whom" (a class, fund, program, service or body, or a person a document names; published only with consent or where DR 6 allows; a whistleblower source stays protected, K1484), "computed from the record or assessed by a member, with causation as its own finding; whether each part is addressed" | `consequences` |
| Action plan | "a project's working material for deciding what to do about one or more matters, suspected or determined: options, their disposition, up to three scenarios with checkpoints a member judges" | `action-plans` |
| Action | "one outward engagement: its kind, addressee, risk tier, clock (each deadline with its basis), what it rests on, lifecycle, correspondence, and its own outcome" | `actions` |
| Filing and communication | what the group sends, prepared from the record: "Tier 1–2 filings from the profile's templates, the Tier 3 counsel packet for named counsel, and communication drafts; each approved by a member and sent by the member's own hand, carrying the in-band stamp" | `filings` |
| Escalation | "the seven-stage protocol that pursues one noncompliant determination until compliance is restored and its consequences are addressed" | `escalation` |

How they connect (L27): inquiry opens a plan (suspected) → publication and a determination make matters determined → consequences measure a breach → a chosen option starts an action → a filing/communication prepares what the action sends → a breach action joins its determination's escalation → replies return as correspondence → an action resolves with its own outcome → the plan closes by a member's act. "Monitoring (layer 10) watches the actions' clocks and the escalations' triggers and tells members; the queue carries the reminders."

**§4 The rules (L29–43).**
1. **Humans decide.** A member takes every committing act: "declaring a standard, determining, assessing a consequence, choosing an option, approving, sending, advancing a stage, resolving, closing". The machine "may find, compare, compute, propose and draft, always labelled as machine work" (DEC-24, DEC-27; Roadmap §10).
2. **The gate is at the outward act, not the reasoning** (DEC-26), "and a member may pass it openly" (Bob, 2026-09-30). A plan may rest on unestablished premises, "shown as hunch debt". A breach-asserting action "is refused by default unless it rests on a live noncompliant determination"; a member may proceed "only by an attributed act with a stated reason", and the action and everything prepared from it carry that disclosure. "No tool has the final word" (Requirement 12). "An action that seeks evidence (a records request, a request for comment) is never gated."
3. **No significance, no score.** Whether/how urgently to act is a member's judgment, recorded only in acts and reasons; "No field holds significance, severity, priority or a score."
4. **Compliance counts.** A compliant determination may start a plan: "recognition and success stories are actions."
5. **Every deadline names its basis, and members are told without being nagged** (DEC-10, DEC-69, DEC-70, DEC-94). A deadline reminder "is the member's own request, set when the option is chosen from defaults the member sees"; it fires as asked and offers another reminder or none; overdue notifies once; nearing changes display only; "no outside channel"; nothing repeated unless asked (K613–K615).
6. **Addressees are roles, not people.** A government office by role and body; a reporter or outlet; an organisation or another civic group by role and organisation; or a described audience; "never a private individual"; the page may show who holds the office on the date (K1484, C2 row 5). "An action asserting a breach is addressed to an office."
7. **Nothing leaves by a system path.** "The instance transmits nothing." A member sends by the venue's own means and records the sending with the bytes sent; addressed material carries the in-band stamp (Publication §3 rule 9). "A plan is never published (DEC-25); a counsel packet is never published and never fileable as it stands."
8. **No catalogue, no budgets.** Suggested options come "from reasoning over the matter and from the group's own earlier plans, never from a fixed list; the plan holds no costs, assignees or hours" (Bob, 2026-09-29).
9. **The doctrine's limits.** No position on what policy should be (OP1). Political accountability "asks officials to act on a breach, requests oversight and audits, testifies, and supports legislation that restores or enforces an existing requirement; lobbying is an option only for that. Policy advocacy and candidate support are not actions." Any group may use Civicsmith; one with a stake discloses it (D6).
10. **The work varies, not the person.** A project may declare its kind of work (reporting, fixing, legal, oversight, other), shaping only what the assistant suggests. "No attribute of a member or user gates, filters or orders anything" (DEC-17, DEC-54); people in the record may be searched/sorted by held facts and ranked by measured quantity (K1484, K1471); a judgment score only as a hypothesis-layer signal (K1473).
11. **Jurisdiction lives in data.** Kinds, venues, templates, offices, legal organisations, deadlines and holidays come from a jurisdiction profile; "a missing fact reads undetermined, never a default".
12. **Hope for good faith; prepare for opposition** (Bob, 2026-09-30; DR 13, 14). Bad actors identified "by evidence, never by role"; the system is "fully prepared for responses that amount to war: stonewalling, retaliation, discrediting, legal harassment". "Every plan is checked for a branch that answers a hostile response"; pressure against the group "is recorded as evidence" (OP8) and can open an inquiry; what counsel/another group needs to carry on (counsel packet, published case, evidence package) "survives the group's disruption".
13. **The venue sets the standard of evidence** (Bob, 2026-09-30). Federal courts accept co-attested Grade B since FRE 902(13)–(14) (2017). "No action is refused for its evidence grade." A filing resting on a contestable grade says so; every filing and counsel packet shows each exhibit's grade and, where the profile states it, the venue's standard. DEC-81's "Grade A stays the ceiling" is read as "the highest grade the product offers, not a minimum".

**§5 The contradictions, reconciled (L45–64).** Sixteen, each with authority. Action-relevant outcomes:
- #1 only a breach action needs a published finding (records requests, request for comment, review copy may precede any finding).
- #2 Requirement 7's mechanical "next stage activates" → "a met trigger is proposed, with its age; a member advances or declines with a reason" (K102).
- #3 escalation has **seven** stages everywhere (K14).
- #4 clock: "an action's clock entries are stored with their basis, overdue is derived when read, and the one machine write is the mechanical pending→overdue mark; a records request's next-stage due date lives on its correspondence entry" (`actions` R12, R25, R33; `monitoring` R34).
- #5 free-text counterparty → office by role and body (`actions` R9; D1).
- #6 kinds: product kinds are "those whose rules it enforces (records request, request for comment, other); every other kind comes from the profile; old kinds read as written" (`actions` R10).
- #7 **three outcome vocabularies kept apart**: an action's **resolution** (complied, denied, escalated, withdrawn, completed); a correspondence entry's **outcome** (granted, denied, partial, reversed, affirmed, none stated); an escalation's **evaluation** of a response (complied, partial, denied, none).
- #8 edges `action_basis`, `responds_to`, `references` added to State Rules' closed vocabulary.
- #9 State Rules' distribution ladder is publication's; "this layer sends nothing through distribution".
- #10 risk tier: a kind's tier from the profile and an action's tier set by a member; a filing governed by the stricter; "undetermined is never read as 1" (`filings` R2).
- #11 plan vs escalation: "the plan is the strategic layer above; each determined breach keeps its own escalation; a legal option joins it; the plan never moves an escalation".
- #12 audience stays a reader of a published case; user-type difference lives in the project's declared kind of work (D4).
- #13 this document replaces Case Making's action sections as authority.
- #14 "Layer 3" ambiguity: Action is build layer 9; FA's "Layer 3: Action" is the functional layer.
- #15 "war"/"protection network" vs good faith: both hold (rule 12).
- #16 DEC-26 vs Req 12 and Req 8: refuse by default, attributed override with reason, disclosed; evidence-seeking never gated.

### (c) Doctrine fences

- AI never files or sends; never takes a committing act; machine work labelled (§2, rule 1).
- Breach-asserting action: refused by default without a live noncompliant determination; attributed, reasoned override disclosed on everything prepared from it (rule 2). Evidence-seeking never gated.
- No significance/severity/priority/score field (rule 3).
- Compliance recorded with equal care; success stories are actions (rule 4).
- Reminders only as the member asks; overdue once; no outside channel; no nagging (rule 5).
- Addressees are roles, never a private individual; breach action addressed to an office (rule 6).
- Instance transmits nothing; member sends by own hand and records bytes; in-band stamp; plan never published; counsel packet never published and never fileable as is (rule 7).
- No fixed catalogue of options; no costs, assignees, hours in a plan (rule 8).
- No policy advocacy, no candidate support; lobbying only to restore/enforce an existing requirement; stake disclosed (rule 9).
- No member/user attribute gates, filters or orders anything; project's kind of work shapes only suggestions (rule 10).
- Jurisdiction in data; missing = undetermined (rule 11).
- Bad actors by evidence, never role; hostile-response branch checked; pressure recorded as evidence (rule 12).
- No action refused for evidence grade; contestable grade disclosed (rule 13).
- Consequence: a named person published only with consent or per DR 6; whistleblower source protected (§3).
- Nothing non-public leaves by a system path (DEC-31, §7.5).

**§2 An investigation is a project (L48–59).** Objective = what the project is trying to establish; "**The objective is the measure throughout, and the evidence decides it.**" An investigation "gathers information to make a determination: whether a contract award was made properly …". ## 5. Tensions or inconsistencies across these documents

1. **Breach gate scope.** Case Making §6a (L1052–1053) says "An outward act's pre-flight REFUSES when its step is not `established`" and §4 (L966) that a step "may be blocked because the finding has not reached the project's declared strength". Action rule 2 (L32) gates only breach-asserting actions, on a "live noncompliant determination" (not strength), allows an attributed override, and never gates evidence-seeking actions. Action §5 #13 makes Action authoritative, but Case Making's sentences still read as live.
2. **Plan structure.** Case Making §3 (L934–946): a decision tree of STEPS with "outcome-keyed branching", dependencies, deadlines, resources, disposition chosen · declined-with-reason · done · blocked. Action §3 (L22): "options, their disposition, up to three scenarios with checkpoints a member judges". The two shapes differ (tree of steps vs options plus ≤3 scenarios); "checkpoint" has no definition in these four documents.
3. **Resources and costs.** Case Making §6b (L1078) gives each plan step a free-form RESOURCES list ("money, member hours, expertise"). Action rule 8 (L38): "the plan holds no costs, assignees or hours"; MONEY ladder: "action-plans R26 (no budget or cost key in a plan)". Investigation §3 (L71) lets an *investigation step* carry a money cost ("a records-request copying fee"), and a records request a step needs "is started as an action". So a fee can be costed on the step but not in the plan. Whether a resources list survives is unclear.
4. **Action kinds.** Case Making §4 (L960–962): seven shipped `action_kind`s (`cpra_request`, `grand_jury`, `controller_referral`, `public_comment`, `media`, `litigation_support`, `other`) are "that repertoire's first draft". Action §5 #6: product kinds are only records request, request for comment, other; the rest come from the profile; old kinds "read as written". Case Making D-149: law-neutral `records_request` "ADOPTED as the direction" and "NOT BUILT"; `cpra_request` unchanged.
5. **Variation by user type.** Case Making §4 (L957–959): plans vary "by USER TYPE (`AUDIENCES.md` §0)". Action rule 10 / §5 #12: the project's declared kind of work varies, never the person; no member attribute gates anything.
6. **Lifecycle and outcome vocabularies.** Case Making §2 (L151): states `planned → active → awaiting_response → resolved | abandoned`. Action §5 #7: resolution `complied, denied, escalated, withdrawn, completed`, plus correspondence outcomes and escalation evaluations. Action §6 adds resolution `completed` to State Rules. How states map to resolutions (e.g. `abandoned` vs `withdrawn`) is not stated in these documents.
7. **Clocks: stored vs derived vs never encoded.** Case Making D-147/D-149: "The clock is never encoded"; "The plane encodes no law's rules". Action §5 #4: clock entries are stored with their basis, and there is "the one machine write … the mechanical pending→overdue mark". Ladders §2 TIME: "Computed dates are derived on read, never stored", and the `overdue:` flag is named as a thing that "blocks" ("a read that caches 'now'"). Ladders TIME L2 / §4.4 plan profile rule sets (CPRA, FOIA, etc.) that compute deadlines, risk "encoded law going stale (D-149)", contained by citation, horizon and member adoption. These three positions still need reconciling in design.
8. **Overdue as notice vs finding.** Action rule 5: overdue "notifies once". Ladders §3/§10: an overdue *duty occurrence* is a "Noticed" FINDING-kind queue item, never a violation. Case Making §8: non-response is an "OUTCOME … sayable at full strength". Ladder TIME H2: non-response is "L1 (as a fact), L4 (as a leg)". Case Making §8 says an absence-as-leg is deferred and must be "an observation-as-leg, never an action-as-leg". The ladder's "L4 as a leg" sits ahead of that deferral.
9. **Counterparty vs addressee.** Case Making §2: free-text `counterparty`. Action rule 6: office by role and body. Ladders ORG L0 still lists "an action's counterparty" as free text; L2 says "the add flow's named addressee collides with actions R9 (`app.html:19983`)". So built UI and canon disagree today.
10. **Determination timing vs investigation objective.** Investigation §2 frames an investigation as gathering "information to make a determination", and "the matter can be determined" when the record holds what the objective needs. Action §3 and Ladders (K102; LAW L4 "determination stays after publication") require published findings first. That gives the sequence investigate → publish → determine → act on a breach, which Investigation §2 states only implicitly.
11. **Plan publication.** Action rule 7 says a plan is never published (DEC-25). Case Making's incomplete list still says "DEC-25 deferred with its provisional". This is consistent in substance but stale in Case Making.
12. **Escalation trigger source.** Action §3: escalation pursues "one noncompliant determination". Ladders COURTS L3: "A breach of an order feeds `conformance` and `escalation` as any duty does". The ladder implies duties feed escalation, but by Action an escalation exists only through a determination. Ladder ORG D3/O1 has escalation R12 follow `oversees`/`appoints` lines; Action is silent on how the next addressee is chosen.
13. **Consequences only after a breach.** Action rule 4: compliance counts, and success stories are actions. Consequences are defined only for a breach (Action §3; ANALYSIS L1 `CONSEQUENCE_NOT_NONCOMPLIANT`). Nothing measures the effect of a compliant act or a success.
14. **Joint action.** Action §8: deferred, cross-instance machinery undesigned. Ladders ORG L5 plans "Cross-group coordination keyed on bodies" and shared packs. These are planned at different levels with no link between them.
15. **Resources/"hours" vs no measuring members.** Case Making's resources include "member hours". Investigation §3/§5 and Ladders forbid measuring members (no per-member figures). An hours list could be read as a per-member measure.
16. **Machine-written events in the "what we did" lane.** Ladders EVENTS L2: the group's acts read via `registerEventSource` "filled by `actions`, `publication`, `docket` and `escalation`". Action rule 7: the member records the sending. The lane is a read, not a write, so this is consistent, but no document says which action lifecycle moments appear in the lane.
17. **Reminders vs Investigation waits.** Action rule 5: a reminder is set "when the option is chosen from defaults the member sees". Investigation §3: a step's by-when date with basis, and a reminder "stays hers alone". TIME B2's "remind me the day before" is the same need. It is not stated whether a step's wait and an action's clock are one mechanism.
18. **Surface numbering.** Case Making names S10 THE ACTION PAGE and S11 THE ACTION PLAN, "Ten surfaces are specified today" (stated as dated). Action §7.8 binds named sketches (plan page, start-and-send, tier 2 filing draft, tier 3 counsel packet). The S-numbers are stale.

## 6. Terms with multiple senses

- **action**: (i) the group's one outward engagement (Action §3); (ii) Bob's word for world happenings, renamed **events** (Ladders §5B L501, §5B.1 quote "how actions are related"); (iii) "government action" or "action by some other person or organization" judged for conformance (Case Making L890–891); (iv) "act", meaning any member act that commits the group (Action rule 1); (v) the build/functional layer "Action" = layer 9 vs FA "Layer 3" (Action §5 #14).
- **step**: (i) an investigation step, "work done in pursuit of an answer", "not an action" (Investigation §3); (ii) a candidate STEP in an action plan, which "produces an `action`" when taken (Case Making §2–§3); (iii) the start-and-send sketch's "step order" (Action §7.8). Investigation's D23 explicitly owes a name separating investigation work from the action plan.
- **plan / planning**: (i) the action plan `PLN-` (Action §3); (ii) the AI run mode "plan" (`deployed: false`; Ladders TIME L5, QUESTIONS cost note); (iii) "Planning", new AI work for interview → questions and steps (Investigation §10); (iv) the `## Plan` heading of an action bundle (Case Making §2).
- **outcome**: (i) an action's own outcome/resolution (Action §3, §5 #7); (ii) a correspondence entry's outcome (granted … none stated); (iii) an escalation's evaluation; (iv) a step's outcome per question (helped / dead end) (Investigation §3); (v) a plan step's possible OUTCOMES for branching (Case Making §3); (vi) DEC-14's impact "OUTCOME" (non-response as a dated fact); (vii) an act of discretion's outcome (Ladders §6B.5).
- **resolution / resolve**: an action's resolution; a contradiction's RESOLVE mechanism and DEC-76 resolution kinds; entity "resolution" (matching to the registry, grades A–D).
- **escalation / escalated**: the seven-stage protocol vs the resolution value `escalated`.
- **determination / determine**: conformance's per-standard determination (Action §3, "a determination, not a judgment"); the purpose of an investigation "to make a determination" (Investigation §2); "undetermined" as a value.
- **standard**: (i) Action's Standard (law, policy, commitment held); (ii) members' word for any held law, policy or commitment (Ladders B20); (iii) STANDARDS construct = documented technical standards (§6C); (iv) "standard of evidence" set by a venue (Action rule 13); (v) "short of the standard", the project's declared strength or bar (Case Making §6a).
- **finding**: a concluded inquiry or published finding; the queue's reserved FINDING kind `temporal-expectation-due` shown as "Noticed" (Ladders §3); "a finding about the record" vs "about the world".
- **case**: the group's published production (DEC-72); "court case" renamed "proceeding" (Ladders §7.1, "taken words"); Bob's "make a case".
- **docket**: the group's public response log (DEC-116) vs a court's register.
- **obligation**: a body's duty (members see "obligation", DEC-107); the queue's internal code `OBLIGATION` = a member's to-do; banned as a money word in Civicsmith's voice (MONEY C3, K1486).
- **matter / subject**: "Matters" is the member word for what a plan addresses (DEC-114); "Subject" is an entity on the Subjects screen; the internal requirement term is still "subject"; Investigation uses "the matter can be determined".
- **counterparty / addressee / partners**: free-text counterparty (Case Making), role-based addressee (Action rule 6), `partners` (joint action, Action §8), `group` addressee kind (D1).
- **clock / deadline / due date / window**: an action's clock entries (Action §3); the `action-clocks` module; the records-request `due_by`/`due_cite` (Case Making D-147); a due date's four basis kinds, only `rule` being a "deadline the law sets" (Ladders §3); the group's own `window`/checkpoint.
- **risk tier**: per kind (profile) vs per action (member), the stricter governing a filing (Action §5 #10).
- **monitoring / watch / follow**: layer-10 monitoring of clocks and escalation triggers (Action §3); the `monitoring` module watching pages (`per_meeting`, `NOT_MONITORED`); "following" a question to receive finds (Investigation §5); the `following` module for portals (Ladders §6B L5); following a proceeding or a body (COURTS, EVENTS); "watch" a lead or a project gone quiet (Investigation §3, §7); standing questions (QUESTIONS L5).
- **review / reviewer**: the review copy (Publication); reviewers of a case (Investigation §11); a guide reviewed by another member; `## Review Notes` on an action; a policy's `review_due`.
- **support / supported**: plan element support status (established / short / hypothetical); a find "supporting" a question; activists "supporting when they can".
- **consequence**: Action's Consequence construct (what a breach did); Case Making's loop "action → consequence → back in as evidence" (the response to the group's own action, D-128).
- **noticed**: a machine signal or overdue occurrence in the queue ("Noticed"), vs ordinary notification (Action rule 5).
