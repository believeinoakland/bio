## U16 · Follow-up on outcomes: did the city do what it said?

### 1. Situation

The group's earlier work produced a published case. The case finds that the City of Oakland's Finance Department (the Controller, a counterparty in the first profile) spent sewer-service fee revenue on general-fund purposes, against the ordinance that restricts that fund. This is illustrative. The Roadmap's origin story is a sewer-fund matter, but no facts about it are asserted here.

From that case:
- members recorded a **noncompliant determination** against the restricting ordinance;
- they recorded a **consequence**: $X moved out of the fund in FY2024–25, computed from the budget lines;
- an **escalation** is open on the determination;
- the group sent a **grand jury complaint** (profile kind `grand_jury`, Tier 1) to the **Alameda County Civil Grand Jury**.

In June 2027 the grand jury publishes a report with a finding and a recommendation: "the City should restore the diverted amount to the sewer fund and publish a quarterly fund report". Within the statutory period, the **Oakland City Council** adopts its response. On the restoration it answers "has not yet been implemented, but will be implemented … by 31 March 2028". On the quarterly report it answers "requires further analysis".

Separately, the **City Auditor**'s follow-up report on an older audit of the same fund lists one recommendation as "partially implemented".

The group's questions:
- Did the City do what it said?
- When do we stop watching?
- What does "consequences addressed" mean here?
- How does the plan close, and what does it record?

### 2. What real practitioners do

**The grand jury's mandated reply.**
- Penal Code §933(c): the governing body of a public agency comments on a final report to the presiding judge within 90 days. An elected county officer or agency head responds within 60 days (verify which city officers, beyond the Council, are bound).
- Penal Code §933.05(b) gives four allowed answers to each recommendation:
  1. implemented, with a summary;
  2. not yet implemented, but will be, "with a timeframe for implementation";
  3. requires further analysis, with the scope of the analysis and a date it will be ready for discussion, "not to exceed six months from the date of publication of the grand jury report";
  4. will not be implemented because it is not warranted or not reasonable, with an explanation.
- Answer 2 has no cap on how far away its date may be. The six-month cap belongs only to answer 3 ([Penal Code §933.05](https://california.public.law/codes/penal_code_section_933.05); county respondent instructions, e.g. [Santa Cruz County](https://www.santacruzcountyca.gov/Portals/0/County/GrandJury/GJ2009_responses/Instructions.htm)).
- The grand jury cannot enforce implementation. A later grand jury may choose to report on compliance with earlier responses; some California grand juries publish "compliance" or "continuity" reports, e.g. [Placer County](https://www.placer.courts.ca.gov/sites/default/files/Compliance%20%20Accountability%20Report%20FINAL.pdf). Whether Alameda's does so regularly needs to be checked (verify).
- Real failure points:
  - a response that answers "will be implemented" with no date, or with a vague one;
  - a "further analysis" answer whose six months pass with no report;
  - nobody comparing the response with what actually happened.

**Auditors' recommendation follow-up.**
- City auditors keep a recommendation tracker. Each recommendation's status is reported periodically: implemented, partly implemented, not implemented, or no longer applicable. For example, the San José City Auditor reports semi-annually, and the Oakland City Auditor publishes follow-up reports (Charter §403). Frequency and categories differ by office (verify for Oakland).
- Auditors close a recommendation only on **evidence of implementation**, not on management's word. Internal-audit standards require confirming implementation (Global Internal Audit Standards 2024, Standard 15.2; the IPPF 2017 numbering was 2500) (verify the numbering).
- The U.S. GAO tracks open recommendations and closes them as "implemented" or "not implemented". It generally stops active follow-up after about four years (verify).

**Law firms.** A judgment or settlement is tracked to performance:
- a compliance date in the docket calendar;
- a demand when the date passes;
- a motion to enforce.

The matter is closed with a **closing letter** to the client saying what was achieved, what remains, and what the firm is no longer watching. Firms send a closing letter precisely so that nobody assumes the firm is still watching.

**Newsrooms.** They run the "one year later" follow-up and promise trackers (for example PolitiFact's), which rate each promise as kept, compromised, broken or stalled. A tracker's rating is a judgement. Civicsmith may record the facts that rating rests on, but not the rating.

**Community activists.**
- Attend the meeting where the response is adopted and comment on it.
- Calendar the promised date.
- Watch the agendas and the quarterly reports.
- File a records request for the implementation documents when the date passes.
- Declare victory publicly (a "thank you" post) or restart the pressure.

The usual failure point is that energy fades and no one is clearly holding the date.

**What "consequences addressed" means in practice.** The harm itself is undone or compensated, not just the conduct stopped. Here that means the amount is restored to the fund. Operational Principle 6: "Compliance restored. Consequences addressed." Partial redress does not stop the clock (Roadmap §5).

### 4. What Civicsmith should do

**The follow-up has to be held as the government's obligation, not the group's to-do.** Every promise in a response becomes a proposed duty that a member adopts in one act (`duties` R2). Each proposed duty carries:
- the obligor office;
- the promised date, with basis `commitment` and the quote;
- `arising_in`, the report;
- the reported status, quoted.

For example, each recommendation answered under §933.05(b)(2) or (b)(3) becomes one proposed duty. The (b)(3) duty carries the statutory six-month date, so it has two bases: the law's (`rule`) and the City's own (`commitment`), shown side by side.

**Did they do it?** The system shows each promise with its occurrence state:
- met, met late, overdue (as a question), pending, or undetermined;
- the evidence a member matched to it (`duties` R12);
- the latest reported status, quoted.

"Overdue" stays "Noticed": a question, never a breach (D241). Whether a missed promise is a breach depends on whether the commitment binds the body, which is read from its text (K1722; `conformance` R27). A Council-adopted response may bind as a "public commitment" (Action §3), or may be only a benchmark ("later than promised").

**When the group stops watching.** This is always a member's act with a reason, never a timer. It should be offered at three moments:
- when every promise is met or withdrawn;
- when the plan closes;
- when a member asks.

The offer is: keep watching a body, page or promised date until a date the member picks, or stop. "Keep watching" reuses `following`, monitoring and standing questions. Each watch shows its end date. Nothing nags (DEC-94).

**"Consequences addressed".** Each consequence part is marked addressed or not, with evidence (`consequences` R9). The plan and the escalation show the parts side by side, with the computed totals (`consequences` R7). "Partly addressed" is shown as the parts, never as a percentage or a score (Action rule 3).

**How the plan closes, and what it records.**
- The member's reason (R20).
- Beside it, a **closing summary computed from the record**, not chosen by anyone:
  - for each matter: its determinations as they stand; whether compliance is restored (by the reading Bob chooses in Q16.1); its consequence parts, addressed or not;
  - each action's resolution and last correspondence;
  - each escalation's state (ended, suspended since, or open);
  - each obligation's occurrence state;
  - what is still being watched, until when.
- No rating ("kept", "broken") and no score.
- The closing summary is working material and never published. What the public sees is the docket entry and any new edition the group chooses to publish.

**Recognition.** When the City does restore the fund, the plan should offer the success options (Action rule 4): a thank-you communication to the Council and Controller, and a docket "outcome" entry. A compliant determination of the restoring act is recorded "with the same care".

**AI help.**
- **Belongs here:**
  - reading the Council's response and proposing one duty per §933.05 answer, each tied to its quote;
  - reading the Auditor's follow-up report and proposing the reported status, quoted;
  - drafting the closing summary's prose from the computed facts, labelled.
- **Stops here:**
  - it never says whether the City complied (a member determines);
  - it never marks a consequence addressed (`consequences` R9: a machine is refused);
  - it never closes, never stops a watch, never writes the closing reason (K1841).

**By hand.** The same response is entered through a checklist that follows §933.05:
- one row per recommendation;
- four choices, with the date field required for choices 2 and 3;
- the six-month date computed for choice 3 from the report's publication date (profile data: a sourced rule for §933.05(b)(3) and §933(c), not held today).

### 5. Gaps

1. **[defect / doctrine-question]** "Compliance restored" cannot be recorded when the City cures by a new act. `escalation` R14 asks for a compliant determination of the same act, and an act is one event. Most breaches are past acts that are remedied by later ones, so no such escalation can ever end. (Q16.1)
2. **[never-designed]** Nothing records what came of a plan as a whole. Action §1 says the layer runs "to … recording how it ended"; only R20's reason exists. (Q16.3)
3. **[never-designed]** When and how a group stops watching: no offer, no end date on watches, no link between plan close and the watches it set. (Q16.4)
4. **[capability-unbuilt]** The profile holds no grand jury response rules: §933(c)'s 60 and 90 days, and §933.05(b)(3)'s six months from publication. The first profile's `deadlines` has none (`oakland-alameda.mjs` deadlines section).
5. **[reach]** Duties, `when_duty` phases, following, consequences, conformance, escalation and plan close all have no screen. The `temporal-expectation-due` item's door is not drawn (R6 §3).
6. **[capability-unbuilt]** "Tell me if the Council misses this date" (standing questions, QUESTIONS L5) and the "what we did" lane (EVENTS L2).
7. **[doctrine-question]** Must verifying a fix go through a new published edition? `conformance` R2 says yes, which is heavy for a group that only wants to note "the City did it". (Q16.2)
8. **[capability-unbuilt]** Consequences of a success are never recorded. Consequences exist only for a noncompliant determination (`consequences` R1; R1 tension 13). A restored fund is the reverse of a consequence, and it can only be shown as "addressed".
9. **[capability-unbuilt]** Holiday lists for 2027 are absent from the first profile. Any business-day count for a follow-up past 31 December 2026 reads undetermined (`civil-time` R16; profile `holidays`). Follow-up is by nature long-dated, so this will bite soon.
10. **[never-designed]** A link from a docket entry (the City's response filed on the published case) to the plan checkpoint it should prompt is "only a prompt", with no stored link (`docket` R3, Suggestions).

### 6. Candidate questions for Bob

**Q16.1 · What counts as "compliance restored" when the government fixes a past breach with a new act?**

*Background.* Escalation ends only when compliance is restored and consequences are addressed (OP6). The built rule asks for a compliant determination of the *same* act. A transfer made in 2025 stays a transfer made in 2025. The City's fix is a new act in 2028, a restoring transfer, so the rule can never be met.

*Options.*
- **(a) Keep the rule.** A group then ends an escalation only where the act is a continuing one (e.g. "the City's ongoing withholding of the records"); otherwise it suspends. *Example:* the sewer escalation stays "suspended since 2028-04-02" forever, after the money came back.
- **(b) A curing act counts.** A later act by the same office, determined compliant against the same standard and named by the member as the cure of the breached act, restores compliance. *Example:* "Resolution 2028-031 restoring $X" is determined compliant against the fund ordinance, linked as the cure, and the escalation can end once the consequence part is addressed.
- **(c) A member's attested close.** No determination is needed; the member closes with a reason and evidence. *Example:* "Funds restored, see resolution." It is lighter, but it bypasses the determination's discipline and the published finding.

*Trade-offs.*
- (a) is honest but makes OP6's "to completion" unreachable for most breaches.
- (c) weakens "compliance is recorded as carefully as noncompliance".
- (b) keeps both: the cure is a published finding and a member's determination, held to the same rules.

*Recommendation:* **(b)**, with the cure link made by a member and visible on both determinations.

**Q16.2 · Must the group publish again to record that the government fixed it?**

*Background.* A determination rests on published findings (K102; `conformance` R2). So recording "compliant" for the cure means publishing a new edition of the case (or a new case) with that finding.

*Options.*
- **(a) Yes, always.** *Example:* the group publishes edition 3: "The City restored $X on 2 April 2028."
- **(b) A docket "outcome" entry may stand in for the group's own records**, while the determination still waits for a published finding. *Example:* the docket shows the outcome now; the edition follows when the group is ready.
- **(c) Allow a compliant determination on unpublished findings.** *Example:* the escalation ends without the public seeing why.

*Trade-offs.* (c) breaks the layer's contract. (a) costs a publication but is exactly "compliance counts". (b) gives quick public acknowledgement without lowering the bar for the determination.

*Recommendation:* **(b) leading to (a)**: the docket outcome entry at once, and the determination when the edition is published. Doctrine is unchanged.

**Q16.3 · What does a closed plan record?**

*Background.* Today a plan closes with a reason only.

*Options.*
- **(a) Reason only** (as built).
- **(b) Reason plus a closing summary computed from the record:** per matter, the determinations, compliance restored or not, consequence parts addressed or not, each action's resolution, each escalation's state, each promise's state, and what is still watched. No member-chosen rating.
- **(c) As (b), plus a member-chosen outcome per matter** (achieved, partly, not, not pursued).

*Trade-offs.* (c)'s choice reads like a score ("partly achieved") and invites the judgement Action rule 3 forbids. (b) is all facts, so nobody has to judge.

*Recommendation:* **(b)**. Optionally, (b) plus a status-report-style note the member writes (Investigation §3).

**Q16.4 · How does a group stop watching?**

*Options.*
- **(a) Silently**: the watches simply continue until someone remembers.
- **(b) At plan close, and when every promise is met, the plan offers the watches it holds** (following a body, watching a page, a standing question on a promised date): keep each until a chosen date, or stop each. Each watch shows its end.
- **(c) An automatic sunset** (e.g. 12 months).

*Trade-offs.* (a) wastes effort and hides what is still running. (c) is a machine deciding. (b) is one act, at the moment the decision is natural, and matches the investigation's "watch or close".

*Recommendation:* **(b)**.

## U17 · A new member picking up a half-run plan

### 1. Situation

The plan is from U16, partway through:
- one matter is determined; one is still suspected (an open question: "were the 2025–26 transfers made too?");
- three options are chosen, one declined ("media now — declined: wait for the grand jury report");
- scenario 1 has two phases, and one checkpoint is due next week;
- the escalation is at stage 4, with a proposed advance to stage 7 waiting in the queue;
- a records request to the Finance Department is awaiting a reply; its clock is past due by two days;
- a legal pressure mark sits on the City Attorney's letter, with the litigation hold in place.

The member who did most of the work, M., has moved away and left the project.

R., new to the group, is invited by the project's owner. She has never seen the plan. She does not use the assistant.

### 2. What real practitioners do

**Law firms.**
- When a lawyer leaves, the matter gets a **transition memo**: status, open deadlines, strategy, client contacts, what was decided and why. Responsibility is formally reassigned and recorded.
- Deadlines live in a **central docketing system**, often double-entered by the docketing department and the lawyer. They never live only in one lawyer's calendar.
- Missed deadlines and calendar errors are among the most common causes of malpractice claims (ABA Profile of Legal Malpractice Claims; verify the current edition's figures).
- The new lawyer reads the file's **correspondence index** and **pleadings index** first.

**Auditors' offices.** Audit work is kept in workpapers with an index and a status sheet: open items, review notes and who cleared them. A reassigned auditor works from the index and the supervisor's review notes. The audit's "why" is in the planning memo.

**Newsrooms.**
- A story handed to a new reporter comes with a **story memo**: what's confirmed, what's not, sources and their status, outstanding records requests and dates, and what has been promised to sources (embargoes, anonymity).
- The common failure is a promise to a source that the new reporter doesn't know about.

**Community groups.**
- Onboarding happens through meeting minutes, a shared drive and a "where we are" call.
- Common failures:
  - the departed volunteer's personal reminders die with her;
  - the new member re-does work, such as filing a duplicate records request;
  - the new member repeats an option the group had already declined for a reason.

### 4. What Civicsmith should do

**What R. must be able to see without asking anyone:**
1. **Where it stands, on one page**, computed from the record (not AI) and readable in two minutes:
   - the matters and their support (established / short / hypothetical);
   - every wait in UX §15's one line ("Waiting on the Controller's reply, due 12 October, CPRA §7922.535(a); two days past");
   - what is due next (checkpoints, proposed stages, promises);
   - holds in place;
   - premise overrides disclosed;
   - what was sent, with its in-band stamp.
2. **Why**, in the group's words: declined options with their reasons, revisions with reasons, the escalation's opening reason. These are already held (R6 history; DEC-89).
3. **Who did what**, by handle, as history: never a figure about a member (Investigation §3).
4. **What the machine contributed**: adopted options keep their disclosure (R32).
5. **What has been promised outward:**
   - the action's `contact`;
   - any embargo or anonymity commitment. These have no field today; a request-for-comment window does (DEC-13).

**Continuity of time-bound items.** This is the point where Civicsmith must do what law firms do.
- The action's **clock entries are the group's central docket**: they belong to the action and are visible to every participant.
- **Reminders stay personal** (DEC-94).
- When a member leaves a project, two things should happen:
  - the project's owners are told **once** which dated items routed to her: actions she created with pending clocks, scenarios she set, escalations she opened. This is a FINDING ("Noticed").
  - any joined member may **take up** an item in one act. The act changes only where its to-dos route and lets her set her own reminders. It is recorded with who and when. It is not an assignee field on the plan (Action rule 8), and nothing is assigned to anyone by someone else. Whether this is consistent with "no assignees" is Bob's (Q17.1).

**Membership and sight rules.**
- Joining gives full sight of the project's past. Nothing is filtered by join date, consistent with "no attribute of a member gates, filters or orders" (Action rule 10).
- What stays withheld:
  - another project's hidden material (DEC-36, withheld whole);
  - other members' personal reminders;
  - a member's private notes she has not shared (Investigation §8);
  - assistant spending of accounts she does not own (Investigation §6).
- A newcomer may act at once, as any joined participant may (`projectAuthority` "joined"). Under DEC-87/88's friction, the first outward act gets the full pre-flight like anyone's. Nothing extra is gated for being new.

**For the member who uses the assistant.** "Brief me on this plan": a summary drafted from the record, every sentence tied to its source (as the Investigation's notes are), labelled machine work. Questions answered closed-book: "why did we decline media?" is answered from the decline reason, quoted.

**For the member who doesn't.** The same "where it stands" page is deterministic, so she needs nothing else. A **"Pick up a plan"** wizard script (Civicsmith library, no AI; BOB's to add, K2241) walks her through:
- read the matters;
- read the waits;
- read the declined options;
- take up the items routed to the departed member;
- set her own reminders.

**AI help stops** at summarising and pointing. It never decides what she should do next, and never writes a reason on her behalf.

### 5. Gaps

1. **[never-designed]** No hand-over for a departing member's dated items. Routing to "creator, else owners" does not say what happens when the creator leaves, and M.'s reminders die with her. (Q17.1)
2. **[defect]** `queue-producers` R15–R17's "else" is unspecified for a creator who has left the project or been deactivated. Items may route to someone who cannot see the project (verify in code).
3. **[never-designed]** No "where this plan stands" read: a computed briefing or a member-written status report for plans. (Q17.2)
4. **[reach]** The plan page, escalation, hold strip and `contact` have no screen. A newcomer in the old UI sees actions only, and so cannot see why anything was decided.
5. **[never-designed]** Outward promises made to people (embargo, anonymity, a "we'll send you the case first") have no home on the action. A newcomer can break them without knowing. This is shared with U10 and U2.
6. **[capability-unbuilt]** No wizard for picking up a plan; "Welcome a new member" covers the group only.
7. **[doctrine-question]** Does "take this up" conflict with Bob's "no assignees" (K590 (3); `action-plans` R26)? (Q17.1)

### 6. Candidate questions for Bob

**Q17.1 · When a member leaves, who carries her dates?**

*Background.* Reminders are a member's own (DEC-94). To-dos route to whoever created the thing they concern. Bob ruled out assignees in a plan. A law firm would never let a deadline live in one person's calendar.

*Options.*
- **(a) Nothing changes.** To-dos keep routing to her, or to the owners where "else" applies. *Example:* the overdue Controller reply sits in the owners' queue, unexplained.
- **(b) Owners are told once which dated items routed to the departing member, and any joined member may "take up" an item by one recorded act.** It changes where its to-dos go and lets her set her own reminders. No one assigns anyone. *Example:* R. takes up the records request; its overdue notice and next checkpoint now come to her.
- **(c) Automatic reassignment to owners.**

*Trade-offs.*
- (a) loses deadlines, the classic failure.
- (c) moves work to people without their act.
- (b) is volunteering, recorded, not assigning. It is closest to "the member chooses", and it keeps the clock central.

*Recommendation:* **(b)**. "Take up" is a routing preference a member chooses for herself, never a field naming who must do the work.

**Q17.2 · How does a newcomer learn where a plan stands?**

*Options.*
- **(a) The plan page and its history only.**
- **(b) A computed "where it stands" page** (waits, next dates, holds, overrides, declined reasons), identical for both kinds of member.
- **(c) As (b), plus plan status reports**: the system drafts from the record, a member edits, the report is kept dated, never published and never required (as Investigation §3 rules for projects).

*Recommendation:* **(c)**. (b) is the floor that serves the member who never uses the assistant. Status reports give the departing member a place to leave her "transition memo".

## U19 · The same records request in another state

### 1. Situation

A group in **Houston** asks the **City of Houston**'s Public Works department for the bid tabulations on a sewer contract. A sister group in **Albany, New York** asks the **City of Albany** for the same kind of records.

Both run Civicsmith. The question is what their jurisdiction profiles must supply and what stays law-neutral.

### 2. What practitioners face

**Texas Public Information Act (Gov't Code ch. 552).**
- The body produces "promptly". If it cannot within 10 business days, it certifies in writing the date and hour it will (§552.221(d)).
- To withhold, the body must ask the **Attorney General** for a ruling within **10 business days** of receipt and tell the requester (§552.301(b), (d)). It sends its arguments within 15 business days (§552.301(e)).
- Failing these deadlines makes the information presumed public (§552.302).
- The AG rules within 45 business days, extendable by 10 (§552.306).
- A request for clarification is allowed (§552.222). The clock's effect is per the Texas Supreme Court in *City of Dallas v. Abbott* (2010): the period runs from the clarified request (verify).
- An **itemized cost estimate** is due if charges exceed $40. The requester must answer in writing within **10 business days**, or the request is considered withdrawn (§552.2615). **This is a deadline that binds the group.**
- "Business day" is defined in §552.0031 (verify the text on closures and skeleton-crew days).
- Enforcement:
  - complaint to the county or district attorney, or the AG (§552.3215);
  - mandamus (§552.321);
  - attorney fees (§552.323).
- Requests must go by the body's designated means (§552.234).
- Texas has **two time zones**: El Paso and Hudspeth counties are in Mountain time.

**New York FOIL (Public Officers Law art. 6).**
- Within **5 business days** of receipt, the agency grants, denies in writing, or acknowledges with an approximate date. That date is reasonable, generally within 20 business days of the acknowledgement; beyond that, the agency gives reasons and a date certain (§89(3)(a)).
- Failure to answer is a **constructive denial** (§89(4)(a)).
- An **administrative appeal** to the agency head is due within **30 days** of the denial (the group's deadline). The agency decides within **10 business days** (§89(4)(a)).
- Then an **Article 78** proceeding, with attorney fees (§89(4)(c)).
- Fees are up to 25 cents a page for copies (§87(1)(b)(iii)).
- Each agency has a Records Access Officer. The Committee on Open Government gives non-binding advisory opinions.
- (Section numbers verify against current text.)

**California, for contrast.** 10 days with a 14-day extension, no administrative appeal, a court petition (§7922.535, §7923.000 et seq.).

**Also, for a profile.** Some states limit access to their own citizens (e.g. Virginia; upheld in *McBurney v. Young*, 569 U.S. 221 (2013)). Requester eligibility is a jurisdiction fact.

### 3. Today in Civicsmith

Already law-neutral:
- **The kind.** `records_request` is a product kind carrying `law` (`action-grammar` R3; `checks.mjs`:66). The working page §4.2's "only the California-specific kind exists" is out of date: `cpra_request` is the legacy kind and reads as written (`actions` R41).
- **The lifecycle vocabulary.** Sent: request, fee-waiver request, appeal, court filing. Received: acknowledgement, fee estimate, fee-waiver decision, extension notice, production, denial, appeal or court decision. Outcomes: granted, denied, partial, reversed, affirmed, none stated (`action-grammar` R5; D-147).
- **Every governing law named by citation**, by the agency asked (D-149).
- **Fee quotes** as evidence, never judged (D-148).
- **The `civil-time` engine.** Calendar and business counts, closure lists, roll, extension, tolling, hours, cutoffs, uncertain dates, and the body's side against the group's side (R9–R19). No place in code (R27).
- **The profile schema:**
  - `records_laws`, `deadlines` (units, count, starts, extension, tolling, citation, status), `holidays` and closure lists, `weekend`, `time_zone`;
  - `counterparties` (`oversight`, `elected`, `hours`);
  - `action_kinds` (tier, venue, channel cutoff and receipt rule, evidence standard, template);
  - `legal_organisations` (`jurisdictions` R7, R23–R26, R33, R39–R48).
- **Several profiles combined**, with conflicts withheld (`jurisdictions` R14, R15, R29).

| step | state |
|---|---|
| A Texas or New York group creates `records_request` with `law: "Tex. Gov't Code ch. 552"` | usable (the law is free text; the list of laws by citation is usable) |
| Due date computed | **undetermined**: no Texas or New York profile exists, and the product holds no default (`jurisdictions` R27) |
| Record each reply's stage | usable, but some stages have no word: "the body asked the AG for a ruling", "AG ruling", "clarification requested", "certified production date" |
| A deadline that binds the requester (TX §552.2615's 10 business days; NY's 30-day appeal) | **the schema can hold it only as `applies_to: claim`** (a legal claim's period). No `deadlines` field says whose duty it is, though `civil-time` R17–R19 distinguish group and body sides |
| An office in Mountain time in a Texas profile | **one `time_zone` per profile** (`jurisdictions` R41). Per-office zones are not held (verify whether `zoneOf` can take an office's zone) |
| A third party in the lifecycle (the Texas AG rules on the body's request; the NY Committee advises) | not designed: the addressee is one office; a ruling authority is neither addressee nor venue |

### 4. What Civicsmith should do

**Law-neutral (product), unchanged across states:**
- the kind and its `law`;
- governing laws by citation;
- the lifecycle shape (each stage its own correspondence entry naming what it answers, a next-stage due date with its citation);
- outcomes;
- quotes as evidence;
- the clock with its basis kind;
- overdue as a question;
- the addressee as an office;
- the gate (evidence-seeking is never gated);
- what is sent, with its stamp;
- the hold;
- reminders as asked.

**Jurisdiction data (profile), per state:**
1. **Laws.** `records_laws` with their levels; the standard sources a determination can cite.
2. **Rules.** Every period as a sourced `deadlines` entry: response, extension, AG-ruling request, AG decision, acknowledgement-to-decision, appeal filing, appeal decision, estimate response. Each needs:
   - **whose duty it is**, the body's or the requester's (new field);
   - **what starts it**, including another party's act ("the AG's receipt of the body's request", known only when the copy arrives);
   - **its consequence as a fact**, quoted, never applied: "presumed public" (TX §552.302), "constructive denial" (NY §89(4)(a)), "considered withdrawn" (TX §552.2615).
3. **Calendar.** Weekend; holiday and closure lists per office (state and city); the time zone **per office** where a state has two; the business-day definition's text.
4. **Venues and kinds.**
   - Kinds: records request; administrative appeal (NY); AG complaint (TX); Article 78 or mandamus (Tier 2 or 3, with an advisory).
   - Each kind's venue and its accepted means ("designated email only", §552.234).
   - Any public listing of requests.
   - The venue's evidence standard where known.
5. **Lifecycle stages a state adds**, as profile vocabulary mapped onto the product's stages: "AG ruling requested" is a kind of extension notice by a third party; "constructive denial" is the `none stated` outcome on a passed date.
6. **Offices.** Counterparties, including records access officers and oversight bodies: the AG's Open Records Division (TX), the Committee on Open Government (NY, advisory, `oversight: false`?).
7. **Eligibility** (citizens-only states).
8. **Legal organisations** (e.g. the Freedom of Information Foundation of Texas, the New York Coalition for Open Government; verify).
9. **Templates** through the formal process.

Every fact is sourced, with a status and a confirmation horizon (K1445). A missing fact reads undetermined, never California's value.

**Both kinds of member.**
- By hand, the Texas member sees "Due: undetermined — no sourced Texas rule held. Enter the date you were told, with its basis." She can type a clock entry with `rule` basis and its citation. It is held as hers and flagged as not computed.
- With the assistant, the AI may read the statute text the group has captured and propose a clock entry, labelled "legal information", which a member adopts (`clockPropose`'s pattern). The AI never invents a rule (D250) and never writes profile data.

### 5. Gaps

1. **[capability-unbuilt]** No profile for any state but California (and the test profile). Every out-of-state deadline is undetermined. (Q19.1)
2. **[never-designed]** A deadline that binds the requester (the group) has no place in the profile schema except `claim`, and its consequence ("considered withdrawn") has no field. This matters because the group's own missed statutory date must never be shown as a finding about the government (D234), yet it changes the request's legal state.
3. **[capability-unbuilt]** Per-office time zones within one profile (Texas, Oregon, Florida and others).
4. **[never-designed]** A third party in the request's lifecycle (an AG ruling, an ombudsman), and a clock started by another party's act that the group learns of later.
5. **[capability-unbuilt]** Profile vocabulary for state-specific stages, mapped onto the product's stages. Today the stages are a closed product vocabulary; whether a profile may extend it is BOB's to design.
6. **[never-designed]** Requester eligibility as a profile fact.
7. **[defect]** Working page §4.2 says the law-neutral records request is unbuilt. It is built (`action-grammar` R3); Case Making's "NOT BUILT" (R1) predates it.
8. **[doctrine-question]** Who writes and confirms a new state's rules. (Q19.2)

### 6. Candidate questions for Bob

**Q19.1 · How far ahead should Civicsmith support places outside California?**

*Options.*
- **(a) Only when a group there asks.** The product works anywhere, with dates undetermined until that state's profile is researched and sourced. *Example:* a Houston group gets a working request and a "Due: undetermined" until the Texas rules are in.
- **(b) Ship a short federal and multi-state core now**: FOIA (held already) plus the records laws of the largest states, each sourced and confirmed.

## U20 · A plan started at the first suspicion, before anything is published

### 1. Situation

A parent in the Grandview walk-through (Investigation design §2) brings a new suspicion. The district is **Oakland Unified School District**; the Grandview rebuild is the design's fictional example.

The suspicion: a construction contract's change orders were approved without the Board vote that the district's board policy requires above a threshold. Nothing is established yet:
- a project and a question exist;
- one agenda is captured;
- the parent holds a hunch;
- no finding is published.

The members want to plan now:
1. a records request for the change orders;
2. a request for comment to the district;
3. a tip to the district's **Office of the Inspector General**, or to the Alameda County Civil Grand Jury (verify OUSD's oversight office);
4. a talk with a lawyer;
5. a public "working on" notice;
6. since a Board action on 6 October may have been taken without proper agenda description, a Brown Act cure-and-correct demand (30-day window, §54960.1(b)).

### 2. What real practitioners do

**Investigative newsrooms.**
- A suspicion is a "tip". The rule is: report before you publish.
- The early acts are **quiet**: records requests, often worded broadly so as not to tip the subject; documents gathered; sources interviewed.
- The **request for comment** comes last, with a reasonable deadline, once the story is solid.
- Nothing is published until it is verified. Legal review happens before publication.
- Defamation risk lies in **asserting** a fact that turns out false. Asking is not asserting.

**Auditors.** A hotline tip opens a **preliminary assessment**: is there enough to open an audit or investigation? The tipster's identity is protected. No finding is made until the work is done (e.g. the Oakland City Auditor's whistleblower programme; verify).

**Lawyers.**
- Want to be involved **early**, before anything is sent: privilege attaches to consultations, and early advice prevents admissions and missed windows.
- Check limitation and pre-suit windows first: Brown Act 30 or 90 days; Government Claims Act 6 or 12 months (§911.2).

**Activists.**
- Often file a records request at once, which is right.
- Sometimes go public too early ("the district is hiding…"). That risks a defamation claim, an anti-SLAPP fight (CCP §425.16 protects petitioning, including records requests and grand jury complaints, per Roadmap App. B), and the group's credibility.

**Common failure points.**
- Tipping the subject: an early public notice, or a records request on a public portal, may lead to records being "lost".
- Accusatory wording in early letters.
- Missing a short statutory window while waiting to be sure.

### 4. What Civicsmith should do

**A plan may open the moment a question exists**, and should say plainly what it rests on.
- Every matter shows its support. Every option resting only on hypothetical matters says so where it is shown, in the preview and in the start dialog (R19, R37).
- The plan page carries a **hunch-debt line**: "This plan rests on 1 hunch and 0 findings. Nothing resting on it can be published."
- Nothing in the plan leaves (R25).

**What may leave at this stage, and how:**
- **Evidence-seeking acts leave freely**: records requests; requests for comment; a request to an oversight office to *examine*, worded as a request (see Q20.1). They are never gated (Action rule 2). The preview shows the venue's public-listing fact ("Oakland's portal lists requests publicly"), so the group can weigh tipping the subject. Guidance says how to word a broad request.
- **Breach-asserting acts** (a demand that names a violation; a public statement of wrongdoing) are refused by default. A member may proceed with a stated reason, disclosed on the face of everything prepared (rule 2; `filings` R24). When a statutory window is running, the refusal is shown **with the window computed beside it** (Q20.3).
- **Counsel** may be briefed at any stage (Q20.2). The packet shows each matter's support, never fileable, never published (R10, R11).
- **A working-on notice** is public. The owner sees its warning and, at the suspicion stage, a specific line: "Nothing is published yet. The subject will see this notice." The group decides. It is never blocked (Requirement 12).
- **A review copy** of a draft case to one person, marked pre-publish (DEC-31).

**What may never leave:** the plan (DEC-25); a hunch as a fact; a counsel packet as a filing (R10); anything by a system path (rule 7).

**Wording of what leaves.** Every communication and filing addressed outward should get the check the case account gets:
- a sentence the record contradicts is refused;
- an unsupported assertion is flagged;
- framing only under a declared bias, marked (Investigation §11).

*Example:* "the district hid the overruns" in a request for comment is flagged as not supported, with suggested wording such as "the records we hold show no Board vote on change orders 14–19; please tell us if one exists".

**AI help.**
- **Belongs here:**
  - proposing evidence-seeking options and the questions to ask;
  - drafting neutral wording;
  - flagging assertive sentences;
  - computing (deterministically) the windows that are running;
  - proposing a broad records request that does not name the suspicion.
- **Stops here:**
  - it never frames a suspicion as fact;
  - never says there is a claim, or what to file (K1474);
  - never writes the override reason (K1841);
  - never sends.

**By hand:**
- the same hunch-debt line and the same window computation;
- a checklist "before you send anything at the suspicion stage": is it a question or an assertion; is the subject tipped by the venue; is a window running; does counsel need to see it;
- the request-for-comment template with a neutral structure: what we hold, what we ask, by when.

### 5. Gaps

1. **[doctrine-question]** Is a tip or complaint to an oversight office on a suspicion a breach-asserting action (gated) or an evidence-seeking one (never gated)? Not stated. K597 (5) covers the confidential route but not the gate. (Q20.1)
2. **[defect / doctrine-question]** `filings` R8 refuses a counsel packet on an action with no determination and no override. Consulting a lawyer before any finding is what practitioners do, and it is not an outward assertion. (Q20.2)
3. **[doctrine-question]** A statutory window shorter than the path to a published finding and a determination (Brown Act §54960.1: 30 days). The override exists, but nothing designs the moment. (Q20.3)
4. **[capability-unbuilt]** The case-account sentence check does not apply to what is sent (communications, filings, requests for comment). (Q20.4)
5. **[capability-unbuilt]** Venue facts about publicity (a portal that lists requests publicly) are not in the profile. Without them, the preview cannot warn about tipping the subject.

### 6. Candidate questions for Bob

**Q20.1 · Is a tip to an oversight office, at the suspicion stage, held back like a breach claim?**

*Background.* Records requests and requests for comment are never held back. A claim that a rule was broken needs a determination, unless a member overrides openly. A grand jury complaint or an Inspector General tip sits in between: it asks an office with power to look, but it usually says what the group suspects.

*Options.*
- **(a) Treat it as a breach claim.** Refused without a determination; override with a reason. *Example:* the group's grand jury complaint carries "Rests on an unestablished premise".
- **(b) Treat it as evidence-seeking.** Never held back; the action shows each matter's support. *Example:* "We ask the Inspector General to examine whether change orders 14–19 were approved as BP 3311 requires."
- **(c) Evidence-seeking if it asks, breach-asserting if it asserts.** The member marks which. The wording check (Q20.4) flags assertions in a "request to examine".

*Trade-offs.*
- (a) blocks the normal first move of real groups, and grand jury complaints are a Tier 1 act "any individual can initiate" (Requirement 8).
- (b) lets an accusation pass as a question.
- (c) follows what is actually sent.

*Recommendation:* **(c)**.

**Q20.2 · May a group brief a lawyer before anything is determined?**

*Background.* Today a counsel packet needs a determination, or the member must state an override, which puts "Rests on an unestablished premise" on the packet.

*Options.*
- **(a) Keep it.** *Example:* the group's early briefing carries the disclosure.
- **(b) Allow a briefing at any stage without an override.** It shows each matter's support and stays never fileable and never published. *Example:* "Briefing · matters: 1 suspected (hypothetical)".
- **(c) Allow early briefings only for Tier 3 matters.**

*Trade-offs.* (a) treats a private consultation with the group's own adviser as an outward assertion, which it is not. Asking counsel early is what lawyers want and what avoids mistakes. (b) loses nothing: the support status is shown on every matter.

*Recommendation:* **(b)**.

**Q20.3 · What happens when the law's window is shorter than the time to publish?**

*Background.* A Brown Act demand must be sent within 30 days of an agenda violation. Publishing a case and recording a determination may take longer. Today the group can override openly, with a disclosed reason.

*Options.*
- **(a) Keep the override as the only path**, and show the window beside the refusal. *Example:* "Due 5 Nov (30 days, §54960.1(b)). Proceed with a stated reason, or publish and determine first."
- **(b) As (a), plus a short path:** publish a one-finding case quickly. The publication checks still apply; nothing is waived.
- **(c) Exempt statutory pre-suit demands from the gate.**

*Trade-offs.* (c) makes a whole class of breach claims ungated, which erodes rule 2. (a) and (b) keep the rule and make its cost visible in time.

*Recommendation:* **(b)**.

**Q20.4 · Should what the group sends be checked sentence by sentence against the record, as a published case is?**

*Background.* Bob ruled that a case's account is checked: unsupported sentences are flagged, contradicted ones refused, "lying is not bias" (D56). Letters, requests for comment, tips and filings are not checked. At the suspicion stage they carry the most risk.

*Options.*
- **(a) No.** The sender is responsible.
- **(b) Flag only**, for anything addressed outward.
- **(c) The same as a case:** flag unsupported sentences and refuse contradicted ones, for every filing and communication. Framing only under a declared bias, marked.

*Trade-offs.* (a) leaves the group exposed exactly where defamation risk lives. (c) is consistent with D56 and costs little more than (b): the contradicted-sentence refusal already exists for the case.

*Recommendation:* **(c)**. Questions are never flagged as unsupported, because asking is not asserting.

## Cross-cutting: what these five use cases share

1. **A computed "where it stands" read, the same for both kinds of member.** U16's closing summary, U17's briefing, U18's stage map and U20's hunch-debt line are one capability: deterministic facts from the record (support, waits with their basis, next dates, holds, overrides, determinations, consequence parts, escalation stage) on one page. AI may phrase it, never decide it.
2. **The government's promises and deadlines held as duties.** Response promises (§933.05), statutory replies, a "will report quarterly". Duties are built (adopt, occurrences, `when_duty` phases, told once) but unreached. Follow-up (U16), continuity (U17) and out-of-state rules (U19) all need them on screen.
3. **Profile data is the bottleneck for by-hand members.** Missing today: templates (none approved); the rules for common Tier 1 acts (§54960.1, §933, §933.05); venue facts (eComment cutoffs, public portals, designated channels); and for other states, rules that bind the requester, per-office zones, third-party rulings, eligibility, and 2027 holidays. With these, the by-hand path works without AI.
4. **The gate needs three refinements, not a change.** Classify oversight tips by what they say (Q20.1). Let counsel be briefed early (Q20.2). Show a running statutory window beside the refusal (Q20.3).
5. **Endings need design.** How compliance is restored by a curing act (Q16.1), what a plan records when it closes (Q16.3), how watching stops (Q16.4), and what happens to a departing member's dated items (Q17.1). The layer's contract runs "to … recording how it ended", and today only a reason is recorded.
6. **The wording check should follow everything outward** (Q20.4), with AI drafting labelled and the reasons always the member's own.
7. **Reach first.** Almost every step above is "built, no screen". The queue's per-kind doors, the plan page, duties, the wizard runner and the counsel packet are the minimum surfaces these use cases need.
8. **Corrections for the record (BOB):**
   - the UTC "overdue" defect on the working page and in H2 appears fixed (K1657; `monitoring` R34/R50);
   - the law-neutral `records_request` kind is built (`action-grammar` R3), contrary to working page §4.2;
   - the routing of R15–R17's "else" for a creator who has left the project is unspecified.
