## B. Consolidated decisions for Bob

Sixteen decisions. 1–4 unblock the most design (the gate and what may leave touch fourteen of the twenty use cases); 5–8 shape how actions end and what the group is told; 9–16 are narrower.

### B1 · Asking or asserting: which outward acts your breach rule holds back

**Raised as** U1-Q1, U3-Q1, Q-U6b, Q20.1; touched in U4, U14.

**Background.** You ruled on 30 September that an action claiming a breach is held back unless the group has published a finding and a member has determined the breach; a member may go ahead anyway by an open act with a reason, and everything prepared from it then carries that disclosure. Records requests and requests for comment are never held back. You also ruled that a confidential referral to an oversight office is an action the member sends by her own hand. What the rule does not say is where three common acts fall:
- a complaint to an oversight office (the Auditor's hotline, the grand jury, an inspector general) that asks the office to look, often before the group has published anything, and often where the office asks complainants *not* to go public first;
- enforcing the group's own records request (a request for mediation, an ethics complaint, a court petition), where the only "breach" is what the group's own dated correspondence shows;
- a joint letter or public comment that repeats the group's case.

Today Civicsmith decides by a single switch the member sets ("this asserts a breach"); nothing explains it, and if the member doesn't set it, nothing checks the text. If she does, a confidential complaint goes out with "rests on an unestablished premise" at its head.

**Options.**
- **(a) Keep the rule as written; every such act is a breach claim.** *Example:* the Auditor complaint about split purchase orders carries the override disclosure; Dana must publish "the City withheld the Measure U emails" as a case before she can ask the ethics commission to mediate.
- **(b) Treat all of them as evidence-seeking, never held back.** *Example:* the grand jury complaint and the ethics complaint go straight out, whatever they say.
- **(c) Draw the line by what the text does.** An act that *asks* an office to examine something, or that enforces the group's own request on the strength of its own dated record ("sent 15 Oct; determination due 26 Oct under the records law; reply withheld these items citing these sections"), is evidence-seeking and never held back. Any sentence that *asserts* a breach as the group's finding ("staff illegally split the contract") still needs a published finding and a determination, or your open override. Before approval, sentences that read as assertions are pointed out; the member rewrites them, or confirms in her own words that the act only asks, or treats it as a breach claim. *Example:* "five purchase orders, consecutive numbers, same vendor, each just below the bid threshold; we ask the Auditor to examine them" goes out ungated; "the City routinely hides records" in the ethics complaint is pointed out and needs a determination behind it.

**Trade-offs.** (a) is faithful to the letter, but makes the most ordinary first moves of real groups slow and public, puts a self-incriminating disclosure on confidential complaints, and turns the override into a routine click that loses its meaning. (b) is fast but lets a group file accusations under the name "examine". (c) is how auditors, inspectors and newsrooms want complaints written, and keeps your standard exactly where the group's own claim is made; its cost is one more thing for the member to read before approving.

**Recommendation: (c).** It answers the four questions with one rule, honours "an action that seeks evidence is never gated", and keeps the protection where it matters. It works the same for a member without the assistant: the facts offered come from the record, and the pointing-out can come from a fixed list of assertive phrasings as well as from the assistant.

**Checked against your rulings.** Two workers recommended that flagged sentences be "flagged, never blocked" (U3-Q1 (c), Q20.1 (c)). Left there, a member could mark an act "asking" and send assertions through without either a determination or your open override, which would hollow out the 30 September ruling. The corrected version above keeps the decision with the member (rule 1: the machine only points out) but requires her to resolve each flagged sentence before approval, with her confirmation recorded on the action. The confirmation is an attributed act, so it is visible to every reader, as your override is.

**For BOB:** amends the reading of Action rule 2 and `actions` R8 (breach flag) with a classification "asks / enforces own request / asserts", resolved per flagged sentence at `filingApprove` (`filings` R6); the ledger-only class for own-request enforcement (U1-Q1 (c)); confidential referral K597 (5); DR 8 Tier 1 "anyone may initiate". Pairs with B2's wording check, which supplies the flags.

### B2 · Checking what leaves against the record

**Raised as** Q20.4, Q11-2; the flyer, thank-you, leave-behind and joint-letter checks in U11–U14; U2's claims assembled from findings.

**Background.** You ruled that a published case's account is checked sentence by sentence: an unsupported sentence is pointed out, one the record contradicts is refused ("lying is not bias"). Nothing the group *sends* is checked: letters, requests for comment, complaints, flyers, testimony. At the suspicion stage, and in public-facing material near an election, that is where a group's exposure to a defamation claim lives. A related question is what an outward message may rest on: a breach claim needs a published finding, but nothing says whether a flyer or a talk at a community meeting may state facts about a body that the group has not yet published.

**Options.**
- **(a) No check.** The sender is responsible. *Example:* "the district hid the overruns" goes out in a request for comment.
- **(b) Point out only.** Unsupported and contradicted sentences are both pointed out, nothing refused.
- **(c) The same as the case account, with your open-override pattern.** Unsupported sentences are pointed out; a sentence the record contradicts is refused by default, and the member may still send it only by an open act with a reason. Questions are never pointed out (asking is not asserting). Claims about what a body did or failed to do rest on the published case, or on the group's own dated correspondence; statements about what the group itself is doing ("we have asked the City for its mailers") need no case. *Example:* the flyer's three facts each link to a finding of Edition 2; "They lost track of $X" is pointed out because the finding says "reports not filed for two years".

**Trade-offs.** (a) leaves the group exposed exactly where it is most at risk. (b) is gentle but lets a sentence the group's own record contradicts go out under its name. (c) applies the standard you already set for the case to everything that carries the group's name, and keeps the member as the one who decides.

**Recommendation: (c).**

**Checked against your rulings.** The worker's (c) refused contradicted sentences outright. Applied to an outward act, an outright refusal would be a tool with the final word, which Requirement 12 and your 30 September ruling rule out; the corrected (c) uses your refuse-by-default, override-openly pattern instead. The no-assistant member gets the record-based part of the check (each claim linked to its finding, a claim with no link pointed out); only the reading of free prose for assertions needs the assistant, and that reading is always labelled machine work.

**For BOB:** extends D56's case-account check (Investigation §11) to `filings` R23 communications and R1–R5 filings; Q11-2 (A)+(B); override per Action rule 2 pattern (DEC-76 disclose-not-block for unsupported). Feeds B1's flags.

### B3 · When the law gives fewer days than it takes to publish

**Raised as** Q-U7a, Q20.3; U18 gap 7.

**Background.** A Brown Act demand to cure or correct must be sent within 30 days of an action taken without proper notice. Under your rule, a demand that claims a breach needs a published finding and a determination, or the open override. Today, if the group can't publish and determine in time, the override's disclosure is printed first on the letter the City reads, and an action sent under override can never later join the escalation, even after the group does determine. Nothing shows the member, at the moment of refusal, how many days remain.

**Options.**
- **(a) Keep the override as the only path, and show the window beside the refusal.** *Example:* "Due 5 Nov (30 days, Gov. Code §54960.1). Your matter is suspected, not determined: publish and determine first, or proceed with a stated reason, disclosed on the letter."
- **(b) As (a), plus a quick route:** help the group publish a short one-finding case (agenda, video, minutes) and determine within days. Every publication check still applies; nothing is waived. *Example:* published day 4, determined day 5, demand sent day 6, no override.
- **(c) Keep the disclosure on the group's copy only, not on the letter.**
- **(d) Let an action sent under override join the escalation once a determination is later made,** with the disclosure kept in its history.

**Trade-offs.** (a) is honest but leaves the group choosing between a weakened letter and a missed window. (b) keeps your standard and fits the work: these cases are usually small and documentary. (c) conflicts with your ruling that a departure is visible to every reader; the letter is the one place the City reads. (d) removes a trap: having determined, the group should not lose the escalation path for a letter already sent.

**Recommendation: (b) together with (d).** Not (c).

**Checked against your rulings.** (b) waives nothing. (d) revises an earlier rule that an overridden action never joins an escalation; that rule is recorded as K600 (a), and BOB must check whether it was your ruling or BOB's detail before presenting (d) as a change for you.

**For BOB:** Action rule 2 (c); `filings` R24 (disclosure first); `escalation` R23 and K600 (a) for (d); `conformance` R2; a fast one-finding publication path is a design for the lane, no publication check waived; window computed by `action-clocks` R2 from a §54960.1 profile rule (not held).

### B4 · What stays inside: confidential acts, counsel's advice, a member who is the source, and promises made to people

**Raised as** Q-U6a, Q-U8a, U3-Q2; U10 (embargo, attribution terms); U17 gap 5 (promises a newcomer can't see).

**Background.** You ruled that nothing non-public leaves by a system path and that the plan is never published. But the group's own list of "what we did", which is shown beside "what they did" and can be frozen into a published case's timeline, includes every letter on every action. So today a confidential grand jury complaint, or a lawyer's advice recorded as correspondence, could reach a published timeline; counsel's advice is also visible to every member who can see the action, which can waive privilege. Separately, nothing says what the group's shared record may hold about a member who is herself the whistleblower, and promises the group made to people (an embargo time, a source's anonymity, "we'll send you the case first") have no place, so a newcomer can break them without knowing.

**Options.**
- **(a) Three stated scopes, each set by a member's act with a reason:**
  1. *Confidential act:* an action marked confidential never appears in a published case or timeline; inside the group it is seen as any action is. *Example:* the case timeline shows the letters to the Director, not the grand jury complaint.
  2. *Counsel matter:* correspondence with counsel and counsel's dates are seen only by members the group names, are never published and never evidence, and packets carry a privilege marking; sharing outside warns, as information, that it may waive privilege. *Example:* three named members see counsel's emails; the other twenty see only that a counsel matter exists.
  3. *A member's own role as a source* stays in her hidden project; the group's complaint rests on the documents she chooses to share, and she may name herself to named members by her own revocable act.
  
  Promises made to people (embargo terms, anonymity, attribution terms) are recorded on the action as notes every participant sees.
- **(b) Redaction instead of exclusion.** Published as "a confidential referral", body not named. *Example:* "12 Oct · confidential referral". Reveals that a referral exists, which grand juries dislike.
- **(c) No special rules.** Members leave things out of the case by hand, and keep counsel outside Civicsmith. Error-prone: the timeline is assembled by the system, not chosen line by line.

**Trade-offs.** (a) costs three small marks and their rules, and closes the one leak path all four use cases share. (b) is weaker for confidential referrals. (c) relies on members remembering, against a system that publishes automatically.

**Recommendation: (a).** It applies your "nothing non-public leaves by a system path" at the one place the system assembles what leaves, matches how law firms and complainants behave, and keeps the group's memory in its record.

**Checked against your rulings.** "Seen only by members the group names" could read as a member attribute filtering sight, which rule 10 forbids. The corrected design gives a counsel matter its own project membership (the same mechanism hidden projects already use), so sight follows membership of a project the members joined, never an attribute of the member. A member who later testifies before a grand jury: the record holds only that she appeared and when, unless she records her own account.

**For BOB:** `actions` R66 (every correspondence entry into the "what we did" lane) against K1494 (frozen published timeline), DEC-31, K597 (5), DEC-25; Investigation §7, DEC-36, K1484 row 7 for the source; rule 10 / DEC-17, DEC-54 for the counsel circle (use project membership); outward-promise notes are a lane detail (§C).

### B5 · When a breach is over: a curing act, harm "addressed", and whether to publish again

**Raised as** Q16.1, Q-U9a, Q16.2.

**Background.** Your escalation ends only when compliance is restored and the consequences are addressed. As built, "restored" requires a compliant determination of *the same act*. Most breaches are past acts (a transfer in 2025) cured by a later one (a restoring transfer in 2028), so no such escalation can ever end; it stays "paused" forever. "Consequences addressed" is a member's act with evidence, but nothing says what counts when harm can't be undone (a year in portable classrooms). And a determination rests on a published finding, so recording that the City fixed it means publishing again.

**Options.**
- **On restoring compliance:** (a) keep "the same act" (only continuing acts can ever end); (b) **a curing act counts**: a later act of the same office, determined compliant against the same standard and linked by a member as the cure, shown on both determinations (*example:* "Resolution 2028-031 restoring $X", linked as the cure); (c) a member closes with a reason and evidence, no determination.
- **On harm addressed:** (a) **a member's reasoned statement with the evidence, per part, with optional plain words offered** (remedied, mitigated, compensated, acknowledged, cannot be undone); (b) a fixed list only; (c) end on compliance alone, consequences left open and shown. *Example:* "Mitigated: the district now reports monthly; the lost year cannot be restored; recovery plan adopted (cited)."
- **On publishing again:** (a) always; (b) **a dated outcome entry on the published case at once, and the determination when the next edition is published**; (c) a compliant determination on unpublished findings.

**Trade-offs.** On restoring: (a) makes "to completion" unreachable for most breaches; (c) lowers "compliance is recorded as carefully as noncompliance"; (b) keeps both. On harm: a fixed list alone forces a label where judgement is needed; the optional words give readers a consistent vocabulary and never let "acknowledged" pass as "fixed". On publishing: (c) breaks the layer's contract; (b) gives a quick public acknowledgement without lowering the bar.

**Recommendation: (b) curing act; (a) reasoned statement with optional words; (b) outcome entry, then the determination with the next edition.**

**Checked against your rulings.** No conflict: the curing act is a member's determination on a published finding; the optional words are categories, not a score, and no "partly addressed" percentage is shown.

**For BOB:** `escalation` R14 and `conformance` Terms ("same act" = event equality) — the built rule is the defect in §D; `consequences` R9; `docket` R1 outcome; OP6; Action rule 4.

### B6 · How a plan and a pursuit end, what they record, and how watching stops

**Raised as** Q-U9b, Q16.3, Q12-1, Q16.4.

**Background.** You ruled that compliance counts and that recognition and success stories are actions. Today a plan closes with a reason only; an escalation can be paused but not honestly stopped without winning; the effect of a success (the reports now cover $600M of bond spending) has nowhere to go, because consequences exist only for breaches; and the things a group set running (following a body, watching a page, a promised date) keep running until someone remembers them.

**Options.**
- **Stopping without winning:** (a) **"stopped pursuing", with a reason**, a third state beside "ended" and "paused", never read as success, reopenable (*example:* "Stopped March 2027: the oversight committee took the matter over"); (b) pause only; (c) close the plan, leave the escalation paused.
- **What a closed plan records:** (a) the reason only; (b) **the reason plus a closing summary computed from the record**, per matter: determinations, compliance restored or not, consequence parts addressed or not, each action's ending, each escalation's state, each promise's state, what is still watched; (c) as (b) plus a member-chosen rating per matter (achieved / partly / not).
- **A success's effect:** (a) **a success record per matter**, the member's statement on evidence, with optional figures computed from cited sources, never a causal claim beyond the evidence; (b) the action's own ending only; (c) widen consequences to include "benefits".
- **Stopping watching:** (a) silently; (b) **at plan close, and when every promise is met, the plan offers once each watch it holds**: keep until a date she picks, or stop; every watch shows its end date; (c) an automatic sunset.

**Trade-offs.** A rating per matter (c) reads as a score; an automatic sunset is a machine deciding; a silent watch hides what is still running. "Stopped" is honest and supports a hand-over to another group. A widened consequence bends a construct built around breach and causation.

**Recommendation: (a) stopped with a reason; (b) computed closing summary, never published, with an optional note the member writes; (a) success record; (b) the one-time offer at close.**

**Checked against your rulings.** Rejected (c) on the closing summary: a member's "partly achieved" is the significance judgement rule 3 keeps out of fields. The success record must not say the group's action *caused* the fix unless evidence other than the group's own act shows it (your rule that the group's own act is never evidence of impact). The one-time offer at close is a single question at a natural moment, not a reminder, so it fits "told once".

**For BOB:** `escalation` R15 (suspend) + new state; `action-plans` R20 (close) + computed summary; `consequences` R1 (`CONSEQUENCE_NOT_NONCOMPLIANT`), R2 arithmetic reused outside breach; DEC-14 / `actions` R26 on causation; `following`, `monitoring`, standing questions (QUESTIONS L5) end dates; DEC-94; working page §4.3 "Did it work?".

### B7 · When something an official said becomes a promise Civicsmith holds the body to

**Raised as** Q13-1; U16's promises in a grand jury response; U12's "promised, not done".

**Background.** Standards include an office's public commitments, and a group's later breach claim can rest on one. A deputy director saying "the remaining streets will be done by June" in a private meeting is narrative until it is in the record. A Council-adopted reply to a grand jury ("will be implemented by 31 March 2028") is in the record, but whether it binds or is only a benchmark is read from its text. The investigation design already draws the line between an account and evidence.

**Options.**
- **(a) Only once the body confirms it in writing or states it publicly.** Until then it is kept "as heard" in each member's own account, watched only as the group's expectation, never a standard. The group's confirming letter asks the office to correct the summary; silence is recorded as a dated fact. *Example:* the Director's reply email confirms "by June"; the member holds it as a commitment and adopts it as a duty to watch.
- **(b) The group's confirming letter suffices if the office does not correct it within the group's window.** *Example:* no reply in 14 days, held as "unrefuted".
- **(c) Any member's account may serve as a standard, labelled testimony.**

**Trade-offs.** (a) is strictest and matches your narrative/evidence line. (b) is how lawyers write, but silence is not agreement, and a breach claim could rest on a sentence nobody can check. (c) invites weak breach claims.

**Recommendation: (a), with the office's silence recorded as a dated fact** that may be published as such ("we wrote on 3 October asking the Department to correct our summary; no reply by 17 October").

**Checked against your rulings.** No conflict. Promises in a written response are proposed as duties for a member to adopt in one act, never adopted by the machine; a missed date is told once as a question, never as a violation.

### B11 · Where the no-policy line falls: testimony before a deciding body, and elections

**Raised as** U5-Q1, Q11-1; U12 (thanks to an elected office near an election); U9 (stage-7 drafts).

**Background.** Your rule: Civicsmith takes no position on what policy should be. Political accountability may ask officials to act on a breach, ask for oversight and audits, testify, and support legislation that restores or enforces an existing requirement; policy advocacy and candidate support are not actions. Two places test it:
- *Testimony* before a utilities commission or at a rulemaking almost always touches policy ("should ratepayers pay for this?"), though the group's finding is factual.
- *Elections:* a group telling voters what the record shows about the last bond, with a new bond on the ballot. In California, money spent on material that urges a vote can make a group a campaign committee with filing duties.

**Options.**
- **Testimony:** (a) only enforcement testimony resting on a determination; (b) **factual testimony on any matter (published findings with their grades), plus asking a body to examine or apply an existing requirement**, without asking for a policy outcome (*example:* "The City paid $X for these 14 streetlight projects (exhibits A–D); we ask the Commission to examine whether the same projects are in the utility's request"); (c) any position, disclosed as the group's own view.
- **Elections:** (A) **inform and fence the assistant**: when an option or message names a pending measure or candidate, a short notice says Civicsmith takes no position on how to vote and that spending on material urging a vote may be campaign activity under state law (with the citation and the state ethics agency's advice route); the assistant never drafts vote advice; (B) as (A), and refuse to approve any message containing vote words; (C) nothing special.

**Trade-offs.** On testimony: (a) silences factual help to a body deciding now; (c) crosses your no-policy principle. On elections: (B) is a word filter that is easy to evade and refuses legitimate quotes (the ballot question itself); (C) leaves members unaware of a real legal trap.

**Recommendation: testimony (b); elections (A) plus the member's own statement at approval.**

**Checked against your rulings.** The worker's election (A) said "nothing is refused". Your rule 9 makes vote and candidate advocacy *not actions*, so Civicsmith should not prepare, approve or stamp one as the group's. The corrected recommendation: (A)'s notice and assistant fence, and, when a message names a pending measure or candidate, the approving member states that it urges no vote; a message she says urges a vote is not recorded or stamped as the group's action. This is her decision, not a word filter, so it keeps your Requirement 12 and puts rule 9 where Civicsmith speaks in the group's name. The same notice appears when thanking an elected office whose holder is a candidate. The assistant points out sentences stating a policy preference, for the member to rewrite, in testimony and stage-7 drafts alike.

**For BOB:** Action rule 9, OP1, K14, K590 (6); Requirement 12; `escalation` R12 purposes; profile election facts (§E); `action-plans` R12 `lobbying` flag.
