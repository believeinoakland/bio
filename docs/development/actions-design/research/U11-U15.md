## U11 · An awareness campaign on a bond measure

### 1. Situation

The "Fruitvale Infrastructure Watch" group has a published case. It finds that the City of Oakland's Finance Department did not publish the annual accountability reports for the 2016 infrastructure bond (Measure KK) that Gov. Code §53411 requires. Under that section, the chief fiscal officer reports each year on the amount of bond funds collected and spent and the status of the projects. The facts here are illustrative; **verify** them against the City's actual filings before using them as an example.

The members determined `noncompliant` for fiscal years 2022–23 and 2023–24, and `compliant` for 2024–25.

In this hypothetical, the City Council has now placed a new general obligation bond, "Measure X", on the November ballot. The group wants voters to know what the record shows about the last bond's reporting. It does not want to tell them how to vote. It plans:
- a one-page flyer;
- a community meeting at a branch library;
- a "working on" notice (DEC-111) on its project, "Bond accountability, KK and U".

### 2. What real practitioners do

**Neutral voter-education groups**, such as the League of Women Voters (its "Pros & Cons" sheets) and CalMatters' voter guides, do the following:
- **They separate information from advocacy.** They state what the measure does, what the law requires, and what the record shows, with sources. They never say "vote yes" or "vote no", and never use wording that "unambiguously urges" a result.
- **They review every flyer line by line against its sources before printing.** They print a "paid for by" line or the group's name and date. They keep the source list available.

**Election law that applies to the group.**
- **Political Reform Act.** A person or group becomes a "committee" when, in a calendar year, it:
  - receives contributions of $2,000 or more to support or oppose a measure; or
  - makes independent expenditures of $1,000 or more; or
  - makes contributions of $10,000 or more.

  (Gov. Code §82013(a)–(c), web-checked; **verify** the current text.) A committee must register (Form 410), file reports and print disclaimers (Gov. Code §84501 ff.).
- **Express advocacy.** What counts as express advocacy, and so as an "expenditure", is in FPPC Reg. 18225: express words such as "vote for/against", or a communication that, "taken as a whole and in context, unambiguously urges a particular result" (**verify** the subsection).
- **Factual material.** A factual flyer that urges no vote, paid for by the group, is ordinarily not an expenditure. Its timing, wording and images can change that reading. Practitioners ask the FPPC for written advice (Gov. Code §83114(b)) when unsure.
- **Oakland.** The Oakland Campaign Reform Act (OMC ch. 3.12) and the Public Ethics Commission add local disclosure rules (**verify** which apply to measure committees).
- **Tax status.**
  - A 501(c)(3) may advocate on a ballot measure: the IRS treats it as lobbying, within limits (the IRC §501(h) election).
  - It may never support or oppose a candidate.
  - A 501(c)(4) has wider room.
  - Many small civic groups are unincorporated, and the committee thresholds govern them.

**What the City may not do, which a group can watch.**
- A local agency may not spend public funds to support or oppose a measure (Gov. Code §54964; *Stanson v. Mott* (1976) 17 Cal.3d 206; *Vargas v. City of Salinas* (2009) 46 Cal.4th 1).
- Fair, impartial informational material is allowed.
- A group can ask for the City's measure mailers by records request and hold them to §54964. That enforces an existing requirement, so it sits inside the doctrine.

**Ballot materials.**
- The City Attorney writes an impartial analysis (Elections Code §9280).
- For/against arguments are filed with the City Clerk by the Clerk's dates (Elections Code §9282 ff.). Writing one is advocacy.
- During the public examination period (10 days), any voter may seek a writ to correct false or misleading ballot material (Elections Code §9295; **verify** the section applies to city measures as written). Courts treat this as enforcing an accuracy requirement, not advocacy. It is litigation, Tier 3 in practice.

**Bond accountability laws that the facts rest on.**
- Gov. Code §§53410–53411 (specific purposes, accountability measures, annual report).
- For school bonds under Proposition 39: a citizens' oversight committee and annual performance and financial audits (Cal. Const. art. XIIIA §1(b)(3); Ed. Code §§15278–15282). This is the Grandview case's frame.

**The community meeting.**
- Library meeting-room policies may bar campaign events or require a disclaimer that the library does not endorse the event (**verify** Oakland Public Library's policy).
- Practitioners publish an agenda, invite the office (the Finance Department, the City Auditor) to present or answer, keep a sign-in sheet that is voluntary and not required, and make clear the meeting takes no position on the measure.

**Common failure points:**
- a flyer's headline ("They lost track of $X") outruns the finding ("reports not filed for two years");
- an image or slogan reads as "vote no";
- volunteers distribute with a campaign's literature;
- the group takes money for the flyer from a measure committee, which can make it an agent of that committee;
- a correction of a flyer error comes late or quietly;
- the group misses that the measure's opponents are quoting its case. The group cannot control that, but it should say on record that it takes no position.

### 3. Today in Civicsmith

1. **The case and the determinations.** The group has a published case, and members `determine` per year (conformance R1–R5): built-no-screen. A `compliant` determination for 2024–25 is recorded with the same obligations (conformance R5): built-no-screen.
2. **Open a plan** whose matters are the noncompliant determinations (action-plans R1–R3, category `awareness`, R9): built-no-screen.
3. **Add options:**
   - "Flyer: what the KK reports show" (awareness, addressee `{state: audience, description: "voters in Council District 5"}`, actions R9 audience arm, R10 via the option);
   - "Community meeting at the Fruitvale branch";
   - "Ask the City for its Measure X informational mailers" (a records request).

   Built-no-screen. The option's `lobbying` flag (R12) does not apply, and nothing marks an option "election-related".
4. **The plan's checks** (R19), such as outward options resting only on hypothetical matters or no hostile-response branch: built-no-screen. No check looks at election timing or advocacy wording: not designed.
5. **Start the flyer option** (`optionStart` R18, with preview R37): built-no-screen. Alternatively, a member creates an `action` of kind `media` or `other` with an audience addressee in "Add something new": usable. There is no kind for a flyer, meeting or campaign. The first profile's kinds are `records_request`, `grand_jury`, `controller_referral`, `public_comment`, `media`, `litigation_support`, `request_for_comment`, `other` and Tier 2/3 legal kinds (`oakland-alameda.mjs`:457–485).
6. **Draft the flyer text** with `communicationPrepare` (filings R23), labelled a draft. A member then approves it (`filingApprove`, R6). The approved bytes carry the in-band stamp (R22). The member records it sent (`filingRecordSent`, R7): built-no-screen. Writing help with the assistant ("Help me write this", DEC-153) is not built. No check compares the flyer's sentences with the published findings. The case-account check of D56 exists only for case accounts: not designed for communications.
7. **The meeting.** The meeting is an action with an `audience` addressee. Its agenda is a communication draft. What was said is recorded as a testimony correspondence entry (actions R15–R16, `held_as: testimony`): `actioncorrespond` is usable. Questions raised at the meeting have no structured place, and nothing turns them into inquiries: not designed.
8. **The "working on" notice.** `prepareNotice`/`postNotice` (network-notices R1–R5) is owner-only, carries the outward-act warning, the slug, wording up to 280 characters, optionally the body and matter, `since`, and "Interested in collaborating". The copy signs the activity level monthly (R12–R13). Built-no-screen. The notice is not an action, and nothing links it to the plan.
9. **The records request** for the City's measure mailers: an action of kind `records_request` (usable), with clocks per U1.
10. **Closing.** The action resolves `completed` (actions R7) and the plan closes with a reason (R20): built-no-screen. Whether the awareness work reached anyone is recorded nowhere: not designed. No measuring is allowed of members; measuring the audience is undecided.

### 4. What Civicsmith should do

**The capabilities the group needs:**

- **An awareness action on a published case.** An outward communication to a described audience (flyer, meeting, post, newsletter) prepared *from a published edition*. Each factual sentence is tied to the finding it rests on, using the same per-sentence check the case account uses (D56).
  - A sentence the record does not support is flagged before approval.
  - The flyer carries the case's link and edition, and a correction line if the edition changes.
  - An awareness action never asserts a breach unless a live noncompliant determination stands (Action rule 2). Here it does.
- **An election-period fence, shown as information.** When an option's matters or text name a measure or a candidate on a pending ballot, the start preview shows a plain notice:
  - Civicsmith supports no vote and no candidate (Action rule 9; K14).
  - Election law may treat spending on communications that urge a result as campaign activity. The profile gives the thresholds and their citations (Gov. Code §82013) as procedural facts (K1474 (iii)).
  - The group can get written advice from the FPPC.

  The notice informs and never refuses (Requirement 12). The doctrine itself (no vote advocacy) is a refusal of *Civicsmith's own help*: the assistant never drafts "vote yes/no" text.
- **Ballot-measure facts in the profile** (rule 11):
  - the election calendar and the Clerk's argument deadlines;
  - the 10-day public examination window, held as a `rule` deadline with citation;
  - the §54964 standard;
  - for school districts, the Proposition 39 oversight requirements.
- **A watch on the City's own conduct.** A records request for the City's measure communications. A standard (§54964) held in `standards`. A determination if the City's mailer "unambiguously urges".
- **The meeting as an event the group hosts.** Agenda (a communication draft), an invitation addressed to offices to attend or answer, and the member's account afterwards (testimony). Questions raised become proposed inquiries a member accepts. Attendee names are never recorded: the people present are private individuals (§9 of Investigation; Action rule 6).
- **The "working on" notice beside the plan.** From a plan whose matters belong to a project with a notice, show that the notice exists and its level. Offer the owner the notice act, with its warning, when an awareness option starts. Never post it automatically.
- **Recording what came of it** without measuring people or reach. The action's own outcome is `completed` plus an optional member account ("about 40 attended; the Finance Department sent a deputy, who said the reports would be posted by 1 November"). A commitment heard at the meeting can become a dated commitment to watch (see U13).

**For the member who never uses the assistant (D42):**
- the same flyer check runs on her own words;
- the election notice and the profile facts appear in the preview whether or not AI is on;
- a plain template library offers group-authored flyer and meeting-agenda templates (filing-templates R1–R15, `use: brief` or a new communication use).

**Where AI help belongs:**
- turning findings into plain-language sentences, each tied to its finding;
- translations (Spanish, Cantonese and Vietnamese are common in Oakland);
- a meeting agenda;
- anticipated questions, with the record's answer to each.

**Where it stops:**
- no vote recommendations;
- no persuasive framing beyond the record;
- no judgment of whether the group has "become a committee". That is legal advice. The assistant shows the thresholds and the FPPC advice route.
- Drafts are proposals (D2).
- The member prints and distributes.

### 5. Gaps

1. [never-designed] No awareness action shape: an audience-addressed communication tied sentence by sentence to a published edition, with a correction path when the edition changes.
2. [doctrine-question] Whether an awareness communication must rest on a published case at all, or may rest on unpublished work. Publication's "leaves one way" against Action rule 7's communication drafts.
3. [never-designed] No election-period fence: no information notice about campaign-law thresholds, and no rule that the assistant never drafts vote advocacy.
4. [capability-unbuilt] The profile holds no election facts: ballot calendar, argument deadlines, §9295 examination window, §82013 thresholds, §54964 standard.
5. [never-designed] A hosted meeting has no shape: agenda, invited offices, account afterwards, questions → proposed inquiries. Its kind would be `other`.
6. [never-designed] Nothing links a "working on" notice (network-notices) to a plan or its options; the notice is invisible from the plan.
7. [reach] No screen for plans, communications, approvals, sending or notices (all built-no-screen).
8. [capability-unbuilt] Writing help and translations for communications (DEC-153; ladders QUESTIONS L2).
9. [never-designed] No record of what an awareness action achieved, beyond `completed` and the member's account. Whether any reach figure is allowed is undecided.
10. [doctrine-question] When a published case is quoted by a measure campaign, Civicsmith offers nothing: no docket entry kind for "used by a campaign", and no statement of the group's neutrality.

### 6. Candidate questions for Bob

**Q11-1 · What Civicsmith does when a group's work touches an election.**

*Background.* Your rule is "policy advocacy and candidate support are not actions" (K14, Action rule 9). A bond measure is a policy question on the ballot. A group may tell voters what the record shows about the last bond, but in California, money spent on material that urges a vote can make the group a campaign committee with filing duties. The question is how Civicsmith behaves near an election.

*Options:*
- (A) **Inform and fence the assistant.** When an option or communication names a pending ballot measure or candidate, the preview shows a short notice ("Civicsmith takes no position on how to vote. Spending on material that urges a vote may be campaign activity under state law (Gov. Code §82013); the state ethics agency gives written advice."). The assistant never drafts vote advice. Nothing is refused. *Example:* the flyer says "the City filed no KK reports for two years (case link)". The notice appears, and the member prints it.
- (B) **Refuse vote words.** As (A), and Civicsmith also refuses to approve a communication containing "vote yes/no", "support/oppose Measure X" or a candidate's name with a call to vote. *Example:* a member types "Vote No on X" and approval is refused with the reason.
- (C) **No special handling.** The general doctrine stands, and members are trusted.

*Trade-offs:* (A) keeps your Requirement 12 (no tool gates an action) and gives honest information. (B) protects the group and the doctrine more strongly, but it is a word filter that is easy to evade and can refuse legitimate quotes, such as quoting the ballot question. (C) leaves members unaware of a real legal trap.

*Recommendation:* **(A)**. It matches how you treated the breach gate (refuse only what doctrine forbids, disclose otherwise) and how K1474 treats legal facts. The doctrine is enforced where Civicsmith has its own voice, in the assistant, and the member stays the author.

**Q11-2 · Must an awareness communication rest on a published case?**

*Background.* A breach claim needs a published finding and a determination. A flyer or meeting talk is the group speaking to the public, which is close to publishing.

*Options:*
- (A) **Yes, for any factual claim about a body.** It cites a published edition. Each sentence is checked against the findings, as the case account is. *Example:* the flyer's three facts each link to a finding of Edition 2.
- (B) **Only breach claims need it.** Other statements may rest on the record unpublished, labelled "from our working record". *Example:* "We have asked the City for its mailers" needs no case.
- (C) **No rule.** The member decides.

*Recommendation:* **(A) for claims about what a body did or failed to do, with (B)'s allowance for statements about the group's own activity.** This keeps "what the group stands behind leaves one way", and lets a group say what it is doing.

## U12 · A success story: recognising a department that fixed what it was asked to fix

### 1. Situation

After the group's records request, and its letter citing Gov. Code §53411 (U11's case), the City of Oakland Finance Department posted the missing Measure KK annual reports. It also adopted a written procedure in its Administrative Instruction for posting each report by 31 December. The members determine `compliant` for the new year's report against §53411 and against the City's own new procedure, a public commitment. They want to thank the department publicly at the Council's Finance and Management Committee open forum, send a letter, and say so in the case. In the Grandview variant, the school district's bond oversight committee began reporting construction delays at each meeting after the case.

### 2. What real practitioners do

**Auditors.**
- Auditors' offices track recommendations to "implemented". The Oakland City Auditor issues follow-up reports on recommendation status (**verify** current cadence). The GAO publishes "recommendations implemented" and the savings attributed to them.
- Government Auditing Standards (the Yellow Book, GAGAS) require auditors to follow up, and to report corrective action as carefully as findings.
- The finding is closed only on evidence, such as a posted report or an adopted procedure, never on a promise.

**Newsrooms.**
- They run "impact" follow-ups ("after our story, the City posted…"). They state causation carefully: "after", not "because", unless an official says so on record.
- They keep an impact log, and many nonprofit newsrooms report impact to funders.

**Community groups.**
- They thank publicly: at open forum (Brown Act §54954.3 public comment), by letter to the department head copied to the City Administrator and Council, or through a ceremonial commendation that a Councilmember brings.
- They name the office, and often the staff who did the work, since public employees acting in an official role may be named.
- They keep watching for a year to see whether the fix holds. The next report is the test.

**Failure points:**
- declaring victory before the fix is verified;
- claiming credit the record cannot support;
- thanking an elected official in a way that reads as campaign support during an election period. The City Auditor and the Council are elected. Gov. Code §54964 limits the City's resources, and the Political Reform Act applies to the group's spending;
- never recording that the problem recurred.

### 3. Today in Civicsmith

1. **Compliant determination** (conformance R1–R5, "compliant carries the same obligations"): built-no-screen.
2. **A plan may start from a compliant determination** (K590 (5); action-plans R1–R3), with an option of category `awareness` or `other`: built-no-screen. There is no "recognition" category; categories are mitigation, legal, awareness, journalistic, grassroots, other (R9).
3. **An action** addressed to `{role: "Director of Finance", body: "Finance Department"}`, kind `other` or `public_comment`: usable through "Add something new". The old add flow's named addressee can collide with R9 (R1 tension 9).
4. **The thank-you letter and the open-forum remarks** as communication drafts (filings R23), approved and recorded sent (R6, R7): built-no-screen.
5. **Escalation end.** If the group had an escalation open on the noncompliant years: `escalationEnd` needs a live compliant determination of the same act per standard, and the consequences addressed (escalation R14; consequences R9 `addressedRecord`). Built-no-screen. A success after an escalation can therefore close it properly.
6. **The plan's outcome.** The action resolves `complied` (actions R7). The plan closes with a reason (R20). Built-no-screen. Nothing records what was achieved across the plan: not designed (working page §4.3).
7. **The effect of the success.** Consequences exist only for breaches (`CONSEQUENCE_NOT_NONCOMPLIANT`, consequences R1). The effect of a success, such as "$600M of bond spending now reported annually", cannot be recorded: not designed (R1 tension 13).
8. **The causal claim.** "After the group's request" is an action outcome, and an impact claim needs a leg outside the group's own actions (DEC-14; actions R26): built-no-screen. This is right and needs no change.
9. **Watching whether the fix holds.** A duty adopted from §53411 with yearly occurrences (duties R2–R14). Its overdue raises "Noticed" (notice-producers R5). Built-no-screen. A watch of the City's report page (following R10 `followPortal`; monitoring): built-no-screen.

### 4. What Civicsmith should do

- **Recognition is an action kind of its own in the product's vocabulary.** It is not a profile kind, because it is law-neutral like `request_for_comment` (actions R10). Its rules:
  - it rests on a live compliant determination, or on a member's stated reason when the success is not against a standard, such as a department's good service;
  - it is addressed to an office, and the read shows the holder that day (actions R9, R25);
  - its text may name staff acting in their official roles (K1483), but never thanks a candidate as a candidate.
- **A success record for the plan and the matter.** "What came of it" is recorded per matter as a member's statement resting on evidence (the posted report, the adopted procedure). It is not a score. It is recorded at the same weight as a breach's consequence, and it may name what the fix covers, using the arithmetic over cited figures that consequences already has (consequences R2), outside the breach frame. This closes R1 tension 13.
- **Verification before recognition.** The start preview of a recognition option shows the determination it rests on and its evidence, and warns when the fix is only a promise: "Promised, not yet done. The record holds a commitment, not a posted report." This mirrors auditors' "implemented on evidence".
- **Keep watching, if the member chooses.** On recognition, the member may adopt the duty (yearly report) and a watch of the page. The next occurrence is told once if missed. Recurrence reopens the matter in the same plan or a new one. Nothing nags (DEC-94).
- **The case gains a dated "since our case" entry** on the docket. This is the group's own lane, "what we did", never evidence (docket R26). The case may add a compliant finding in a new edition.
- **Election-period caution, as in U11 (A).** When the addressee is an elected office and an election in which the holder is a candidate is pending, the preview says recognition will be read publicly and may be taken as support. The member decides.

**For the member who never uses the assistant:** recognition templates in the group's library; the same preview facts. **AI help:** draft the thank-you from the determination and evidence, with every claim cited; flag "because" sentences the record cannot support. **Stops:** no praise of persons beyond their official acts; no causal claim beyond the evidence; no publication or sending by the machine.

### 5. Gaps

1. [never-designed] No recognition kind or category; success is squeezed into `awareness`/`other`.
2. [never-designed] No record of a success's effect: consequences are breach-only (consequences R1). There is no "what came of the plan" (working page §4.3).
3. [never-designed] No "fix holds?" pattern linking recognition to adopting the duty and a watch. Pieces exist in duties, following and monitoring, but nothing joins them.
4. [doctrine-question] Recognition of an elected office's holder during that holder's campaign: whether Civicsmith warns, and whether recognition may name individuals.
5. [reach] Compliant determinations, plans, communications and escalation end have no screen.
6. [capability-unbuilt] Docket entries tying the group's later success acts to the case: the docket has the group lane (R26), but no link from an action's outcome to a docket entry.

### 6. Candidate questions for Bob

**Q12-1 · How is a success recorded?**

*Background.* You ruled that "compliance counts" and "recognition and success stories are actions". Today Civicsmith can record that a body complied, but not what the fix achieved, or that the group's plan ended in success.

*Options:*
- (A) **A success record per matter**, a member's statement on evidence, with optional computed figures ("Reports for FY22–24 posted 3 Oct; procedure AI-xxx adopted; covers $600M of bond spending"). It is shown in the plan, the case's docket and the "what we did" timeline. No score.
- (B) **The action's own outcome only** (`complied` plus the member's account), as today.
- (C) **A consequence record widened to successes** ("benefit" parts beside "harm" parts).

*Recommendation:* **(A)**. (C) bends a construct built around breach and causation, and (B) loses what a reader most needs. (A) keeps the facts sourced and avoids judging.

**Q12-2 · May a recognition name the people who did the work?**

*Background.* Actions are addressed to offices, never people (rule 6). You also ruled that people materially involved are named as a finding needs (K1483).

*Options:*
- (A) **Addressed to the office; the text may name staff in their official roles**, with a reminder that public thanks names them publicly. *Example:* "To the Director of Finance … thanks to the Budget Bureau's reporting team, including Ms. A. Rivera."
- (B) **Office only; no names in text.**
- (C) **Names allowed, with each named person's consent recorded.**

*Recommendation:* **(A)**. It is consistent with K1483. Public employees in official acts are already nameable, and the warning covers the rest.

## U13 · Meeting the official: requesting a meeting, preparing, recording, follow-up

### 1. Situation

The group's determination finds that the Oakland Department of Transportation (OakDOT) missed its own published paving-plan commitment. The 5-Year Paving Plan, adopted by the Council, listed 14 streets in District 5 for 2025, and 6 were paved. This is illustrative; **verify** the actual figures.

The group asks for a meeting with the OakDOT Director. Separately, it asks for a meeting with the District 5 Councilmember. At the Director's meeting, the deputy director says the remaining 8 streets will be done "by June 2026", and that the department will post a revised schedule within 30 days. The group wants to:
- prepare for the meeting;
- record what was said and agreed;
- confirm it in writing;
- hold the department to the date.

### 2. What real practitioners do

**Requesting the meeting.**
- A short written request to the office (the Director's executive assistant, or the Councilmember's chief of staff) states the purpose, the group, the attendees by role, three time options and the documents attached.
- Requests and calendars are public records:
  - officials' calendars are disclosable under the CPRA, subject to exemptions (**verify** current case law on calendars, e.g. *Times Mirror Co. v. Superior Court* (1991) 53 Cal.3d 1325 on the Governor's calendar);
  - the group's emails to officials are public records too (Gov. Code §7920.530). The Design Requirement 11 starter kit already warns of this.

**Preparing.**
- The materials are:
  - a one-page "leave-behind" (the finding, the record, the ask);
  - an agenda sent in advance;
  - named roles (who speaks, who takes notes);
  - the ask in one sentence;
  - anticipated answers with the record's reply to each.
- Auditors call the equivalent an "exit conference" with management. The auditee's written response, with a corrective action plan and target dates, becomes part of the report.
- Lawyers treat it as a "meet and confer". They follow it with a confirming letter, because what is not in writing did not happen.

**Law around the meeting.**
- *Brown Act.* A meeting with one Councilmember is not a "meeting". A series of one-on-ones that develops a majority's collective concurrence is a prohibited serial meeting (Gov. Code §54952.2(b)(1)). The burden is on officials, but a group that ferries positions between members can create the problem.
- *Recording.* California is an all-party-consent state for confidential communications (Penal Code §632). Ask before recording audio; most practitioners take notes instead.
- *Lobbying.* Under the Oakland Lobbyist Registration Act, an individual must register who is paid $1,000 or more in a calendar month to communicate with City officials to influence a City action, or whose paid duties as an employee, officer or director include such communication (an "in-house lobbyist") (OMC 3.20.030(D), web-checked; **verify** the code text). Unpaid volunteers are generally not lobbyists. A paid staff member of a funded nonprofit may be. Lobbying "only to enforce or restore an existing requirement" (K590 (6)) is Civicsmith's doctrine; registration is the law's separate question.
- *Gifts.* Not offering anything of value (Political Reform Act gift limits; Oakland Measure OO for lobbyists) is a standard practice note.

**After the meeting.**
- Within 24–48 hours, a follow-up email to the office summarises:
  - what was discussed;
  - each commitment, with who (by role), what and by when;
  - what the group will do;
  - a request to correct anything misstated.
- Silence after a reasonable time is itself recorded. Newsrooms confirm on-record ground rules before an interview and send quotes for accuracy checks. Advocacy groups log every contact with an official.

**Failure points:**
- no written follow-up, so the commitment evaporates;
- notes kept by one member on a personal device and lost;
- unclear who said what;
- treating a staffer's oral "we'll try" as an agency commitment;
- recording without consent;
- the meeting becoming a negotiation in which the group trades away its ask without a record.

### 3. Today in Civicsmith

### 2. What real practitioners do

- **Sign-on letters:** one text, many organisational signatories. Each organisation approves the final text through its own process. Signatures are listed by organisation, with a contact person.

## U15 · The group under pressure: a threatening letter, the litigation hold, anti-SLAPP, the record as evidence

### 1. Situation

The Grandview Parents' published case names the construction-management contractor, illustratively "Bayline Construction Management, Inc.", as the author of reports showing that the district knew of the delay. The group sent the district a demand that its bond oversight committee be told (a breach action, stage 2 of the escalation). Then three things happened.

1. **A letter from Bayline's law firm.** It says the case is defamatory, demands a retraction within 10 days, demands that the group "preserve all documents", and threatens suit.
2. **The district's response to the demand.** It includes a sentence that the district "will evaluate all legal options regarding the group's members' conduct at board meetings".
3. **A demand from a board member.** A school board member writes demanding removal of her home address, which appears in a captured campaign filing quoted in an exhibit. Gov. Code §7928.215 is in the profile as a 48-hour lawful demand.

### 2. What real practitioners do

**On receiving the threat letter:**
- Do not respond substantively without counsel.
- Log receipt (date, how received, envelope or email headers).
- Calendar every date in it.
- Notify any insurer: a media-liability or directors-and-officers policy.

Free or low-cost help for California groups and journalists includes the First Amendment Coalition, the California Anti-SLAPP Project, the Reporters Committee for Freedom of the Press legal hotline, the EFF and the ACLU of Northern California (**verify** current intake). The first profile already lists the First Amendment Coalition, for `constitutional_claim` only (`oakland-alameda.mjs`).

**The litigation hold.**
- The duty to preserve arises when litigation is reasonably anticipated. A demand letter that threatens suit usually triggers it.
- California has no independent tort for spoliation by a party (*Cedars-Sinai Medical Center v. Superior Court* (1998) 18 Cal.4th 1). It does have evidentiary inferences (Evid. Code §413; CACI 204, willful suppression) and discovery sanctions (CCP §2023.030).
- In federal court, FRCP 37(e) governs lost electronically stored information.
- Practitioners issue a written hold notice to every custodian. It:
  - suspends auto-deletion (email retention, chat apps with disappearing messages, assistant transcripts);
  - preserves originals with metadata;
  - is re-issued periodically;
  - is released in writing when the matter ends.

**The retraction demand.**
- Newsrooms review each challenged statement against the record:
  - correct what is wrong, promptly and visibly;
  - stand by what the record supports;
  - never retract to make a threat go away.
- California's retraction statute (Civil Code §48a) limits damages unless a correction is demanded within 20 days of knowledge of publication. It applies to daily or weekly news publications and broadcasters (**verify** whether it reaches an online civic group; courts have differed).
- The limitation period for defamation is one year (CCP §340(c)).
- Privileges:
  - fair and true report of official proceedings and public records (Civil Code §47(d));
  - statements in official proceedings (§47(b)).
- Public-figure plaintiffs, and limited-purpose public figures such as a public contractor on the matter, must show actual malice (*New York Times v. Sullivan*).

**Anti-SLAPP.**
- CCP §425.16 allows a special motion to strike a claim arising from protected activity, including:
  - statements made before or in connection with an issue under consideration by a legislative, executive or judicial body;
  - statements in a public forum on an issue of public interest;
  - other conduct in furtherance of free speech on a public issue (§425.16(e)).
- Two steps: the defendant shows protected activity, then the plaintiff must show a probability of prevailing.
- Filed within 60 days of service of the complaint (§425.16(f)). Discovery is stayed (§425.16(g)). A prevailing defendant gets mandatory attorney's fees (§425.16(c)). The order is immediately appealable (§425.16(i)).
- Exemptions are in §425.17. The Roadmap's appendix notes that records requests and grand jury complaints are protected activity (R2).
- Counsel files it. The deadline runs from service, not from the letter.

**Government retaliation.**
- A public body's threats over speech at board meetings may support a First Amendment retaliation claim (42 U.S.C. §1983) if acted on.
- Practitioners document it: dates, exact words, who said it, in what capacity, as pressure evidence (Operational Principle 8).
- Public comment is protected (Brown Act §54954.3(c): a body may not prohibit public criticism of its policies or employees). **Verify** that this applies to a school board; boards are under the Brown Act.

**A subpoena** for the group's records, if it comes:
- The California reporter's shield (Cal. Const. art. I §2(b); Evid. Code §1070) protects people "connected with" news publications from contempt. Whether it covers a civic group is fact-specific (*O'Grady v. Superior Court* (2006) 139 Cal.App.4th 1423 extended protection to online news). **Verify**.
- Counsel moves to quash.

**The home-address demand.**
- Gov. Code §7928.215 requires removal of an elected or appointed official's home address or telephone number within 48 hours of a written demand. Civicsmith's profile holds it as a `lawful_demand` (K1493).
- It is complied with openly, as a court order is (K1480).

- missing the 60-day anti-SLAPP window because nobody tracked service;

  - each challenged statement with the finding, the edition and the sources it rests on, and each exhibit's grade;
  - the chronology in both lanes (filings R33);
  - the threat and other pressure entries;
  - the deadlines held in the profile with their citations: anti-SLAPP 60 days from service (§425.16(f)), the defamation limitation (§340(c)), and the §48a correction window, marked **verify** for non-news publishers;
  - the privileges that may bear, as candidate theories labelled as such (filings R14).

  The packet keeps "Not legal advice. Not for filing." It is never published.
- **A response review, not a retraction reflex.** The challenged statements are opened for re-evaluation against the record (docket R1 → reevaluation). Each is marked "stands", "corrected in edition N" or "withdrawn" by members, with reasons. A correction is a new edition with its notice (never silent). The group's reply to the law firm is a communication draft, and the preview says that counsel's review before replying is advised.
- **Opponent deadlines are shown, not adopted as law.** A demand's date ("retract within 10 days") is shown as "their stated deadline" (a new basis kind, or `window` labelled as the other side's). It is never presented as a legal deadline (K1431). A reminder is set only if asked.
- **Help finding counsel.** Profile legal organisations for *defence* matters: anti-SLAPP, media law. Today `legal_organisations.evaluates` lists only Tier 3 offensive kinds. Shown as contacts, never chosen for the group.
- **Lawful demands complied with openly.** The 48-hour home-address demand gets a to-do with its clock and citation (Gov. Code §7928.215), an act that redacts the item in a new edition, and a docket entry naming the demand. The demand itself is held as evidence and may also be pressure if it is a pretext. Members decide.
- **Pressure as its own inquiry.** From a pressure mark, the member may open an inquiry ("Did the district threaten members for comments at board meetings?"), resting on the captured statement. Retaliation by a public body is a government act that may later be determined against the Brown Act's protection of public criticism (§54954.3(c)), and the First Amendment.
- **The work survives disruption** (Action rule 12). The case file (DEC-112), the counsel packet and the evidence package export are what a partner group or counsel carries on with. A "continuity" view lists them and when each was last exported.

**For the member without the assistant:** every step above is a plain form with profile facts. The hold notice comes from a group-library template.

**AI help:**
- summarising the threat letter: what it demands, by when, and which statements it challenges, each quoted;
- matching each challenged statement to its finding and sources;
- drafting the hold notice and a holding reply ("we have received your letter and are reviewing it with counsel").

**Stops:**
- never says whether the case is defamatory;
- never says whether to retract;
- never says whether anti-SLAPP applies or should be filed (K1474: "never … what to file", "never 'you have standing'");
- never contacts counsel or the sender;
- never deletes or edits anything during a hold.

### 5. Gaps

1. [never-designed] Pressure that does not arrive as a reply to an action has no home. A letter to the group about its case must be forced into an invented action.
2. [capability-unbuilt] A docket `legal` pressure mark raises no litigation hold (docket Suggestions, "not drafted"; R6 §2.5).
3. [reach] The litigation-hold to-do is visible but cannot be answered; no screen exists for hold, release, preview or pressure.
4. [capability-unbuilt] The device half of the hold (DEC-113; N521) and the held-project strip are not built.
5. [never-designed] No hold notice to members covering material outside Civicsmith (email, phones, chat apps).
6. [defect] The counsel packet requires a determination or an override (filings R8). A defence packet would be mislabelled as an overridden breach claim.
7. [capability-unbuilt] The profile holds no defence-side claim deadlines (§425.16(f), §340(c), Civil Code §48a) and no defence legal organisations.
8. [never-designed] No basis kind for an opponent's stated deadline. `commitment` and `window` mean other things (K1431).
9. [never-designed] No pressure timeline across actions, docket and testimony (ladders EVENTS E5 at L1).
10. [capability-unbuilt] The `lawful_demands` act and its 48-hour clock: the profile holds the fact; **verify** whether a member act and to-do exist.
11. [doctrine-question] Whether Civicsmith offers any guidance when a group is subpoenaed or sued: the DR 11 starter kit promises "how to respond to legal threats", and DECISIONS notes that "nothing in the design tells members what a court can reach".

### 6. Candidate questions for Bob

**Q15-1 · What should a litigation hold reach?**

*Background.* Your DEC-113 hold stops deletion of what Civicsmith holds: assistant transcripts on devices, purges, scheduled publishing. In a real lawsuit, the duty to preserve reaches every member's email, texts and chats about the matter. Civicsmith cannot reach those, and must not watch members.

*Options:*
- (A) **Add a hold notice to members.** It is prepared when the hold is placed, approved and sent by a member's own hand, and lists in plain words what to keep. No tracking of who read or complied. *Example:* "Litigation hold, Grandview: please keep every email, text and Signal message about Bayline or the rebuild from 1 Jan 2025. Turn off disappearing messages in group chats. Do not delete or edit."
- (B) **(A) plus acknowledgement:** each member may record "I have read the hold notice" (optional, never counted or shown per member).
- (C) **Keep the hold to Civicsmith's own material.** Members are told only by the strip.

*Trade-offs:* (C) leaves the group's biggest spoliation risk untouched. (B) gives counsel a record that notice was given, but it edges toward measuring members. (A) gives the record "notice was sent, with these bytes, on this date" without tracking people.

*Recommendation:* **(A)**. Sending the notice is itself recorded with its bytes, which is the proof counsel needs. Your no-measuring rule holds.

**Q15-2 · Should the counsel packet serve the group's defence?**

*Background.* The counsel packet was designed for a group pressing a breach claim, and it needs a determination. A group that is threatened or sued needs the same kind of packet (facts, sources, chronology, exhibits with grades, deadlines) to defend itself.

*Options:*
- (A) **A defence purpose for the packet,** resting on the challenged publication and the threat, with defence deadlines from the profile. Never published or fileable.
- (B) **Use the existing packet with an override,** as today, with the disclosure.
- (C) **No defence packet;** counsel works from the case file.

*Recommendation:* **(A)**. (B) stamps the group's defence as "a breach claim on an unestablished premise", which is false and harmful in front of counsel. (C) wastes what Civicsmith already assembles.

**Q15-3 · How far should Civicsmith go in telling a threatened group about anti-SLAPP and related protections?**

*Options:*
- (A) **Profile facts only,** shown on the threat and in the packet: the statute, its 60-day window from service, mandatory fees, where to find counsel. No assessment of whether it applies.
- (B) **(A) plus the condition-by-condition layout you allowed in K1474 (iv),** for example "statement made in a public forum on an issue of public interest: met / unmet / undetermined", always routed to counsel.
- (C) **A general guide in the starter kit only.**

*Recommendation:* **(A) now, (B) when the backward question (D-165) is taken up.** (B) is the same procedural reasoning you deferred, and a threatened group is the riskiest place to try it first.

## Cross-cutting patterns across U11–U15

1. **Most of these actions are not "against a government office over a breach".** An awareness flyer goes to an audience. Thanks go to an office for compliance. A meeting is two-way. A coalition has partners. Pressure arrives from a private firm. The built model handles all of them only as kind `other`, with the breach machinery unused or misfitting. The kinds needed are law-neutral product kinds, like `request_for_comment`: **awareness communication, recognition, meeting, joint/sign-on, and a pressure record not tied to an action.** Each needs its own preview facts and stops.
2. **"As heard" against "held".** Meetings (U13), community meetings (U11) and threats delivered orally (U15) produce narrative. Each needs the investigation lane's line: an account is kept in the member's words, and becomes a held commitment or evidence only when the record holds it (D20). One "heard → confirm in writing → held" pattern serves all three.
3. **Communications checked against the published record.** U11's flyer, U12's thanks, U13's leave-behind, U14's joint letter and U15's reply all make claims about bodies. One per-sentence check against the published findings (the D56 case-account check, widened to communications) serves all of them, for the assistant user and the unaided member alike.
4. **Profile facts as information, never advice.** Each use case needs jurisdiction data that does not exist yet: election thresholds and calendars (U11); recording consent, the serial-meeting rule and the lobbyist threshold (U13); common-interest privilege notes (U14); anti-SLAPP, limitation, retraction and lawful-demand windows, and defence legal organisations (U15). The K1474 pattern applies throughout: show the cited fact and route to counsel; never "you should", "you are a committee" or "file this".
5. **Outcomes beyond the breach frame.** Success effects (U12), awareness results (U11), coalition results (U14) and the defence outcome (U15) have no record. The plan needs "what came of it" per matter, as a sourced member statement, not a score.
6. **Watching after the act.** Commitments (U13), fixes (U12) and the grand jury's report (U14) all need "adopt the duty, follow the page, tell me once if missed". The duty and following modules hold the parts, but nothing on an action offers them (working page §4.1, "nothing proposes the next act").
7. **Every one of these stops at reach.** Communications, approval, recording the sending, plans, duties, holds, pressure, notices and the doorbell are all built-no-screen. Even the parts that exist cannot be used until the action screens and the queue's per-kind doors are drawn.
8. **The doctrine's edges are exercised here more than anywhere.** No vote or candidate support (U11, U12), addressees as offices while thanking people (U12), lobbying only to enforce (U13), sovereignty between groups (U14) and preparing for opposition (U15). Each needs one plain rule shown at the moment it matters, enforced in Civicsmith's own voice (the assistant), and disclosed rather than refused for the member's own acts.
