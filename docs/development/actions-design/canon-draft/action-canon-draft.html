1. What the layer is for

The Action layer is where a group turns what it has learned into what it does. It covers the whole span: from the first suspicion that something is wrong or working, through planning, preparing, deciding and sending, to tracking the response and recording how it ended. Functional Architecture v3 names it "Turn findings into outputs: work products, publications, escalations, communications, and ongoing tracking."

Different groups act differently on the same record: a reporter reports, an activist works to fix, a lawyer supports or changes a claim, an oversight body refers, an administrator responds. A group may take many actions of different kinds on one matter, or none; it may act on a success as well as on a breach (Bob, 2026-09-29).

new Actions take many shapes: asking a body for records or comment; enforcing the group's own requests; complaining to an oversight office; speaking or testifying at a meeting; taking part as a formal party; working with the press; meeting officials; recognising success; acting jointly with other groups; briefing counsel, and defending the group. A plan may be developed and acted on before any case is published, so a published case may carry a record of the actions taken (Bob, 2026-10-09, Actions D22).

2. The contract

changed An action rests on the record. One asserting a breach rests on a member's determination against a standard held in the record, made on findings that are published, checked for publication and held, or another group's findings the group has accepted. The group plans and decides every act; the AI proposes and prepares, and never files or sends. Compliance is recorded as carefully as noncompliance. Every deadline names its basis. Civicsmith holds evidence and takes no position on policy; members and groups may, and Civicsmith is never their instrument (Actions D11).

How they connect: an inquiry opens a plan (suspected); publication, changed or a checked and held case, and a determination make its matters determined; consequences measure a breach; a chosen option starts an action; a filing or communication prepares what the action sends; a breach action joins its determination's escalation; replies return as correspondence, new and each recorded response sets off a re-examination of the plan (rule 16); an action resolves with its own outcome; the plan closes by a member's act, new keeping a closing summary. Monitoring (layer 10) watches the actions' clocks and the escalations' triggers and tells members; the queue carries the reminders.

4. The rules

  1. Humans decide. A member takes every act that commits the group: declaring a standard, determining, assessing a consequence, choosing an option, approving, sending, advancing a stage, resolving, closing. The machine may find, compare, compute, propose and draft, always labelled as machine work, and never does any of these acts (DEC-24, DEC-27; Roadmap §10).
  2. [changed] The gate is at the outward act, not the reasoning (DEC-26), and a member may pass it openly, on stated grounds (Bob, 2026-09-30; Actions D1, D18). A plan may rest on premises not yet established, shown as hunch debt. Outward acts are classed by what their text does: asking an office to examine something, seeking evidence (a records request, a request for comment), and enforcing the group's own request on its own dated record are never gated. A sentence that asserts a breach as the group's finding needs a live noncompliant determination; sentences that read as assertions are pointed out before approval and resolved by the member, her resolution recorded. Without a determination, an act asserting a breach may proceed only on one of two grounds, a legal deadline that falls before a determination could be made (cited, with its date) or an imminent irreversible act (what, and when), together with the member's reason; the ground and the reason are disclosed on the action and everything prepared from it. Where the law's window is short, the window is shown beside the refusal with a quick route to a checked case; an action sent on these grounds may join the escalation once a determination is made, its override kept in its history (Actions D3). No tool has the final word (Requirement 12), and a departure from the group's standard is visible to every reader. Counsel may be briefed at any stage, for a claim or for the group's defence, without a determination (Actions D9).
  3. No significance, no score. Whether a matter warrants action, and how urgently, is a member's judgment, recorded only in acts and their reasons: a declined option's reason, an escalation stage declined with a reason. No field holds significance, severity, priority or a score. new The planning run lists every option it proposes, with no cut-off (Actions D17); no ending is rated.
  4. Compliance counts. A compliant determination is recorded with the same care as a noncompliant one, and may start a plan: recognition and success stories are actions. new A success has its own record (§3); recognition may name staff acting in their official roles.
  5. Every deadline names its basis, and members are told without being nagged. Reminders follow Bob's notification rulings (DEC-10, DEC-69, DEC-70 and DEC-94): a deadline reminder is the member's own request, set when the option is chosen from defaults the member sees; it fires as asked, and its response offers another reminder or none; an overdue date notifies once; a nearing date changes display only; no outside channel; nothing is repeated unless the member asks (K613–K615). new A missed date is shown as a question until a member determines noncompliance on the record. The group's own obligations to a venue are held as its own deadlines, distinct from a body's duties (Actions D15); an action to a body that owes no reply never records silence as non-response; the other side's stated deadline is never presented as a legal one.
  6. Addressees are roles, not people. An action is addressed to a government office by role and body, a reporter or outlet, an organisation or another civic group by role and organisation, or a described audience; never a private individual; the page may show who holds the office on the date, from the record (K1484, C2 row 5). An action asserting a breach is addressed to an office. new A reporter's name is kept inside the group as the role's holder and never published by the system; partner groups on a joint action are organisations by role (Actions D12, D16).
  7. Nothing leaves by a system path. The instance transmits nothing. A member sends by the venue's own means and records the sending with the bytes sent; anything addressed carries the in-band stamp (Publication §3 rule 9). A plan is never published (DEC-25); a counsel packet is never published and never fileable as it stands. new A published case's record of actions holds the group's outward acts only: never the plan's deliberation, never a confidential act, never a counsel matter (Actions D4, D22).
  8. No catalogue, no budgets. Suggested options come from reasoning over the matter, changed from the routes the jurisdiction's sourced guides list, and from the group's own earlier plans, never from a fixed list; the plan holds no costs, assignees or hours (Bob, 2026-09-29).
  9. [changed] The doctrine's limits. Civicsmith takes no position on what policy should be (Operational Principle 1), and it does not rule on policy even if some members and some groups do (Bob, 2026-10-11, Actions D11). Its outputs, published cases (which hold evidence, not narrative), suggested options, templates, guides and the assistant's drafts, never take or propose a policy or vote position. Political accountability asks officials to act on a breach, requests oversight and audits, testifies to facts and asks a body to examine or apply an existing requirement, and supports legislation that restores or enforces an existing requirement; lobbying is an option only for that. Policy advocacy and candidate support are not actions: Civicsmith must not be weaponized by members or groups with a policy agenda, so its only involvement in material that supports a policy position is assessing whether the positions taken are supported by the evidence in the record, and pointing out where they are not; it holds, prepares, sends and certifies nothing of it. When a message names a pending measure or candidate, the campaign-spending rule is shown once as legal information. A member may speak at a meeting for the group or as herself. Any group may use Civicsmith; a group with a stake in a matter discloses it (D6).
  10. The work varies, not the person. A project may declare the kind of work it does (reporting, fixing, legal, oversight, other), which shapes what the assistant suggests and nothing else. No attribute of a member or user gates, filters or orders anything (DEC-17, DEC-54); the people the record holds may be searched and sorted by any held fact and ranked by a measured quantity (K1484, K1471), and a judgment score appears only as a signal in the hypothesis layer (K1473).
  11. Jurisdiction lives in data. Kinds, venues, templates, offices, legal organisations, deadlines and holidays come from a jurisdiction profile; a missing fact reads undetermined, never a default (build/layers.md, "No jurisdiction in the product"). new Each profile carries an approved set of starting templates, offered only after a member has chosen the act, and a sourced procedure guide for each kind of action, labelled legal information, not legal advice; legal facts are shown with citations at the moment they matter. A member may draft a jurisdiction rule from its primary source, confirmed by a second member (a professional for Tier 2 and 3 matters); it applies in that group at once and may be offered to the shared profile. A state is supported when a group there asks (Actions D8).
  12. Hope for good faith; prepare for opposition (Bob, 2026-09-30; Design Requirements 13 and 14). People are presumed to want better outcomes, and a bad actor is identified by evidence, never by role. The system is nonetheless fully prepared for responses that amount to war: stonewalling, retaliation, discrediting, legal harassment. So every plan is checked for a branch that answers a hostile response; pressure against the group or its supporters is recorded as evidence (Operational Principle 8), changed wherever it arrives, and can open an inquiry of its own; what counsel or another group needs to carry a matter on (the counsel packet, the published case, the evidence package) survives the group's disruption. new Any member who can see an action or case may place a litigation hold with a reason; a plain notice tells members what to keep outside Civicsmith, and no one's reading or compliance is recorded (Actions D10).
  13. The venue sets the standard of evidence (Bob, 2026-09-30). Courts and other venues hold different standards: the project's research found that federal courts have accepted co-attested Grade B evidence since the 2017 amendments to Federal Rule of Evidence 902(13)–(14) (docs/development/GRADE-A-CAPTURE.md), and other venues accept other grades, though a lower grade may be contested by the opposition. No action is refused for its evidence grade. Where a filing rests on a grade the opposition could contest, it says so, so counsel and members can prepare (rule 12). Every filing and counsel packet shows each exhibit's grade, and where the profile states a venue's standard, shows it beside them. DEC-81's "Grade A stays the ceiling for adversarial or legal use" is read as the highest grade the product offers, not a minimum.
  14. [new] What the group sends is checked against its record (Actions D2). Every filing and communication gets the case's sentence-by-sentence account check: a sentence the record does not support is pointed out; one the record contradicts is held back by default and sent only by a member's open act with a reason; a question is never flagged. Claims about a body rest on findings (rule 2's three kinds) or the group's own dated correspondence.
  15. [new] What stays inside. Three scopes, each set by a member's act with a reason (Actions D4): a confidential act, seen inside the group and never in anything published; a counsel matter, a project of its own whose members alone see counsel's correspondence and dates, never published or used as evidence, its packets marked privileged; and a member's own role as a source, kept in her hidden project. Promises the group made to people are notes on the action every participant sees. A reply to a request for comment may be quoted unless the reply says otherwise, a rule stated in the request; at signing, each open request for comment is shown as a fact, never a gate (Actions D13).
  16. [new] Responses change the plan only through members. A response is recorded wherever it is found, in the office's own terms. Each recorded response sets off a re-examination of every action taken or planned in the project and its linked projects: dates recomputed, phases whose start now holds opened, options whose premise or date changed marked with why, the profile's next routes offered, and, where the account owner has enabled it, the assistant's labelled proposals; what the group chose changes only by a member's act (Actions D21). A response that may itself be an infraction becomes a new matter, suspected until a member determines it, kept in the same project or a linked one as the member chooses, the link kept both ways; the body's response is evidence, the group's own act never is (Actions D20).
  17. [new] Endings are honest (Actions D5, D6). Compliance is restored by a curing act: a later act of the same office, determined compliant against the same standard and linked by a member as the cure. Harm is addressed by a member's reasoned statement, with evidence for each part. A dated outcome entry goes on the published case at once, the determination with its next edition. An escalation may be stopped with a reason, never shown as success, and reopened. A closed plan keeps its reason, a closing summary assembled from the record and an optional note, never graded and never published. When a plan closes, members are asked once, for each watch it set running, whether to keep it until a date or stop it. When a member leaves, the owners are told once which dated items were hers, and any member may take one up by one act; a "where it stands" view shows each plan's state (Actions D14).

4A. Records requests new

The records-request rules Bob ruled in Case Making (D-147, D-148, D-149), whose action sections are history, have their home here: a request and its reply are one round trip, with the reply's outcome from a fixed vocabulary; its due date cites a governing law; a fee quote is held as evidence; every law that governs the body and the request is named; reconsideration is a stage on the request, while mediation, an ethics complaint and a court petition are new actions linked to it, each with its own addressee, tier and clock.
