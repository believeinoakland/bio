1. What the layer is for

The Action layer is where a group turns what it has learned into what it does: from the first suspicion that something is wrong or working, through planning, preparing, checking, deciding and sending, to tracking the response and recording how it ended.

Groups act differently on the same record: a reporter reports, an activist works to fix, a lawyer supports or changes a claim, an oversight body refers, an administrator responds. A group may take many actions of different kinds on one matter, or none, and may act on a success as well as on a breach. Actions take many shapes: asking a body for records or for comment; enforcing the group's own requests; complaining to an oversight office; speaking or testifying at a meeting; taking part as a formal party; working with the press; meeting officials; recognising success; acting jointly with other groups; briefing counsel; defending the group. A plan may be developed and acted on before any case is published, so a published case may carry a record of the actions the group has taken.

Civicsmith supports members at every stage, both members who work with the assistant and members who don't: everything in this document works without AI, and the assistant, where the account owner has enabled it, adds labelled proposals.

2. The contract

An action rests on the record. One asserting a breach rests on a member's determination, against a standard held in the record, made on findings that are published, checked for publication and held, or another group's findings the group has accepted. The group plans and decides every act; the AI proposes and prepares, and never files or sends. Compliance is recorded as carefully as noncompliance. Every deadline names its basis. Civicsmith holds evidence and takes no position on policy; members and groups may, and Civicsmith is never their instrument.

3. The constructs

Action plan a project's working material for deciding what to do about one or more matters, suspected or determined: every option proposed and the group's own, their disposition, and any number of scenarios laid out in phases with checkpoints a member judges; a scenario no longer in use is set aside with a reason and kept action-plans

Filing and communication what the group sends, prepared from the record: Tier 1–2 filings from the profile's templates, the Tier 3 counsel packet, and communication drafts; each checked against the record, approved by a member and sent by the member's own hand, carrying the in-band stamp filings
Escalation the seven-stage protocol that pursues one noncompliant determination until compliance is restored and its consequences are addressed, or until a member stops pursuing it with a reason; a met trigger is proposed with its age, and a member advances or declines with a reason escalation

How they connect: an inquiry opens a plan, its matters suspected; a determination, on published, checked-and-held or accepted findings, makes a matter determined; consequences measure a breach and a success record measures a success; a chosen option starts an action; a filing or communication prepares what the action sends; a breach action joins its determination's escalation; responses return as correspondence and set off a re-examination of the plan; an action resolves with its own outcome; the plan closes by a member's act with its closing summary. Monitoring (layer 10) watches the clocks, duties and triggers and tells members; the queue carries the to-dos.

4. The rules

  1. Humans decide. A member takes every act that commits the group: declaring a standard, determining, assessing a consequence or a success, choosing an option, approving, sending, advancing or stopping a stage, resolving, closing. The machine may find, compare, compute, propose and draft, always labelled as machine work, and never does any of these acts.

  2. The gate is at the outward act, not the reasoning, and a member may pass it openly on stated grounds. A plan may rest on premises not yet established, shown as hunch debt. Outward acts are classed by what their text does: asking an office to examine something, seeking evidence (a records request, a request for comment), and enforcing the group's own request on its own dated record are never held back. A sentence that asserts a breach as the group's finding needs a live noncompliant determination. Sentences that read as assertions are pointed out before approval, from a fixed list of phrasings for every member and by the assistant where enabled, and the member resolves each one: she rewrites it, confirms in her own recorded words that the act only asks, or treats the act as a breach claim. Without a determination, an act asserting a breach may proceed only on one of two grounds, a legal deadline that falls before a determination could be made (cited, with its date) or an imminent irreversible act (what, and when), together with the member's reason; ground and reason are disclosed on the action and on everything prepared from it, and an override without a ground is refused. Where the law's window is short, the window is shown beside the refusal, with a quick route to a small case checked for publication. An action sent on these grounds may join its escalation once a determination is made, the override kept in its history. No tool has the final word (Requirement 12), and a departure from the group's standard is visible to every reader.

  3. What the group sends is checked against its record. Every filing and communication gets the case's sentence-by-sentence account check: a sentence the record does not support is pointed out; one the record contradicts is held back by default and sent only by a member's open act with a reason; a question is never flagged. Claims about a body rest on findings of the three kinds in §2 or on the group's own dated correspondence; statements about what the group itself is doing need neither.

  4. No significance, no score. Whether a matter warrants action, and how urgently, is a member's judgment, recorded only in acts and their reasons. No field holds significance, severity, priority or a score. The planning run lists every option it proposes, with no cut-off, collapsed to its summary; an option left unticked is undecided, and declining one is an act with a reason. No ending is rated. Suggested options come from reasoning over the matter, the routes the profile's guides list and the group's own earlier plans, never from a fixed catalogue; the plan holds no costs, assignees or hours.

  5. Compliance counts. A compliant determination is recorded with the same care as a noncompliant one and may start a plan. Recognition is an action, resting on a compliant determination or a success record; its text may name staff acting in their official roles, with a reminder that public thanks names them publicly.

  6. Every deadline names its basis, and members are told without being nagged. A due date's basis is a rule (cited), the body's own commitment, a dependency, or the group's own window, computed in the office's local day. A reminder is the member's own request; an overdue date notifies once; a nearing date changes display only; no outside channel; nothing repeats unless the member asks. One date gives one reminder: an investigation step's "by when" is the action's clock entry. A missed date is shown as a question ("Noticed: no reply recorded by 25 Oct") until a member determines noncompliance on the record. Receipt is recorded as a fact where the law counts from it. An action to a body that owes the group no reply never records silence as non-response. What the group itself owes a venue (service, filings, contact reports, a requester's own deadlines) is held as the group's own deadlines, distinct from a body's duties. The other side's stated deadline is never presented as a legal one.

  7. Addressees are roles, not people. An action is addressed to a government office by role and body, a reporter by role and outlet, an organisation or another civic group by role, or a described audience; never a private individual; the page may show who holds the office on the date, from the record. An action asserting a breach is addressed to an office. One action is made per office addressed, others copied on it; a venue's delivery point is held on the venue, never as the addressee. A reporter's name is kept inside the group as the role's holder and never published by the system. Partners on a joint action are organisations by role.

  8. Who speaks. At a meeting a member speaks for the group, approved, stamped and held to every rule here, or as herself, in her own notes and not as the group's act. Press terms (on the record, embargo, release time) are noted on each press action. A request for comment states that the reply may be quoted unless it says otherwise; at signing, each request for comment still open is shown as one line of fact, never a gate.

  9. The doctrine's limits. Civicsmith takes no position on what policy should be, and does not rule on policy even when members or groups do. Its outputs (published cases, which hold evidence, not narrative; suggested options; templates; guides; the assistant's drafts) never take or propose a policy or vote position, and the planning run never suggests advocacy. Political accountability asks officials to act on a breach, requests oversight and audits, testifies to facts and asks a body to examine or apply an existing requirement, and supports legislation that restores or enforces an existing requirement; lobbying is an option only for that. Policy advocacy and candidate support are not actions. Civicsmith must not be weaponized by members or groups with a policy agenda: its only involvement in material that supports a policy position is assessing whether the positions taken are supported by the evidence in the record, and pointing out where they are not. Each claim and position is shown as supported, not supported or contradicted; nothing is held back, prepared, sent, recorded as the group's act or certified, and the check carries no mark outside. When a message names a pending measure or candidate, the campaign-spending rule is shown once as legal information. Any group may use Civicsmith; a group with a stake in a matter discloses it.

  10. The work varies, not the person. A project may declare the kind of work it does (reporting, fixing, legal, oversight, other), which shapes what the assistant suggests and nothing else. No attribute of a member or user gates, filters or orders anything; no member is named spokesperson; no one's reading of a notice is tracked.

  11. Jurisdiction lives in data. Kinds, venues, templates, offices, legal organisations, deadlines, holidays and the stages each law has come from a jurisdiction profile; a missing fact reads undetermined, never a default. Each profile carries an approved set of starting templates, written by a named person from public sources, reviewed by a professional, approved and attributed, and offered only after a member has chosen the act; and a sourced procedure guide for each kind of action, labelled legal information, not legal advice, listing the replies to expect and the routes in the profile's order, unranked. Legal facts are shown with their citations at the moment they matter. A member may draft a jurisdiction rule from its primary source, confirmed by a second member (a professional for Tier 2 and 3 matters); it applies in that group at once and may be offered to the shared profile. A state is supported when a group there asks.

  12. Hope for good faith; prepare for opposition. People are presumed to want better outcomes, and a bad actor is identified by evidence, never by role. The system is nonetheless prepared for stonewalling, retaliation, discrediting and legal harassment: every plan is checked for a branch that answers a hostile response; pressure against the group or its supporters is recorded as evidence wherever it arrives, and can open an inquiry of its own; lawful demands are complied with openly. Any member who can see an action or case may place a litigation hold with a reason; nothing named in it is purged, and a plain notice tells members what to keep outside Civicsmith. Counsel may be briefed at any stage, for a claim or for the group's defence, without a determination: the packet shows each matter's support as it stands, and a defence packet holds each challenged statement with its findings and sources, the pressure received and the defence deadlines; it never says "you have a claim". What counsel or another group needs to carry a matter on survives the group's disruption.

  13. The venue sets the standard of evidence. No action is refused for its evidence grade. Every filing and counsel packet shows each exhibit's grade and, where the profile states it, the venue's standard beside it; where a filing rests on a grade the opposition could contest, it says so. Grade A is the highest grade the product offers, not a minimum. Taking part as a formal party before a venue is a Tier 2 action, with its advisory and the counsel packet offered.

  14. Nothing leaves by a system path. The instance transmits nothing. A member sends by the venue's own means and records the sending with the bytes sent; anything addressed carries the in-band stamp. A joint text is approved in each group's own copy, its fingerprint matching, each signatory added by its own organisation's act. A plan is never published; a counsel packet is never published and never fileable as it stands.

  15. What stays inside. Three scopes, each set by a member's act with a reason: a confidential act, seen inside the group and never in anything published; a counsel matter, a project of its own whose members alone see counsel's correspondence and dates, never published or used as evidence, its packets marked privileged; and a member's own role as a source, kept in her hidden project, the group's act resting on the documents she chooses to share. Promises the group made to people (an embargo, a source's anonymity) are notes on the action that every participant sees. A published case's record of actions holds the group's outward acts only: never the plan's deliberation, never a confidential act, never a counsel matter.

  16. Responses change the plan only through members. A response is recorded wherever it is found (a direct reply, a press quote, a report, a decision), in the office's own terms; a reply to a request for comment is laid out claim by claim. Each recorded response sets off a re-examination of every action taken or planned in the project and its linked projects: dates recomputed, phases whose start now holds opened with their options offered, options whose premise or date changed marked with why, the profile's next routes for that reply offered, and, where enabled, the assistant's labelled proposals. What the group chose changes only by a member's act. A response that may itself be an infraction becomes a new matter, suspected until a member determines it, kept in the same project or a linked one as the member chooses, the link kept both ways: the body's response is evidence, the group's own act never is; a slow or unwelcome reply is not an infraction; the case changes only through evidence; the plan gains the matter; the chain is shown, never counted. A body's promise in a response, or a report's required responses, may be adopted as duties with their dates.

  17. Endings are honest. Compliance is restored by a curing act: a later act of the same office, determined compliant against the same standard and linked by a member as the cure. Harm is addressed by a member's reasoned statement with evidence for each part, with plain words offered (remedied, mitigated, compensated, acknowledged, cannot be undone) and no percentage. A dated outcome entry goes on the published case at once, and the determination with its next edition. A member may stop pursuing an escalation with a reason: it is never shown as success or as ended, proposes no next stage, and may be reopened where it stopped. A closed plan keeps its reason, a closing summary assembled from the record (never machine prose, never published, never rated) and an optional note. At close, and when every promise in a matter is met, members are asked once, for each watch the plan set running, whether to keep it until a date or stop it; every watch shows its end date.

  18. Work carries on when people change. A "where it stands" view shows each plan's waits with their basis, next dates, holds, overrides, declined options and set-aside scenarios with their reasons, escalations and watches. Members may draft status reports from the record, never required, never published. When a member leaves, the owners are told once which dated items were hers, and any member who can see an item may take it up by one act; nobody assigns anybody.

5. Records requests

A records request and its reply are one round trip, the reply's outcome taken from a fixed vocabulary. Its due date cites a governing law, and every law that governs the body and the request is named. A fee quote is held as evidence. Reconsideration is a stage on the request; mediation, an ethics complaint and a court petition are new actions linked to it, each with its own addressee, tier and clock. Which stages a law has comes from the profile.

6. Things kept distinct

  • An action's resolution (complied, denied, escalated, withdrawn, completed), a correspondence entry's outcome (in the office's own terms, from the profile), and an escalation's evaluation of a response (complied, partial, denied, none) are three different things, each kept.
  • The plan is the strategic layer above; each determined breach keeps its own escalation; the plan never moves an escalation.
  • The product's own kinds are those whose rules it enforces (records request, request for comment, other); every other kind comes from the profile. A kind's risk tier comes from the profile and an action's is set by a member; a filing is governed by the stricter, and an undetermined tier is never read as Tier 1.
  • An action's addressee and the person who owes a duty are different: an action about a person's statutory duty is addressed to the office that enforces it.

7. Not yet

Certification of an output by a licensed professional, and with it court-petition templates; compensation claims for formal parties; and the question "if this finding holds, what could we do, and what else would have to be true?" each wait until a group needs them.
