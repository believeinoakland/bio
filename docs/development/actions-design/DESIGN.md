## 1. What the design does

Bob's direction (investigation lane D43; this lane's start, 2026-10-09): support members "throughout the action plan to the greatest and most appropriate extents possible", for members who use the assistant and members who don't (D42). The research found the action machinery built and green but out of reach, and serving one shape: a group acting against an office over a breach. The design therefore does three things:

1. **Widens what an action is** to the shapes real groups use (§3): asking, enforcing the group's own requests, complaining to oversight, speaking at meetings, taking part as a party, working with the press, meeting officials, recognising success, coalitions, briefing and defending with counsel.
2. **Follows each action through its whole life** (§2): plan, prepare, check, decide and send, track, answer the response, escalate, end, hand over.
3. **Fixes the line between Civicsmith and the people using it** (§4): Civicsmith holds evidence and never takes a policy position (D11); members decide every act (Action rule 1); what stays inside stays inside (D4).

## 2. The life of an action

### 2.1 Opening and planning

- **A plan opens at the first suspicion** (K590 (10); D22) on a question, never on a bare lead (S1 §C 20). Its matters are *suspected* until determined. The plan shows its **hunch debt**: which options rest on premises not yet established (Action rule 2).
- **All options are listed** (D17): the planning run proposes every option its reasoning finds, with no cut-off and no paging by five (replaces K660 (2); `action-plans` R34 tray and its `after` cursor; `skills` R28). Options stay collapsed to their summary; no score is shown (rule 3); unticked is undecided; declining is an act with a reason (K590 (7)). Order and filters: options are grouped by matter, then by phase of the scenario they serve; filters by kind, by "rests on" (evidence-seeking / determined / hunch) and by state (lane's detail).
- **No catalogue** (rule 8) still holds: options come from reasoning over the matter, the group's earlier plans and **the profile's routes for that kind of situation** (D8 guides), never a fixed list. The planning run never suggests an advocacy option (D11b; `action-plans` R12 `lobbying` only to enforce or restore an existing requirement).
- **Scenarios: no limit** (D19; revises K590 ruling 8). A scenario no longer in use is **set aside** by an act with a reason, kept and restorable; the screen compares any two or three side by side. Branches inside a scenario (checkpoints judged by a member; phases started by a body's deadline met or missed, K1466 read as "the phase opens and offers its options") carry most contingencies.
- **Acting before publication** (D22) and **checked and held** (D23): a project may run its case through every publication check (objective's condition at the bar, sentence-by-sentence account check, the five screens) and hold it, unsigned, with no time limit, to act first. Any change to the evidence the check covered voids it until re-run. The state is shown to members as a state, never a fault.

### 2.2 Preparing

- **Prepared from the record.** A filing or communication drafts from the findings and inquiries the member chooses, not only from a published case (S1 §C 6); a request for comment assembles its claims from the chosen findings as a record summary (like escalation R29's pre-assembled reason, a sourced list labelled machine work, K1841).
- **Starting texts and guides** (D8): an approved Tier 1 template set per profile (records request; records request with immediate-disclosure request; follow-up on a passed date; reconsideration; ethics-commission mediation; written public comment; grand jury complaint; Brown Act cure-and-correct demand, at Tier 2 with its advisory, S1 §C 3), each written by a named person from public sources, reviewed by a professional, approved and attributed (K903 (6), K921, `filing-templates` R8–R10). **A template is offered only after the member has chosen the act.** A sourced procedure guide per kind per profile, labelled "legal information, not legal advice", lists expected replies and routes in profile order, unranked; members read it and the assistant reads it as its reading list. Profile facts are surfaced with citations on their trigger only (paid attendee → lobbyist threshold; recording → all-party consent; threat letter → anti-SLAPP window). Whether a protection applies waits for deferred D-165.
- **The assistant** (where the account owner enabled it, investigation D34–D39) drafts proposals a member adopts; it stays with facts and requests to examine or apply existing requirements; it never drafts or proposes a policy or vote position (D11b), never says what to file, never says "you have a claim".
- **Who speaks** (D12): public comment is chosen at preparation "for the group" (approved, stamped per `filings` R22, held to every rule here) or "as myself" (kept in her own notes, no group stamp, not the group's act). Several speakers on one item are one action with several sendings, each with its member link (`contact`) visible only inside the group (S1 §C 5).
- **Addressing** (rule 6): an office by role and body from the registry, a reporter by role and outlet (the journalist's name kept inside as the role's holder, never published by the system, D12), an organisation or group by role, or a described audience. Several offices: one action per office addressed; "copied" is a role on the same action (S1 §C 4). The venue's **delivery point** ("Clerk of the Council") is held on the kind's venue, never as the addressee (S1 §C 9). **Partners** on a joint action are organisations by role (D16).

### 2.3 Checking before anything leaves

Three checks, each pointing out and leaving the decision to the member (Requirement 12):

1. **Asking or asserting** (D1): the text is classed by what it does. Asking an office to examine, and enforcing the group's own request on its own dated record, are never held back. A sentence asserting a breach as the group's finding needs a determination (2.4) or the override (2.4). Sentences that read as assertions are pointed out from a fixed list of phrasings (all members) and by AI where enabled; each is resolved by the member before approval (rewrite; confirm in her own recorded words that it only asks; or treat the act as a breach claim). Her confirmation is recorded.
2. **The record check** (D2): everything the group sends gets the case-account check (Investigation §11, D56). Unsupported sentences are pointed out; a sentence the record **contradicts** is held by default and sendable only by an open act with a reason (this override keeps any reason; it is not D18's). Questions are never flagged. Claims about a body rest on the published, checked-and-held (D24) or accepted (D16) findings, or the group's own dated correspondence; statements about the group's own doings need neither.
3. **The election notice** (D11): when the text names a pending measure or candidate, the campaign-spending rule is shown once with its citation, as legal information. Never a block.

**Advocacy material is not an action** (D11a; rule 9 stands). A member may bring text she wrote that takes a policy position; Civicsmith's only part is the **evidence check**: each factual claim and each position sentence marked *supported* (findings cited), *not supported*, or *contradicted*. It holds nothing and certifies nothing; no mark travels with the text; the text and its check are kept in the member's own notes (lane's detail), never in the group's action record, never prepared, templated, suggested, stamped or sent through Civicsmith, never in a published record of actions.

### 2.4 Deciding and the breach gate

- **What a breach claim may rest on.** A live noncompliant determination, made by a member against a standard in the group's record, on findings that are (a) published, (b) **checked and held** (D24; "rests on findings checked for publication on <date>, not yet published"; voided by any change to the evidence until re-run; re-pinned to the edition on publication), or (c) **another group's findings the group has accepted** (D16, D16a; DEC-96; a member of the claiming group determines; the action names "findings of <group> (edition of <date>), accepted by us on <date>"; if the source withdraws or revises, the determination reads undetermined until a member re-evaluates, and actions resting on it are marked).
- **The override, narrowed** (D18): without a determination, a breach-asserting act may proceed only on a stated ground (a **legal deadline** shorter than the time to determine, cited with its rule and date; or an **imminent irreversible act**, what and when) plus the member's reason; both are disclosed on everything prepared. A groundless override is refused, naming the two grounds.
- **When the window is short** (D3): the window is computed and shown beside the refusal ("Due 5 Nov (30 days, Gov. Code §54960.1)"), and a quick route offers a one-finding case run through the publication checks (now "check the case", D24). An action sent under override may **join the escalation once a determination is made**, the override kept in its history (revises K600 (a)).
- **Counsel at any stage** (D9): a counsel packet needs no determination and no override (the gate sits at the outward act; the packet goes only to the group's own counsel by a member's hand). It shows each matter's support as it stands; a **defence purpose** holds each challenged statement with its finding and sources, the pressure received and the defence deadlines cited.
- **Party participation** (D15) is a Tier 2 kind with its advisory and the counsel packet offered.

### 2.5 Sending and recording

Unchanged doctrine (rule 7): the instance transmits nothing; a member sends by the venue's own means and records the sending with the bytes sent, carrying the in-band stamp. Added:
- **Receipt as a recorded fact** (how, when), the clock's start for any kind whose rule counts from receipt (S1 §C 9).
- **Requests for comment state the quoting rule** (D13): a reply is on the record unless it says otherwise.
- **Press terms** (D12): a terms note on each press action ("on the record; embargo 22 Oct 06:00"); a review copy carries the embargo and a release time linked to a scheduled edition (S1 §C 21).
- **Joint texts** (D16): one text approved in each group's own copy, with a matching body-only fingerprint (S1 §C 22), each signatory added by its own organisation's act; built with the first action screens; partners recorded now.

### 2.6 Tracking

- **Every deadline names its basis** (rule 5; K1431: rule, commitment, dependency, the group's window), computed by `civil-time` in the office's local day (K1657), including time-of-day and from-service counts (S1 §C 19). **One date, one reminder** (S1 §C 1): an investigation step's "by when" is the action's clock entry; when the office's duty is adopted its "Noticed" item replaces the clock's overdue item.
- **A missed date is a question**, "Noticed: no reply recorded by 25 Oct", until a member determines noncompliance on the record (Ladders §10; D20).
- **"No reply is owed"** (S1 §C 8): actions to a body that owes nothing (a grand jury) never record silence as non-response.
- **"Our deadlines"** (D15): what the group owes a venue (service, filings, ex parte reports, a requester's own deadlines) are dated items owed by the group, distinct from a body's duties (`duties` R23); reminders only as asked; a missed own-deadline notifies once. The other side's stated deadline ("retract within 10 days") is its own basis kind, never a legal deadline (S1 §C 15).
- **Spoken promises** (D7): kept "as heard" in members' own accounts until the body confirms in writing or states publicly; then a member may adopt it as a commitment and a watched duty. The group's confirming letter's unanswered date is recorded as a dated fact.
- **Duties proposed from responses**: a grand jury report's "responses required" and a reply's promises are proposed as duties with their dates and quotes (COURTS L3); "tell me if they miss this date" standing questions (QUESTIONS L5).

### 2.7 When a response comes

- **Recorded where it arrives**: a direct reply (`responds_to`), or **found elsewhere** (a press quote, a report, a decision) linked by a member to the action it answers (S1 §C 10). Outcomes are recorded **in the office's own terms** from the profile's `response_statuses` (S1 §C 11). A reply to a request for comment is laid out **claim by claim**: new evidence / disputes without evidence / not addressed (S1 §C 7).
- **Re-examination** (D21): on every recorded response, a deterministic pass for all members re-examines every action taken or planned in the project and linked projects: clocks and windows recomputed, phases whose start now holds opened, options whose premise or date changed marked with why, the profile's next routes for that kind of reply offered (e.g. reconsideration, mediation, petition after a denial; S1 §C 2: reconsideration a stage on the request, the rest new linked actions). Where enabled, the assistant reads the reply against the plan and proposes, labelled. The options list is updated; what the group chose changes only by a member's act.
- **A response that is itself an infraction** (D20): a new matter, suspected until a member determines it against its rule; kept in the same project or opened as a linked project (member's choice each time, linked both ways, "arose from"). Principles: the body's response is evidence, the group's own act never is; good faith first; the case changes only through evidence (a relevant reply goes to the published case's docket at once, `docket` R1; findings change in the next edition only if the evidence does); the plan gains the matter, with its own options and escalation; the chain is shown, never counted.
- **Pressure on the group** (rule 12; S1 §C 16): recorded wherever it arrives, readable across actions, docket and testimony, without inventing an action to the sender; a `legal` mark anywhere raises the hold to-do. Lawful demands are complied with openly (a to-do, its clock and citation, a redacting new edition, a docket entry; S1 §C 17).
- **The hold** (D10): any member who can see the action or case places one with a reason (also from a pressure mark or a counsel matter); a plain-words **hold notice** is prepared and sent by a member's hand (email, texts, chats with disappearing messages off, notes, photos); no per-member read or compliance record. Nothing named is purged (DEC-113, K1252).

### 2.8 Escalation

Seven stages, a met trigger proposed with its age, a member advances or declines with a reason (unchanged). Added:
- **Stage names as questions** for the UX pass (S1 §C 14): "Is it documented? Did we tell them? How long do we give them? What did they say? What can we file? Are we still watching? Who answers for it?"
- **Evidence-seeking acts shown in its history** as context, not as stage acts (S1 §C 13).
- **Ending** (D5): compliance restored by **a curing act** (a later act of the same office, determined compliant against the same standard and linked by a member as the cure; fixes `escalation` R14's "same act"); harm addressed by **a member's reasoned statement** with evidence per part and optional words (remedied, mitigated, compensated, acknowledged, cannot be undone), no percentage; a **dated outcome entry** on the published case at once, the determination with the next edition.
- **Stopped pursuing** (D6a): a state with a reason, never success, never "ended"; no next stage proposed; reopenable with a reason at the stage where it stopped; the published record of actions shows "stopped pursuing, <date>", the reason internal unless written into the case.

### 2.9 Ending a plan, and success

- **Closing summary** (D6b): at close, the reason, a summary assembled from the record (a frozen copy of "where it stands": matters and determinations, cures, consequences addressed, each action's resolution, escalation states, watched promises, declined options and set-aside scenarios with reasons, what is still running) and an optional member note. Never machine prose, never published, no rating.
- **Success record** (D6c): per matter, the member's statement of what changed and when, on evidence, figures computed from cited sources; sequence may be stated; causation only on evidence other than the group's own act; carried into the next edition as an outcome. Consequences stay breach-only. **Recognition** actions rest on a compliant determination or a success record and warn when the fix is only a promise (U12); thanks may name staff in their official roles with a one-time reminder (D12).
- **Watches** (D6d): at close, and when every promise in a matter is met, each watch the plan set up (following a body, watching a page, a promise watched as a duty, a standing question) is listed once: keep until a member-chosen date, or stop now. Every watch then shows its end date. Watches another plan or project relies on are shown, not offered for stopping.

### 2.10 Handing over

- **Where it stands** (D14): a computed, read-only view per plan (waits with basis, next dates, holds, overrides with reasons, declined options, set-aside scenarios, escalations, watches with end dates); the view D6b freezes.
- **Status reports**: drafted from the record by a member (assistant where enabled, labelled), never required, never published.
- **When a member leaves**: the owners are told once which dated items routed to her; any member who can see an item may **take it up** by one act (where its to-dos go); no assignee, no automatic reassignment (closes `queue-producers` R15–R18's "else").

## 4. What stays inside, and who is who

- **Civicsmith and the people using it** (D11): Civicsmith's outputs (published cases, which hold evidence only; suggested options; templates; guides; assistant drafts) never take or propose a policy or vote position. Members and groups may; Civicsmith does not police their views and is never their instrument ("not weaponized"): its only part in advocacy is the evidence check.
- **Three scopes** (D4), each set by an act with a reason: a **confidential act** never appears in any published product or timeline (`actions` R66 excludes it from the "what we did" lane) but is seen inside the group; a **counsel matter** is its own project, members join it, its correspondence and dates are seen only by its members, never published or used as evidence, packets carry a privilege marking with a warning on sharing outside; a **member's own source role** stays in her hidden project, the group's complaint resting on the documents she chooses to share. **Promises to people** (an embargo, a source's anonymity) are notes on the action every participant sees.
- **The published record of actions** (D22): outward acts only, which their recipients have seen; never the plan's deliberation (DEC-25), never confidential acts or counsel matters; escalations shown with their state, including "stopped pursuing".
- **No member attribute decides anything** (rule 10): taking up an item is a member choosing where her own to-dos go; no named spokesperson; no read-receipts on hold notices.

## 5. Support for both kinds of member

Every row of §2 works without AI (D42): the assertion list, the record check, computed dates, templates, guides, facts on trigger, the re-examination's deterministic pass, "where it stands", the closing summary. AI where enabled adds proposals, always labelled: options, drafts, reading replies against the plan, claim-by-claim bearings, status-report drafts. It never drafts a position (D11b), never decides, never sends.

## 7. A capabilities ladder for actions (proposed ACTIONS ladder)
