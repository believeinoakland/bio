## M-175 · 2026-09-25 · D-606 — THE PER-PAGE OCR LOOP: WHAT BOUNDS IT, WHAT IT COSTS THE PLANE, AND ITS CONTROL. **The bound that bites is not the plane's subrequest ceiling (10,000) or its CPU. It is Cloudflare's stated limit of 32 Worker invocations per request. That is THEIR claim: miniflare does not enforce it, so it cannot be measured here. The loop is therefore capped at 24 member calls per request. A 58-page scan reads 24 pages in one acquire and leaves 34 unread, stated. Reading that tail on a later request is D-616.**

**A 58-page scan in one acquire, at the budget:**
- **Subrequests.** 24 OCR binding calls, plus one calibration read from the store, plus at most one `PDF_WORKER` call. That is about 26 of 10,000.
- **Plane-side cost of the loop.** Measured with the stub, 3 runs per arm, after warm-up. The whole 58-page acquire took 41 / 36 / 37 ms of wall time with the loop (24 calls) and 31 / 32 / 30 ms without it (1 call). The loop's 23 extra calls plus the merge therefore add about 6 ms on this runtime, and that figure includes the stub's own time. This is an upper bound on the plane's added CPU, which is negligible against 30 s.
- **Wall time.** At least 24 × 10.4 s of member CPU, so ≥ 250 s for one acquire on the deployed runtime. That is a floor taken from CPU alone; per-page wall time on the deployed member is not measured. M-165 recorded 858 s of miniflare wall time for 183 pages (≈ 4.7 s per page) over the 21 documents.
- **Pages.** 24 transcribed, 34 left unread. Each unread page keeps its marker, the basis says so, and `tier3_candidate` stays true.

**Why 24.** 32 (their claim) minus a reserve of 8, counted from the code for the other invocations one acquire makes or sits behind: the request itself; the store's `SELF` hop when a monitor drives the acquire (`store.mjs` `SELF.fetch(…op=acquire…)`); one `PDF_WORKER` tier-2 call; the render arm's `BROWSER`; and margin. The margin covers what the count cannot see: whether the originating alarm or DO invocation counts toward the 32 is UNDETERMINED. **If the claim is wrong in the tight direction, the loop degrades, not the acquire.** A call that throws ends the loop; the pages from that page on stay unread and are stated; the acquire succeeds. The suite's throw arm drives this. The limit itself belongs on the deployed runtime and is DIST's to measure. The measurement is one Worker making 33+ binding calls from one request, which no lane but DIST may deploy.
