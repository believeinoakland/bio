## M-145 · 2026-09-24 · D-517 — THE TJ DISPLACEMENT DISTRIBUTION: THE SECOND WORD-GAP RULE'S OWN POPULATION, ITS VALLEY, AND WHAT UNIFYING THE TWO CONSTANTS WOULD COST

**Headline.** Tier 1 has **two** word-gap rules and they disagreed by 2.5x: a jump a positioning operator makes
between two runs is judged at **0.25 em** (D-502, measured, M-141), and a displacement the producer writes INSIDE one
shown run — a number in a `TJ` array — was judged at a **hand-picked -100 thousandths, 0.1 em** (D-481). Measuring the
second population says the hand-picked figure **was right**: over 14,067 TJ numeric elements in M-141's own nine
documents, the elements where a space is a *claim about a word boundary* — the 3,904 that follow a letter or digit —
sit **3,818 at or below 0.020 em** and **86 at or above 0.180 em**, with **NOTHING between**. The valley is
`(0.020, 0.180]` em and **0.100 is its midpoint**. So the constant is CONFIRMED rather than replaced; what it lacked
was the measurement, and what changes is that it now carries one. **Unifying the two constants at 0.25 em is refused
BY MEASUREMENT**: 75 of those 86 real word gaps lie in `(0.180, 0.25]`, and at 0.25 em the corpus loses **75 words and
gains 9 glue tokens** on `legistar-73545` alone. What IS unified is the **emitter**: this rule pushed its separator
straight onto `pieces` and so alone escaped both halves of D-502's withdrawal rule, and on `softSpace` the corpus
loses **122 space characters and 12 whitespace-only lines while not one token, word, glue token or non-whitespace
character moves**.

### The distribution

14,067 numeric elements: **8,432 forward** (`v < 0`, the pen moves right — a gap), **5,635 backward**, none exactly
zero. The discriminator the run-gap population did not need is **what the element follows**. A positioning operator's
jump is always *between* runs, so any gap there is a candidate separator; a TJ number sits wherever the producer put
it, so a space there either separates two words or **doubles a separator already present**. Counting the forward

**THE VALLEY IS `(0.020, 0.180]` EM AND IT IS EMPTY, not merely thin.** The largest letter-adjacent forward element at
or below 0.100 em is **0.0200**; the smallest above it is **0.1800**; and the smallest above 0.025 em is also 0.1800.
Of the 3,904 letter-adjacent elements, 3,818 are intra-word kerning and 86 are the producers' own word gaps, with a
gap of a factor of nine between the two populations. **0.100 em is that valley's arithmetic midpoint**, so D-481's
hand-picked -100 is confirmed to three digits by a distribution it never saw.

**Backward: nothing to decide.** Of 5,635 backward elements **not one exceeds 0.075 em** (2,959 ≤ 0.005; 1,611 to
0.010; 948 to 0.025; 101 to 0.05; 16 to 0.075; zero above). So the one-sided test and the magnitude test are
**byte-identical on this corpus** — an equality that costs nothing to produce, recorded as such and **not** as evidence
that either form is right. The rule stays forward-only on a structural reading instead: a backward displacement inside
ONE shown run is the producer tightening or overprinting, never a new run, whereas a backward JUMP between runs is a
column drawn out of order, which is the case D-502 measured its magnitude form for. Two synthetic suite arms pin the
asymmetry so it is not tidied away.

### The predicate, checked rather than reasoned about

The site now reads `-it.v / 1000 > TJ_WORD_GAP_EM` instead of `it.v < -100`. The displacement in ems **is**
`-v / 1000`: the horizontal-scale factor cancels, since the device advance is `(-v/1000)·tfs·th·|m|` while one em of
that same text is `tfs·th·|m|`. That the two predicates are one was decided over **every one of the 417 distinct TJ
values the corpus holds**, plus 14 boundary cases added by hand (−100 itself and its neighbours in both directions,
±`Number.MIN_VALUE`, ±1e300): **431 values, agreement on all of them.** Not argued from IEEE-754.

### What this can and cannot see

- **Nine documents is M-141's whole corpus and four of them carry the entire TJ population.** 12,207 of the 14,067
  elements are in `legistar-73450`, `-73545`, `-73550` and `-73618`; two more documents contribute 2,992 with no text
  at all; **the three that D-502's own headline moved contribute none.** A producer that writes word gaps as TJ
  numbers *and* the footer shape D-502 fixed is not represented here, and would be the document that tests both rules
  at once. **The sweep is the guard, not the number**: it is re-runnable over any corpus, and its shape — flat across
  the valley, collapsing at zero and decaying above 0.18 em — is what a reader should check on a new one.
- **The letter-or-digit discriminator is a proxy for "a word boundary", and it is a coarse one.** It cannot tell a
  separator that belongs between two words from one between a word and a punctuation mark it should hug, and it counts
  an element following `,` or `.` in neither class (the "other" column: 170 elements, 3 of them in the contested band).
  What it does do is bound the question from both sides, which the raw band counts alone could not.
- **The glue counter cannot tell a concatenation from a compound**, unchanged from M-133 and M-141: the 17 that survive
  include `eComment`, `CalTrans`, `McGuire` — the documents' own words — and it still cannot see two lowercase runs
  glued (`ofthe`).
- **A whitespace-only line is not the same thing as a line the document did not write.** 71 of the corpus's 83
  whitespace-only lines survive with this rule OFF entirely, so they are not this rule's and this row did not look for
  whose they are.
- **Nothing here measures the run-gap population.** Unify-down's cost above is read off M-141's sweep and this row's
  own token counts, not off a fresh distribution of same-baseline jumps; that is why the option is recorded and
  declined rather than taken.
