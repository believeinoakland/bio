# HOME · The investigation's home and timeline

Unit A-HOME, phase 2 of the investigation study (`PROTOCOL.md` §A). Capability: one place every part of the system adds to and reads from over an investigation's life (questions, objectives, plan, documents and why each is held, requests, explanations, findings, contradictions, waits, runs, member notes and decisions, a chronology of the subject and a log of the investigation itself), and its relation to project, inquiry and intent.

Sources: `RESUME.md`, `notes/P0.md`, every `notes/*.md` (C1–C6, D1, D2, M1–M4) and every `research/*.md` (B1–B5), each read whole; one product line checked (`build/rulings.md` K1627). Product claims cite a note and, through it, the product (for example `notes/M1.md` §1.11 → `intent` R18). Outside-world claims cite the research note that holds the URL. *(inference)* marks my own reasoning.

**Naming.** Bob called it a "case file" (`RESUME.md`). In this product a "case" is the signed, published production of a project (DEC-72; `notes/C2.md` §1.1), and a "case file" is the published case's recreatable archive (DEC-112; `notes/D1.md` DEC-112). Bob's ruling of 2026-10-06 already names the home: "The project becomes the investigation's home, with one view and a timeline" (`build/rulings.md` K1627). This study therefore speaks of **the investigation** (a project's investigation) and its **home**, and proposes member-facing names in §8. These words are already taken and must be avoided:

- *case* and *case file* (DEC-72, DEC-112);
- *docket* (DEC-100: responses to a published case);
- *obligation* (a public body's duty, DEC-107);
- *subject* (an entity, DEC-114);
- *matter* (what an action plan addresses, DEC-114);
- *lead* (a member's tip, and a contradiction candidate; DEC-76, DEC-88);
- *Investigating* (a project stage, DEC-79).

(`notes/D1.md` §4 item 13; `notes/C5.md` §4 item 13.)

## 1 · The need

### 1.1 What the scenarios ask of a home

P0's cross-cutting table names **holding** as a need in all fourteen scenarios: "One durable place, over weeks to years, for documents (with source, date retrieved, version), requests sent and their clocks, notes, leads, the chronology, hypotheses and their status, and what the member accepted". The need is longest in S2 (years), S4 and S11 (`notes/P0.md` §2). The other needs all write into or read from the same place:

| need (P0 §2) | what the home must hold or show | scenarios that show it sharpest |
|---|---|---|
| Timing | a chronology from many dated sources; several clocks at once (promise, contract, forecast; statutory deadlines on the government; deadlines that bind the member) | S1, S2, S11, S14 |
| Access regimes and refusals | each records request, its legal clock, extensions, rolling production, redactions, nonresponse. P0: "the records request is a first-class object ... and it is also evidence" (§2 obs. 3; ten of fourteen scenarios) | S1–S5, S12 |
| Competing explanations | 3–5 live explanations per scenario, each with what would confirm or refute it, its current grade, and UNDETERMINED where the deciding record is withheld | all |
| Reading with purpose | each document's kind and *why it is in the file* | S1, S2, S6, S8, S9, S11, S14 |
| Monitoring | the next event that changes the picture (each board change order, each monthly report, each filing period) | S1, S2, S7, S8, S10, S11 |
| Member involvement | several members on one file; members also *generate* evidence (site observations, complaint logs, court-watching) | all; S1, S5, S9, S12 |
| Answer and action | a chronology, a comparison table, attributed causes with grades, what is unknown and how to learn it, then actions and monitors to completion | all |

P0's observations make the home the spine of the engine *(P0's own inference)*:

- "Time dominates. Every scenario's answer is built on a chronology" (obs. 4).
- "Iteration is driven by arrivals ... most of an investigation's life is waiting" (obs. 5).
- "Scale varies by two orders of magnitude: S10 is days and a dozen documents; S2 and S11 are years and hundreds. The design must make the small case trivial and the large one sustainable" (obs. 6).
- "The member is a co-investigator, not only an approver" (obs. 7).

### 1.2 Bob's school rebuild, as a home must carry it

S1 (`notes/P0.md` §1) runs for months to years:

- **Week 1:** a public-record sweep, and a first chronology "with gaps marked".
- **Weeks 1–2:** a records request for the contract, schedules, change-order log and construction-manager reports.
- **Weeks 3–8:** production arrives in batches, and each batch re-plans ("a CO coded 'differing site condition' opens a soils line").
- **Months 2–6:** monitoring every board agenda and monthly report.
- The investigation stays open "until occupancy and DSA certification" (OP6).

Its spine is a chronology: "board approval → design contract → DSA submission → DSA approval → bid opening → award → NTP → each change order → each forecast slip → today", which answers "when did the district first know?". Three clocks run side by side:

1. the promise (one school year, source unknown);
2. the contract time, extended by change orders;
3. each monthly report's forecast.

Its answer is "a dated chronology of promised vs contracted vs forecast completion; each slip attributed to documented causes with the evidence and its grade; money table; what remains UNDETERMINED and which record would settle it".

A home for S1 must therefore hold at least:

- the parent's question and its sub-questions, and the objectives O1–O5;
- the plan and its open lines;
- some fifteen kinds of document, each with why it was sought ("the contract: for completion date, LD rate, extension clauses");
- the records requests and their clocks;
- the eight families of explanation and their state;
- the members' own observations ("crews idle");
- every run the assistant made and what it proposed;
- what members accepted, and why;
- the subject's chronology with its three clocks;
- the investigation's own log (what was asked, refused, decided).

### 1.3 The need restated as requirements on the home *(inference from 1.1–1.2)*

1. **One view** per investigation that answers "where are we?" without the member opening a dozen screens (K1627 "one view").
2. **Two chronologies kept apart**: what happened in the world, and what we did. A missed deadline of our own must never read as a finding about the district (`notes/C2.md` D27, Case Making §3).
3. **Why each document is held**, recorded where any later reader or run can find it (Bob, `RESUME.md`: "what is the reason that that document is in the case file?").
4. **Waiting made visible**: every wait says what, from whom, by when (DEC-98).
5. **Continuity across runs, members and months**: nothing an investigation must remember lives only in a transcript, in a run's scratch state, or in one member's account.
6. **Small case trivial, large case bounded**: a dozen documents or several hundred, with bounds stated.
7. **Works without AI** (K1547; DEC-120). The home is a record and a view; the assistant adds to it.

## 2 · Best practice that applies

Every tradition studied keeps a home of this kind, and the traditions agree on its shape.

**Lessons that bind the home's design**, each with its source:

- **H-L1 · One chronological spine, two kinds of entry.** The SBI master file is chronological; MCM keeps the investigational chronology apart from the decision log; Bellingcat's timeline rule is "Don't fill in gaps" (`research/B1.md` §2.1, §1.2; `research/B2.md` §1.1).
- **H-L2 · A closed record.** Every task names what raised it, and every document names what fetched it or who sent it (`research/B2.md` lesson 1). For us, every held document says who brought it, by which route, and why.
- **H-L3 · Decisions separate from activity, written for a stranger**, including why a line was *not* followed (`research/B2.md` lesson 9).
- **H-L4 · Gaps in the file are the next tasks.** SBI: "the structure is telling you what data it needs"; CoST keeps a disclosure checklist (`research/B1.md` §2.1; `research/B5.md` L3). The home shows *sought and not held* as plainly as *held*.
- **H-L5 · A refusal and a silence are entries**, not absences. CoST step 5; SBI: "If there's no report, that's a story" (`research/B5.md` L4; `research/B1.md` §1.4).
- **H-L6 · Handover and resumption are the test.** A newcomer, or a later run, reads the file and continues: MIM handover, Bellingcat's logging off, Tactical Tech, Anthropic's progress file (`research/B2.md` §1.1; `research/B1.md` §1.2–1.3; `research/B4.md` §1.2).
- **H-L7 · Append-only history.** Compiled and versioned views come from the same entries, as in OCDS releases and records (`research/B5.md` §2.1, L2).
- **H-L8 · Preservation apart from analysis.** Bellingcat's analysis database "does not preserve content" (`research/B1.md` §1.2). Our record already separates capture (bytes) from reading and meaning. The home is analysis, and must never become a second store of bytes *(inference)*.
- **H-L9 · Light touch for volunteers.** The court-grade method "may not be compatible" with volunteer coalitions; keep provenance and logs automatic (`research/B1.md` §1.2, lesson 17).
- **H-L10 · Dormancy is a state, not an end.** Investigative Maintenance; re-open on new material (`research/B2.md` §1.1, lesson 11).
- **H-L11 · Keep the person informed on a cadence** (APP 28-day updates, `research/B2.md` lesson 16). In this product DEC-69 and DEC-94 temper this: no nagging, and no outside channel. The home must therefore *be* the cadence the member returns to, rather than a message sent *(inference; see §4 G11)*.

**How an engine would use this as is** *(inference)*:

1. A member opens a project "Grandview rebuild", states its objective, and opens the parent's question.
2. Captures land as held captures in the project. The member releases them and cites passages as legs.
3. A records request is an `action` with a clock, and the reply returns as correspondence.
4. The member records the district's promise as a `standards` row of kind `commitment`, and as a duty with basis `commitment` (`notes/M4.md` §4 rows "a promise" and "its slip").
5. Members hold hypotheses on the working inquiry.
6. A `check` run is opened on the one question (`notes/M2.md` §1.1).
7. `events.timeline` can draw the world chronology over the entities the member names.

What the member cannot do is see any of this **in one place**, know **why** a document is there, or read **what the group did** in order. Each piece answers only within its own module.

**The canon has foreseen the home:**

- Layer 7's contract reads: "What the investigation below has established: the group's intent, with progress computed against the record, and which findings still stand when their basis changes" (`notes/C1.md` §1.6).
- Framework §12.2's pursuit record is, by design, the investigation's running log (`notes/C3.md` §1).
- The Intake Doctrine names "a named investigation scope" for blanket direction, without defining it (`notes/C4.md` §4 item 1).
- Membership already draws the privacy line where an investigation's working state falls: "What project participation scopes is the group's thinking ... where an argument has got to, what has been ruled out, what is being prepared" (MEM §7.9, `notes/C4.md` §1.3).

### 4.2 What exists but does not fit

1. **The project's stage is coarse.** It has four stages driven by legs and conclusions, "with no notion of open leads, outstanding records requests or objectives" (`notes/M1.md` §1.12). It stays the coarse "where are we". The view supplies the detail, and must never compose it into a progress figure (D3, DEC-82; `notes/M1.md` D13).
2. **The objective's condition speaks only of progressions.** "An objective the shape cannot express keeps its text with progress stated as not computable" (intent R4; `notes/M1.md` §4 item 2). For "what's going on at Grandview?" the objective will usually be text plus the plan's open lines. The home must show "progress: not computable" honestly rather than invent a figure.
3. **Held captures are listed per member and per project, not per question or purpose,** and "nothing about held captures is notified" (capture R77; `notes/M3.md` §2.2). That rule is right for the home too: it is status where the work lives, never a notification.
4. **`events.timeline` keeps our acts out of the world lane by design** (`notes/C5.md` §1.4, "Excludes the group's own acts"; events R28–R30), and the home must keep that wall. A non-response, though, is both something we did (we sent a request) and an outcome about the body (DEC-13: a non-response is a fact; `notes/M4.md` §1.19). The duty occurrence (`overdue`) carries it into the world lane, and the correspondence entry carries it into ours *(inference)*.
5. **Leads are private to their author and to the projects they are shared to** (MEM §7.9; observation-log R15). A lead joins the home only when shared, and sharing is a heavy act: "a disclosure that cannot be un-read" (DEC-88; `notes/D1.md`).
6. **Machine sight is wide.** "A machine credential ... sees every bundle" (membership R43; `notes/M4.md` §5 item 9), while a hidden project's run output is "the PROJECT'S THINKING" (BOB #32; `notes/C4.md` §1.3). A home that a run reads must stay within the sight of the run's principal (MEM §7.9 D33).
7. **"The budget is recorded and never SHOWN" (F11)** (`notes/C2.md` §1.3; `notes/C6.md` §1a), while `workobjective` shows "the run's budget and scope" beside its reason (DEC-88). What the home shows of a run's spend is A-COST's to settle.

### 4.3 Tensions the design must respect

- **Purpose may direct what is sought, never what is recorded or shown** (Framework invariant 7, `notes/C3.md` §2). Admission is by provenance, never relevance (ID §1a, `notes/C4.md` D1). A document's stated purpose is therefore an *annotation*: a document with no stated purpose is still held and still shown, and a purpose never hides or ranks anything *(inference)*.
- **Thinking is scoped; evidence is shared** (MEM §7.9). Purposes, the plan, notes and the journal are thinking. Documents, questions and findings are the shared record. A project does not own a line of inquiry (Bob 2026-09-18; `notes/C4.md` D32).
- **Our plan is not their progression** (Case Making §3; `notes/C2.md` D27). The home's two lanes must never share an object.
- **No read surveillance.** DEC-68: "logging what a member LOOKED AT is a doctrine question about surveilling members" (`notes/D1.md`); observation-log R24: "a member's own searching, viewing or reading writes nothing" (`notes/M2.md` §3.12). The home may say a document was *read by the record* (content axis: extracted, indexed). It may never say that a member opened it.
- **Assistant work is never members' work** (DEC-111; `notes/D1.md`). Every journal entry names its actor class, and the assistant's entries say "the assistant, at X's request" (DEC-90, DEC-125).
- **No score, no priority** (DEC-89; ACT §4 r3). Nothing in the home ranks documents, explanations or members. Order is by time or by a stated quantity only (K1471; `notes/C6.md` §2 row 12).

## 6 · Recommendation: Option B

### 6.1 The shape in one paragraph

**The investigation is the project.** Its home is one view of that project, composed from the owners that already hold the work, plus three things the owners do not hold:

- why each document is held or sought (`holdings`);
- the members' own working notes (`notes`);
- which entities the investigation is about, so its chronology can be drawn (`investigation`'s subject set).

The view shows two chronologies side by side and never merged: **what happened** (the subject's events, with the promise and contract clocks) and **what we did** (the journal, derived from every owner's append-only history, with decisions as a filter of it). Nothing in the home is evidence by being in the home; nothing is a score; nothing notifies. It is status where the work lives (DEC-97, DEC-104 pattern). A run, a new member or a returning member starts from it.

### 6.2 Relation to project, inquiry and intent

| construct | role in the investigation | change |
|---|---|---|
| **project** | the home itself: membership, owners, sight (hidden or discoverable), the bar (DEC-72), the computed stage, the plans. One investigation is one project (decision D-H1) | none |
| **inquiry** | the questions. They are shared and recursive, and are never owned: "a project doesn't own an area of enquiry to the exclusion of others" (`notes/C4.md` D32). The home *draws on* them, as the project already does through `cites` and basis versions; several homes may draw on one question, each with its own CURRENT version and conclusion (`notes/C6.md` §2 row 20) | inquiry R55 amended so a wait may be set for the project (D-H5) |
| **intent** | the objectives, their conditions, progress and gaps, and the pursuit record. The home reads `pursuitOf` into its journal and `gaps` into its sought list. `workObjective` opens a run whose first read is the home's `resumeContext` | intent unchanged; `investigation` reads it |

The scope's three constructs thus map onto three layers: project (layer 2, membership), inquiry (layer 6), intent (layer 7). The home sits **beside intent in layer 7**, whose contract already names "the investigation below" (`notes/C1.md` §1.6) *(inference)*.

### 6.3 New and changed modules

#### `holdings` (new; layer 6, directly after `capture-requests`)

**Responsibility.** Why each document is held, or is sought, in a project's investigation, and by whom, serving what. It answers "why does the group have this?" from store state (ID §4; `notes/C4.md` D2).

**Stored, per `HLD-` row:**

- `project`;
- `target`: a held capture's digest, or, for a document *sought*, its kind and description and where it is expected (an address or an office);
- `kind`: the document's role in words from the profile's document-kind vocabulary (PROCUREMENT's list; none in code, D31);
- `purpose`: what it is for, in the words of whoever stated it;
- `serves[]`: question ids, the objective, and plan-step ids that a later layer registers;
- `brought`: actor class, actor, and route (member capture, named request, sweep, a run's capture request, doorbell pull, records-request reply);
- `set_aside`: a member's reason;
- the history of each change. Rows are append-only (H-L7).

**Derived on read, never stored** (one home per fact, CL §10 D368):

- the state: *sought → requested* (from a capture request or an `action`) → *held* (collected) → *released* → *read by the record* (content axis: extracted, indexed; `retrieval.contentAxis`) → *cited* (a leg in a question the project draws on) → or *set aside*;
- for a sought row, its look state in D-129's words: Nobody looked; Looked, absent; Looked, could not tell (`notes/M2.md` §3.12).

**Services**, each in a sentence:

- `hold({project, target, kind?, purpose, serves?, by})` records why a held document is in the project. A member may call it. So may a machine credential inside a run, with its label: "the assistant sought this, at Rosa's request, to …" (DEC-53 machine-attributed writes; DEC-84 (14) "the machine's reason stays the machine's").
- `seek({project, kind, description, where?, purpose, serves})` records a document the investigation expects and does not have. This is the file's gap made visible (H-L4).
- `revise` changes a row and keeps its history. `setAside({reason})` is a member's act only, like `capture` R79's set-aside.
- `holdingsOf({project, viewer, state?, kind?, serves?, page})` lists a project's holdings with derived states; it is bounded, states its truncation, and orders by time or kind only.
- `whyHeld({captureSha, viewer})` lists every holding the viewer may see for one document, across projects.
- `registerServes(kind, check)` lets intent and the planner (layer 7) register the ids a holding may serve.
- `onHeld(listener)` tells later modules (the planner's re-plan trigger) when a sought row becomes held.

**Fences:**

1. A capture lands whether or not any holding names it, and an un-annotated capture is listed as "held, no stated purpose" (inv. 7; ID §1a).
2. A purpose filters nothing, ranks nothing and gates nothing.
3. A holding is never a leg (refused like a lead, `LEAD_NOT_EVIDENCE`; `notes/M1.md` D5).
4. "Read" is never a member's viewing (DEC-68).
5. A hidden project's holdings answer as absent (DEC-36).
6. Sought rows never fetch: fetching stays with standing intent and capture requests (ID §4: "fetching is policy-governed, never caller-governed").

**Why a new module rather than fields on existing ones.** Purposes arrive by six routes (member capture, named request, sweep, run, doorbell, records-request reply). They must also exist *before* a capture (sought), and they belong to the project's thinking while the bytes belong to the shared corpus (MEM §7.9). A field on `capture-requests` covers one route. A note on `citation` covers only what is already a leg. Neither can hold the sought list *(inference)*.

#### `notes` (new; layer 6, after `hypotheses`; subject to D-H4)

**Responsibility.** A member's own working words in an investigation: "what I think", as distinct from a lead ("what I was told", observation-log) and a hypothesis ("an explanation held on a question", K1467).

**Stored, per `NTE-` row:** author, project, scope (`private` or `project`), text, an optional `about` (question, holding, entity), and history. A note is member-only (`MACHINE_CANNOT_NOTE`). It is never evidence: refused as a leg, like a lead.

**Services.**

- `note`, `revise`, `withdraw`.
- `notesOf({project, viewer})`. Private notes reach only their author, never administrators (as with leads, MEM §7.9).
- A note becomes a question, lead or hypothesis only by the member's own act, recorded as "from note NTE-…" (DEC-24: the member chooses the question).

**Doctrine.** DR6 applies: a note names a person only with a recorded basis before anything publishes (`notes/C1.md` D22), and a note never publishes (D-H3).

#### `investigation` (new; layer 7, after `intent` and `reevaluation`, and after the planner if A-PLANNING places it in layer 7)

**Responsibility.** The one view of a project's investigation, its two-lane timeline, its journal, and the context a run reads first. It owns one small table, the **subject set**, and writes nothing else.

**Stored.** The subject set: the entities the investigation is about (the district, the school `place`, the bond `fund` or `program`, the construction `contract`, the contractor). Members add and remove entries, with history. A machine may *propose* additions from holdings' resolved entities, labelled; a member adopts them (`notes/C2.md` D13: adopt, defer or dismiss).

**Services:**

- `investigationOf({project, viewer, asOf?})` returns the view in sections. Each section is bounded, names its truncation, and answers hidden things as absent:
  - questions, with state, CURRENT stance and **strength pair against the project's bar** (never one badge, DEC-82);
  - objectives, with condition, progress or "not computable" (intent R4), and gaps;
  - the plan (from A-PLANNING's registration);
  - holdings, by state and kind. These are counts by state, never a percentage of completion;
  - waits, one line each, "what, from whom, by when" (DEC-98), from registered sources: action clocks, capture requests, dated waits, duty occurrences, monitoring rechecks;
  - explanations: hypotheses per question, "Hunches to clear" (DEC-104), contradictions on the project with duty/lead weight;
  - runs: mode, who started, state, what each proposed and what members did with it;
  - findings and conclusions;
  - shared notes;
  - the project stage, and what the next stage needs (project-stage R4).
- `timelineOf({project, viewer, from, to})` returns two lanes, shown side by side and never interleaved (events R28–R30):
  - **What happened** is `explore.timelineOver(subject set)`, plus the occurrences and recorded transitions of the duties that concern the set (the promise's commitment-basis due date and the contract's completion duty: the S1 clocks; `notes/M4.md` §4 rows "a promise", "its slip"), plus dated facts of held documents;
  - **What we did** is `journalOf`.
  - Each entry carries its source and grade; imprecise dates show as bands (EDTF); gaps are left as gaps (H-L1); "overdue" appears as a question, never a violation (duties R11).
- `journalOf({project, viewer, from, to, actor?, kind?, decisionsOnly?})` returns the log of the investigation itself. It is derived on read from registered sources, each an owner's own append-only history:
  - intent `pursuitOf` (triaged proposals, capture requests and outcomes, dead ends);
  - holdings history;
  - inquiry state history and exclusions;
  - basis-version acts with reasons;
  - contradiction dispositions (accepted a recommendation, or unaided; DEC-77);
  - queue dispositions;
  - ai-runs open and close, and their productions;
  - actions and their correspondence (sent, received, `no_response`);
  - action-plan choices;
  - monitoring's changes to held sources.

  Each entry gives: when; who (a member's handle, "the assistant, at X's request", or "the daemon"); the act; the object; and the reason, verbatim. `decisionsOnly` keeps the reasoned member acts: this is the decision log (H-L3), with no new store.
- `registerSource({lane, kind, read})` lets modules in later layers (actions and action-plans in layer 9, monitoring in layer 10) contribute entries without `investigation` using them. This is the established inversion pattern (`registerEventSource`, `registerTriggerSource`; `notes/M4.md` §1.19).
- `resumeContext({project, viewer, bound})` is the compact, bounded read a run takes at open: objectives and gaps, the plan's open lines, holdings with purposes and states, open waits, live explanations, and the journal since a date. It is reached through **one op added to the run's plane reach** (a reviewed edit, agent-worker R37). It gives a run started by any member, weeks later, the same starting point (H-L6; G9). It returns references and short text, never document bytes (the reports-not-documents rule, `notes/M2.md` T1).
- `subjectAdd`, `subjectRemove`, `subjectPropose`, `subjectsOf`.

**Fences:**

1. No composed figure, percentage or score anywhere (DEC-82, DEC-89).
2. No notification. The queue is unchanged and already groups by project (`notes/M1.md` §1.17).
3. Nothing about what a member viewed (DEC-68).
4. Assistant entries are always labelled, and never counted as the group's activity (DEC-111).
5. Every read applies the viewer predicate. A run's read stays within its principal's sight (MEM §7.9 D33; membership R43's wide machine sight does not reach the home).
6. A section the substrate cannot fill says so in DEC-86's words ("Nobody looked", "Undetermined, because …") rather than appearing empty.

**Dormancy without a new stage.** When nothing is actionable and waits remain, the view says "Waiting on 3 things; next expected 14 October", derived from the waits. That is Investigative Maintenance as a display, not a stage (H-L10). Arrivals (a held reply, a monitoring change, a wait falling due) reach the queue as they do today, and the home reflects them *(inference)*.

**Small amendments elsewhere:**

- `inquiry` R55: a dated wait may be set for a project, and is then answered to its joined participants (D-H5).
- `actions` R66–R67 built: the group's own acts feed the "what we did" lane (`notes/M4.md` §1.21).
- `capture-requests`: a request may carry `purpose` text and `serves`, which become the holding on completion. Today it carries a `purpose` enum only (`notes/M2.md` §3.4).
- `agent-worker` R37: add the `resumeContext` read to `PLANE_OPS`.

### 6.4 How the parts work over Bob's school rebuild *(inference)*

1. **Week 0.** The parent opens a project "Grandview rebuild" and states its objective. The interview (A-PLANNING) asks where "one school year" came from. Her question becomes an inquiry. Her memory of the promise is a note, and her site visit ("fenced, no crews") is a `testify` observation.
2. She adds the district, the school, the bond program and the contractor to the subject set. The plan's documents-to-find become **sought** holdings: the bond measure, the award item, the contract (time and LDs), the baseline schedule, the change-order log, the CM monthly reports, the DSA tracker. Each has a purpose and the question or step it serves.
3. **Week 1.** A run opened by `workObjective` reads `resumeContext`, finds the board award item and the DSA tracker online, and files capture requests with purposes. On completion they become **held**, labelled "sought by the assistant, at the parent's request, to establish the award date and contract sum".
4. The records request for the contract and schedules is an `action`. The home's waits show "Waiting on the district's records officer: contract, schedules, change-order log; response due 14 October (Gov. Code 7922.535)". The contract holding reads **sought → requested**.
5. **Weeks 3–8.** Production arrives. Each reply is correspondence, and the holdings it answers become held. READING proposes the contract's completion date as a dated fact and a duty, and the member adopts them. The timeline's world lane now shows three dates side by side: the promise (if its source was found), the contract's completion date, and each report's forecast. "Overdue" shows as a question.
6. The baseline schedule was not produced. Its holding reads "Looked, absent: withheld under the litigation exemption, reply of 2 November", and the refusal is also a journal entry (H-L5).
7. **Month 6.** A second parent joins. She reads "What we did": what was requested, refused and decided, with each reason, and which entries were the assistant's. A run she starts reads the same `resumeContext`. Nothing depended on the first parent's account or transcript.
