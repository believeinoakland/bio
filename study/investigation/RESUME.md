## What Bob asked (his words, 2026-10-06)
> Suppose a member who is a parent asks the assistant... "My kids' school, Grandview Elementary, is finally being rebuilt. The process of designing the new campus, putting the construction out for bid, choosing the winning bid, and construction starting took 3 years. We were promised that reconstruction would take no longer than 1 school year. But now it's been 2 years, and the project still isn't close to being done. What's going on?"
> It seems to me that the system should have an "engine" that takes the details that it's been given (maybe asks a few follow on questions if it needs to) and starts digging into what's going on. It might research the original RFP, submitted bids, the award of the winning bid, its terms, budget, project status reports, contractor info/history, and so on. All to get at the heart of - what's going on. This is what investigators have traditionally done - whether they're lawyers, cops, journalists, or community members.

> Couldn't an AI researcher be given enough context about the purpose of the document to inform it to know how to read it? ... what is the reason that that document is in the case file? (Oh, so it's an invoice. Or it's an RFP.) With that context, the researcher could then study the document to better understand its contents. (The section of an invoice. The TOC of an RFP.) With that understanding, the researcher could explore deeper and deeper until it is able to extract content and develop meaning and connections about what it says.
> This planning function... it has context about all the various things going on in the investigation. At a tactical level, it understands why it has this document and this content. At a higher level, it's able to stay focused on its objective(s).
> An investigation is an ongoing, iterative process. So there should be a "case file" construct that various elements of the system add documents to and refer to throughout an investigation.
> Yes, the machine proposes and a member accepts. So the member is kept "in the loop" and involved as the investigation progresses.
> Let's start by really thinking through the capabilities that we're looking to add. As always, we need to appreciate what we already have in the substrate and how to use those services. We want to explore and thoroughly understand the problem set, thoroughly research best practice, understand the canon as it applies to this challenge, and finally come up with a proposed architecture that is exactly what's called for with everything considered. ... this research [should] be set up to be scalable given the enormity of the scope.

## To resume, in order
1. `git fetch origin study/investigation` and check it out (a worktree is fine); work in its `study/investigation/` (the study folder). The sources are the branch's own tree: no rebuild needed.
2. `python3 status.py`; find the earliest phase with a unit not `done`.
3. For each such unit, start a background worker (Agent tool, general-purpose) whose prompt is `prompts/<id>.txt` with STUDY replaced by the study folder's absolute path. Units of one phase run in parallel. A `partial` unit resumes itself (PROTOCOL.md, checkpoint rule).
4. Commit and push the study folder after each unit completes (plain git, on `study/investigation` only).
5. Phase 4: the S-SYNTHESIS worker drafts; BOB reviews it against the studies, corrects it, renders it as a page for Bob (P17: shown rendered), and records nothing in the product until Bob rules.
