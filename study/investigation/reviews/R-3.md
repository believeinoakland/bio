# R-3 · Review of LOOP and COST, and of the six studies' consistency

Unit R-3, phase 3 (`PROTOCOL.md` §V). Scope: `studies/LOOP.md` and `studies/COST.md` in full, and how all six studies fit together. I read all six studies whole. Where a claim mattered, I checked it against the notes it cites (listed under Sources opened). Severity:
- **blocking**: the synthesis cannot proceed until this is settled;
- **should fix**: the synthesis must settle it;
- **minor**: a correction.

**Overall.** LOOP and COST are sound, keep to doctrine, and are mostly well cited. Each one's recommendation (LOOP Option B, a thin loop over the existing owners; COST Option B, allowance, meter and test investigations) follows from its evidence.

The main problems lie *between* the studies. The six were written in parallel, and READING says it did not use the others' content (`READING.md` Sources opened). As a result, at least five constructs are designed two to four times, each time with a different owner and layer. The synthesis must choose one design for each. The blocking findings (X1–X4) are where studies contradict each other on architecture or on the deployment gate.

## 5 · Where the six studies already agree

These hold across all six studies and need no change:
- the machine never holds a hypothesis (K1473);
- cause is member-authored (DEC-84 (10));
- no score and no priority (DEC-89);
- no outside notification (DEC-94);
- no read surveillance (DEC-68);
- the plan and the home are never published (DEC-25 by analogy);
- each run is paid by the starting member's own account (K1502);
- a no-AI floor at every first step (K1547);
- the project is the home (K1627);
- the review threshold is ≥95% over ≥30;
- the baselines (promise, approved schedule, contract time, baseline schedule) are fixed before a slip is measured.
