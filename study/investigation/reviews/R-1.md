# R-1 · Review of studies/READING.md and studies/PROCUREMENT.md

Unit R-1, phase 3 (`PROTOCOL.md` §V). Both studies were read whole. Where a finding depends on a claim, I checked the cited notes (`notes/M2.md` §3.4; `notes/M3.md` §2.12, §2.15, §3.3; `notes/M4.md` §1.6–§1.10, §4, §5; `notes/D1.md` DEC-74, DEC-75, DEC-77, DEC-121 and gap 3; `notes/C2.md` AR `:119–137`; `notes/C3.md` §16; `notes/C5.md` §1.6, MONEY and ANALYSIS realism lines; `notes/P0.md` S1 and §2; `research/B4.md` §1.3, §1.5). I also looked at the other four studies for the constructs the two share (see §4).

**Overall.** Both studies are well sourced and keep to doctrine. The central idea of each is sound: READING's purposeful, verbatim-anchored reading with profiles as data, and PROCUREMENT's "no new owner module, vocabulary amendments plus a pack". Most product claims I spot-checked match their notes (for example: 52 of 600 multi-class documents, C3 §16; `doctypeFor` "about one in twelve", M3 §2.6; extraction R42 "never above B"; money R14 naming change orders; `contracts_with` with OCDS roles; the B4 figures 51.5%, "five examples", 110x). The serious problems are in how the two studies fit with each other and with the substrate's evidence rules. Two constructs are designed twice, in incompatible ways: the proposal path and the record of why a document is held. One grade rule is stated three different ways.

Severity counts: **3 blocking**, **11 should fix**, **8 minor**.

## 1 · Blocking findings

### R1-B1 · Two incompatible proposal paths for world facts
- **Claim.** READING §6.4 adds a new module, `fact-proposals` (layer 6), which holds every proposed fact and adopts it by calling the owner's member act. It states that "the world-fact owners (layer 5) gain no machine write". PROCUREMENT §6.3 builds the path "once, in each owner module": `proposeEvent | proposeLine | proposeMoneyFact` in layer 5. It then states "No new module and no layer change" (§6.8).
- **Evidence.** The two designs exclude each other. LOOP L4 (`studies/LOOP.md` l.265) sides with PROCUREMENT: propose paths go in `events`, `lines` and `money`. M4 §5 item 1 lists the propose → adopt pattern that already exists inside owners: duties R2, standards R9, money sets R13. READING's `factAdopt` would call `duties.adopt` and `standards.adopt`, but those acts adopt proposals from their own module's store (M4 §1.6, `propose` → `adopt` R1–R3). A second tray in `fact-proposals` would therefore duplicate those stores, or would call `adopt` with a proposal the owner never held.
- **Fix.** Synthesis picks one design. I recommend PROCUREMENT's and LOOP's shape, because it is the existing pattern: each owner gets propose → adopt, as duties and standards already have. READING's `fact-proposals` then shrinks to the run-side parts only, which can sit in `run-productions`:
  - the verification refusals (quote not found, cites unread, field not in profile, grade offered);
  - the grouping of one reading's proposals into one queue item;
  - `acceptanceRates`.

  This also settles PROCUREMENT's layering defect (R1-S6).

### R1-B2 · The grade of an AI-located fact is stated three ways, and two of them break doctrine
- **Claim.**
  - READING §6.3: the quote "is the publisher's text"; its cap is the text unit's cap, "unchanged by the model, which only *located* it". The interpretation is the member's, on the meaning axis.
  - READING §5 Option B and §4.2 F4 say the opposite: they reuse `propose-reading`'s "grade rule and `ai(fn, version)` chain step".
  - PROCUREMENT §6.5: "an AI reading is a weakening `ai(fn, version)` step, at most B".
- **Evidence.**
  - Built: extraction R43 appends an `ai(fn, version)` step to the capture's chain for every proposed reading, and R42 caps it at B (M3 §2.12).
  - Canon: EXTRACT's proposed reading carries an `ai(function, version)` step "graded by what it names and never A" (C2 AR `:119–137`).
  - Doctrine: DEC-75 says "a step whose fidelity was never measured has cap UNDETERMINED, stated, never a letter". D1 infers this covers AI extraction.

  So PROCUREMENT's "at most B" matches R42 but ignores DEC-75. READING §6.3 departs from both R43 and the canon line, and never says it is amending them. Bob and the synthesis cannot tell which grade a member-adopted "contract time 540 days" carries. PROCUREMENT §6.10 shows "grade B".
- **Fix.** READING's split is the better rule:
  - the quote is verified by code, so the model adds no transcription;
  - the value is parsed by code;
  - the interpretation is the member's act.

  But it is a change to a built requirement (extraction R43) and to a canon production rule, and it touches evidence doctrine (DEC-75). It must therefore be a **Decision for Bob** with its own recommendation, not a design detail. Both studies then cite the one rule. Also state the residue honestly: the *location* claim ("this passage is the contract time") is a judgement on the meaning axis. When the member adopts it unaided, the record should say so. When the member adopts as proposed, DEC-77 (b) requires the record to keep that it was accepted as a recommendation.

### R1-B3 · "Why a document is held" has three homes
- **Claim.**
  - READING §6.1 step 1 and §9: a *held-for record*, owned by HOME, is required before a reading starts.
  - PROCUREMENT §6.5 step 2: "placing the document at a stage of the contract's declared flow … The stage *is* the document's role … no new 'purpose' field is needed".
  - PROCUREMENT §6.5 step 1 adds that "the purpose travels with the capture request (`capture-requests` already carries `purpose` …)".
- **Evidence.**
  - HOME builds `holdings` (`studies/HOME.md` l.219, l.281). PLANNING builds `whyHeld` from plan steps (`studies/PLANNING.md` l.252, l.283).
  - A stage cannot carry two purposes for one document. READING §1.2 R1 needs exactly that: the same contract read once for the schedule and once for the change-order clause.
  - A stage cannot hold purposes for non-procurement documents (S2, S8, S14).
  - `capture-requests`' `purpose` is the conduct enum `investigate | acquire`, not a reason (M2 §3.4, l.129). PROCUREMENT misreads it (also R1-S5).
- **Fix.** Use one construct: HOME's `holdings` is the record. PLANNING's step link and PROCUREMENT's stage placement are two acts that *write* a holding: placing a document at a stage creates or updates a holding with "serves: stage X of flow Y". READING reads `holdings`, and PLANNING's `whyHeld` reads them too. PROCUREMENT §6.5 steps 1–2 and §9 should say this.
