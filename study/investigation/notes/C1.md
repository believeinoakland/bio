### 1.1 `BIO_Complete_Roadmap_v5.md` §§1–12 (the mission of record)

**Standing.** The header and the 2026-08-10 banner say §§1–12 are "current doctrine" and §§13–15 "history" (`BIO_Complete_Roadmap_v5.md` lines 83–99). Its Focus/Project vocabulary is superseded by the INQUIRY collapse (DEC-72; header "Incomplete sections" §9). The Design Requirements govern it on conflict (§Companion documents, lines 170–178).

**§1 Origin — the system was born from an investigation.** The sewer-fund matter is the archetype of what Bob's school-rebuild question asks for: a documented promise (the Auditor's four recommendations, an October 2023 implementation deadline), a slip ("As of April 2026, the city has implemented none of them", line 230), money traced across sources (OpenGov transfers "may have been relabeled as cost allocations or overhead charges", lines 233–238), a records request with a statutory clock (CPRA 26-3028; the 10-day deadline under Gov. Code 7922.535 expired "with no response", lines 252–259), and two research products (legal pathways; structural diagnosis) (lines 261–280). It shows the recurring investigative moves: a hypothesis about relabelling (competing explanation), a request scoped by fiscal years and document kinds (ACFR statements, cost allocation plan, auditor follow-up), and an escalation menu tied to a missed deadline. *(inference)* Every element Bob describes for the school case — promise, schedule, slip, documents by kind, money — already appears in the founding case; the engine is a generalisation of what was done by hand there.

**§2 Strategic pivot.** "What it cannot ignore is SUSTAINED ATTENTION applied through legal mechanisms" (lines 300–302); "a compliance-verification framework (not policy advocacy) is genuinely non-partisan" (lines 307–309); the system must "demonstrate a credible path to results" (line 304). Bearing: an investigation must be holdable over months (the city's strategy is "run out the clock", line 288) and must stay framed as compliance against stated standards, not opinion.

**§§3–5 Values and principles.** Four core values and eight operational principles (lines 337–388). Most load-bearing for investigation: CV3 "Accountability is built on credible facts"; CV4 "Our government must follow the law and its stated policies" — note *stated policies*, which brings a district's own published schedule promise ("no longer than 1 school year") inside scope as a standard *(inference)*; OP2 "Show your work. Always."; OP3 "The clock runs. Deadlines are deadlines."; OP4 "Follow the evidence wherever it goes. When the evidence changes, the findings change."; OP6 "Compliance restored. Consequences addressed." with "partial compliance doesn't stop the clock" (lines 390–392).

**§6 Design requirements (orientation only;** the authoritative text is `BIO_Design_Requirements_v2.md`, summarised in §1.2 below). The roadmap's summary of DR6 already carries the 2026-10-05 amendment (people named with documented acts, positions, ties and financial interests, K1483; lines 436–440).

**§7 Platforms.** Forum, directory, work-product hosting; "remains genuinely not started" (header). Bearing: minor — an investigation's outward discovery of other groups' work depends on a directory that is specified, not built.

**§8 Evidence packages and risk tiers.** Tier 1 (CPRA requests, grand jury complaints, State Controller referrals, Brown Act reports, media) "Templates included"; Tier 2 (CPRA court petitions) with advisory notes; Tier 3 (Prop 218, CCP 526a, consent decree motions) evidence published, no templates, and a **counsel packet** "(the facts with their citations, a chronology, exhibits with provenance, the standards' text, candidate legal theories and remedies, and any deadline that binds a claim)" never published nor fileable (lines 526–541). Bearing: an investigation's end products are already specified downstream (Action), and the counsel packet's contents list is effectively a description of what a mature investigation file must yield: facts with citations, a chronology, exhibits with provenance, standards, theories, binding deadlines *(inference)*.

**§9 AI skill architecture (8 skills).** "Every skill is advisory, transparent, optional" and "Skills are invisible infrastructure; users interact with interface functions, not skills directly" (lines 548–552). The skills most relevant to an investigation engine:

| Skill | What canon says | Bearing on the engine |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Context/Landscape | "Assembles what's known about a group's area of interest. Surfaces relevant laws, audit and grand jury reports, BIO work products, news, CPRA status." Orient phase (lines 572–577) | The first step of any investigation: what is already known, including other groups' work |
| 2 Data Archive | Baseline collection, monitoring for new publications, change detection "preserving both versions as evidence" (lines 579–585) | Holding documents over time; a removed status report is evidence |

| 4 Legal/Policy Lookup | Maps applicable laws, policies, regulations, case law "to specific government actions" (lines 593–597) | Supplies the standard a promise or contract is measured against |

| 6 Government Compliance Analysis | Structured comparison "what the standard requires, what the city did, where they align, where they diverge, and what additional information would resolve ambiguous cases … presents them for human evaluation rather than rendering a determination" (lines 606–619) | The analytical core; its "what additional information would resolve" output is the seed of planning |
| 7 Compliance Evaluation | Evaluates BIO work products; "Output is descriptive, not pass/fail" (lines 621–628) | Quality of the investigation's output, not the investigation itself |

No skill in the inventory is an *investigation planner*: nothing takes an objective and proposes the next document to seek, and nothing reads a document "knowing why it is in the file" *(inference; confirmed by absence across §9 and the Functional Architecture's inventory)*. Skills 7 and 8 "remain unbuilt as named skills" (header).

**§10 Group workflow (5 phases).** Orient → Investigate → Document → Escalate → Monitor; "New evidence triggers re-investigation (OP4)"; "Human chooses focus"; "Human applies judgment"; "At every phase: AI skills provide support. Humans make every decision." (lines 642–668). Bearing: the canon's lifecycle is already iterative and human-decided; an engine fits inside Orient + Investigate and feeds Monitor.

**§11 Trust and inter-group awareness.** Five trust levels now shown as an **origin mark** (DEC-92): Ours / Another group's / Accepted by our group / Not yet evaluated / Flagged — "Flagged" only "when a member's recorded evaluation names specific issues, never a machine's" (lines 697–704). DEC-96: accepting another group's work is "a reasoned act naming one edition" that "changes no grade" (lines 706–711). DEC-112: another group's case is imported read-only and confirmed by **recreation** (Recreated / Recreated in part / Did not recreate) — "Recreating is not endorsing" (lines 713–717). DEC-111: "working on" signals are project-backed and signed; activity level "computed from members' own work (never the assistant's)" (lines 735–743). "No group owns an issue" (line 732). Bearing: two groups may investigate the same school; an engine should find others' published work and "working on" signals (Skill 1), and anything machine-made must be labelled "Machine work" (line 699).

**§12 UX (seven categories).** Context, Search, New Developments, Monitoring, Communications, Projects, Settings (lines 758–786). Search is "Persistent requests with lifecycle: active, complete, failed, canceled. Accept results to Context. Refine, re-run, duplicate. System commentary. Source skill attribution." (lines 764–767) — the canon's oldest statement of a machine-proposes / member-accepts loop for retrieval. Principles: "Trust signals are ambient", "Skills are invisible infrastructure", "Search results carry provenance into Context when accepted" (lines 788–812). The "Platform (resolved, v4)" paragraph is stale (header).

### 1.3 `BIO_Functional_Architecture_v3.md` (whole; the functional analysis)

Three **concurrent** layers — Information, Analysis, Action — "not phases" (lines 101–118). The analysis is unchanged since April; the July resolution notes point to TAD v10 and State Rules, many of which describe the retired substrate (header).

**Layer 1 Information.** *Discover* (public government information by kind — regulations, court decisions, stated policies, financial documents, audits, public records, news — and BIO network information), by "user-initiated search … and AI-driven surfacing" (lines 126–145). *Retrieve and extract*: "An ACFR is a 180-page PDF … Budget documents are formatted for reading, not analysis" (lines 147–154); the output is "JSON in tidy/long form for line items, carrying provenance, source locator, content hash, criticality, and fact/analysis/judgment classification", persisted as an Information bundle (lines 156–163). *Archive* with SHA-256; dynamic data as a three-layer snapshot (raw WACZ, normalised dataset, rendered view) (lines 165–188). *Monitor and notify*: new / modified / removed, "the removal is flagged as potential evidence of concealment"; modification propagates a re-evaluation flag to every citing object (lines 190–214). Five of eight skills are Layer 1 because "information management is the largest and most labor-intensive part of a group's work, and where AI support has the highest leverage" (lines 233–236).

**Layer 2 Analysis.** "The most judgment-intensive layer … the human evaluates every flagged discrepancy and makes every determination" (lines 244–250). Functions: (1) compare actions to standards — structured comparison "what the law requires, what the city did, where they match, where they don't, and what questions remain unanswered" (lines 252–261); (2) cross-reference multiple sources — "Government noncompliance often becomes visible only when multiple data sources are compared" (lines 269–276), with discrepancies persisted (then) as a Focus, "related to existing Focuses in a graph with agent-proposed, human-decided edges, and triaged elevate/defer/dismiss with recheck triggers" (lines 283–288); (3) classify findings as fact / analysis / judgment — "facts are verifiable, analysis is reproducible, and judgment is debatable" (lines 290–307); (4) evaluate significance — human judgment; "the temptation to let policy preferences color compliance judgments is strongest when evaluating significance" (lines 309–323). **Outputs: Compliant / Noncompliant / Unclear**, where Unclear "triggers a return to Layer 1 (more information needed) and may result in CPRA requests, additional research, or outreach to other groups" (lines 325–341). This Unclear → Layer 1 loop is the canon's only explicit statement of investigative iteration driven by an information gap *(inference: it is the nucleus of a planning function)*.

**Layer 3 Action.** Document (work product with "machine-checked citation register grounding every load-bearing claim in archived, hashed primary sources", lines 367–382); evaluate and publish (Compliance and Argument Evaluation; readiness ladder, lines 384–397); escalate; communicate; track toward resolution — "every date-bearing clock entry carries the statute, order, or commitment it derives from, and overdue entries are marked, never silently stale" (lines 416–428). The last is directly relevant to Bob's "promised one school year": a promise is a clock entry with a derivation *(inference)*.

**Cross-cutting.** Search fans out over heterogeneous sources via "a thin orchestrator over independent, optional per-source adapters, each emitting a common record shape with provenance, source identity, recency, and trust" (lines 443–461). Change detection per source type (lines 479–502). Trust travels with data "so that when a human evaluates a flagged discrepancy, they can see the provenance and trust level of each data point involved" (lines 504–519).

**The three-layer workflow (Bob, 2026-07-27)** — the most important passage in this source for the study. Layer 2 "articulates objectives that define what it wants to see in the data store: a specific document, a kind of document, a document set that could contain a piece of evidence it needs, with the requester indicating whether found documents should be kept up to date and when or how often the data layer should look for updates (served today by named requests, monitored gathering with frequency, and source-status tracking). It uses AI skills to define and achieve focused analytical objectives, done in the context of a FOCUS." Project work is done "under the declared bias in force and graded by the epistemics ladder: evidence identified from Information, analysis built from evidence, conclusions graded on how they follow only from evidence and analysis" (lines 593–613). This is, in Bob's own earlier words, an objective-driven analysis layer that asks the data layer for documents by identity, kind, or set — i.e. the planning→gathering contract the investigation engine needs. *(inference)* The investigation study should treat this as the canon's prior statement of the engine's shape, not a new idea.

## 4. Gaps and tensions found

1. **No planner in canon.** The eight-skill inventory (Roadmap §9; Functional Architecture inventory) and the eleven layers have no module or skill that takes an objective and proposes what to look for next. The nearest are Bob's 2026-07-27 framing of Layer 2 ("articulates objectives that define what it wants to see in the data store", Functional Architecture final section), `intent`'s "gaps are the work list" (layers.md layer 7), Skill 6's "what additional information would resolve", and the un-added "Discovery" candidate (layers.md layer 7). The study should decide whether the planner *is* Discovery (layer 7) or a new module, and that is a Bob decision (layers.md ruling 5) *(inference)*.
2. **Investigation vs Analysis vocabulary.** The canon has three "layer" schemes that do not reconcile: the Functional Architecture's Information / Analysis / Action; Bob's 2026-07-27 foundation / analysis / UI ("the section neither points to them nor reconciles its layer names", Functional Architecture header); and the eleven build layers. Layer 7's contract calls inquiry "the investigation below". The study must name where "investigation" lives without a fourth scheme *(inference)*.
3. **"Case" collision** (noted in `RESUME.md`): canon's case is the published production (DEC-72; System Design §1, row 8). The roadmap's Tier 3 counsel packet and Bob's "case file" talk about the investigation's working file. Canon has the *project* as working home (K1627: "The project becomes the investigation's home"), which avoids the collision.
4. **Objective is not a first-class object.** "goals and aspirations as objects" ABSENT; "an objective is a zero-leg open inquiry" and "the objective lives on the project today" (System Design row 8; layers.md layer 7). A planner keyed to objectives needs a decision on whether a zero-leg inquiry suffices *(inference)*.
5. **Procurement absent from canon in scope.** No document kind, actor model or lifecycle for RFP → bids → award → contract → change orders → progress reports → completion. DR6 now mandates naming award decision-makers and ties, but the reading and meaning machinery for bid documents is not in scope canon. Capability Ladders (C2/C3 scope) may cover contracts under MONEY/obligations; A-PROCUREMENT should check.
6. **Purposeful reading absent.** Canon reads by *document type* (doctype readers, Skill 3) not by *why the document is held*; AI-proposed readings are "kept out of coverage" (System Design row 5) and the EXTRACT role is "NOT deployed in the fleet" (row 11). K1627's "reading profile" earned by a recurring kind has no canon antecedent in scope except the doctype registry.
7. **Interview/intake absent.** "a prompt entry, INTERPRET, the classifier and the wizard" ABSENT (System Design row 11). Roadmap §10 says "Human chooses focus" but nothing in scope guides a member from a story to objectives.
8. **Stale status sources.** System Design §3's state column is rendered from a retired file (`requirements/README.md`); the Roadmap §§1–12 header still says `node tools/decided.mjs` (tools retired, layers.md §Paths that are not product). Every built-claim must be re-verified against `build/` and code (M units).
9. **layers.md internal drift.** Status says "11 layers, 58 modules" but the table lists far more; layer 9's section describes `standards` as a layer-9 module while the table and `modules.json` put it in layer 5 (K1438); `hypotheses`, `explore`, `answers`, `following`, `people`, `money`, `duties`, `events` appear in the table with no descriptive row in layers.md. The A units should read their requirements in `build/requirements/` rather than rely on layers.md prose.
10. **DR12 vs an "engine".** An engine that drives an investigation risks becoming *required* or *gating* (DR12, DR14). The design must keep every step doable by hand and every machine output a proposal (D3–D6), and publish its method in human-readable form (DR4, DR12) — a skill.md-like description of how it plans and reads *(inference)*.
11. **Trust mark vs machine grading.** DEC-92 forbids a single trust score and machine "Flagged" marks; an engine's "confidence in an explanation" must not become a score (D9, D25). Competing explanations need per-leg grades, not a ranked probability *(inference)*.
12. **Assistant activity excluded from signals.** DEC-111's activity level is "computed from members' own work (never the assistant's)" — an engine running research for weeks must not inflate a group's "working on" signal *(inference)*.
13. **Cost is barely in canon.** Beyond AI run bounds and DR2's time budget, nothing in scope bounds what an investigation may spend on AI or on records-request fees across months. A-COST must build from `run-rules` and D-148.
14. **School-specific standards.** A district's schedule promise is a "public commitment" standard (layers.md `standards`), but who made it (board resolution, bond measure text, press release, community meeting) determines its weight; canon in scope does not grade *kinds* of commitment *(inference)*.
15. **Multi-jurisdiction bodies.** School districts are not city departments; the jurisdiction-profile rule (layers.md) and DEC-111's "entries are filed under the public body they examine" (DR10) imply profiles must model districts, bond oversight committees and state agencies (e.g. a state architect's approval) as bodies *(inference)*.
