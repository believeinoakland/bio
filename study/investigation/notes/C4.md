### 1.1 BIO_Intake_Doctrine_v1_1.md (ID) — how material enters the record

**What it is.** The governing doctrine for admission to the store: "the State Rules specification governs bundle shape; this doctrine governs admission to the store" (ID §0). Header v1.1, content at v1.2 (ID header "Incomplete sections"). Several sections are "draft position" (§1, §5), and §10 is explicitly open. Its actor model names a daemon on a retired substrate; "the rules stand, the actor is now the plane's scheduler" (ID Status line).

| question | what ID says | cite |
|---|---|---|
| Unit of evidence | The unit of admission is the **evidentiary series**, not the document: one Information bundle = one line of evidence (e.g. "the Sewer Service Fund transfer series"); attesting documents are captures inside it; a document attesting two lines is captured in each. Draft position. | ID §1 |
| What may be held | **Provenance, never relevance.** Any information with honest provenance is admissible; relevance "to a stated need is not an admission criterion"; the cost of irrelevant material is attention (criticality, navigation), not legality. Gathering requests constrain what the daemon fetches, not what the store contains. | ID §1a |
| What may not be held | Material unlawful to possess: discovery-then-action, by an authorized member, prompt recorded destruction via **expunge** (tombstone with fact, date, authority, reason class; never content). Lawful-but-confidential → redaction (§4a). Personal facts: "no import bulk-copies home addresses or phone numbers (K1485), and addresses and phone numbers are never published (K1493); otherwise any personal fact a cited document states may be held (K1455)." | ID §1a |
| The intake contract | Every submission carries: the capture (bytes, graded); per-document provenance (locator or "in hand" + custody notes; authority = issuer, free text; retrieved instant; capture method; capturing actor class daemon/session/member); classification **fact / analysis / judgment** and **crucial / supporting**; a received work product from another group is "analysis by rule, never fact"; the normalized dataset with content hash (hash over normalized dataset, or canonical text extraction). Lands as a gated Information bundle; "No intake path writes live state". | ID §2 |
| Material from outsiders (doorbell) | `op=knock`: anyone, no account, may hand material (≤8 MiB with evidence storage); writes only an inbox row; enters the record only when a signed-in member **pulls** it (admits at `collected`, no vouching). Source = "unnamed knocker" with KNOCK receipt; capturing actor = the pulling member. Optional knocker secret proves continuity (pseudonym), never identity. Note travels with capture as knocker's words "never as evidence of its truth"; contact never leaves inbox. DEC-78 (pulling) is RULED and NOT BUILT. DEC-108: inbox UI, lower limits (5/source, 10 total per 10 min), optional gatekeeper "labelled as machine work", one-week discard archive, count-only tally. | ID §2a; Incomplete sections |
| Source identity over time | A source's identity is a **history of disclosures**, dated and attributed; each capture keeps the source as it stood at receipt; five rules (group never first to make a source more public; hostile exposure recorded as the exposer's claim; "known to the group, not recorded" is first-class; consent asked at publishing; firmer identity reaches findings "as a re-evaluation notice, never a silent regrade"). | ID §2a (DEC-78 item 5) |
| Grades | Two orthogonal provenance axes: **source** (who issued, authority) and **capture chain** (how verifiably the group got its copy). The grade is on the chain axis only: "never the source's credibility and never the information's worth". A = WACZ/chain-of-custody (no route builds one; deferred, DEC-81 item 4); B = fetched bytes hashed at receipt (chat surface and fetch layer produce it); **co-attested B** (RFC 3161 timestamp + third-party co-archive) is sufficient to publish (DEC-81); C = reference only, cannot back a load-bearing claim in an external work product. Verified state requires ≥B on every attesting document; upgrades are accretive. | ID §3 |
| Ratification review | Ratifying crucial or contested material requires the reviewer to verify co-attestations, and "the review surface presents the source material itself, never only an AI summary". Capture failure recorded honestly as attempted and unavailable. | ID §3 (F4), §7 |
| Weight and corroboration | "weight is the same everywhere in this doctrine: source authority, times chain integrity, times corroboration." Corroboration is a **first-class provenance edge** between independent records attesting the same fact, carried in the reference graph "so evidentiary weight is readable from the store, not asserted in prose." | ID §3a |
| Member-original records | Recordings, room notes, documents in hand: chain of custody; a recording of a public meeting is primary evidence (Cal. Gov. Code 54953.5); notes are a witness account with testimonial weight. | ID §3a |
| Verifiability | Every trust primitive must have a non-BIO verification path (SHA-256, RFC 3161, WARC/WACZ, public co-archives, plain JSON, C2PA). Distribution container: BagIt in ZIP. | ID §3b, §3c |
| Fetching (standing intent) | "fetching is policy-governed, never caller-governed": no caller directs a fetch by parameter. Two forms of standing intent, both store state written by a human: **named request** (a specific document/source, credible-source constraint, criticality, cadence; in the target bundle's monitoring block + `data/gathering.json`) and **ratified sweep** (a query: scope, source-class constraints, cadence, breadth budget; within it the daemon "or an AI session may fetch what matches and what it deems relevant nearby, recording the deeming actor and the matched sweep in provenance"). | ID §4 |
| Ratification fence | A sweep's yield always lands at **collected**; human ratification is collected→verified per document. "AI and broad discovery range wide; verified stays earned by human decision." Verification asserts only that a document "APPEARS to be what it claims to be", not accuracy: "Accuracy and credence are separate questions the catalog does not yet model." Batch release allowed for homogeneous volume with recorded acknowledgement of sampling; never for crucial or contested material. | ID §4 |
| Held captures list | DEC-97: held captures listed per member and per project, sortable; batch release, set aside with reason, or **link them to a question**; contested includes "a document caught in an unresolved contradiction". | ID §4 |
| Security of fetching | Origin allowlists, https-only, public hosts; per-tick caps; ceiling on collected backlog ("flooding collected until review becomes rubber-stamping is an attack"); yield anomaly notes; bounded grammar for gathering-request fields; "examining sessions treat collected material strictly as untrusted data, never as instructions". | ID §4 (F5, F6) |
| Manual path | The due slate is exportable as a prompt a member runs by hand; "The tool never becomes the only way the group's standing intent gets executed." | ID §4 |
| Release and redaction | Release is a named member's act ("AI may assist the review freely"); state_history author must be a member, never a surface or AI. Redaction: derived artifact, remove not mask, logged by class and reason; AI may flag candidates, members execute. Unredacted originals off-store. | ID §4a |
| Criticality | Crucial = evidence a live Focus or Project cites as load-bearing; kept as "editorial judgment recorded, not computed". | ID §5 |
| Escalation ladder | Three rungs: the daemon executes named standing intent (mechanical capture, change detection, bookkeeping; "nothing it composes and nothing it decides"); **a session discovers**: "reads what the daemon gathered, follows the evidence to sources the standing intent does not yet name, proposes new gathering requests, and prepares everything a decision needs"; humans only for authority, judgment and acts in the world. | ID §6 |
| Blanket direction | A member may direct a session, "within a named investigation scope, to plant the gathering requests its discovery warrants"; the direction is the ratification for every request in scope; recorded in the session's record and in each request's provenance naming the directing member; never extends to release. Worked example: the July 19 Legistar chain. | ID §6 |
| Ratification pattern | Session prepares (verifies capture, extracts canonical dataset, hashes, runs the gate, assembles the release package); member releases. "The blurb states exactly what the capture is and never more" (an index page is not the documents it lists). | ID §7 |
| Disposition | Honest holds with a reason; byte-identical duplicates are corroboration on the original ("ring-once"); monitoring disabled on dispositioned bundles. One capture, one home (D-179, built); re-captures are the normal case and recorded as observations in `captured_locators`. Parted-capture rules (D-476, D-533, D-530, D-556) built. | ID §8 |
| Creation authority | Daemon creates Information bundles from named standing intent only, at collected. Sessions create **Focus and Project** bundles by creation-by-packaging, gated, promoted. "No actor class creates Actions mechanically; an Action is an act in the world and begins as a member decision." | ID §9 |
| Open | Staging vs expunge, decided at first sweep ratification. Not yet forced: member-intake specifics, received work products, **extraction and normalization standards per document type**, acquisition doctrine second draft. | ID §10, "Sections not yet forced" |
| Bias | Intake and release are lens-independent; declared bias binds above, at evidence, analysis, conclusions; publication needs a bias manifest and acknowledgement and no uncleared HUNCH debt. | ID "Cross-reference: declared bias" |

**Bearing on investigation.** ID already defines the intake half of an investigation engine: who may fetch what (standing intent, sweeps, blanket direction within "a named investigation scope"), the session's discovery role (follow evidence, propose requests, prepare decisions), the fence (collected until a member ratifies), the provenance a reading must carry, the grade/weight model and corroboration as edges. It is silent on reading a document with purpose: "extraction and normalization standards per document type" are an unforced section (ID "Sections not yet forced"), and accuracy/credence are explicitly "not yet model[led]" (ID §4). *(inference)* The phrase "named investigation scope" (ID §6) is the only place the doctrine names an investigation as a scope; it is not defined as a construct.

### 1.2 BIO_Action_v0_1.md (ACT) — the Action layer

**What it is.** Canon (whole), approved by Bob 2026-09-30; the level-1 home of the Action layer (build layer 9), gathering Functional Architecture v3 Layer 3, Design Requirements 7–8, State Rules §4.4, Case Making's action sections, Publication §6/§8 and DEC rulings (ACT Status). §6 lists canon edits applied "at a tranche boundary (K592)".

| question | what ACT says | cite |
|---|---|---|
| Span | From "the first suspicion that something is wrong or working, through planning, preparing, deciding and sending, to tracking the response and recording how it ended." Groups act differently (reporter, activist, lawyer, oversight body, administrator); may act on success as on breach. | ACT §1 |
| Contract | "An action rests on the record, and one asserting a breach rests on a published finding and a standard held in the record. The group plans and decides every act; the AI proposes and prepares, and never files or sends. Compliance is recorded as carefully as noncompliance. Every deadline names its basis." | ACT §2 |
| Constructs (each with home module) | **Standard** (`standards`): statute, regulation, ordinance, court order, adopted policy **or public commitment**, captured text with citation and period in force. **Determination** (`conformance`): member's judgment compliant / noncompliant / unclear per standard, resting on published findings. **Consequence** (`consequences`): what a breach did and to whom, causation its own finding. **Action plan** (`action-plans`): a project's working material for deciding what to do about matters "suspected or determined": options, dispositions, up to three scenarios with checkpoints. **Action** (`actions`): one outward engagement, kind, addressee, risk tier, clock (each deadline with basis), lifecycle, correspondence, outcome. **Filing and communication** (`filings`). **Escalation** (`escalation`): seven-stage protocol per noncompliant determination. | ACT §3 |
| Flow | "an inquiry opens a plan (suspected); publication and a determination make its matters determined; ... replies return as correspondence; ... Monitoring (layer 10) watches the actions' clocks and the escalations' triggers and tells members; the queue carries the reminders." | ACT §3 |
| Humans decide | Rule 1: machine may "find, compare, compute, propose and draft, always labelled as machine work", never declare a standard, determine, choose, approve, send, advance, resolve or close (DEC-24, DEC-27). | ACT §4 r1 |
| Gate at outward act | Rule 2 (DEC-26, option (c)): reasoning may rest on unestablished premises "shown as hunch debt"; a breach-asserting action is refused by default unless it rests on a live noncompliant determination; a member may override by attributed act with reason, disclosed. "An action that seeks evidence (a records request, a request for comment) is **never gated**." | ACT §4 r2, §8 |
| No scores | Rule 3: "No field holds significance, severity, priority or a score." Rule 10: judgment score appears only "as a signal in the hypothesis layer (K1473)". | ACT §4 r3, r10 |
| Deadlines & reminders | Rule 5: every deadline names its basis; reminders are the member's own request, overdue notifies once, nearing changes display only, no outside channel (DEC-10, 69, 70, 94; K613–K615). | ACT §4 r5 |
| Addressees | Rule 6: roles, never private individuals; page may show office holder on a date from the record. | ACT §4 r6 |
| Nothing leaves by system path | Rule 7: member sends by venue's own means, records the bytes sent with in-band stamp; a plan is never published (DEC-25). | ACT §4 r7 |
| No catalogue | Rule 8: suggested options come "from reasoning over the matter and from the group's own earlier plans, never from a fixed list"; no costs, assignees, hours. | ACT §4 r8 |
| Doctrine limits | Rule 9: no position on what policy should be; a group with a stake discloses it (D6). | ACT §4 r9 |
| Kind of work | Rule 10: a project may declare its kind of work (reporting, fixing, legal, oversight, other), shaping only what the assistant suggests; no member attribute gates/filters/orders anything (DEC-17, DEC-54); people in the record searchable by any held fact (K1484, K1471). | ACT §4 r10 |
| Jurisdiction | Rule 11: kinds, venues, templates, offices, deadlines come from a jurisdiction profile; "a missing fact reads undetermined, never a default". | ACT §4 r11 |
| Opposition | Rule 12: good faith presumed, bad actor identified by evidence never by role; plans checked for a hostile-response branch; pressure recorded as evidence and "can open an inquiry of its own". | ACT §4 r12 |
| Evidence standard | Rule 13: venue sets the standard; no action refused for grade; filings show each exhibit's grade; FRE 902(13)–(14) accepts co-attested B. | ACT §4 r13 |
| Outcome vocabularies | resolution (complied, denied, escalated, withdrawn, completed); correspondence outcome (granted, denied, partial, reversed, affirmed, none stated); escalation evaluation (complied, partial, denied, none). | ACT §5 row 7 |
| Clock | Stored entries with basis; overdue derived at read; one machine write = pending→overdue; a records request's next-stage due date lives on its correspondence entry. | ACT §5 row 4 |
| Edges | `action_basis`, `responds_to`, `references` added to the closed edge vocabulary. | ACT §5 row 8, §6 |
| Joint action | Deferred; membership never crosses a group boundary. | ACT §7 item 6, §8 |
| "Matters" | Member word for what a plan addresses (DEC-114); internal term "subject". | ACT §7 item 7 |

**Bearing on investigation.** Bob's school scenario is ACT-shaped at both ends: the promise "no longer than 1 school year" is a **Standard** of kind "public commitment" (ACT §3), and a records request or request for comment is an evidence-seeking **Action**, never gated (ACT §4 r2), with clocks whose deadlines name their basis (r5) and replies returning as correspondence (§3). An inquiry "opens a plan (suspected)" (§3), so the action plan already holds suspected matters before any finding. *(inference)* The investigation engine sits upstream of ACT: it produces the findings and determinations ACT consumes and the records requests ACT tracks; it must not perform ACT's member acts.

### 1.3 BIO_Membership_Architecture_v2.md (MEM) §§1–7 — members, roles, capabilities, projects

**What it is.** v2.0 (July 26, 2026), "specified by Bob Krause in session", the construct-1 home (membership and authority); "Membership adds accountability and access control. It does not add integrity" (MEM preamble, §2). Its Status line records a long series of built items (REC-/D- numbers) through 2026-10-01.

**Roles and positions.** There are three instance-level roles and, per project, three positions:

| role / position | what it may do | cite |
|---|---|---|
| Founder (solo participant) | is the administrator; membership apparatus invisible until a second person (DR 2) | MEM §4.1 |
| Administrator | custodial powers over **membership only**: add (invite), deactivate/reactivate member, set capabilities, confirm/withdraw expertise, approve signing keys; holds every working capability; floor of two; irrevocable except by §4.7 vote (consensus to add, majority-with-target-in-denominator to remove). **Sees every project; directs nothing** ("Sight and authority are separated here on purpose") | MEM §4.2–4.9, §5 |
| Root of trust (ADMIN_TOKEN / hosting account holders) | can take over the instance; not bounded by membership; `governorconfig` (a host's fetch appetite) is the operator's, not an administrator's | MEM §4.6, §4.8, §4.9 (BOB #23) |
| Member | holds capabilities set by an administrator | MEM §5 |
| Machine credential | "has no member behind it and therefore holds no capabilities"; bounded by the op table and confinement rules; creates ownerless projects; an `ai` credential "sees no more than its principal" | MEM §5, §7.1, §7.9 (BOB #16) |
| Project owner | sole initial owner = creator; invites (only owners), removes participants, deactivates/reactivates, sets discoverable/hidden, answers join requests, adopts bias set; ownership a set (floor 1; consensus to add; majority removal; at 2 both agree) | MEM §7 opening, §7.1, §7.2, §7.7, §7.10, §7.11, §7.14; REC-134 |
| Joined participant | working rights per capabilities: revise the project's document, cite/sever edges, stance, conclusion, feed judgements; deliver a case ratification; fork (with create-projects) | MEM §7.5, §7.9 (REC-134, REC-137), §7.12 |
| Invited, not joined | view rights to the **skeleton** only: the Focuses it stands above, the Information it cites, the Actions it initiates; not content, analysis record, work product, evaluations, session log, participants | MEM §7.5, §7.9 |
| Uninvited | hidden project: nothing, "not its existence"; discoverable project: existence and name, and may request to join | MEM §7.9, §7.14 |

**Capabilities** (MEM §5): `contribute` (create and revise bundles in the working corpus), `publish` (ratify; also needs a registered signing key: "The capability governs the surface; the key governs the authority"), `create projects`, `administer`. "A capability a member does not hold is absent from their interface, not present and refused." "Capabilities gate a session, not a credential." Expertise (MEM §1.3) is declared by the member and confirmed by an administrator, "operational routing information: who should look at a franchise-fee question, who can read an ACFR, who is qualified to judge a Brown Act claim"; "Confirmation gates nothing."

**Attribution** (MEM §1.2, §3): consequential acts are attributed to a handle (author of a promotion, attestor of a ratification, capturing member of hand-carried material, never its source). Cover (admin-assigned label, not a legal name) and handle (member-chosen) are paired only for administrators.

**Projects** (MEM §7):
- A project is a State Rules §4.3 record object: "a bundle with an objective, an analysis record, a work-product readiness ladder, recorded evaluations, and a lifecycle of forming, investigating, matured, closed"; MEM adds participation only (§7 opening). Deactivation = `closed` / `abandoned`; reactivation = `closed`→`investigating`; also `resolved`, `superseded` (§7.11). (Verified: `build/requirements/project-stage.md` R2, R4 derive the four stages; `investigating` is reached by `held_question_with_leg`.)
- **Containment** (§7.9): Information refers to nothing above it; a Focus (now **inquiry**, per the rename noted in the Status line) `cites` Information; a Project stands above Focuses (`elevated_into` on the Focus) and `cites` Information directly; a Project `initiates` Actions. "Nothing in this hierarchy is exclusive."
- **The evidence corpus stays shared; participation scopes "the group's thinking"**: "where an argument has got to, what has been ruled out, what is being prepared. That is the material with strategic and tactical value before publication." (§7.9). Derived reverse edges into projects "MUST be filtered by the viewer's position" (§7.9).
- **A project does not own a line of inquiry** (Bob, 2026-09-18): "Anybody can ask a question related to anything ... A project doesn't own an area of enquiry to the exclusion of others." A run over an inquiry consults no project; a run over a project keeps the joined-participant gate (§7.9, DEC-63 amended; built REC-145).
- **AI runs** (§7.9, BOB #16): tick and close are the run's **principal**'s acts; "A run nobody drives ends by its own lease and bounds"; run context is the closed vocabulary `RUN_CONTEXTS` (`inquiry`, `project`); a machine credential sees no more than its principal. A hidden project's run output is "the PROJECT'S THINKING until something outside uses it; the bytes stay shared, only the run's attribution is withheld" (BOB #32; built D-486).
- **Leads** are readable by their author and participants they were shared to, "never by administrators" (§7.9, citing `MEMBER-KNOWLEDGE-DESIGN.md` §5).
- **Case ratification**: signatures must include an owner of the publishing project; delivery by a joined participant or the founder (§7.9; built REC-137). `op=ratify` refuses a project bundle outright (REC-140).
- **Fork** (§7.12): joined participants with create-projects; clone records `derived_from`; carries no participants; "Fork is the remedy available to participants when they disagree with where a project is going".
- **Discoverable or hidden** (§7.14): owner's recorded act; existing projects read HIDDEN; creator chooses with neither preselected; directory paged with published bound; request to join (grant = invited, never joined). Steps 1–3 built; step 4 surfaces NOT BUILT (MEM Incomplete sections).
- **Ids of gated objects are opaque** (`PROJ`, `CASE`, `DRAFT`, `RVG`, `TASK`): "a COUNT is a disclosure of existence" (§7.9, BOB #16, #17; built REC-141, REC-151, D-432).

**Bearing on investigation.** *(inference)* MEM gives an investigation two candidate homes and a sharp rule between them: the **inquiry** (a question, shared, anyone may ask and run on it) and the **project** (participation-scoped "thinking", with owners, an objective, a lifecycle whose second stage is literally `investigating`). Its privacy line falls exactly where an investigation's working state would fall: evidence gathered is shared; the plan, what has been ruled out and what is being prepared are the project's. Roles map to member-in-the-loop acceptance: who may accept a machine proposal on a project is a joined participant (for revisions) or an owner (for scope-like acts such as bias adoption and visibility); administrators cannot. Expertise is routing information only, so "who should read this ACFR" may be suggested but never gate.

## 4. Gaps and tensions

1. **"Investigation" is named but not a construct.** ID §6 speaks of "a named investigation scope" for blanket direction, and MEM §7's project lifecycle has a stage `investigating`, but no source in scope defines an investigation object, its scope record, or where a blanket direction is stored beyond "the session's record and ... each planted request's provenance" (ID §6). Blanket direction has no module requirement (§3 above). *(inference)* An investigation engine needs exactly this scope record; it should be the place a blanket direction lives.
2. **Two homes for the investigation, with different privacy.** The inquiry is shared and "a project doesn't own an area of enquiry" (MEM §7.9); the project scopes "the group's thinking" (MEM §7.9). An investigation's plan, rejected explanations and next steps are "thinking" and would leak if held on a shared inquiry *(inference)*. Conversely, a plan held only in a project cannot be run by a non-participant on the same question. A design must say which parts live where; MEM gives the rule (evidence shared, thinking scoped) but not the split for investigation artefacts.
3. **Sessions may create Focus/Project bundles by packaging** (ID §9), but a machine-created project is ownerless (MEM §7.1) and hidden (MEM §7.14 (b)), and only an owner can invite, set visibility or adopt bias. *(inference)* So an engine must not create the project; the member does, and the engine proposes into it. Consistent with ACT §4 r1 and ID §6, but worth stating as a design constraint.
4. **Run lifecycle vs an investigation's duration.** A run is driven only by its principal and "ends by its own lease and bounds" (MEM §7.9). An investigation runs weeks or months (RESUME.md). *(inference)* The engine must be many bounded runs over a persistent home, not one long run; and since tick/close are the principal's, co-investigators cannot drive each other's runs.
5. **Accuracy is unmodelled at intake.** ID §4: "Accuracy and credence are separate questions the catalog does not yet model." An investigation's core work (is the status report true? does the invoice match the contract?) is credence work; it must sit in the inquiry/hypothesis/strength layers, not at verification. *(inference)*
6. **Extraction standards per document type are unforced** (ID "Sections not yet forced"), and ID's only reading rule is the blurb rule (ID §7). Bob's "reading with purpose" has no doctrine home in this scope.
7. **Discovery reach vs "never caller-governed".** ID §4 allows an AI session within a ratified sweep to fetch "what it deems relevant nearby", and ID §6 lets a session "follow the evidence". *(inference)* An engine following leads (bid documents → contractor history → litigation) needs either a sweep or a blanket direction covering that breadth before it fetches; the doctrine permits this but the member must ratify scope first. Origin allowlists (ID §4 F5/F6) may block court or contractor-licence sites not on the list.
8. **Backlog ceiling vs investigation yield.** ID §4 makes the collected-backlog ceiling a security control. *(inference)* A vigorous investigation will fill collected fast; the held-captures list (DEC-97) and batch release (never for crucial/contested) are the relief valves, and crucial material requires per-document co-attestation checks, a real member cost.
9. **Review must show source, not summary** (ID §3, §7) — an engine that summarises documents for members must always pair the summary with the source at the moment of acceptance.
10. **Doctrine vs canon header staleness.** ID's header says DEC-78 pulling is NOT BUILT; `capture` R65 states `pullKnock` with no not-yet-met mark (§3 above). One of the two is out of date; a design should trust the module and confirm at code.
11. **No scores (ACT §4 r3) vs prioritising leads.** An investigation planner ranks leads and hypotheses. ACT forbids significance/priority fields in the Action layer and admits a judgment score only "as a signal in the hypothesis layer (K1473)". *(inference)* Any lead ranking must be a hypothesis-layer signal or an order (cf. `op=search` publishes "an ORDER, never a score", MEM Status line, D-447), not a stored priority.
12. **The promise as a Standard.** Bob's "no longer than 1 school year" is a public commitment, a Standard kind (ACT §3; `standards` R-intro). ACT requires a standard "held as captured text with its citation and period in force". *(inference)* If the promise was oral (a meeting), its capture is a member-original record (ID §3a) or a meeting recording; the engine must find or create that record before a determination is possible.
13. **Evidence-seeking actions are ungated** (ACT §4 r2) but "nothing leaves by a system path" (r7): the engine may draft a records request, a member sends it, and the reply returns as correspondence with a basis-named clock (ACT §3, §5 row 4). This is a ready loop for an investigation's records requests; jurisdiction profiles supply deadlines, and missing ones read undetermined (r11).
14. **Expertise routing exists but is inert.** MEM §1.3 names routing ("who can read an ACFR") as the purpose of declared expertise, and §5 says it gates nothing. ACT §4 r10 forbids member attributes to "gate, filter or order anything". *(inference)* An engine may suggest "a member who declared CPA might read this" only if that suggestion does not order or filter members — a tension worth a ruling.
15. **Administrators cannot see leads** (MEM §7.9) and direct nothing. *(inference)* Investigation leads held as member knowledge are private to author and sharees; an engine's proposals stored as leads would inherit that privacy.
16. **Joint investigations across groups are out of scope**: membership never crosses a group boundary (MEM §2; ACT §8). Received work products from another group enter as analysis, never fact (ID §2).
