## 0. The answer in one page (the C3 focus)

**How a document's structure and meaning are represented.** There are four layers, and each one is a dated opinion over immutable bytes (framework §3):
- CAPTURE: the bytes, their hash, the locator, the authority, the instant, and a grade.
- PROFILE: what the capture IS. Recognisers produce it, one judgment per axis (stack, content type, and later format, authority class and access mode), each with a confidence of `certain`, `likely`, `possible` or `none` (§4).
- READING: what the capture CONTAINS. This means entities with stable keys and named facts, plus facts about the document as a whole. A content type produces it (§7).
- OBSERVATION: what HAPPENED, meaning a change, a confirmation or a connection (§3, §6).

Structure is represented three ways:
- **Regions**: evidentiary, presentational and mechanical, each with its own digest (§5).
- **Extents**: the address of a part inside a container. Part II names the kinds as `pdf-page`, `sheet-cell`, `sheet-range`, `doc-para`, `doc-table`, `slide-shape`, `image`, and `dom`, which is refused until it has a producer (§14.2, §15).
- **Position**: where a reference was read. This was added by FW-17 (§14.4–14.5).

Meaning is carried by entities resolved ACROSS documents, never extracted from one (§3). The other carriers are graded connections (§8, §8.1), progressions (§8.2), identifier spaces (§8.3) and themes (§8.4). In Part II, CONTENT is the unit a finding points at. It is content-addressed as `hash(capture_sha, canonical extent, chain)` (§14.2, front matter).

**What an AI or a member may propose about a document, and how it is accepted.** Invariant 8 governs: *"Derived things inform; authored acts bind"* (§2). Under it:
- A machine may PROPOSE a connection (grade C correspondence), a theme placement (a "hunch", grade C, never membership: §8.4), a content row (`op=contentmint`, carrying a mint label: §14.4–14.5), a mention in a connection's machine pair (`chosen: false`: §14.5), and narrowing proposals "labelled as machine work" (REC-86).
- Each of these becomes binding only through a member's attributed act. The acts are `op=themeplace`, `op=connectionchoose`, NARROW, TRANSCRIBE with a second member's attest, and a member's leg citing a machine-minted row.
- A finding is "a PROPOSAL, never an assertion"; adoption is authored and dated, and a deferral records its reason (§12, discovery loop).
- *"An assistant may propose at any point in the loop and adopt at none of them"* (§12).
- The AI EXTRACT role is **half built**: the plane's door exists, but no assistant calls it (front matter, Incomplete §14.5).

**Whether reading can be guided by purpose.** The framework has two directions (§1.1). Bottom-up runs from what sources publish: bytes, then capture, then reading, then entities, then progressions, then findings. Top-down runs from a member's goal. They meet at an objective's SATISFACTION CONDITION, written in the framework's own vocabulary so that progress is computed rather than reported (§12). Invariant 7 limits purpose: *"A goal may direct what is SOUGHT. It must never filter what is recorded, retained, or shown"* (§2). §13.1 adds that bias "never shapes what is captured or monitored".

So purpose may legitimately direct WHICH documents are sought, and WHICH stages and junctions are checked. In the framework, how a document is read comes from what the document IS, through its profile and content type, not from WHY it was fetched. Nothing in the framework models "why this document is in the file" (absent). The nearest constructs (inference):
- the progression stage a document fills (§8.2 `typical content`, e.g. RFP at stage 4, signed agreement at stage 8);
- the content-type recogniser that picks the reader (§4, §7);
- the satisfaction condition that names entity, progression and stages (§12).

The intent layer's objects (aspiration, goal, satisfaction condition, pursuit record) are a **stated deferral**: designed at concept level, absent in code, with a trigger (Incomplete §12).

## 3. How the framework answers the C3 focus questions, in more detail

**(a) Representing structure and meaning.** The representation is layered and attributed. Structure comes in two kinds:
- **Physical**, built: page and rect, sheet cell and range, paragraph, table, shape, image, plus a document's regions and digests (§5, §15).
- **Semantic**: a reading's entities with keys and named facts (§7), resolved to registry entities with graded resolutions (§3, §8.1). These thread progressions (§8.2) and are matched across identifier spaces (§8.3).

A document's kind is a PROFILE judgment by a recogniser with a confidence (§4). Because the registry is first-certain-wins, one document has one kind, with an additive multi-class pass beside it (§16, FW-18). For extraction method and fidelity, the CHAIN is the authority, per page (§16, D-284). What is **absent** is a document's LOGICAL structure: an outline or TOC, sections and headings, defined terms, an invoice's line items as rows, a contract's clauses and schedule of values. Two things come closest:
- the staff-report, minutes and ordinance readers' named facts (§16);
- the evidentiary envelope of office files (§16).

Positional text (REC-206) would give tier-1 units a page and a rect, which is a prerequisite for deriving any section structure from a PDF (inference). It is designed and not built.

**(b) Proposal and acceptance.** The canon's pattern is uniform across every place it appears (§14.4, §8.4, §14.5, §18.1, §12):

| Machine may propose | Labelled as | Member's binding act | Built? |
|---|---|---|---|
| A passage worth citing (EXTRACT) | Mint label, machine work | A member's leg cites the row | Plane built; no assistant caller (absent) |
| A connection | Its earned grade A/B/C, `asserted_by` system | Resolution testimony (grade D) or confirming a C | Built (§8.1, §15 entity axis) |
| Which mention is on point | `determining_pair`, `chosen: false` | `op=connectionchoose` | Built, plane and surface |
| Narrower legs | `op=narrowcandidates`, machine work | `op=narrow` (new basis version, old kept) | Plane built; UI not |
| Theme membership | Hunch, grade C | `op=themeplace` | Built |
| Passage carried into a newer version | Graded notice, never stored | ADOPT or KEEP | Notice built; acts specified (REC-223) |
| A finding from junction checks or monitoring | Proposal with grade and basis | Adopt, make a focus or problem, or defer with reason (`proposal_dispositions`) | Built for derived findings (REC-6/7) |
| A decomposition, a records request, an assembled progression | Proposal | Adoption, authored and dated | Specified (§12); the intent objects are deferred |
| Machine-read text | Chain step, cap | Attestation or second-member transcription attest (up to B) | Built at the plane |

Nothing an AI reads ever binds: attestation is refused to machines, and fidelity rises only through people.

**(c) Purpose-guided reading.** The canon guides reading by **kind**, not by purpose. The content-type recogniser selects the reader (§4, §7), and readers are written only from measured pages (inv. 4). Purpose enters at three points:
- A satisfaction condition names the entity, the progression and the required stages, which defines what to SEEK and which junctions to check (§12).
- *"Specificity of reference is worked for"*: on-point passages are chosen relative to "the point being made at the referring end" (§14.4). This is the only place the canon ties a passage's selection to its use.
- The four-level search obligates saying which level was searched (§14.3).

Invariant 7 forbids purpose from filtering what is recorded or shown. The framework never says WHY a document is in a file. The nearest is a stage in a progression instance, placed by threading an entity (§8.2). A purpose-guided reader would therefore need a new construct (inference). Its doctrine-safe shape: purpose may choose WHAT to extract and in what order, never what is kept or shown, and the AI's extraction is a labelled `ai(function)` chain step that a member must cite.

## 4. Gaps and tensions

1. **No construction-execution progression, no promise object.** §8.2's procurement table ends at `amendment`, and junction checks stop at amounts and payments past the term. Bob's case is about a **promised duration** ("no longer than 1 school year") against status reports. The canon has `within` (one interval between stages) and temporal "absence with a due date" (§8). It has no promise or commitment whose source is a statement in a document, and no schedule-slip measure (absent; inference). The out-of-order stage shape needs each document's OWN date and is deferred (§8.2).
2. **Chains, not graphs.** §11 bends: *"the first real procurement case will probably need the graph"* (one bond measure funding many schools; one contract amended into a different scope).
3. **Documents can be several kinds; first-certain-wins.** §16 measured 52 of 600 documents as multi-class. Investigation files are dominated by compound documents (board packets holding staff reports, resolutions, bid tabulations and contracts). The canon's answer is an additive pass beside the engine, not a split of one document into typed parts (inference).
4. **Only five content-type readers, all for city-council materials.** There is none for RFPs, bids, contracts, change orders, invoices, payment applications, schedules or status reports (§16). Inv. 4 requires each reader to be written from a measured page, so adding readers is per kind, and costly in measurement.
5. **No logical-structure extent.** Addresses are physical. PDF table extraction was measured NO-GO (M-55), and positional text is unbuilt (REC-206). Reading an RFP "by its TOC" has no representation (absent).
6. **AI EXTRACT has a door and no caller.** The `ai(function)` chain step is designed and unemitted, and `ASSISTANT-PILOT.md` §5 excludes EXTRACT from the pilot by name (§14.5, §15). The canon's machinery for an AI reading a document with purpose is the least built part.
7. **The member's lead (D-194) and firsthand observation (D-184) are unhomed** (§17, §18 piece 5). An investigation is mostly leads.
8. **Intent objects are deferred.** Aspiration, goal, satisfaction condition and pursuit record are concept-only. Their trigger is a member walking a question to a published case from the surface (Incomplete §12). An investigation engine's "objective" and "running log of what we tried" would either trigger this design act or need it (inference). Note the vocabulary clause: satisfaction conditions are scoped to values the record holds, with *"an amount or a fund only once the budget-or-dataset type exists"* (Incomplete §12).
9. **Content-grain search and the general observation log are designed, not (per this document) built.** *"which passages mention X"* is unaskable (§17, §18 pieces 2–3). Build state must be checked by the module units: the front matter defers to `status.mjs`.
10. **Identifier spaces are measured for the City of Oakland only.** The school district's systems (a separate procurement portal and board-docs system) would start at grade C (§8.3; inference). Contract/PO numbers are unpublished at source in Oakland.
11. **Pairwise connection bound.** 32 documents per entity at the default (§8). A multi-year capital project with monthly reports can exceed it; the model reopens at that point.
12. **Tension: K1481 against §12's unattended surfacing.** The §12 rules on volume (aggregate, age) were written for unattended assistants and are now fenced. An investigation engine that "starts digging" after the member's opening words is consistent with K1481 only as a run started by a member's act, or as a member-authored standing question (inference).
13. **Tension: "purpose guides reading" against inv. 7 and §13.1.** Purpose may direct seeking. It may not filter recording or showing, and bias may not shape capture. A planner that reads only what it needs must still record what it looked at and found (the four-level search obligation, §14.3).
14. **Run-suggested legs are not pinned** (D-579): a run's `op=suggest` version legs name only a bundle (§18.1). AI-suggested citations in an investigation would lack a version pin.
15. **Self-consistency notes.** The changelog calls "derived things inform" invariant 9, while the list numbers it 8 (§2 against framework:133–138). §3 says "Nothing in this document models a claim yet", which is overtaken by the claim-as-field design (§18 item 6). The front matter's Incomplete §14.5 bullet repeats one paragraph three times (framework:9). Part I is frozen, so stale statements are corrected only in the front matter.
