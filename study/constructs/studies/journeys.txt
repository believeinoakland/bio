Kinds of group
Groups differ as much as members do. Your examples, 4 October:
ProfessionalA newsroom or journalist, a law firm, an inside or outside auditor.
Issue-specificLibraries, labor, a ballot measure, schools.
Neighbourhood or communityA neighbourhood, a business community, parents.
Catch-allA whole city, a single agency, a county.
An inside auditor is worth noting: it means a public body itself may run a copy. The design must not assume the group always stands outside government. A group says which kind it is in journey 2.
2. The wide path
Your words, 3 October: the path to success must be "wide enough and inclusive enough for all audiences". Proposed as eight rules every journey is checked against.
A front door in every journeyEach journey has a first step a newcomer can take alone, with no prior knowledge, and the screen shows where it starts.
Wizards on the main roads, never gatesThe main journeys can be walked with a wizard. Nothing is locked behind one, and anyone can leave it at any step: stopping is a non-event.
You can always see where you areEvery journey shows its stage and what comes next, so a newcomer never wonders whether they are done.
Explained where it appears, and findable againA term is explained the first time a member meets it, at that spot, in plain words. After that it is never pushed again, but the explanation is always one hover, focus or tap away on the term itself, and in one list of every term, for when a member forgets.
Heavier steps slow you downFriction rises with an act's weight, so no one stumbles into anything permanent, signed or outward.
Any order, and pick up where you left offSteps can be taken in whatever order the work allows; only a step that truly needs another first waits for it. A member can leave a journey for other work at any point, and come back at any time to find it exactly as they left it, with their unfinished work listed where they will see it.
Many front doorsA member can start from whatever drew them in: most often a problem they live with, or else a code section, a court case, a news story, a budget, a meeting, something they experienced, a tip. Civicsmith offers a sensible first step from each, and no journey assumes everyone starts from a question.
Fast for those who knowProfessionals and experienced members move quickly: search, keyboard, acting on many items at once, no repeated teaching.
3. Where members start
Most people come to this work because something in their own lives isn't working: the street outside, the block they live on, the bill they pay, the services they were promised. Others come through something they read: a code chapter, a court case, a news story. Each can be a member's first motivating interest, and each needs a sensible first step. No journey should assume everyone starts from a question.
A problem they live with
The most common way in. At the city auditor's open forum you attended (4 October), these were the reasons people gave most often for coming. Each is a frustration; Civicsmith's job is to help turn it into a question about the city's own standards that the record can answer.

PotholesThe streets in their neighbourhood go unrepaired.Find the city's claim or target ("90% of reported potholes filled") and its own records of every report. Members' photos are a sample that tests those records, not a count of every pothole.Do the city's own records support its claim, and do members' spot-checks show that "closed" means "repaired"? (journey 6)
Illegal dumpingDumped furniture and trash keep reappearing on their block.Find the city's cleanup target and its records of reports; members record sightings on their own blocks with place, date and photo, a sample that tests the records.Are reports cleared as the city says, and do the same places keep coming back? (journey 6)
A franchise the city holdsThe city's franchise with its waste hauler.Capture the agreement and declare its service levels, rates and fees as standards.Is the hauler delivering what the franchise requires, and is the city enforcing it? (journeys 13 and 8).
Police overtimeOvertime costs that keep running past the budget.Capture the adopted budget, the actual spending, and any overtime policy or audit.How do the actuals compare with what was budgeted and what policy allows? An accountant member checks (journeys 8 and 7).

Civicsmith checks the city's claims against its records; campaigning for or against a measure is outside what a group's record prepares (journeys 7 and 16).

What this means for the design:
Frustration is welcome and the record stays disciplined: a member's own notes can say how angry they are; the question, the evidence and the case say what the documents show.
Civicsmith holds the city to its own laws, standards and statements. It takes no side on what policy should be, including on a ballot measure, while still checking the city's claims about one.
Every kind of document is captured, pointed at and cited the same way: a code section, a court filing, an article, a budget, minutes, a contract, a dataset.
The group's home asks "What brought you here?" and every empty project offers "Start from…", with a problem the member lives with first and these documents after; none is the "right" way in.
The assistant can suggest first questions from whatever the member starts with, such as one question per requirement in a code section, each labelled as a suggestion.
Professionals bring their own angles: lawyers start from filings, accountants from financial reports, policy analysts from codes and rates. Their journey (8) routes questions to them.
4. Seventeen journeys
In the order people meet them, from a group's first day to a stranger reading its case. Each card says who takes the journey, its newcomer's front door, its wizard, and what changed from the list gathered earlier. Open "Steps" for the path: steps are listed in their usual order, but members may take them in any order the work allows, leave, and come back later. Marked steps are the heavy ones: permanent, signed, outward (the world sees it), reasoned, terminal or irreversible.

5. Wizards
Outlines only: what each wizard covers, where it starts, and its steps. A step may offer an optional side trip into another wizard; when that wizard finishes, or is stopped, the member returns to the step they left. Any wizard reachable as a side trip can also be run on its own later. Each step says what to do and why; a wizard points at the real control and never presses it; what it puts in a field is a labelled draft until the member keeps it; stopping is fine. The three required wizards must always work, or the release is held.

J8. Invitation links for the group's website
Your direction, 4 October: requests to join belong to the group's own website, which controls who may apply; Civicsmith only provides a way for that website to obtain an invitation link for an approved person, leading into the joining journey. The cover name an invitation needs comes with the website's request, and an administrator can change it. This is a new capability, so it goes to the development process as a requirement.
Answer: "J8: yes", or your changes.
J9. One administrator is enough
The membership rules now require two administrators before a group can add ordinary members, and stop an administrator resigning when only two remain. Draft ruling: a group may run with one administrator; members can be added from the start; Civicsmith recommends a second administrator once, at setup, saying why, and never again; only the last administrator is stopped from resigning. Votes to remove an administrator still need enough administrators to vote, as today. This changes the membership rules, so it goes to the development process.
Answer: "J9: yes", or your changes.
J10. Keep a list of gaps
Keep section 6 on this page and carry it into each later step. Each gap stays listed until it closes or you set it aside, so the journeys never promise what Civicsmith can't yet do. Gaps waiting on the capability question come back to you with the development process's answer.
Answer: "J10: yes", or "J10: no".
J11. Where a problem starts
Journey 4 begins with a member describing a problem in their own words, saying exactly how they feel. Today the only place for that is a firsthand observation, which the project's members can see. Draft ruling: a member may keep notes that only they can see, never cited and never published, and turn a note into an observation or a question when they choose. The alternative is to start from an observation the group sees from the outset, which keeps everything in the shared record but asks members to write for the group from their first words.
Answer: "J11: private notes", or "J11: observations only".
