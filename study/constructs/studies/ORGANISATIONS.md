# Study: ORGANISATIONS and OBLIGATIONS (phase 2, BOB #110, 2026-10-05)

Analyst: ORGANISATIONS. Read whole: `constructs-brief.md`, `ANALYSIS-PROTOCOL.md`, `digest/ORGANISATIONS.md` (959 lines), `digest/DOCTRINE-REGISTER.md` (2,814), `digest/CROSS-REGISTER.md` (1,049), the `## Modules` sections of `notes/M1.md`–`M5.md`; primary sources and URLs in §9. Citations: reader note and its source line (e.g. "C1: RM §1 L219"), register entries `D<n>` / `X<n>`, requirement ids, code `file:line`.

**The construct.** Bodies of every kind (governments, departments, bureaus, boards, courts as bodies, special districts, joint powers authorities, contractors and vendors acting for government, unions, legal organisations, other watchdog groups); offices (posts) versus the people holding them over time; the lines between them (part of, reports to, oversees, appoints, funds, contracts with / acts for, responsible for, holds the records of); change over time (renames, mergers, re-organisations); and **obligations**: who owes what to whom, by when, under what authority. The group's own internal organisation (members, administrators, projects, owners) is fully modelled and covered (`membership` R1–R91; UC-135–146, D1) and is out of scope except where noted.

Real-world practice (§9): watchdogs and journalists map "who is affected and who is interested", bureaucrats as well as elected officials (GIJN); investigative data models carry positions, occupancies and dated relationships as first-class objects (FollowTheMoney `Position`/`Occupancy`/`Directorship`/`ContractAward`; LittleSis's twelve dated relationship categories; OpenSanctions' Position–Occupancy–Person model); civic-tech obligation registers state "who must act, what they must produce, by when, and how often" (NYC Legislation Implementation Tracker: 8,185 obligations from 2,138 local laws). Failure modes seen in practice: name collisions read as identity (Aleph treats matches as "leads to check", PS §2 l.73, C10); stale structure after re-organisation; missing end dates (OpenSanctions stops treating an occupancy with no end date as current once it began over 40 years ago); local coverage absent from national datasets (OpenSanctions covers municipal posts only "where we have reliable, maintained sources"; Google's representatives API was turned down on 30 April 2025).

## 2. Levels of support

| level | what a member can do |
|---|---|
| **L0** Free text | Name a body or office only as a string (a capture's `authority`, an action's `counterparty`). |
| **L1** Registry | Register bodies, offices, people in public roles, funds, contracts with aliases; resolve documents to them with a grade; see what concerns a subject; declare constitutive relations for bias; address actions to profile offices by role and body. |
| **L2** Structure as of a date | Record and read evidenced, dated, graded lines (part of, reports to, oversees, appoints, funds, contracts with / acts for, responsible for, holds records of) and who held each office when; read the structure "as of" any date; record renames and mergers; link actors and addressees to the registry; identifiers from source systems. |
| **L3** Obligations held | Hold who owes what to whom, by when, under what authority: duties, prohibitions and powers, each sourced to a provision, order, contract clause or commitment in force, versioned; see an office's duties and powers. |
| **L4** Declared versus observed | The record derives each obligation's occurrences and their state (met, met late, overdue, pending, discharged, undetermined, with why); findings of the delta reach members with their derivation; an act is checked against the actor's powers; accountability chains are walked as of a date; obligations feed determinations, escalation targets and questions; patterns with denominators. |
| **L5** Network-scale civic model | Structure and obligation registers shared between groups as recreatable, signed material; standard identifiers (OCD-IDs, org-id schemes) and exports (Popolo, OCDS milestones); cross-group coordination keyed on bodies. |

Lowest level serving each need: **L1**: A1, D4 (index key only). **L2**: A2, A3, A4, A5, A6, B1, B2, B3, B4, B6, D1, D2, D3, D5 (agency's level and parent). **L3**: C1, C4, C5, C7, C8, C9 (by construction). **L4**: B5, C2, C3, C6, E1, E2. **L5**: E3 (disclosure across groups) and the network form of D4.

**Placement on the ladder.** *Built:* L1 complete; L2 absent apart from constitutive `member_of` (undated, ungraded, untraversable) and flat profile offices; L3/L4 present only as a fragment: `progressions` holds the declared flow of generic patterns (minutes after a meeting) with missing/overdue findings, but no obligor, no beneficiary, no source in force and no institution scope. *Usable by a member today:* L1 (Subjects screen, without correction or defect acts) plus the progressions screen. Every place where the product names a body for action (`{role, body}` in layer 9) is text, unconnected to the registry, and unreachable from the interface. The structural observation holds for this construct in a specific form: the registry itself sits low (layer 5, read by inquiry, contradiction and intent), but every organisation-bearing fact the Action layer uses, and the law an obligation rests on (`standards`, layer 9), is invisible to investigation (X23, X25).

## 5. Proposed architecture

**Target.** L4 for one city's bodies, reached in two stages; L5 later on demand. Why: Bob's description of the work ("relationships, responsibilities, obligations, reporting lines of all types") and his 1 August definition of the analytic product as the delta between how things are supposed to flow and how they really flow (NOTIFICATIONS src 80–109) are L4 by definition; every core journey that touches a body (5, 13, the franchise, court case and contract front doors) needs L2 and L3; L5 has no group asking yet (D224: a capability must serve the path).

**What to adopt (§9).**
- *Structure:* W3C ORG's separation of `Organization`, `Post` (a position "that exists independently of the person or persons filling it"), `holds`, `reportsTo` (agents or posts, including dotted-line), `subOrganizationOf`, n-ary `Membership` with `memberDuring`, and `ChangeEvent` (original organisations → resulting organisation). Popolo's field names for posts and memberships (`start_date`, `end_date`, `role`, `on_behalf_of`, `founding_date`, `dissolution_date`, `classification`). FollowTheMoney's `Occupancy` (holder, post, start/end, status) and `ContractAward`. These map one-to-one onto the registry's existing kinds (body/institution = organisation, office = post, person = holder), so nothing is invented.
- *Contracts:* OCDS party roles (buyer, procuringEntity, supplier, payer, payee) on a contract line, and OCDS `Milestone {dueDate, dateMet, status}` as the shape of an obligation occurrence.
- *Identifiers:* OCD-IDs for jurisdictions and divisions; org-id.guide schemes (chart-of-accounts codes `US-COA`, EINs) and source-system ids (Legistar `BodyId`, `PersonId`) as identifiers with a scheme on an entity, so a source-assigned id resolves at grade A (DEC-53 tiers).
- *Obligations:* LegalRuleML's deontic split (obligation, prohibition, permission/right) with a bearer and a reparation; the NYC tracker's operational fields (who must act, what they produce, by when, how often, which law); the obligation-level audit method (modal-lexicon candidates verified, then human-adopted).
- *Sources a group can actually get, free:* Legistar's unauthenticated OData (`Bodies`, `Persons`, `OfficeRecords` with start and end dates; TAD §7.1 measured it unauthenticated, C10), budget documents' department and fund codes (M-119), charters and codes (capture), staff directories (FW-20 reader), the Census of Governments' individual unit and public-use files (special districts, JPAs). Paid officeholder feeds (Cicero, BallotReady) would need a vendor key every instance holds, against sovereign instances (D201), so they stay optional and outside the product.

**Data model.** All append-only (D235), versioned where a member revises (D264), every author the control plane's stamp (entities R4, R28), every absence or unreadable fact `undetermined` with its reason (D55–D57), sight per Membership §7.9 (entities R32).

- **Entity (extend `entities`).** Unchanged kinds, plus `program` (a program, service or function: needed as the target of responsibility, funding and consequences' `program`/`service`) and `place` (a division, with its OCD-ID; DEC-114 already counts a place as a subject). New `identifiers [{scheme, id, basis: content id}]`; an identifier match resolves at A (source-assigned) as R9 does for aliases. `person` stays heavy (DEC-88) and, under this design, is registered only as a holder of a registered office or as named in a documented act in official capacity (§8 D2).
- **Line (new, evidentiary).** `{line_id LIN-, kind, from_entity, to_entity, role?, valid: {from, to, precision}, basis: [content legs], asserted_by: source | system_rule | member, grade A–D, declared_by, at, withdrawn?}`. Kinds (closed, revised by spec): `part_of`, `post_in` (office within body), `holds` (person holds office; `status`: elected, appointed, acting, interim, ex officio), `reports_to` (`line`: administrative | functional | budgetary), `oversees` (audit, inspection, regulatory), `appoints`, `seat_on` (office sits on a board), `funds`, `contracts_with` (OCDS role on each end, contract entity), `acts_for` (vendor or agent for a body), `responsible_for` (office → program/function), `custodian_of` (office → record series or system), `successor_of` (`change`: renamed, merged, split, absorbed; W3C ChangeEvent). A line is a claim about the world: graded as §8.1 grades a connection (A source-assigned identifiers at both ends, e.g. a captured Legistar office record; B exact identifier match; C name correspondence; D member testimony), its grade never composed across scales (D58), weakest end governs (D135). It is **not** a declared relation: `entities` R26 stays true of `proxy_for`, `member_of`, `overlaps`, and bias collision keeps following those alone (DB safeguard 4).
- **As-of reads.** `structureAt({entity, at})` answers the lines valid at a date, each with grade and basis; `holderAt({office, at})` answers holders or `undetermined` (no line covers the date; two lines overlap; the latest basis predates the date by more than the stated horizon). Holders over time follow the built `sources` pattern (dated, evidenced, never merged, as-of read; X95).
- **Chain walk.** `chain({from, kinds, at, depth ≤ 6})`: breadth-first over lines valid at `at`, fan-out bounded (1,000, entities R39's figure), each hop graded, the path's grade its weakest hop, exhaustion or a hop out of view answered `undetermined` (DEC-16's walk pattern, D179). No centrality, density or ranking (D181).
- **Obligation (new).** `{obligation_id OBL-, version, modality: duty | prohibition | power, obligor: {entity_id} (office or body; never a private individual), obligee: {entity_id} | {audience} | "the public", performance: {act: report | publish | respond | meet | decide | deliver | pay | notify | audit | enforce | other, description, deliverable kind?, measure?}, source: {standard_id + passage content ids, in-force version} | {practice: measured custom, basis} , trigger: {event: progression stage or action correspondence or observed document kind} | {recurrence} | {date} | {request}, time: {within n days, count calendar|business, starts} | {due} (computed by TIME's calendar, never here), limits?: (for a power: amount, scope), exceptions: [{description, discharging document kind}], enforcer?: {entity_id, profile action kind}, observed_by: {progression_key, stage_key} | {watch on a source} | {document kind resolving to the obligor}, in_force: derived from the source's period (or stated with basis), declared_by, at, basis statement}`. Member words: "must", "must not", "may"; members see "obligation" only for a duty or prohibition and "power" for a power (§8 D7), one object type because both are read from the same clauses and checked against the same acts. A duty with no held source reads "declared, source not held" (like a declared flow, D-128) and can never ground a breach action (layer 9 contract).
- **Generic obligations from the profile.** A profile deadline (`jurisdictions` R26, e.g. the records law's response period) is an obligation stated for a class of obligors ("the agency asked"); `obligations` reads it as a template, so a records request's clock in `action-clocks` is one occurrence of the addressed office's response obligation, and the two homes of lateness (X52, X60) share one meaning.
- **Occurrence (derived, never stored; progressions R24's rule).** For each trigger observed in the record or each recurrence date in force: `{due, state: met | met_late | overdue | pending | discharged | undetermined, by_days?, evidence: [captures resolving to the obligor of the deliverable kind, graded], why}`. "Overdue" raises a question and never asserts a violation (D241); met occurrences are shown with the same care (invariant 7, D330). A member's recorded decision ages an occurrence without hiding it (progressions R25). Shape: OCDS milestone.
- **Power check (derived).** `withinPowers({act})`: for a conformance act (or a document's act) by office X at date d, the powers in force held by X or delegated to it along `appoints`/`reports_to` lines, with limits, each with its source; the answer is a reading with the level searched, never "unauthorised" (D76, D193, four-level D59).
- **The flow model** (D-128) is the set of obligations and progressions of an institution; the analytic product is the delta (occurrences not met, findings of progressions), reached through the queue's FINDING class ("Noticed") with its derivation (NOTIFICATIONS rule 3), and through conformance when a member determines it.

**Module changes and the total order** (checked against `build/modules.json`).
- *Extend* `entities` (layer 5): `program`, `place` kinds; `identifiers`; resolution by identifier.
- *New* `lines` (layer 5, directly after `entities`): lines, holders, as-of reads, change events, the chain walk; uses `record-grammar`, `record-core`, `membership`, `promotion`, `provenance`, `content`, `entities`. About 1,800 lines, under P6.
- *Move* `local-facts` and `standards` from layer 9 to layer 5, after `progressions` (joint with the TIME and LAW studies). `standards` uses only record-grammar, jurisdictions, record-core, membership, promotion and content; `local-facts` only record-grammar, jurisdictions, record-core and membership: no `uses` edge changes. Their users (`conformance`, `action-clocks`, `filings`, `action-plans`, layer 11) are all later, so nothing breaks; `membership`'s `MODULE_ORDER` (R83) is re-pinned, as for `docket`.
- *New* `obligations` (layer 5, after `standards`): obligations, occurrences, power check, the delta feed; uses `entities`, `lines`, `progressions`, `standards`, `local-facts` (and TIME's calendar service wherever the TIME study places it, at or below layer 5), `content`, `provenance`, `membership`, `record-core`. About 2,500 lines.
- *Change* (requirements only): `progressions` (a definition scoped to an obligor entity, the deferred institution scope; a stage may cite the obligation it observes); `conformance` (actor may carry `entity_id`; an `obligation_against_act` determination names the obligation); `actions` (addressee suggested from `custodian_of`/`responsible_for`; quotes matched by entity before string); `escalation` R12 (oversight and elected targets from `oversees`/`appoints` lines, the profile flag only as fallback); `local-facts` R6 (an office's calendar follows its `part_of`/employer line, X39); `retrieval`/`query-language` (fields `obligor:`, `owed_to:`, `holder:` and an `obligation` arm); `intent` (a condition over occurrences); `inquiry` (a leg may rest on an obligation occurrence's derivation once D-181 is lifted, §8 D8); `queue-producers` (a FINDING kind for an occurrence overdue, worded "Noticed"); `affordances`/`op-declarations` (acts and rungs: `linerecord` reasoned, `obligationdeclare` reasoned, `obligationadopt` reasoned, machine `linepropose`/`obligationpropose` reversible, DEC-88's bands).
- *Effect on users of moved modules:* none beyond the order; layer 9's contract is unchanged ("a standard held in the record").

**The AI's role.**
- *Proposes (reversible, labelled, stored apart; D4, D53):* lines and holders read from org charts, minutes, staff directories and rosters; obligations and powers read from charters, codes, contracts, court orders and adopted budgets (EXTRACT mode); "whose move is it" readings. A member adopts each obligation with a reasoned act; adoption names the clause.
- *Writes directly, machine-attributed (DEC-52's pattern, §8 D4):* only lines and holders taken from source-native structured data where the source assigns identifiers at both ends (grade A or B), e.g. a captured Legistar office record; shown for review in the session sidebar, never as a gate.
- *Answers (FIND, cited):* "who held X on d", "who oversees X", "what must X do and by when", "is X late", "was this act within X's powers", each a cited read of `lines`/`obligations` with grade, as-of date and the level at which absence was found; never a statement of law (D274), never a verdict on a person or office (D177).
- *Run modes:* reads join `check` and `investigate`; obligation proposals need `extract`, deployed only after a measured precision gate (AIR §7.3 point 7, D40). Plan mode reads obligations and lines to suggest addressees and escalation targets.

**Doctrine kept.** Humans decide and the machine proposes (D1, D3, D4); machine attribution (D8, entities R28); labelled drafts (D14, K1364); undetermined first-class and never defaulted (D55, D56, jurisdictions R27); four-level absence (D59); a grade states how a link was established, never truth (D95); a subject match never means the subject did anything (D193); no adversarial attribute, no prior by role, bad actors by evidence (D166; CM §4); addressees and actors are offices, never private individuals (D156, D173, actions R9, Design Requirement 6 as D176); private persons' protections (D160, D163, D165); constitutive relations untraversed (D178, entities R26) with evidentiary lines graded and bounded (D179, D191); no centrality (D181); derived counts filtered by sight (D136, D147); a due threshold raises a question, never asserts a violation (D241); the group's checkpoint is never a finding about government (D234); computed dates derived, never stored (D245); no deadline invented (D250); definitions append-only with the version named (D264); compliance recorded as carefully as noncompliance (D330); OP1 (D380); no jurisdiction in product code: offices, bodies and rosters are data, readers generic (D196, layers.md rules 1–6); a fact is stated once (D368); sovereign instances, no required vendor key (D201); "obligation" on screens only in Bob's sense (DEC-107).

**Runtime and deployment.** Everything runs inside the instance's Durable Object on its SQLite store (10 GB per object on Workers Paid; 30 s CPU per invocation, configurable to 5 min; §9). Tables are small (a city: thousands of entities, tens of thousands of lines, a few thousand obligations). Occurrences and chains are derived per read with published bounds and `truncated` (R14's pattern); the overdue scan joins the one scheduler alarm as a consumer, never a cron (X58, scheduler R14). Rosters and charters enter through `acquisition` under the host governor, so imports are captures with provenance. No new runtime or credential class for the AI (D38). No external paid service. Deployment needs the new tables' migrations at every instance and a re-pin of `MODULE_ORDER`.

## 6. How to proceed

**Stage 1 — Structure (L1 → L2).** *Unlocks:* journey 5 step 3 (the office that holds the records), journey 13's addressee and escalation steps (who oversees the actor), the "court case: name the parties and offices" and "contract" front doors, the journeys' gap "Offices and who held them", DR §6's role-and-predecessor accountability. *Size:* one new module (`lines`), `entities` extended, five modules with requirement changes (`actions`, `conformance`, `escalation`, `local-facts`, `query-language`); about 45 requirements. *Measure first:* (1) Oakland's Legistar: are `Bodies`/`OfficeRecords` published with start and end dates, for which bodies and years; (2) how many registered offices are concerned by more than 32 documents (D-224's stated trigger); (3) whether budget department codes are stable identifiers across two fiscal years (extend M-119); (4) how many org charts and directories the census holds (EBD §2 row 4: ~395 ± 272 directories).
**Stage 2 — Obligations (L2 → L4).** *Trigger:* stage 1 merged and one of: a group tracking a franchise or contract, a code chapter's reporting duties, or a records request's clock against an office. *Unlocks:* C1–C9, the sewer-fund question "was the transfer authorised" (powers), "Waiting on the City Clerk's reply, due 14 October" from data, the flow model's delta in the queue, assistant answers on duties. *Size:* one new module (`obligations`), two layer moves (`standards`, `local-facts`, shared with LAW and TIME), requirement changes in `progressions`, `inquiry`, `intent`, `retrieval`, `queue-producers`, `affordances`, `op-declarations`, `skills`; about 60 requirements. *Measure first:* a hand-built gold set (one municipal-code chapter's duties, one franchise agreement, one consent decree) and the precision and recall of machine obligation proposals against it; published studies show contract-obligation extraction as low as 70.56% best accuracy (§9), so proposals stay member-adopted whatever the figure, and EXTRACT is enabled only above a precision Bob is shown.
**Stage 3 — Network (L4 → L5).** *Trigger:* a second group working on the same body, or a coalition asking (K600 (c)). Shared, signed structure and obligation packs recreated per group (DEC-45/96 provenance, no inherited grade), OCD-ID/org-id exports, Popolo and OCDS-milestone export of an obligation register, patterns with registry denominators (E2).

## 8. Decisions for Bob

**D1. The obligation construct and its word.** Adopt "obligation" as a held record object (OBL-) in the sense Bob gave it on 1 August, home of the flow model. Who may be an obligor on members' screens? (A) public bodies only, as DEC-107 reads today, a contractor's duty shown as "contract term"; (B) public bodies and bodies acting for one under a public law, contract, franchise or grant ("the hauler's obligation under the franchise"); (C) any organisation. **Recommend B:** Bob's own definition of nonconformity reaches "action by some other person or organization" (CM 906–908), the franchise journey needs it, and B still excludes private individuals and purely private dealings.

**D2. Holders of offices (effects on people outside the project).** (A) offices only, no holders; (B) holders recorded for offices the group registers, each tenure cited to a public document, no attribute beyond role, dates and status; bulk import limited to elected and appointed officials and office heads; other staff only when a documented act names them in official capacity; (C) any public employee a public document names. **Recommend B:** Design Requirement 6 names individuals "only in their official capacity in connection with specific documented actions" (D176); A loses predecessor accountability (DR §6), C builds the work-pattern profile DEC-11 warned of.

**D3. Evidenced lines may be walked.** Confirm the doctrine: dated, graded, cited lines (reports to, oversees, part of, acts for…) are claims about the world that the record may traverse, bounded, as of a date, weakest hop governing, undetermined on exhaustion; constitutive relations stay untraversed (entities R26). (A) yes; (B) no traversal, single hops only. **Recommend A:** "who answers for this" and escalation targeting are chains; DEC-16 already rules a bounded walk with undetermined on exhaustion.

**D4. The machine's authority over structure and duties.** (A) the machine writes lines and holders directly, machine-attributed, only from source-native data with identifiers at both ends (A/B), and proposes everything else; obligations and powers are always proposals a member adopts; (B) everything proposed; (C) machine writes all. **Recommend A:** it extends DEC-52/53 exactly as far as they reason ("an A-tier correspondence is not a guess the machine made"), while a reading of law into a duty stays a member's act, as DEC-54 keeps inhaled policy "propose, never install".

**D5. Architecture (layers and product modules).** Two new product modules in layer 5, `lines` (after `entities`) and `obligations` (after `standards`); move `standards` and `local-facts` to layer 5 (no `uses` change; their users all later). Alternatives: (B) obligations in layer 9 beside `standards` (invisible to investigation and the assistant, the observation the brief makes); (C) fold obligations into `progressions` (a flow pattern is not a duty with an obligor, beneficiary and source; it would overload a module the record already reads as "the group's claim"). **Recommend A**, jointly with the LAW and TIME studies' placement of `standards`, `local-facts` and the calendar.

**D6. Entity kinds.** Add `program` (program, service or function) and `place` (a division, with its identifier) to the closed vocabulary (a doctrine change, `entities/index.mjs:25–27`). (A) add both; (B) add neither (responsibility targets become themes, places stay profile data). **Recommend A:** responsibility, funding and consequences already name programs and services; DEC-114 already defines a subject as possibly a place; the directory is keyed on body and place (DEC-111.6).

**D7. Member vocabulary and the office page (UX).** Words: "obligation" (a duty), "power" for what an office may do (not "authority", which members already meet as who issued a document, A&T), "lines" for the structure (Bob's own word) with verbs on each ("reports to", "held by", "acts for"); declared relations keep their names. A Subjects-screen **office page**: holders over time, lines as of a date, duties and powers with their sources, occurrences met and overdue, the documents that concern it. (A) as proposed; (B) structure only, duties on the plan page. **Recommend A:** it gives every journey one place to answer "who, under what authority, by when" and keeps the group's own checkpoints on the plan page (D234).

**D8. Requirement meaning: an unmet obligation as evidence.** D-181 deferred "an observed absence of a reply as a leg" until a real case. With obligations held, an occurrence's derivation (source in force, trigger date, due date, the level searched) is a well-defined, checkable absence. (A) lift the deferral for occurrences of sourced obligations, graded by what was searched and stated; (B) keep deferred. **Recommend A at stage 2:** DEC-14 already makes a body's non-response "a dated, capturable, first-party fact"; the four-level absence rule (D59) keeps it honest.

**Decided by BOB (reported, not asked):** internal names `lines`/`LIN-` and `obligations`/`OBL-`; the queue's internal class code `OBLIGATION` renamed `TODO` in code when `obligations` lands (D320: one word, one meaning; the member label is already "To do"); identifiers stored with a scheme (OCD-ID, org-id prefixes, source-system ids); the roster and org-chart readers are generic content types with system shapes as profile or site data, never names in code (D196); occurrences, chains and power checks derived on read, bounded (depth 6, fan-out 1,000) and published with `truncated`; the overdue scan as a scheduler consumer; recurrence as an RRULE subset agreed with the TIME study; D-224's pair materialisation reopened for office- and body-kind entities at its own trigger; Popolo/OCDS export deferred to stage 3.
