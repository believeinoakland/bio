## TIME

### Errors

1. ✗ **The "concrete defect" (§3, L93) misreads which event the profile rule starts from.** The study says that for the Roadmap's CPRA request (received Thursday 19 March), `computeDeadline` answers Sunday 2026-03-29. The code maps a rule's `starts: received` to the group's first **inbound** ledger entry, and `starts: filed` to its first `sent` entry (`bio-plane/src/action-clocks/index.mjs:695–698`: `d.starts === "filed" ? "sent" : d.starts === "received" ? "received"`). The ledger's directions are `sent | received | no_response` from the group's side (actions R15; `actions/index.mjs:1328`, "responded = direction === 'received'"). The test fixes that reading (`bio-plane/test/m/action-clocks/clocks.test.mjs:166–179`: a `received` rule counts from the inbound entry, and with only `no_response` it answers "no received entry").
   - The Oakland profile's only rule is `records_response … starts: "received"` (`jurisdictions/profiles/oakland-alameda.mjs:247`). For the Roadmap's case (request sent, no reply: RM src 241–258), `clockPropose` therefore answers **undetermined, "the action's ledger holds no received entry"**. It does not answer 29 March. When a reply does arrive, it counts ten days from the City's reply, which means nothing for a response deadline.
   - **Correction.** There are two defects, not one:
     - (a) **Whose receipt?** R26's `received` (the agency's receipt of the group's request) is read as the group's receipt. So the first profile's one rule never computes in the case it exists for: non-response.
     - (b) **No roll-forward.** This is real (`:704`, `t0 + days*86400000`), but it is shown only with a `filed` start, or after (a) is fixed.
   - The proposed time-rule model (§5 item 3, anchors `received | filed | …`) carries the same ambiguity forward. It must name the receiving party (e.g. `received_by_counterparty` / `received_by_group`), or define `received` once in R26.
2. ~ **"start = the UTC date of the ledger instant" (§3 table, action-clocks row).** A ledger `at` is a member-typed `YYYY-MM-DD` (actions R15 `BAD_DATE`, C-33.6). `e.at.slice(0,10)` slices a date, not an instant, so no UTC shift happens at the start.

### Omissions

1. **TIME's expectation and ORGANISATIONS' obligation are one construct, proposed twice.** TIME §5 item 6 models a body's duty as an `expectation` (rule plus derived instances), homed in `progressions`: "a single-stage expectation covers commitments, audit targets and contract dates" (§5 progressions bullet; Stage 2). It gives the duty a queue kind `expectation-due`.
   - ORGANISATIONS §5 models the same duty as `obligations` / `OBL-`, with derived occurrences, in a new module. It explicitly rejects folding it into `progressions` (its D5 option C), and gives it its own FINDING kind.
   - Each study names the other only as a supplier ("the `due` part"; "TIME's calendar"). Neither notices that both own the rule, the instance and the queue kind.
   - **Needed:** one home. ORGANISATIONS' `obligations`, which has obligor, source and in-force, is the better one. TIME supplies only `civil-time` (the computation) and `chronology` (the anchor dates). `progressions` stays the declared flow that observes an obligation.
2. **Layer placements contradict across the studies.**
   - TIME: `local-facts` at the head of layer 5, before `entities` (position 36); `chronology` after `connections`.
   - ORGANISATIONS: `local-facts` and `standards` after `progressions`.
   - LAW (`studies/LAW.md` L5, L168): `standards` after `connections`, before `progressions`.
   - TIME §7 also asks for offices "as entity ids … so the right closure calendar applies", and ORGANISATIONS wants `local-facts` R6 to follow a `part_of`/employer line. Both require `local-facts` to sit after `entities` and the new `lines`, which TIME's head-of-layer placement forbids.
   - One order satisfies all three: `entities`, `lines`, `local-facts`, `connections`, `standards`, `chronology`, `progressions`, `obligations`. It is checked against modules.json: each moved module's uses are at or below layer 4 plus `entities`/`lines`, and its users are all later.
3. **The document date that a "met" or "late" verdict needs (G3, G7) has a better source than PDF readers for meetings.** The Legistar Web API was probed live, unauthenticated, for `oakland` on 2026-10-05.
   - `Events` carries `EventDate` (midnight, no zone) and `EventTime` ("10:30 AM", local text). It also carries `EventAgendaLastPublishedUTC` and `EventMinutesLastPublishedUTC`, and cancellations encoded in the body name ("*Rules and Legislation Committee - CANCELLED"). Special meetings are separate bodies ("*Special Public Safety Committee").
   - For 2026 to date: 130 events, 24 of them cancelled. 105 have minutes, with a median of 15 days from meeting to minutes **last-published**, and 33 of the 105 over 21 days.
   - This answers Stage 2's "measure first (2): Oakland's minutes lag is unmeasured" now, at no cost. It also sets the model's limits:
     - "**Last**-published" is an upper bound. A republished agenda looks late: 29 of 106 agendas were last-published under 72 hours before the meeting day's midnight. Read as notice violations, those would be exactly the false claim D77 ranks worst.
     - Notice compliance can be shown only by a capture made at the deadline. `per_meeting` watching must therefore capture at meeting − notice period, and the at-least-once alarm's lateness must be stated in the finding.
   - The study proposes the Legistar `Events` source (§5) but not these fields or their evidential limits.
4. **The meeting model must normalise vendor encodings** ("- CANCELLED" suffixes, special-meeting bodies, zone-less `EventDate`) as site or profile data, never in code (D196). §5 item 4 does not say where this mapping lives.
5. **The interface with ANALYSIS** names the fiscal-period mapping but not the bitemporal "dataset vintage" read. CF §8.3 APN vintages and id-spaces R10–R12 `roll_year` are already a built vintage construct (`id-spaces`) that `validAt` should generalise, not duplicate.
6. **Snooze "returns on a date OR on new activity"** (PS §4, digest TIME l.140) is a time rule in the queue. The study's one-engine principle (G10) should list it (queue R21–R22) among the callers that delegate.

### Doctrine
- **Sound:**
  - D1's band rule follows D-516 and D77.
  - D2 keeps computed body deadlines as proposals, consistent with DEC-10's "authored" licence and D-149 "the plane encodes no law's rules".
  - The weekend moves into data (D196, Conflicts #48).
  - No AI on a schedule (D13; DEC-24 rule 2).
  - Local facts are confirmed by a member (D15).
- **One tension to name:** D-149 says "an encoded rule goes stale silently". Stage 1 encodes CPRA, Brown Act, OMC, Government Claims Act and FOIA rules into profiles. The containment (citation, status, R44 basis, a confirmation horizon, member adoption per D2) is adequate only if R26 rules gain `status`/basis under R44, as holidays have. Today R26 rules carry `basis: "UNMEASURED"` (Oakland `:248`), which R44 forbids for calendar facts but not for deadlines. **Stage 1 must extend R44's "UNMEASURED is not a basis" to `deadlines`.** Otherwise the first rule set ships as unsourced law.
- The D10 recommendation (no .ics) is a fair reading of DEC-94 (3) for a **subscription feed**. A one-off file the member downloads reaches no one who has not opened the product. The option put to Bob should separate the two.

### Feasibility
- Runtime fit is sound: pure computation; bounded RRULE expansion; consumers on the one DO alarm (SCHEDULER "Do NOT add a second alarm or a cron"; `bio-plane/wrangler.jsonc` has no cron); `Intl` with IANA zones. See Error 9 on `Temporal.Now`.
- **Stage 1's member surface cannot be a tranche item.** Bob's ruling 4 on layers.md (l.48) says "The UI is a placeholder, worked on elsewhere … no tranche plans work on it". The study's own words are that "without it Stage 1 changes nothing a member sees". Stage 1 must therefore name its dependency on the UX design stream (DEC-93, the design branch), and the plane's part must stand on its own (ops, queue kinds, `due` published), so the design stream can bind to it.
- Stage 1 is otherwise concrete enough to plan from. It names its modules and journeys (5, 13, (h), (k)), gives a size, and says what to measure first. The size should be raised as in Error 4.
- `chronology` "holds dated facts" (a store) in layer 5, and `extraction` (layer 4) produces them. The write path is not named. EXTRACT is not deployed, and the readers run in layer 1 inside `acquire`, so the first writer of dated facts must be stated: the reading pipeline at read time, or a re-read job.
