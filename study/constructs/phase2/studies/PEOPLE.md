**The construct.** Every human the work concerns, three regimes kept apart and deliberately joined only where a rule says so:
- **people**: whom the record is about (officials and staff, today and in the past; contractors' principals, lobbyists, donors, board members, parties, witnesses named in documents, relatives a document names), held fully under K1452 and K1455;
- **members**: the group's own people (`membership`), pseudonymous by design (D84);
- **sources**: who gave the group material (`sources`, DEC-78), protected by a consent regime (D77, D124).

For a person: names and identity; positions and career inside and outside government; education, licences, military service and other credentials; memberships; statements and acts; interests and money given and received; stated personal ties; and the evidenced overlaps between people over time that are "the substrate for exploring who knows who" (K1452). Publication is unchanged: Design Requirement 6 governs every published work product (K1452, K1455; DR §6 l.149–156).

**How the work is done, and how it fails (research).**
- *Practice.* Investigators background people from documents first and online second, then corroborate through other people and records not online (GIJN, https://gijn.org/resource/introduction-investigative-journalism-finding-sources-backgrounding/). Watchdog databases hold people as nodes with dated, typed relationships: LittleSis uses twelve categories, among them Position, Education, Membership, Family, Donation/Grant and Lobbying, and distinguishes a position from a membership (https://littlesis.org/help/relationships). OCCRP's Aleph cross-references names across datasets and presents the results as candidates a person confirms or rejects; "matches are suggestive, not definitive" (https://openaleph.org/docs/user-guide/103/cross-reference/; https://gijn.org/resource/using-aleph/).
- *Data models.* FollowTheMoney (Aleph and OpenSanctions) models `Person` plus interval schemata: `Occupancy` (holder, post, dates, status), `Directorship`, `Ownership`, `Membership`, `Employment`, `Family` (person, relative, relationship, start and end) and `Associate` (https://followthemoney.tech/explorer/schemata/). Popolo separates `Person`, `Organization`, `Post` (a position that exists independently of its holder) and `Membership` with `start_date` and `end_date` (https://www.popoloproject.com/specs/). Wikidata's P39 "position held" carries start time, end time, replaces and replaced by (https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Wikidata:WikiProject_every_politician/P39_model).
- *Entity resolution.* OpenSanctions merges source records into canonical ids that keep every former id as a "referent"; ambiguous candidates go to a human or a reviewed assistant, and source ids stay stable while the publisher keeps them (https://www.opensanctions.org/docs/identifiers/; https://github.com/opensanctions/nomenklatura). Probabilistic linkage is the Fellegi–Sunter model: field agreement weighed by m (agreement among true matches) and u (agreement among non-matches) probabilities (https://www.jmir.org/2022/9/e33775); Splink implements it in SQL and links a million records in about a minute (https://github.com/moj-analytical-services/splink).
- *Failure modes.* Name-only matching: TransUnion's OFAC alert matched names only and flagged thousands of innocent people with a common name; the Supreme Court treated dissemination of the false match as a harm akin to defamation (https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/594/20-297/). A missing end date read as "still in office" (OpenSanctions' 40-year rule, cited in ORGANISATIONS §1). Scraping platforms against their terms (hiQ, above). Contributor data used outside its permitted purpose: FEC data may not be used "for the purpose of soliciting contributions or for any commercial purpose", while use in news-like communications is allowed (https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-11/chapter-I/subchapter-A/part-104/section-104.15).
- *Legal limits.* California's CCPA applies to for-profit businesses, so a nonprofit or unincorporated group is outside it, and publicly available government-record information and journalism are excluded anyway (https://legalclarity.org/does-the-california-consumer-privacy-act-apply-to-nonprofits/; https://iapp.org/news/a/cacpa-clarify-exemptions-and-fix-some-technical-errors-but-significant-work-remains). Elsewhere a group may fall under GDPR, whose journalistic exemption (Article 85; UK DPA 2018 Sch. 2 Part 5) is partial and conditional, though the CJEU has held that non-professionals may do journalism (https://ukmedialawpocketbook.com/2021/12/03/chapter-nineteen-data-protection-law-for-journalists/). California bars state and local agencies, not private groups, from posting officials' home addresses online without consent (Gov. Code 7928.205; https://california.public.law/codes/government_code_section_7928.205). The safety concern stands for a group too. Defamation by misidentification (TransUnion, above) is the main legal exposure of a person construct, and publication is where it bites.

## 5. Proposed architecture

**Why L5, and how it is staged.** Bob ruled every construct to L5 (K1432) and people a construct of its own (K1452). Every core need above sits at L1–L4, and Bob's own examples (the deputy director's name, career, schools, licences; staffing then and now; who knows who) are L2 and L4. L5 is where a group keeps pace with a city's turnover without typing: council seats, licences, campaign finance, lobbying and Form 700 filers arrive as public registers with identifiers. The order is forced by dependencies: identity (L1) before any fact (L2), facts before overlaps (L4), structure (`lines`) and events (`events`) before people's facts can be held as one fact in one home (§5.3). Staging follows the first study's stages 0–4 (§6).

**What §5A (provisional, K1459) gets right, and what this study changes.** Tested against the sources and practice:
- *Kept:* one `person` entity per human across every role (ladders §2); every fact about a person a dated, cited, graded line or event; overlaps derived on read, never stored, never ranked, never read as acquaintance (K1453; D88, D91, D98); same-name people never merged without evidence (D78); the machine writes only from source-native data with identifiers at both ends (K1443; D87); organisations of every kind with a closed `sector`; a duty may bind a person by name or role where a law does (K1453 amends K1440: a Form 700 filer, a registered lobbyist).
- *Changed:* (1) **`people` cannot sit directly after `lines` and also compose `events`** (K1453 says it composes `chronology`, which K1439 and K1458 place after `standards`; P4 forbids a module using a later one). `people` goes after `events` (§5.4). (2) **Line kinds are cut to one home per fact.** `employed_by`, `served_in` and `officer_of` duplicate ORGANISATIONS' `holds` (a position is a position, whatever the employer or sector; LittleSis and FtM model one "position/occupancy" relation with a capacity), so they become `holds` with a `capacity`. `gave_to` and `received_from` are money and live in MONEY as money facts with a payer and a payee (K1458; one fact, one home, D198). `lobbies_for` is ORGANISATIONS' `acts_for` with role `lobbyist`; a lobbying contact is an EVENTS communication or meeting. (3) **Added:** scheme identifiers for people; identity claims (`same_as`, `not_same_as`) that link without merging, following `sources` R6 and OpenSanctions' referents; dated person facts that have no second entity (a former name, a death, a locality); `associate_of` for a tie a document states (FtM `Associate`); members' declared ties (DR6's disclosure clause); the source↔person link under DEC-78's protection. (4) **P9's conflict question is not the machine's to choose** (D7, DEC-24 boundary 2): a possible conflict is surfaced only by an interest check a member declares, as a progression is declared (§5.6). (5) **Rosters stay tables**: a payroll or directory row becomes a person entity only by a source-native identifier, a member's act or a member's bulk adoption, so a city's 5,000 employees are not minted as 5,000 registry entries nobody looked at (the MONEY design's "ledgers stay tables", ladders §2, applied to people).

### 5.3 The data model

All append-only (D104); every author the control plane's stamp (entities R4, R28); every unknown `undetermined` with its reason (D26), with "not stated" kept apart from "none" (D28: Wikidata's somevalue/novalue, closed here for people as `{state: none | not_stated | undetermined}` on any fact a source could deny); validity as the first study's one value `{valid: {from, to, precision}, basis}` read by `civil-time.validAt`, EDTF level 1 (synthesis §3.4), so "~2011–2014" and "2023–2024, recalled" are held as written; valid time apart from record time (bitemporal).

- **Person entity (extend `entities`).** Unchanged kind `person`. Gains, shared with ORGANISATIONS O1: `identifiers [{scheme, id, valid?, basis}]` (schemes from profile data: `legistar_person`, licence boards, filer ids, bar numbers, FEC and CAL-ACCESS ids, OpenCorporates officer ids, an e-mail address at an organisation's domain as a dated contact identifier), resolving at grade A as an alias does (ORGANISATIONS §5). The `ENT-` id is widened (no fixed four-digit sequence), or minted opaque (record-core's opaque minter, R6–R9), BOB's choice, fixing §3's defect.
- **Identity claim (new, in `people`).** `{IDC-, a, b, claim: same_as | not_same_as, basis: [legs], how: identifier | corroborated | testimony, grade, by, at, withdrawn?}`. It links and never merges: each record keeps its own resolutions and lines (sources R6; OpenSanctions' referents), and reads offer "this person, and the records claimed to be them" with each claim's grade. `not_same_as` is first-class, so a member's "these are two Michael Houstons" stops a recurring proposal. A disputed pair (claims both ways) reads undetermined. Grade: A when the same scheme identifier sits on both (`same_secret`'s analogue); B, *if Bob rules P-B1 (b)*, when a name match is corroborated by a cited fact valid at both documents' dates (the same post held, the same licence number); D for a member's testimony with its stated basis. The machine never mints D (D35) and never claims `same_as` from a name alone (Publication §3 rule 15(d); X3).
- **Line kinds for people (extend `lines`; closed, revised only by spec).** Each `{LIN-, kind, from: person, to: entity, fields, valid, basis, asserted_by: source | system_rule | member, grade on two axes}` (ORGANISATIONS' two-axis grading: the assertion with its passage, and each end's resolution):
  - `holds` (ORGANISATIONS', widened): `to` an `office` entity, or an organisation with `title` as written when no post is registered; `capacity` closed: employee, elected, appointed, acting, interim, ex officio, board member, officer or director, partner, military service, volunteer, contractor, other; `unit` (an entity, optional). One kind for "a position", so a career is one read and a later registration of the post supersedes the organisation-level line by a member's act citing both, never an edit.
  - `belongs_to` (membership without a position: an association, club, union, political organisation, coalition).
  - `educated_at` (`degree`, `field` as written).
  - `credentialed_by` (`credential` as written, `number` (also a scheme identifier), `status` as the issuer states it: active, lapsed, revoked, suspended, as of the capture's date). Stated, never verified by the machine (D73).
  - `owns_interest_in` (`share` as written, `kind`: equity, real property, income source, gift source, as Form 700 schedules state).
  - `acts_for` (ORGANISATIONS', used with `role: lobbyist | agent | counsel` for people).
  - `related_to` (`relationship` from the `from` person's perspective, as written: FtM `Family`; K1455: no condition).
  - `associate_of` (`relationship` as written: "business partner", "longtime friend"; only where a document states it; never derived).
  - Not lines: money given or received (MONEY), statements and acts (EVENTS), a filer's duty (`duties`).
- **Person facts (new, in `people`).** `{PFA-, person, kind: name | birth | death | locality | address_as_stated | contact_as_stated | other, value as written, valid, basis, how: document | filing | self | third_party, asserted_by, by, at}`, appended, a later entry superseding on read (the `sources` R2 pattern, X16). A dated name is a fact; registering it as an alias is a separate act citing the fact (the alias is a matching key, the fact a claim about the world: two objects, not one fact twice).
- **Member tie (new, in `people`).** `{MTI-, member (stamp), entity, nature as written, valid, declared_at}`, declared only by the member about themselves (DEC-73), readable by that member and administrators (the cover-pairing sight, Membership §3), never joined to a person entity unless the member links themselves, and disclosed at publication at the attribution level the member chooses (publication R17's four levels) when the case concerns that entity (P-B3).
- **Source↔person link (new, in `people`).** A claim that a `sources` record and a person entity are one human, recorded by a member with evidence, readable only by the source's sight list with every read logged (sources R5), never in a published answer except as `sources.publishableAt` allows, never through the person's own reads. The protected fact is "this person was our source"; the person's public-record facts stay ordinary (DEC-78 item 5 and K1455 read together; §8 states it as decided).
- **Interest check (new, in `people`).** A member-declared rule, versioned like a progression definition: `{CHK-, scope: a body or an event kind, condition: participant's lines to a party of the item within a window, kinds considered, declared_by, reason}`. The record evaluates it on read and raises one "Noticed" item per new match with the derivation (the lines and the event, each cited); it never says "conflict" (D4, D53; DEC-89).
- **Reads (`people`).** `personAt({person, at})`; `careerOf(person)` (every `holds`, `belongs_to`, `educated_at`, `credentialed_by` line in date order, imprecise dates placed by `civil-time`, unstated dates listed apart, "placed nowhere and counted"); `credentialsOf(person, at?)`; `holderAt({office, at})` (ORGANISATIONS', read here for people); `staffingAt({body or unit, at})`, which answers lines and, where a roster table is bound, its rows by reference with the table's date; `statementsOf(person)` (an `events` read by participant); `overlaps({a, b, window})`; `pathBetween({a, b, at, maxOverlaps ≤ 3})`; `interestsOf(person, at)`; `identityOf(person)` (claims and identifiers). Every read bounded (500 default, 5,000 maximum, `truncated` by reading one past; D144), viewer-gated (§5.7), with four-level absence (D31): "no position held at level *meaning*; the 2021 directory is captured but not read".
- **Overlaps, defined (L4).** Two people overlap when (i) both have lines to the same entity whose validity intervals intersect (`civil-time`, three-valued; an imprecise intersection is undetermined), or (ii) both are participants of one event of an admitted kind (a co-signed document, a communication between them, a board meeting of seven members), or (iii) a stated tie (`related_to`, `associate_of`) joins them. Each overlap carries its **set size**: how many people the record holds on that shared entity in that window ("both employed by the City of Oakland, 2015–2018, with 5,000 others held" against "both on the 7-member Police Commission, 2019"), so a member sees why a shared employer means little, without any score (D53, D91). `pathBetween` is a breadth-first walk over the person–entity bipartite graph as of a date, weakest hop governing, fan-out 1,000 per hop, entities above a profile-free bound (the measured set-size limit) not expanded but named as "too common to walk", and undetermined on exhaustion (K1442; DEC-16). Never stored, never ranked, never "knows".

### 5.5 How it fits the first study's architecture

- `lines` (K1439, K1442) is the one evidentiary store for every relation between registry entities; people's positions, memberships and ties are lines, so a career walk and an org-chart walk are one mechanism with one bound and one grading (two axes). Nothing about people goes into `entities`' constitutive relations (R26).
- `duties` (K1440 as amended by K1453): a person may be an obligor only where a law binds them by name or role (Form 700 filers under the Political Reform Act; lobbyist registration under a city's ordinance); the duty's occurrence ("the 2025 annual statement was due 1 April") is matched to the filing event, so "she has not filed" is a four-level absence, never "concealed".
- `chronology` → `events` (K1458): statements and acts of people are events with participants; `people` reads them.
- `calculations` and `money`: person-keyed rows (overtime by officer, contributions by donor) join to person entities only through an identifier or a captured crosswalk (ladders §2 ANALYSIS, K1456), and money to and from people is MONEY's.
- `answers` and `ask` (K1450): person reads join the asking scope (registry and lines are already in K1450's widening); `sources*` and member ties stay out.
- Total order: one change to K1453's placement (§5.4). Layer 9 still uses only earlier layers.

### 5.6 The AI's role

- *Proposes (labelled, stored apart, adopted by a member; D18, K1364):* person references and roles from minutes, directories, org charts, résumés, filings and articles (EXTRACT, graded C for a name, B for an identifier in the text, never A; EB §4); `holds`, `educated_at`, `credentialed_by`, `belongs_to`, `associate_of` and `related_to` lines from documents, each with the passage; "same person?" candidates, explained field by field (which fields agree, which differ, from which documents), never with a probability; interest-check definitions in a member's words (a draft until kept).
- *Writes, machine-attributed, shown for review (K1443; DEC-52):* person entities and `holds` lines from source-native records with identifiers at both ends (Legistar `Persons`/`OfficeRecords`; a licence board's record by number; a filer's record by filer id); scheme identifiers read from those records; resolutions at A from those identifiers. Nothing else.
- *Answers (FIND, closed book, `answers` checks):* "who held X on D", "what did she do before", "which licences does he hold, as the board states them", each a cited read with grade, as-of date and level searched.
- *Never:* characterises a person (brand §3), says two people know each other, ranks or scores people, merges records, mints testimony, chooses whom to look into (D7), or reads person facts in plan mode (agent-worker R51 stands). Machine detectors (same-person proposals, interest-check matches) follow IDENTIFY's discipline: deterministic pairing in the plane, a labelled fixture with a false-merge gate before members see them (D167; X96).

### 5.7 Doctrine kept, and what the construct strains

Kept, each by citation: the machine never concludes (D1); derived informs, authored binds (D1, D18); labelled drafts (D9, K1364); grade states how checkable, never true (D43, entities R27); undetermined first-class, "not stated" apart from "none" (D26, D28); four-level absence (D31); no score, rank, reputation or suspicion flag on a person (D53, D64, D-53, Case Making §4); no centrality (D91); evidenced lines walked bounded, constitutive relations untraversed (D89, D98); identity is a graded judgment, never a fuzzy attribution (D78; Publication §3 rule 15(d)); no merge without evidence (D88); correction forward, nothing deleted except expunge (D104, D81); one home per fact (D198); viewer gate and whole-row withholding (D54, D163, D179); members' searches unrecorded (D68); a member's legal name never held (D84); sources protected, the group never first to make a source more public (D77, D124); third parties' words leave only by their own act (D82); bias statements never a verdict on a speaker (D192); addressees are offices (actions R9; D62); DR6 at publication (D61, K1452, K1455); no jurisdiction in code: schemes, register shapes and capacities as written are profile data (D99); no required vendor key (D150).

Strained, and how it is contained:
- *"No attribute of a person gates, filters or orders anything"* (Action §4 rule 10; D64; conflict A4). Read literally, `staffingAt` filters by post. Contained by a reading put to Bob (P-B7): the rule binds gates, priors, bars and machine runs; a member's own query over held facts, ordered by date or name, is permitted; no order by any attribute that stands in for importance.
- *Action-layer person exclusions* (consequences R10, conformance actor; conflicts A2, A3): built on the set-aside rule. Contained by P-B4.
- *Holding people fully vs the replicating store* (TAD §10.4 "a secret placed in it is a published secret"; conflict A12; X23): every member who sees a project, the root of trust and legal process reach person facts. Contained by sight (§5.8 below), no restricted tier invented (Intake §4a: "a restricted copy in a replicating substrate is a fiction"), expunge for what may not be held, and the publication filter.
- *Members anonymised, people held fully* (conflict A6; X15 C1): contained by keeping the regimes apart and joining them only by the member's own act (P-B3).
- *Source regime vs K1455* (conflict A7): contained by protecting the fact of being a source, not the person's public facts (decided, §8).

### 5.8 Sight, publication and runtime

- **Sight inside the group.** The registry stays one per instance (one identity per human; X1). A line or person fact asserted from a captured public document is evidence, visible like its capture (the K102 sight split, D60). A line, fact, identity claim or interest check a member asserts as testimony inside a hidden project is fenced by that project and answers outsiders as absent (D163). Person reads subtract `hiddenBundles(viewer)` (membership R88), never count what is withheld (D54), and a name search across the fence withholds whole rows (REC-36). The registry's frontier rows stay unfenced as today (OBS L47), pending P-B2.
- **Publication (DR6's enforcing home, P-B5).** `case-disclosures` lists every person entity the case's authored statements, subjects, bias lens (DEC-103) and docket name, and for each asks the publishing member's recorded basis: official capacity on a documented act (the `holds` line valid at the act's date and the event, both cited), the person's consent or prior publication (a source, as `sources` R8), or withheld as "a private individual" / a class. An unrecorded basis refuses pre-flight with the person named to the publisher only; nothing is redacted from carried materials (DEC-100 (2), DEC-5): materials are public records travelling whole, and DR6 governs the group's own words and structured fields. A reply naming a private person stays a receipt (docket R8).
- **Runtime.** Inside the instance's Durable Object (SQLite, 10 GB on Workers Paid, `SQLITE_FULL` past it: https://developers.cloudflare.com/changelog/post/2025-04-07-sqlite-in-durable-objects-ga/). Every read indexed and bounded; overlaps and paths computed per request within published bounds. Imports and extraction are captures processed in the background by the one scheduler alarm (batches of 100 per tick, D139, D177): never in the promote transaction (promote CPU is unit-dominated, D177). No cron, no second alarm, no vendor key. Deployment: migrations for the new tables at every instance; `MODULE_ORDER` re-pinned with its listener-order test.

### 5.9 Worked example at realistic volume: Oakland, people across a fiscal decade

*Volumes (sources):* 5,119 employee records for 2024 alone (https://transparentcalifornia.com/salaries/2024/oakland); over ten years, about 51,000 person-year rows, perhaps 10,000–15,000 distinct people (turnover unmeasured: M-P1). Council and boards: 8 council seats plus about 40 boards and commissions of 5–11 seats (to be measured from Legistar `Bodies`, M-P2). Form 700 filers, lobbyists and contributors: from the FPPC search, Oakland's lobbyist datasets and NetFile (counts to be measured, M-P3). Directories in the corpus: about 395 ± 272 (EB §2).

*Stored:* payroll years as ten `calculations` tables (rows by reference; no entity per row). Person entities only where an identifier or a member's act puts them: council and board seats from Legistar (a few hundred people, machine-written at A), filers and lobbyists from their registers (identifier tiers, A), and the people a member works on (hundreds to low thousands). Lines: positions, credentials, memberships and interests at roughly 5–15 per registered person, so 10,000–60,000 lines; at about 1 KB a row with its indexes, under 100 MB of the 10 GB object, beside a bundle store whose measured curve is ~60,800 bundles per 10 GB (D142). Entities exceed 10,000 in a year only if a member bulk-adopts roster rows, which is why the `ENT-` width fix is stage 0.

*Founding case reads (X13):* "Who held Finance Director, Controller, City Administrator and Auditor while the sewer-fund transfers ran, FY2012–2021?" is `holderAt` per post per fiscal year (40 reads, each a handful of indexed rows), with RM App A's approximate tenures held as EDTF (`~2011/2014`) and "undetermined" where two lines overlap or none covers a year, never the profile's office as the holder. "Courtney Ruby, CPA, CFE" is two `credentialed_by` lines as the documents state them, unverified (D73). "Michael Houston was Whistleblower Program Manager during the investigation, then Auditor" is two `holds` lines, and an overlap with the investigation's events by date, never a claim about what he knew.

*Everyday path:* a person page is one entity read, at most 500 lines by an index on `from`, at most 500 events by participant, and a resolutions count: bounded reads in the tens of milliseconds at these volumes, to be measured before stage 1 requirements are drafted (the measured precedent: retrieval at 20,000 documents under ~46 ms, D177). `overlaps(a, b)` reads a's and b's lines (dozens) and, per shared entity, its set size from an index: bounded. A shared employer of 5,000 is named "too common to walk" rather than expanded.
