**Bob's construct, in his words (K1457):** "money – where it comes from and where it goes, but also the rules that govern its movement, the commitments made, the things it buys and amount produced for goods and services."

**The answer in one paragraph.** Money should be held as **stated amounts** (money facts), each one a reading of one source at one **stage** of the public-finance cycle, on one **basis** of accounting, for one **period**, between registry **parties** and **funds**, graded like any citation; and never as a single "amount" or as a world truth the machine asserts. The world occurrence an amount quantifies (a payment, a transfer, an award, an adoption vote) is an **event** (EVENTS owns it); a promise to pay is a **duty** (ORGANISATIONS owns it) that cites the amount; a rule of movement is a **standard** (LAW) or a duty's prohibition; arithmetic is a **calculation** (ANALYSIS); a bulk ledger stays a **table** (ANALYSIS) whose columns carry money roles. A new module `money` (layer 5, placed **before** `calculations`, not after it as the provisional draft put it, so that recipes, search and junction checks can read money facts) holds the facts, the stage and flow vocabularies, member-declared **series** (one flow followed across relabelling and fiscal years, the founding case's need), chain-of-authority and flow reads, reconciliation and the procurement junction checks that progressions R32 has deferred since K102. The provisional draft's stage list is **not** kept as written: real standards (Fiscal Data Package, USAspending, GASB, OCDS) keep *phase* (proposed, adopted, adjusted, actual), *flow kind* (revenue, expenditure, transfer, allocation, balance) and *basis* (budgetary, cash, modified accrual, accrual) on separate axes, and so must the record, or the founding case's own reconciliation (ACFR against OpenGov) produces false contradictions.

## 1. Anticipated needs

Centrality: **core** (a main journey or the founding case cannot finish without it), **regular**, **occasional**. Each need gives a member's words and its evidence.

**A. Holding amounts honestly**
- **A1 core.** Hold an amount as a source states it, with currency, period, stage, parties, fund and the passage or cell it was read from. *"FY22: $2.1M transferred from the Sewer Service Fund to the General Purpose Fund (ACFR, p. 112)."* (C1: FA Layer 2 Fn1 L254–259; C10: TAD §7.1 L693–699 "tidy/long for line items"; ladders §5C M1 as a hypothesis.)
- **A2 core.** Never confuse planned, committed and spent, nor budget basis with GAAP basis. *"That's the adopted budget, not what was spent."* (C8: EB §2 row 6, BOB #32 2026-09-24: "a budget is a PLAN for money not yet spent, an audited statement REPORTS what a closed period did"; USAspending separates obligations from outlays, https://fedspendingtransparency.github.io/whitepapers/outlay/; encumbrance "is not an expenditure, and it is not a liability", https://legalclarity.org/what-is-an-encumbrance-in-governmental-accounting/.)
- **A3 core.** A figure read from a scan or a machine reading is marked as such and never raises a grade. *"Is that $52.6M the auditor's typed number or OCR?"* (C11: DEC-4 l.221–232, "digits … precisely where OCR fails"; D34; X69.)
- **A4 regular.** Amounts at different precision are not contradictions. *"'About $2 million' and '$2,097,431' are the same transfer."* (C5: CONTRADICTION-IDENTIFY §7 src 129–131; X83.)
- **A5 regular.** An amount's revision history is evidence. *"The cost moved from $4.2M to $2.8M three days before publication."* (C11: DEC-11 l.589–593; DEC-5 l.259.)

**B. Where it comes from and where it goes**
- **B1 core.** Follow one flow across fiscal years and **across label changes**. *"Do the post-FY21 transfers continue the franchise fee under new labels?"* (C1: RM §1 l.234–239 "may have been relabeled as cost allocations or overhead charges"; C7: SR §4.3 src 850–851, §4.5 claim C-014; C6: Intake §1 l.166–177, "the Sewer Service Fund transfer series" as the unit of admission.)
- **B2 core.** Sources of money: taxes, fees, charges, grants, bonds, settlements, with the fund they land in. *"How much of the sewer fund comes from ratepayers?"* (Bob, K1457; RM §1.)
- **B3 core.** Uses: transfers out, cost allocations, payees, programs. *"Where did the transferred money go?"* (CPRA 26-3028 asks for "all interfund transfers … all cost allocations charged to the Sewer Service Fund … the city's cost allocation plan", RM §1, opened.)
- **B4 core.** A payment's chain of authority: appropriation, commitment (purchase order or contract), invoice, payment. *"Which appropriation and contract does this payment rest on?"* (C3: CF §8.2 src 836–840, Bob's procurement chain "onward through amendments and payments"; OCDS implementation transactions, https://standard.open-contracting.org/latest/en/schema/reference/.)
- **B5 regular.** Fund balances and their constraints over time. *"The balance fell below the statutory floor."* (C4: CM §Claim src 763–764; GASB 54 classes, https://iowaleague.org/resource/gasb-54-fund-balance-classifications/.)
- **B6 regular.** Money given and received by people and organisations: contributions, gifts, income sources, settlements. *"Did the councilmember's top donors win contracts?"* (Bob, K1452/K1457; FPPC Form 700, https://www.fppc.ca.gov/transparency/form-700-filed-by-public-officials/form700-search.html; Oakland Form 460 data, https://public.netfile.com/pub2/?aid=COAK.)

**C. Rules that govern movement**
- **C1 core.** Hold a restriction on a fund or a fee and set flows against it, as a question. *"Sewer fee revenue may only be spent on the sewer system."* (C1: RM §1 Prop 218 Art. XIII D §6(b)(2); FA Fn1 "Municipal Code 13.04.080"; Carachure v. City of Azusa (2025), sewer franchise-fee transfers to the general fund, https://law.justia.com/cases/california/court-of-appeal/2025/b336778.html.)
- **C2 core.** Transfer authority: was a movement authorised, by what act. *"Was the $2.1m transfer authorised?"* (C11: DEC-16 INQ-2; D2: measures l.78; ORGANISATIONS C6 "powers".)
- **C3 regular.** Procurement thresholds and competition rules. *"Award with no solicitation"; "six contracts at $49,900 under a $50,000 bid threshold."* (C1: MILESTONES M4 l.262–264; C11: DEC-9; split purchases, https://guide.iacrc.org/proof/most-common-schemes/potential-scheme-split-purchases/.)
- **C4 regular.** Restricted proceeds (bonds, grants). *"Bond proceeds moved to the general fund against the voter-approved purpose."* (C9: ACTION-PLAN l.65–68; D2: plan-page l.32.)
- **C5 occasional.** Fee and rate limits, explaining a charge. *"What's this sewer maintenance charge on my water bill?"* (D1: journeys §6 l.529; X76.)

**D. Commitments against payments**
- **D1 core.** A contract's amount against what was paid, including change orders. *"The contract was $2M; we've found $3.4M paid."* (C3: CF §8.2 junction checks src 914–916; progressions R32, deferred, T32 A37.)
- **D2 regular.** Commitments beyond contracts: grants, debt schedules, a body's promise, MOUs. *"$100,000,000 of bond debt authorised."* (D2: matter-page l.29; C13: research M-NEW-4 MOUs.)
- **D3 regular.** Payments past term, award with a different signed amount. (CF §8.2.)

**E. What money buys and delivers**
- **E1 core.** What a payment bought, with quantities. *"What did the $3.4M buy?"* (Bob, K1457: "the things it buys".)
- **E2 core.** Output and unit cost over time. *"Cost per pothole filled, 2019 to 2025."* (Bob: "amount produced for goods and services"; GASB Service Efforts and Accomplishments, cost-per-unit efficiency measures, https://storage.gasb.org/SEA_fact_sheet-2010-07_DB_LP_KB_DMM_DRB_RHA.pdf.)
- **E3 core.** Budget against actuals, across years. *"Overtime ran past budget every year: by how much?"* (D1: journeys §3 l.99; ANALYSIS B1.)

**F. Reconciliation and patterns**
- **F1 core.** Two sources of one figure disagree. *"ACFR transfers-out disagrees with OpenGov FY23-24."* (C7: SR §3.1 src 560–566, PROB-2026-0007; C1: FA Fn2 l.271–276.)
- **F2 regular.** One-time against recurring. *"The FY24 number may include a one-time insurance true-up; check note 14."* (C7: SR §4.6 src 990–991.)
- **F3 regular.** Patterns with denominators: split contracts, change-order growth, vendor concentration, payments before approval. (OCP red-flags guide, https://www.open-contracting.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/OCP2024-RedFlagProcurement-1.pdf; ladders §5C M12 as hypothesis.)

**G. Sources and scale**
- **G1 core.** Read the documents money lives in: ACFRs (PDF, often scanned), budget books (tens of MB), portal exports (CSV/XLSX), staff reports' "fiscal impact". (C6: SOURCE-ACCESS l.146–158, budget book 32.5 MB; C8: EB §2 rows 5–6; M2: staff-report.mjs:34.)
- **G2 regular.** Bring in a ledger (checkbook, payroll, contract register, campaign-finance filings) whole, without copying it fact by fact. *"Load the city's checkbook for FY2024."* (SF's vendor-payments vouchers dataset holds 8.18M rows since FY2007, https://data.sf.gov/City-Management-and-Ethics/Vendor-Payments-Vouchers-/n9pm-xkyq; Cincinnati 1.25M since FY2014, https://data.cincinnati-oh.gov/Growing-Economic-Opportunities/Vendor-Payments-Greater-than-Zero/rngb-iq76.)
- **G3 regular.** Be told when a watched money source changes (a new budget amendment, a restated ACFR). (C10: TAD §7.4 "keyed field-level diff with numeric tolerances".)
- **G4 regular.** Ask in plain words. *"Where did the sewer fund's money go in FY23?"* (QUESTIONS; B1 (c), K1432.)

**How the work is actually done, and how it fails.**
- Follow-the-money practice starts from documents and registries (procurement databases, company and land registries, court records) and compares years for jumps (GIJN, https://gijn.org/academy/on-demand-course-follow-money-investigation-basics/; OCCRP's handbook, https://www.editorandpublisher.com/stories/occrp-publishes-spanish-language-follow-the-money-handbook,239988).
- Procurement monitoring uses data-defined red flags across planning to implementation, each a *risk indicator to examine*, not a verdict (OCP 2024 guide, above).
- Auditors set criteria against condition (Yellow Book; LAW §1) and test cost allocations for "a reasonable methodology" and budget inclusion (https://mrsc.org/explore-topics/finance/accounting/cost-allocation). A cost allocation plan is a legitimate mechanism, which is exactly why relabelling a transfer as an allocation is hard to see.
- **Failure modes:** comparing budget-basis figures with GAAP figures; summing budgeted with actual; double counting interfund transfers in a city-wide total; reading a missing figure as zero; trusting OCR digits; matching a vendor by name; reading a threshold-proximity pattern as proof of splitting; reading one source's re-publication as independent corroboration (D48).

**Real availability and cost (checked).** Free and keyless: city budget and ACFR PDFs; Socrata and OpenGov portals (Oakland publishes financial datasets on Socrata, https://data.oaklandca.gov/Financial/Expenses-by-Category/8jia-fa54, and a transparency portal, https://oaklandca.opengov.com/transparency/); Oakland's Form 460 transaction data by year, exportable, and synced to Socrata (https://data.oaklandca.gov/dataset/Campaign-Finance-FPPC-Form-460-Schedule-G-Payments/xuui-k2nt); FPPC Form 700 portal; USAspending bulk data. Free with a key: OpenFEC (1,000 calls an hour; bulk dumps weekly, https://api.open.fec.gov/developers/), so under K1449 it is built in, off by default. Not free: commercial procurement or corporate data (outside, D201). Legal limits: none on holding public financial records; DR6 at publication for individuals.

## 5. Proposed architecture

### 5.1 Why L5, and how it is staged

Bob ruled every construct to L5 (B1 (c), K1432) and named money a construct (K1457). Every core need above sits at L1–L3; the founding case needs L2 (a series across labels and years) and L3 (a restriction set against a flow); L4 and L5 carry Bob's "the things it buys and amount produced" and the scale of real ledgers. The stages follow dependencies (§6): L1 with stage 1's shared ground (`calc-grammar`, `civil-time`, `lines` identifiers); L2 and L3 with stage 2 (`events`, `duties`, the `standards` move in use); L4 with stage 3 (readers, longitudinal calculation); L5 with stages 3–4.

### 5.2 What to adopt from the real world, and what that changes in the provisional draft

- **Phase, not "stage", for the budget cycle (Fiscal Data Package).** FDP's measures carry `phase` ∈ proposed, approved, adjusted, executed, and `direction` ∈ expenditure, revenue (https://specs.frictionlessdata.io//fiscal-data-package/; https://fiscal.datapackage.org/specifications/fiscal-data-package/), with administrative, functional and economic classifications. OpenBudgets.eu likewise splits budget datasets from spending datasets (https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-2044/paper22/paper22.html). Adopt the split: an amount has a **phase** and a **kind**, separately.
- **Obligation against outlay (USAspending, DATA Act).** Files A–C carry budgetary resources, obligations and outlays per account, program activity, object class and award (https://www.usaspending.gov/data-sources?section=data-source-systems). Adopt "committed" and "paid" as distinct actual stages; never call the committed stage "obligation" (DEC-107's taken word).
- **Encumbrance (governmental budgetary accounting).** Appropriation creates authority, an encumbrance reserves it, an expenditure consumes it; an encumbrance is "not an expenditure, and it is not a liability" (https://legalclarity.org/what-is-an-encumbrance-in-governmental-accounting/; NY OSC, https://www.osc.ny.gov/files/local-government/academy/pdf/commonly-misunderstood-concepts-in-budgeting-11-19-19.pdf).
- **Basis of accounting and fund structure (GASB 34 and 54; the ACFR).** Fund statements are modified-accrual, enterprise funds (the Sewer Service Fund) full accrual, budget schedules on the budgetary basis; fund balance is classed nonspendable, restricted, committed, assigned, unassigned (https://www.ncosc.gov/state-agency-resources/statewide-financial-reporting/gasb-resources/gasb-54-fund-balance-reporting-and-governmental-fund-type-definitions). Adopt `basis` on every amount and GASB 54's classes as a balance's `classification`. This is what makes ACFR against OpenGov comparable: their transfer figures may differ by basis and period, a dissolver, not a contradiction.
- **Contracting cycle (OCDS).** Planning, tender, award, contract, implementation with `transactions` (payer, payee, value, date) and `milestones` (https://standard.open-contracting.org/latest/en/schema/reference/). Adopt OCDS's stage names for the procurement chain's money and its party roles (buyer, supplier, payer, payee), already chosen for `contracts_with` (ORGANISATIONS §5).
- **Investigative entity model (FollowTheMoney).** `Payment {payer, beneficiary, amount, currency, startDate/endDate}` and `ContractAward {supplier, contract, amount, currency}` (https://followthemoney.tech/explorer/schemata/ContractAward/). Adopt the field shape for export at L5; payer and payee are registry entities.
- **Performance (GASB SEA, Concepts Statement 5).** Efficiency measures relate resources to outputs ("cost per lane-mile"; https://storage.gasb.org/SEA_fact_sheet-2010-07_DB_LP_KB_DMM_DRB_RHA.pdf). Adopt: unit cost is a calculation whose inputs are money facts and output counts, never a stored field.
- **Red flags as indicators (OCP; IACRC).** Adopt their *form*: a data-defined indicator over a population, reported as an instance to examine with its denominator (https://www.open-contracting.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/OCP2024-RedFlagProcurement-1.pdf; https://guide.iacrc.org/detection/red-flags-of-corruption-bid-rigging-and-other-schemes/red-flag-multiple-contract-awards-just-under-procurement-thresholds/). Refuse their *risk scores* (D53).

**The provisional draft tested.** Kept: money as facts, never one "amount" field; exact decimals with currency; funds as entities with scheme identifiers; ledgers stay tables with row references; rules as standards and duties; "the machine never concludes". Changed: (1) its one `stage` list mixes three axes ("transferred" is a kind, "owed" a balance, "received" a revenue stage, "written off" an adjustment), so §5.3 splits phase, kind and basis; (2) "commitments are duties with money terms" would give an amount two homes (the duty and the money fact), so the **amount lives in `money`** and a duty *cites* it; (3) `money` after `calculations` would stop recipes, query fields and the procurement junction checks from reading money facts, so `money` goes **before** `calculations` (§5.5); (4) PEOPLE's `gave_to`/`received_from` as stored lines would duplicate contribution amounts, so they become **reads over money facts** (§7); (5) a missing piece is added: the **series**, without which the founding case's relabelling question has no home.

### 5.3 Data model

All append-only (D104); author stamped by the control plane (D158); every absent value `undetermined` with why, never zero (D26, D27, consequences R4); every list envelope-bounded (D144); sight per Membership §7.9 (D179).

**Money fact `MNY-`** (record object of a new type `money`, promoted like a consequence part, consequences R14's precedent):
- `amount`: exact decimal as a string with its scale, plus `as_read` (the figure as the source prints it, `$4.2 million`, `(3,400)`); parsed by `calc-grammar` (the parser moved from `consequences/figures.mjs`, K1448), extended to keep decimals exact (today's doubles hold 15 digits, enough for any city budget, but exactness is the rule, ladders §2 MONEY).
- `currency` (ISO 4217); `sign` explicit (credits and reversals are negative, never a separate magic stage).
- `precision`: `exact` | `rounded {to}` | `approximate` (from "about", "nearly") | `range {low, high}`, so "about $2 million" and "$2,097,431" compare as consistent (contradiction's `precision` dissolver, X83).
- `kind` (closed): `revenue`, `expenditure`, `transfer` (interfund, with `from_fund` and `to_fund`), `allocation` (an indirect-cost charge between funds under a cost allocation plan), `payment` (to a party), `contribution`, `gift`, `income` (a person's reported income source), `settlement`, `debt` (principal issued or retired), `balance` (a position at an instant), `fee_charged` (a rate or charge imposed), `other` with a description.
- `phase` (closed): `proposed`, `adopted`, `adjusted` (budget amendments), `actual`. **Actual `stage`** (closed, per direction): outflows `encumbered` (members see "committed") → `incurred` (expenditure or expense recognised) → `paid` (cash out); inflows `assessed` (billed, receivable) → `collected`; `deobligated`, `written_off`, `refunded` are signed adjustments of a named earlier fact.
- `basis` (closed): `budgetary`, `cash`, `modified_accrual`, `accrual`, `undetermined` (a portal that does not say).
- `period`: an EDTF interval or a fiscal period key (`FY2023-24`) mapped to dates by `civil-time` from the profile's `fiscal_year` (K1445); `as_of` for a balance.
- `parties`: `from` and `to`, each `{entity?, fund?, account?, as_written}`: entities are registry ids (body, institution, person, fund, program, contract), resolved with a grade (entities R9–R12); `as_written` keeps the source's string; a payee never resolved stays a string with resolution `undetermined`.
- `classification`: codes with schemes `{scheme, code, label_as_written}` (fund, department/org, object/account, program, function, project), the FDP's administrative/functional/economic dimensions; schemes are profile data (D99).
- `balance_class` for `balance`: GASB 54 class as the source states it.
- `buys?`: `[{item, quantity?, unit?, as_written}]` (L4), read from an invoice, contract or work order.
- `concerns`: event ids it quantifies (`EVT-` payment, transfer, award, adoption, from EVENTS), duty ids it is the money term of (`DUT-`), a contract or fund entity.
- `source`: exactly one: a content extent (page rect, cell, range, table cell) **or** a table row reference `{table_id, row, column}` (ANALYSIS's canonical table) **or** a `CALC-` output (a total). A money fact is a reading of one source; the same transfer in the ACFR and in OpenGov is two facts, joined by `concerns` the same event (corroboration, D48 decides independence).
- `grade`: the reading axis = the source capture's grade capped by the derivation (OCR ≤ C, machine reading labelled, typed transcription attested by a second member per content R24–R25); the party axis = each end's resolution grade. Never composed (D53, X61).
- `asserted_by`: `source` (machine-read from source-native structured data), `member`, `machine_proposed` (stored apart until adopted, D18).
- `supersedes`: a correction is a successor; a restated figure is a new fact citing the restatement (D104, D41).

**The summation rule** (a doctrine row, ladders §10, restated): a total is a `CALC-` output over money facts of one `kind`, one `phase`/`stage`, one `basis`, one `currency` and one period grain; anything else is refused by name (`MONEY_TOTAL_MIXED_STAGE`, `…_BASIS`, `…_CURRENCY`). Interfund transfers inside one reporting entity are flagged when summed city-wide (each appears as out and in). Undetermined members are listed, never zero (consequences R7's rule, generalised).

**Series `MSR-`** (the founding case's object): a member-authored definition of one flow followed over time: `{label, from_fund, to_fund?, kinds, period, inclusion: [{fact, reason, author}], exclusions: [{fact, reason}], proposed_by?}`. Each member's inclusion of a relabelled line ("FY23 'cost allocation – sewer' counted with the franchise fee because …") is an authored act with its reason; the machine may propose inclusions by fund code, amount continuity and period, labelled, never adopted alone (D18). A series is not a theme (D95): it is the member's claim of what counts, and it can be a leg's input through a calculation. It is what Intake §1 calls "one line of evidence (the Sewer Service Fund transfer series)".

**Money table binding** (in `calculations`, not a second store): a canonical table (ANALYSIS: CSV + Table Schema, hashed, bytes in R2) whose schema marks **money roles** on columns: `amount`, `currency`, `kind`, `phase`, `stage`, `basis`, `period`, `payer`, `payee`, `fund`, `account`, `classification`. The roles use `money`'s vocabularies. A row becomes a money fact only when something needs to cite or link it alone (the citability test, X8); aggregates are recipes over the table. A 40,000-row checkbook is never 40,000 objects.

**Rules of movement.** A restriction ("fee revenue only for the sewer system"), a threshold ("competitive bidding over $50,000"), a transfer authority ("transfers require council resolution") is held as a **standard** at its version (LAW; K1446) and, where it binds a body, a **duty** of modality prohibition or power (ORGANISATIONS; K1440). `money` reads them; the check of a flow against one is a labelled computed fact ("transfer out of a fund whose held restriction names sewer purposes; no authorising event held at level 2") raised as a question, never "unlawful" (D117, D241, D275).

**Commitments.** A contract's awarded amount, a grant award, a debt schedule's payments, an MOU's cost are **money facts** (`encumbered`, or `adopted` for a promise in a budget) concerning the contract entity and the award event; the promise to pay or deliver, where a member declares it, is a **duty** whose `performance: pay` cites those facts. "Committed against paid" is one read across them (§5.4).

### 5.4 Reads (all bounded, as of a date or period, undetermined on exhaustion)

- `moneyOf({entity | fund | contract | event | series, period, kind?, phase?})`: facts with grades, the levels searched, `truncated`.
- `flowsFrom({fund | entity, period, depth ≤ 4, fan_out ≤ 500})`: source → fund → transfer/allocation → fund → payee, each hop a cited fact; weakest hop governs; no ranking, no centrality (K1442, D91).
- `authorityChain({payment})`: appropriation (adopted fact and the adoption event) → commitment (encumbered fact, contract, award event) → payment, each link cited or "not held at level n" (D31); never "unauthorised".
- `committedAgainstPaid({contract | grant})`: commitment, change orders, payments, with the difference as a computed fact; payments outside the contract's validity (`lines`' `valid`) shown with dates.
- `reconcile({fact_a, fact_b})`: aligns kind, phase, basis, period and precision; states each dimension that differs (a dissolver: different basis, different period, rounding) or "same dimensions, amounts differ" (a candidate for contradiction's K4 pairing, its own key "two amounts for one transfer", X55).
- `series({id})`: the member's series by period with totals per phase and basis.
- **Junction checks** (moved from progressions R32): data over a procurement instance (threaded by `progressions`) and its money facts: award with no solicitation (already progressions'), signed ≠ awarded, change orders past a stated share, payments past term, a single response. Each yields a queue FINDING "Noticed" with its derivation (queue-producers), never a verdict (D24: a lawful sole-source must not read as a gap).
- **Detectors** (L4): threshold proximity (contracts clustered under a held threshold, same supplier, same period), payment before approval, change-order growth; each defined as data with its population and denominator, run at a member's act per project, reported per instance, false-alarm rate measured before any member sees one (the IDENTIFY pattern, D167, X96).

### 5.6 How it fits the first study's architecture

- **The obligation thread (`duties`):** money supplies the money term and the payments that discharge or miss an occurrence of `pay`; a commitment is a duty citing money facts.
- **`lines`:** standing relations (`funds`, `contracts_with`) say *who funds or contracts with whom*; money facts say *how much, when, at which stage*.
- **`chronology` → `events`:** the payment or transfer is an event; money quantifies it; sequence (payment before award) is computed by `civil-time` from the events' dates.
- **`calculations`:** tables, recipes, totals, unit costs and budget against actuals are calculations over money facts and money-bound tables; `money` is their typed input.
- **`standards`:** rules of movement, thresholds and restrictions at their version; `inForceAt` the transfer's date.
- **`answers` (QUESTIONS):** money reads are plane rule services (non-mutating) the assistant relays, with `ANSWER_FIGURE_UNSOURCED` refusing any figure not from a money fact or calculation.
- **The validity and as-of model (synthesis §3.4):** periods and `as_of` use the one validity value; record time stays apart (bitemporal): "what the FY22 ACFR said" and "what the restated FY22 figure is" are two facts.
- **Total order:** the only change beyond the ladders' list is `money` moving from after `calculations` to after `duties`. No existing `uses` edge breaks (no built module uses `money`).

### 5.7 The AI's role

- *Proposes (labelled, stored apart, adopted by a member's act; D18, K1364):* money facts read from budget books, ACFR schedules and staff reports' fiscal impact (EXTRACT); column roles for a portal table; series inclusions across relabelled lines; payee resolutions by name (grade C until a member confirms, D78); reconciliation explanations as dissolver candidates; detector instances.
- *Writes, attributed to itself (K1443 extended at BOB's level, below):* money facts from **source-native structured data with identifiers at both ends**: a bound portal or checkbook table whose rows carry a fund code (with name), an account code and an amount, once a member has adopted the table's binding; never from a PDF narrative or a scan.
- *Never:* a figure from its own knowledge (B12 (i)); "diversion", "misuse", "improper" or "unauthorised" as its words (brand §3; D117); a risk score or a ranked vendor list (D53, D91); a total across stages or bases.

### 5.8 Doctrine kept, and what the construct strains

Kept: the machine proposes and a member decides (D1, D18); labelled drafts (K1364, D9); basis and grade on every fact, undetermined first-class and never zero (D23, D26, D27, consequences R4); four-level absence ("no payment found" names its level, D31); machine-read digits never raise a grade (D34); conflicting figures both kept (D39); one system per origin (D48); a summary never better than its worst part (D50); no score, no significance, no ranking (D53, D4, D91; Action §4 rule 3); counts and totals are disclosures under sight (D54); no proportions over a sample (D59); closed vocabularies with one home (D90, D198); relations constitutive and untraversed, lines walked bounded (D89, D98); no jurisdiction in code (D99); sequence is not cause and cause is member-authored (D103; DEC-84 (10), K1461); append-only (D104); the plane encodes no law's rules (D113, D-149): thresholds and restrictions are held standards, never constants; a determination is the member's (D117); every row names its actor (D49); DR6 at publication (D61; K1452).

Strained, and contained:
- **Machine writes from ledgers (K1443).** B7 limited machine writes to lines, holders, proceedings and dates; ledgers are the money analogue (identifiers at both ends). Contained by requiring a member's adoption of the binding once per table and by labelling every such fact `source`-asserted.
- **Detectors and DEC-22 ("the machine does not choose what to look into").** Contained by running detectors only at a member's act per project, as data, with denominators and a measured false-alarm gate; it is put to Bob (§8 M-B2).
- **R26's taken word `budget`.** Contained by naming the phase `adopted`, never `budget`, and keeping the group's own money out of the construct (§8 M-B1).
- **`commitment` (a LAW standard kind) against a committed amount.** Contained by the internal stage name `encumbered`; members see "committed" (§8 M-B3).
- **Corpus export.** Contained by widening corpus-export to module tables (X107).

### 5.9 Runtime and deployment, with realistic volumes

- *Facts:* a city instance holds thousands to tens of thousands of money facts (the founding case: nine fiscal years × a handful of transfers and allocations per year, the ACFR schedules' lines, OpenGov's series: a few hundred facts), small rows in the Durable Object's SQLite (10 GB per object; rows ≤ 2 MB; ≤ 100 bound parameters per statement, https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/platform/limits/).
- *Ledgers:* real cities publish 100,000–450,000 payments a year (SF: 8.18M voucher rows over about 18 fiscal years; Cincinnati 1.25M over about 12; Chicago 469K since 1996, https://data.cityofchicago.org/Administration-Finance/Vendor-Payments/pkr3-4xv7/about). A year can exceed the 20 MiB table bound (ANALYSIS); so money tables are **partitioned by fiscal period** (month or quarter) under the bound, held as bytes in R2 and streamed by recipes, never as SQL rows. Oakland's own payment volume is unmeasured (measure first).
- *Optional index:* at a member's act per table, a compact projection (row, amount, fund, payee key, date; about 80 bytes a row) for payee and fund lookups: a city's decade at 150,000 rows a year is about 120 MB, within the object's 10 GB but counted against the storage curve (D142: about 60,800 bundles per 10 GB); stated before it is built.
- *Everyday path:* no aggregation on a member's read; totals and series are stored `CALC-` outputs keyed `sha(recipe, inputs, method)` (K1448), recomputed at acceptance and publication; flow reads are bounded (depth 4, fan-out 500, `truncated` stated); junction checks and detectors run as scheduler consumers or at a member's act, on the one alarm (D139), never a cron.
- *Deployment:* migrations at every instance; `MODULE_ORDER` re-pin; no vendor key (D150); OpenFEC and similar keyed sources built in, off by default (K1449).

### 5.10 Worked example: the founding case carried to L5 without rework

1. **L1 (stage 1).** A member captures the FY2012–21 ACFRs, the City Auditor's February 2022 report and the OpenGov transfer export. From the export (bound once as a money table: columns fund, account, amount, fiscal year; roles adopted by the member), each year's transfer row is a machine-written money fact (`kind: transfer`, `phase: actual`, `stage: paid`, `basis: undetermined` until the portal's basis is held, `from_fund: Sewer Service Fund (3100 + name)`, `to_fund: General Purpose Fund`). From the ACFR (scanned in part), the member types the transfers-out line as attested transcriptions (grade capped by the attestation, content R24–R25). From the auditor's report, "$52.6 million … FY 2012-2021" is a money fact `kind: transfer`, `period: FY2012–FY2021`, `precision: rounded`.
2. **L2 (stage 2).** The member declares a series "Sewer Service Fund → General Purpose Fund, all labels" and includes FY2012–21 "franchise fee" facts; the machine proposes the FY22–26 "cost allocation – sewer" lines by fund pair, amount continuity ($1.6M–$2.8M) and period; the member includes each with a reason or excludes it. `reconcile` aligns ACFR against OpenGov per year: where they differ by basis (modified accrual against budgetary) or by period, it says so; where dimensions match and amounts differ, a contradiction candidate (PROB-2026-0007) reaches the queue.
3. **L3 (stage 2).** Prop 218 Art. XIII D §6(b)(2) and OMC 13.04.080 are held standards; the member declares the fund's restriction as a duty (prohibition, obligor the City). The series set against it yields the computed fact "transfers out of a fund whose held restriction names sewer purposes: $X over FY2012–21 (series total, stage paid); authorising event: none held at level 2 (Legistar searched)". That is the question "Was the $2.1m transfer authorised?" (DEC-16 INQ-2), evidence, never a verdict.
4. **L4 (stage 3).** The FY2026-27 budget's $2.77M (`phase: adopted`) is set beside actuals; the per-year chart is a calculation over the series; the share of sewer charge revenue ("about 10%") is a `share` with its denominator (revenue facts, `kind: revenue`, `stage: collected`).
5. **L5 (stages 3–4).** A watched OpenGov query's next snapshot is diffed by key; a new transfer row arrives as a new fact, told once; the assistant answers "Where did the sewer fund's money go in FY23?" by relaying `flowsFrom` with grades and levels.
At no rung does an earlier object change shape: facts, series, bindings and standards are the same objects read further. Volumes: a few hundred facts and one small table, far inside every bound.

**Second example, a contract and a person's career meeting it (with PEOPLE and EVENTS).** A $2.0M hauling contract (award event, `encumbered` fact), two change orders ($0.6M, `adjusted` encumbrances), payments from the city checkbook (a money table of 150,000 rows a year partitioned by month; 38 rows match the vendor's id and are referenced as facts because a junction check cites them). `committedAgainstPaid` shows $3.4M paid against $2.6M committed; the junction check "payments past commitment" raises a question. The councilmember who moved the award is a `person` (PEOPLE); her Form 700 lists income from the hauler's parent (an `income` money fact, `to: person`, source Form 700 Schedule C); the derived read "received from the supplier's parent while voting on its award" is shown as cited, dated facts, never as a conflict asserted (K1453). Published, the case names her only in her official capacity for the vote (DR6).

**Stage 2 (what bodies owe): MONEY L2 and L3.**
- *Work:* series; flow, authority-chain, committed-against-paid and reconcile reads; junction checks (progressions R32 moved, the `payment` stage added); restrictions and thresholds read from standards and duties; the contradiction key; events links.

**Risks and containment.**
- *False contradictions between sources* (basis, period, precision): `reconcile` and the three axes.
