**The construct.** Bob (K1457): "the series of things that happen in the world and the relationships between these events that investigations must understand." EVENTS holds what happened in the world (a meeting held or cancelled, a vote, an adoption, a contract signed, an award, a payment made, a report issued, a statement made, an email sent, an appointment, an order entered), when it happened and how precisely that is known, who took part and in what role, what it concerned, which records attest it, and how events relate to one another and to what should have happened. It excludes, by the taken word, the group's own acts (`action`, BIO_Action §3; D127) and the record's own acts (promotion, looking, publishing); those keep their own append-only histories and are read beside world events, never merged into them (D111; X104).

**How the work is done, and how it fails (research).** Investigators build the chronology first and test hypotheses against it; every entry carries its source, disputed dates are marked as disputed, and the timeline is "important in proving cause and effect" only alongside other evidence (https://gijc2015.org/2015/10/29/using-hypotheses-and-timelines/; https://zenodo.org/records/18331368). OCCRP's Aleph offers a timeline as "a chronological view on a manually selected set of entities", entities being people, companies and events in FollowTheMoney's model, whose `Event` carries `organizer`, `involved`, `startDate`, `endDate`, `date` and `location` with `proof` and `sourceUrl` (https://gijn.org/resource/using-aleph/; https://followthemoney.tech/explorer/schemata/Event/). Bellingcat validates an informant's play-by-play against independent records and never concludes from one source (https://www.bellingcat.com/resources/2021/11/17/the-data-and-the-dossier-validating-the-wagnergate-operation/). Auditors write the finding's Cause as "the reason or explanation for the condition", an element the auditor must evidence (https://www.gao.gov/assets/d24106786.pdf). Records-request trackers such as MuckRock carry a due date and a status whose move it is (https://help.muckrock.com/Request-status-definitions-19ef88926963815c9bc4e4a671d3cb6b). The failure modes are well known: a date taken from capture or posting time in place of the act's own date (TIME §3; `docket/index.mjs:122`); one wire report counted as several sources (D48); sequence read as cause (post hoc; DEC-14's Metaketa and FOI evidence, archive l.953–960); a cancelled meeting counted as a missing one (`meeting-calendar.mjs:284`); a missing record read as a missing event (four-level absence, D31); and machine event extraction taken as fact: the best supervised systems reach 76.2 F1 on ACE 2005 argument classification, and guideline-driven LLM extraction trails supervised systems by about 42 F1 on strict arguments (https://www.turing.ac.uk/sites/default/files/2025-07/arc_event_extraction_lit_review.pdf; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13518317/).

**Real availability (checked).** Legistar's Web API is open with no key; a query of Oakland's on 2026-10-05 returned 186 meetings dated 2025 and 852 matters introduced in 2025, and a sample of ten 2025 meetings averaged 20 agenda items each (1 to 44); `EventItems/{id}/Votes` gives each member's vote and `Matters/{id}/Histories` each action on a matter; dates arrive as a zone-less `EventDate` beside a separate `EventTime` string, with `EventAgendaLastPublishedUTC` and `EventMinutesLastPublishedUTC` (https://webapi.legistar.com/Help; measured by this analyst, §9). Court registers, codifier pages and budget books arrive as captures (COURTS, LAW, MONEY). Email and internal communications arrive only through records requests and leaks, as documents (office metadata, DEC-5). No paid source is needed for any rung below L5.

## 5. Proposed architecture

**Why L5, and how it is staged.** B1 (c) (K1432) sets every construct at L5, and K1457 puts EVENTS there. L1–L3 are the core: the founding case is a sequence (audit February 2022, recommendations due October 2023, a label dropped in a two-to-three-year window, a request on 19 March unanswered by 30 March; C1: RM §1), and every journey's front door crosses a body's act on a date (TIME §1). L4 is Bob's own "relationships between these events". L5 is realistic because the main structured source (Legistar) is open and keyless, and because extraction stays a labelled proposal. Staging follows dependency: the substrate first (dates, events, attestations), then sequence and process, then relations and duties, then patterns, then assistance (§6).

### 5.1 What to adopt, and why

- **FollowTheMoney `Event` / `Interval`** for the event's core fields (`organizer`, `involved`, `startDate`, `endDate`, `date`, `location`, `proof`), so Aleph-style exports are a rendering (https://followthemoney.tech/explorer/schemata/Event/; https://followthemoney.tech/explorer/schemata/Interval/).
- **CIDOC CRM's split of an event from its time-span and its participants**: E5 Event, P4 has time-span (E52), P11 had participant with P14 carried out by as a sub-role (https://cidoc-crm.org/sites/default/files/Documents/cidoc_crm_version_7.1.3.html). This is why `when` is a value, not a column per precision, and why "carried out by" is a role on a participant, not a separate field.
- **Open Civic Data `Event` and `VoteEvent` / Popolo `vote-event`**: a vote is an event within a meeting with a motion, a result and each member's vote (https://open-civic-data-docs.readthedocs.io/en/latest/proposals/0007.html; https://www.popoloproject.com/specs/vote-event.html; OCD events https://open-civic-data-docs.readthedocs.io/en/latest/proposals/0004.html). TIME already adopted the OCD Event shape for observed meetings.
- **schema.org `eventStatus`** (scheduled, cancelled, postponed, rescheduled) with `previousStartDate`, and `superEvent`/`subEvent` (https://schema.org/Event): a cancellation is a status of the meeting event, not a second event.
- **Akoma Ntoso's two event families**: a document's `lifecycle` events (generation, amendment, repeal) versus its `workflow` (procedural steps) (https://docs.oasis-open.org/legaldocml/akn-core/v1.0/akn-core-v1.0-part1-vocabulary.html). The same split separates here a document's own dates (dated facts) from the world events a process is made of.
- **OCEL 2.0's event-to-object relations with qualifiers** (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.01975): an event relates to many objects (entities) each with a role, and objects relate to objects; that is exactly participants + `concerns`, and it lets process-mining tools read an export without flattening.
- **W3C PROV-O** for the record's own derivations (attestation → event), not for world causation: `wasInformedBy` is about information flow, never about cause (https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-o/).
- **EDTF level 1** (ISO 8601-2) for every civil date, compared three-valued by `civil-time` (TIME's ruling, K1439; https://www.loc.gov/standards/datetime/edtf.html).
- **The Yellow Book's Cause** as the published element a member authors and evidences (https://www.gao.gov/assets/d24106786.pdf), already DEC-84 (10).
- Not adopted: a general event-sourcing store for the world (the record already event-sources its own acts; D104) and any causal-inference method (DEC-14).

### 5.2 Data model

Every object is append-only (D104), every author the control plane's stamp (D158), every absence or unread value `undetermined` with its reason (D26, D27), sight per membership (D179). Two things are held, and kept apart, because one is a reading and the other a claim about the world (D96; Akoma Ntoso's lifecycle/workflow split):

**(1) Dated fact** (the first study's `chronology` content, K1439). A date a document states about itself or about something, as a reader or member read it: `{capture, extent, kind, value (EDTF), zone?, method: reader \| ai \| member, reader_version, grade, decided_by?}`. Kinds (closed): `meeting`, `adopted`, `effective`, `signed`, `entered`, `issued`, `published`, `received`, `hearing`, `period_covered`, `edited`. Written at read time by the reading pipeline (machine-attributed, "reader-stated dates with their method", K1443) and by a re-read job for captures already held; an AI-proposed dated fact is a labelled proposal a member accepts (D18). Read by `ownDate(capture, kind)` and `datesOf(capture)`. This is what progressions R16 (K1444 (ii)), contradiction K4/R25 and the docket need, and it exists whether or not anyone records an event. A `period_covered` is a period of a document, never an event.

**(2) Event** `EVT-` (a happening in the world; the capability ladders' `EVT-`, tested and narrowed):
- `kind`: closed, revised only by spec (D90): `meeting`, `vote`, `adoption`, `enactment`, `signing`, `award`, `payment`, `transfer`, `filing`, `order`, `hearing`, `issuance` (a report, permit, notice), `publication` (by a body), `statement`, `communication`, `appointment`, `departure`, `inspection`, `other` (with words). The register-row, change-kind and group-act vocabularies stay where they are.
- `status`: `occurred` \| `scheduled` \| `cancelled` \| `postponed` \| `rescheduled` \| `undetermined` (schema.org's enumeration; a cancelled meeting is one event, never a missing one; `meeting-calendar.mjs:284` already owes nothing for it).
- `when`: **derived on read, never stored** (D199): the EDTF value or band of the governing attestation, or, when attestations disagree and no member has chosen, the band covering them with `undetermined` and why (D39). A member may name the governing attestation with a reason (an act, appended).
- `where?`: a `place` entity (K1441) or words.
- `participants[]`: `{entity, role, attestation}`. Roles closed: `actor` (CIDOC P14, "carried out by"), `organizer`, `mover`, `seconder`, `voted` with `value` (aye, no, abstain, absent, recused, excused: the profile's vote vocabulary), `present`, `speaker`, `sender`, `recipient`, `copied`, `signatory`, `party`, `payer`, `payee` (role only; the amount is MONEY's), `subject`. Each is an `entities` id (offices, bodies, institutions, people, K1452); a participant read only as a string stays a string in the attestation's passage until resolved, graded as entities R27 grades any resolution (X2).
- `concerns[]`: entity ids (a contract, fund, ordinance, parcel, proceeding, program), each with its attestation.
- `within?`: the event this one is part of (a vote within a meeting; schema.org `superEvent`). One parent at most; a tree, depth-bounded (D144).
- `attestations[]`: `{source: content extent \| ledger entry \| testimony \| structured row (scheme + id, e.g. Legistar EventItemId), stated_when (EDTF), method, grade (the capture or connection grade, never higher, D34), asserted_by: source \| system \| member (X64), at, by}`. Two attestations from one origin system are one source (D48, origin by `provenance.declareOrigin`); independent ones are corroboration (Intake §3a). A testimony attestation carries the member's `observed_at` and credit level (strength R29–R34).
- `declared_by`, `at`; versions on every member change, the earlier readable (D104).

Identity: "the same event" is the same `EVT-` id (K171's rule, generalised). Two records proposing one event are merged only by a member's act, or by the machine where both carry the same source-native identifier (a Legistar `EventId`/`EventItemId`; K1443): never by date-and-name likeness (D25, D78). A wrong merge is corrected forward by a split that cites the reason; dependants get a re-evaluation notice (D41; X73).

**What is not an event** (one home per fact, K1460):
- *A non-event.* "No reply came", "no minutes were posted", "no solicitation was found" is never stored as an event. It is the derived state of an occurrence of a duty or a progression stage (`overdue`, `missing_predecessor`), computed on read with the level searched (D31, D199; DEC-14: a body's non-response is "a dated, capturable, first-party fact", which the derivation supplies without a stored row). Storing it would freeze an absence that a later capture can end.
- *An expected event.* A meeting a recurrence predicts, a deliverable a duty requires, a stage a flow declares: these are TIME's recurrences, `duties` and `progressions`. An expectation becomes an event only when something attests it (X45, D241).
- *The group's own acts* (actions, correspondence the group sent, plans, publication): their own ledgers (X102). A body's reply already recorded as a received correspondence entry stays there; the event store reads it through a registration (below) and never copies it.
- *An amount.* A payment event carries `payer`/`payee` roles and `concerns` the contract; the amount, currency, stage and fund are a MONEY fact that names the event (`event: EVT-`). A checkbook of 40,000 rows is a MONEY ledger table, not 40,000 events; a row becomes an event only when something cites it alone (the citability test, X8).
- *A change to a monitored document.* `minutes_replaced`, `delisted` and the rest stay docprofile change kinds on the observation and monitoring path; a member may record the body's act behind one (`publication` with `status`, attested by both captures).
- *A relation of structure* (who held a post, who reports to whom): `lines`. An `appointment` event may attest a `holds` line's start; the line stays `lines`'.

**(3) Event relation** (evidentiary, not constitutive, so not `entities` R26's kind; kept apart from `lines` and from law relations, D96; X58): `{from EVT-, to EVT-, kind, basis: [passages], asserted_by, grade: {assertion, ends}, valid?, declared_by, at, status: proposed \| confirmed \| withdrawn}`. Kinds (closed, D90):
- `authorises` (the later event was done under the earlier: "this payment was made under that resolution"), held only when a passage says so or a member asserts it with a basis; it never says the authority was sufficient, which stays a determination (B6's powers rule, K1442).
- `answers` (a reply, response or report to a request, report or order between world actors; the group's own `responds_to` stays on its action, State Rules §5.1 amendment).
- `amends` and `reverses` (an event changing or undoing the effect of another: a change order, a rescission, a voided certification, an appeal reversing). For law, the text relation stays in `standards` (`amends`, `repeals`, LAW §5); the enactment event is linked to the standard by `concerns`.
- `stated_cause`: a cause **as a named source states it** ("the audit says the transfers covered the deficit"), attributed to that source, held as its claim (the "exposer's claim" pattern, UC-150; D124 notes). A member's own causal hypothesis is **not** a stored relation: it stays a claim in the working inquiry and reaches a published finding only as an evidenced Cause on a determination, else "cause not established" (DEC-84 (10); conformance R22). Sequence alone never yields one (DEC-14; D103). The machine never proposes a cause that no passage states.
Sequence (`before`, `after`, `during`, `overlaps`) is **never stored**: it is computed by `civil-time` from `when`, three-valued (TIME's rule; ladders §2), so correcting a date never leaves a stale edge. Process position is **not** a relation: it is a placement on a progression (below). The provisional `part_of` (collides with `lines`' `part_of`) becomes `within` (a field, above) and `follows_from` is dropped (it is process position, or causation in disguise).

**Grading.** An event's date grade is the governing attestation's grade, capped by its method (D34, D35): a reader-stated date on a co-attested capture can be A; an EXTRACT-proposed one is at most C until a member accepts it; a member's testimony is D (strength R29). An event relation is graded on two axes, as `lines` are (R-1 O-E1; synthesis §6): who asserts it (a passage, a system rule from source-native data, a member) and how each end resolved; never composed across scales (D53, X61).

**Reads** (derived on read, bounded, `truncated` stated, D144):
- `eventRead`, `eventsFor({entity? | concerns? | participant? | within? | kind? | occurred? , at?})` (≤200 per page);
- `timeline({scope: inquiry | project | entity | proceeding | contract | fund | action, window?, include: world | ours | both})`: events, dated facts and (by registration) the group's own dated acts, ordered by `civil-time`; ties and bands listed `undetermined_within` (review R17's pattern); undated items counted and listed apart, "placed nowhere, never guessed" (CPR §5); world and the group's own acts in two lanes, never interleaved into one sequence that could read our checkpoint as their act (D111; A10);
- `sequence(a, b)` → `before | after | overlaps | undetermined {why}`;
- `relationsOf(EVT-, {kinds, depth ≤ 6, fan_out ≤ 1,000, at})`: a bounded walk, weakest hop governing, undetermined on exhaustion, no ranking (K1442's doctrine, D98, D91);
- `whoWasSent({event | capture, at})`: participants with roles `recipient`, `copied`, `present`, `voted` at a date, each cited. The answer says "sent to", "copied", "present", never "knew" or "read" (D24, D15).

**Registrations, so later modules feed the timeline without a second store** (K31's pattern, P4): `registerEventSource(module, {read})` lets `actions` (correspondence, state history, clocks), `publication` (ratified editions), `docket` (entries) and `escalation` contribute their own dated acts to `timeline`'s "ours" lane. `filings` R9's chronology becomes `timeline({scope: action})` with its six sources; nothing it shows today is lost.

### 5.3 Module changes

**`events`** (new name and scope of the ruled `chronology`, K1439, K1458; layer 5). Holds dated facts, events, attestations, participants, relations and the reads above. About 2,600–3,200 lines (P6). Uses: `record-grammar`, `jurisdictions`, `civil-time`, `record-core`, `membership`, `promotion`, `provenance`, `extraction`, `content`, `entities`. Tables `date_facts`, `events`, `event_versions`, `event_attestations`, `event_participants`, `event_relations`, each declared to purge (record-core R21; D156) and to export (below).

**Position in the total order: after `connections`, before `standards`** (a within-layer position, BOB's; P4). Proposed layer 5: `entities`, `lines`, `local-facts`, `connections`, **`events`**, `standards`, `people`, `progressions`, `duties`, `bias`, `observation-log`, `query-language`, `retrieval`, `calculations`, `money`. Reasons, checked against `build/modules.json` (§9): `events` needs no `standards` read (an enactment `concerns` an `ordinance` entity), while `standards` can then cite an enactment or effective-date event as its `period_basis` (LAW §5's Legistar `MatterEnactmentDate`), which the reverse order forbids; `people` composes events (a person's statements, votes and acts), so it follows `events`, and `progressions` and `duties` read events, so they follow it. No existing `uses` edge breaks: `events` is new; its uses are all earlier. This changes K1439's and the ladders' "after `standards`" (a position, not a layer).

**Extended modules** (requirement changes):
- `docprofile` / `reading-pipeline` / `extraction` (1, 4): readers emit typed dated facts in the profile's date grammar (not only "Month D, YYYY" at UTC midnight); the minutes reader's items, motions, movers, tallies and roster, and the calendar reader's meetings and statuses, become event candidates keyed by the source's own ids (`MeetingDetail.aspx?ID=`, the file number); vendor encodings (a "- CANCELLED" suffix, zone-less `EventDate` + `EventTime`) are profile or site data (TIME's R-1 T-O4 rule). A Legistar Web API reader (Events, EventItems, Votes, Matter Histories) is a generic content type; its output is source-native, so the machine may write those events, participants and votes attributed to itself (K1443; DEC-52).
- `progressions` (5): a placement may name an **event** (with its `when`) as well as a document; R16 anchors on the placed event's or document's own date (K1444 (ii)), undetermined with the capture instant as a bound when none; the deferred out-of-order shape is built; an event may be placed in several instances (one approval, many contracts: CF §11's fork); junction checks (R32) read MONEY facts through the event (amounts stay MONEY's).
- `duties` (5): an occurrence's `evidence` names the event(s) that meet it; the deliverable's date comes from `events` (synthesis §3.1, "never from capture time").
- `conformance` (9): an act is an event. `determine` takes `act: {event: EVT-}` or the existing inline act, which it records through `events` in the same transaction; the act's `actor {role, body}` becomes the event's `actor` participant resolved to the office entity (the `lines` bridge); "the same act" stays one id (K171), now `EVT-`. Existing `ACT-` ids are carried as aliases of their events (a one-time migration; none is reachable by a member today, UI 0). `escalation` R14's exit and `action-plans` R5's `determined_since` follow unchanged in meaning.
- `actions` (9): registers its ledger and history with `registerEventSource`; a received entry may name the world event it records (`event: EVT-`), never duplicated.
- `filings` (9): R9's chronology is the `timeline` read; R18's "every item names its source" holds.
- `contradiction` (6): K4 pairing and R25 ordering read `ownDate` and `civil-time`, not raw strings (fixes `contradiction/index.mjs:598–600`); a `time_or_occasion` coordinate may name the two events; the reversal timeline (DEC-77 (2)) is `timeline` over the two sides' events.
- `docket` (8): an entry carries the statement's own date as a dated fact beside its filing day; a docket `outcome` stays "never evidence" (R17) and becomes an event only from a capture.
- `query-language`, `retrieval` (5): field `occurred:` (inclusive local-day ranges, EDTF bands answered in/out/undetermined), `event:` kind and `participant:`/`concerns:` arms; one meaning arm (spending one of four compound terms, D178; measured before it ships).
- `reevaluation` (7): cause arm `event_changed` (an attestation added or withdrawn moves `when`; a relation withdrawn; a merge or split).
- `strength` (6): independence sees attestations of one event from one origin as one source (X19).
- `monitoring` (10): `per_meeting` follows TIME's recurrence; a watched calendar or register adds observed events mechanically.
- `queue-producers` (11): sequence-anomaly findings come from `progressions` (one producer, X49); the reserved `temporal-expectation-due` is produced by `duties` (synthesis §3.1). No events-only producer is added.
- `corpus-export` (8): widened from bundles to declared module tables (`registerExportTables({tables, rederive})`), so events, attestations and relations travel and are re-derived on import (X107; DEC-112). This also repairs `entities`, `connections` and `progression_*`.
- `case-grammar`, `case-authoring`, `case-checker` (8): a published case may carry a **Timeline** section: the frozen `timeline` of its findings' events at signing, each item with its attestation passage, filtered by the publication rule for people (DR6; X27). Bob's decision (§8 E5).
- `affordances` (11): re-exports the event kinds, statuses, roles and relation kinds by reference (X114); acts `eventrecord`, `eventattest`, `eventmerge`, `eventsplit`, `eventrelate` reasoned; `eventpropose` reversible, machine-only proposals.

**Renames of the taken internal word "event"** (D128; BOB's, internal only): docprofile's significance grade `event`/`notice`/`routine` → `significant`/`notice`/`routine`, and its "shared event catalogue" → "change kinds" (`site-profiles` R13); the queue's DEC-16 "event, one state, N homes" → "notice" in requirement text; `tasks`' "capture events" → "capture notices"; `wizard-scripts`' tally column `event` → `step_kind` at its next migration. The brief's renames are confirmed; "chronology" survives only as the counsel packet's section title.

### 5.4 How it fits the first study's architecture

- **`civil-time`** (L1): every date comparison, band, local day and recurrence; `events` holds values and never computes a day itself (G10).
- **`lines`**: structure and tenures. An `appointment` or `departure` event may be a `holds` line's attestation; "who held the office at the act's date" is `lines.holderAt(event.when)`, joining PEOPLE to a conformance act (X10) without storing a holder on the event.
- **`duties`**: what should have happened; occurrences matched to events; absence stated by level.
- **`standards`**: law's text and its versions; enactment events dated in `events`, read by `period_basis`; the act's date drives `inForceAt` (conformance R3), now with EDTF bands (in/out/undetermined).
- **`progressions`**: the declared flow and its instances; process membership of an event is a placement.
- **`calculations`**: counts over events with denominators (lateness, F1, F2) as recipes; never a stored score.
- **`answers`**: "what happened between X and Y" as a cited `timeline` read, never from the model's memory (B12 (i)).
- **The obligation thread** (synthesis §3; ladders §3): EVENTS supplies what actually happened, so an occurrence's state is matched to events, and "overdue" remains a question (D241).

### 5.5 The AI's role

- **The machine writes, attributed to itself** (K1443's line): dated facts read by a reader with its method; events, participants and votes from source-native structured data with identifiers at both ends (Legistar `EventId`, `EventItemId`, `PersonId`, `MatterId`), shown for review, never a gate.
- **The machine proposes, labelled and stored apart, a member adopting each** (D18, D20): events, participants and relations read from prose (minutes narratives, staff reports, grand jury reports, emails), with the passage beside each; a `stated_cause` only where a passage states the cause, quoted; merges of two events. Proposals are bounded per run and measured on a gold set before members see them (D167): extraction is well below the reliability the record needs (76.2 F1 best supervised arguments, guideline LLMs about 42 F1 behind on strict arguments), so nothing extracted is accepted by default (D19).
- **The machine never** infers cause, links two events by sequence or likeness, states that someone "knew", ranks events by importance (D53), or runs unattended (D13; standing EXTRACT stays a provisional NO, AIR §7.3 point 7).
- **Answers** (QUESTIONS L3): timeline and sequence reads with the window and level stated; "not held" where nothing attests an event.

### 5.6 Doctrine kept, and where the construct strains it

Kept: the machine never concludes (D1); labelled drafts (D9, K1364); basis and grade on every date and relation, undetermined first-class (D23, D26); four-level absence for "did it happen?" (D31); sequence is not cause (D103) and cause per DEC-84 (10) (D108, K1461); referential and temporal kept apart (D96); constitutive relations untraversed, evidentiary ones walked bounded with no ranking (D89, D98, D91); no significance or score (D53; DEC-89); the group's own checkpoints never findings (D111); no jurisdiction in code: kinds, vote values, vendor encodings and date grammars are profile or site data (D99); append-only, corrected forward (D104); one home per fact (D198); derived state never stored (D199); DR6 at publication (D61); actions R9 unchanged (the group's addressee stays an office).

Strained, and how it is contained:
- **DEC-60 / "roles are not types"** (D94; X56, X57, T2). Typed relations between events are claims about the world with their own cited basis, not evidence roles between claims; the record computes over them only to walk and display, never to support or weaken a claim. Stated in the requirement text so no reader takes it as reintroducing support/undercut/rebuttal.
- **K1458's "a member's labelled hypothesis" as a stored `causes`** conflicts with DEC-84 (10), which keeps a hypothesised cause in the working inquiry. This design follows DEC-84 (10); Bob confirms (§8 E2).
- **State Rules §5.1's closed edge vocabulary** (T5): event relations are module rows in `events`, not bundle `references`, so §5.1 is not stretched again; its spec text names the three families (constitutive relations, bundle edges, evidentiary rows in `lines`, `events`, `standards`).
- **D-181 (an action is never a leg)** is untouched: an event is not a leg kind; a finding cites the attestation's passage, as today.

### 5.7 Runtime and deployment, with a worked example at realistic volume

**Volumes** (measured, §1): Oakland's 2025 had 186 meetings and about 3,700 agenda items (186 × the sampled mean of 20); 852 matters were introduced. If a group loads all of them mechanically, with eight members' votes on, say, 40% of items, a year is about 4,000 events, 12,000 vote participants, 8,000 other participants and 8,000 attestations and dated facts: about 32,000 rows. At about 400 bytes a row with indexes, ten years is roughly 130 MB, about 1.3% of one Durable Object's 10 GB (https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/platform/limits/; D142's bundle curve is not spent, because events are rows, not bundles at 176,657 bytes each). Prose-derived events are hundreds per investigation, not thousands.

**The everyday path** (K1432): a `timeline` read over one project is an indexed range scan on `(scope, sort_key)` with the 200-row page and `truncated`; dated facts are written at read time inside the existing promote transaction (a few rows per capture); the Legistar load, re-reads and the meeting-recurrence comparison run on the one scheduler alarm as consumers with zero idle cost (D139), in batches under the 100-bound-parameter and 30 s CPU limits (D178; Cloudflare limits above). Nothing an everyday read does grows with the corpus beyond its page.

**Worked example 1: the founding case across fiscal years.** Events: twelve `transfer` events FY2012–FY2021 (each `concerns` the Sewer Service Fund and General Purpose Fund entities, attested by ACFR pages; amounts are MONEY facts naming them); the Auditor's report `issuance`, February 2022 (`2022-02`, EDTF month precision); four recommendations as `duties` with a commitment due date October 2023 (`2023-10`); a `publication` event "franchise fee label no longer used" with `when` = `2021/2024` (an interval band attested by comparing two budget books), whose sequence against the 2022 audit reads **undetermined**, honestly; the group's CPRA request (its own action, "ours" lane) sent 19 March 2026; the duty occurrence (statute, 10 days, rolled to 30 March by `civil-time`) reads `overdue` with "no received entry at level 2 (document) for this action". The `timeline({scope: project})` shows the two lanes, one band, one undetermined pair and no causal edge; the counsel packet's chronology is the same read. Nothing in it is a new store of an absence.

**Worked example 2: the contract and the emergency** (Bob's sentence, IS §5): a `signing` event (contract signature page, A) dated 2 April; an `adoption` of an emergency declaration (minutes, A) dated 16 April; a `stated_cause`? none: no passage says the emergency caused the award. The `authorises` relation is proposed only if the resolution's text says it authorises the award, and the sequence read answers `after` with both passages; the progression for procurement (need → solicitation → award → contract) shows `missing_predecessor` (no solicitation) with the exception document's date after the signing visible. The member concludes; the record states.

**Worked example 3: a vote and who was present** (Legistar, machine-written): meeting `EVT-` (EventId), item vote `EVT-` within it (EventItemId), eight `voted` participants (PersonId → `person` entities from `lines`' seat import), the motion text as the attestation passage; a minutes capture later attests the same vote, and its `outcome_changed` change kind (a body editing its own minutes) is a `publication` event of the minutes, never a second vote.

**Deployment.** New tables' migrations at every instance; `MODULE_ORDER` re-pinned (membership R83); the profile gains the vote-value vocabulary and the Legistar client name in `systems` (data); the test profile carries a fictional body's meetings (D99). No vendor key, no outside service (D150); the Legistar API is a public source fetched by `acquisition` under the host governor (D157).
