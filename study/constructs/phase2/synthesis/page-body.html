The Constructs Study II

You named three more constructs: people, events, and money. You asked that the research be done with full knowledge, and that the architecture be load-bearing, built on best practice, and aware of how all the constructs fit together. This page is the answer, with the decisions that are yours.

How it was done

Twenty-three readers read every source whole. They covered the whole canon, all your rulings, the design stream's journeys and use cases, and the requirements of 75 modules, each checked against the code. Two workers condensed what they found into a register of 213 rules and system requirements, and a map of 118 interactions between the nine constructs. Three analysts studied people, events and money, each researching how investigators, auditors and journalists actually work and the standards they use. A fourth modelled all nine constructs as one system. Three reviewers then checked every claim against the sources, the code and the web, and walked three real cases end to end. A synthesis joined it all, and I reviewed it.

The three worked cases were the sewer-fund transfers over nine fiscal years (FY 2012–2021); a councilmember's career meeting a contract's award, change orders and payments, with a family tie; and the police consent decree's tasks tracked through events and payments.

The answer

People. Today a member can register a person and match documents to them, and nothing more. Needed: identity that links two records without merging them, so a wrong match can be undone. Careers, credentials, memberships, interests and ties held as dated, cited facts. Who decided, signed or carried out each act. One rule for how a published case names people.

Events. Today nothing holds something that happened in the world. There are only fragments: the government act judged in a determination, votes left as text in minutes, and "overdue" counted from when the group captured a document instead of from the meeting. Needed: one store of events with their participants and roles, timelines, links between events, and a due date matched to what met it.

Money. Today a member can only cite a figure. Needed: amounts held with what kind they are (revenue, spending, transfer), how far they got (proposed, adopted, committed, paid) and on what accounting basis. Money trails a member follows across fiscal years and relabelling. Reconciling two sources of one figure, and commitments set against payments.

Connections hold it together. As you said, connections are the heart of an investigation. Every kind of relationship (a donation, a vote, an award, a payment, a post held, a family tie, an act against a rule) keeps its one home. All of them share one shape and one way to explore them. Your own chain is one exploration:

Donorgave to Campaign committeeacts for Councilmembervoted aye Voteauthorised Awardconcerns Contractpaid under Payments from Fund 1010

Each step is cited and graded, and the weakest step sets the grade of the whole chain. Nothing is ever ranked, and the machine never asserts a connection on its own.

What changes in the six

Time. Every date is recorded as a date-time with its precision and time zone. A document's own date and an event are kept as two separate things. A due date's "met or overdue" is recorded as it was known, so a plan step can wait on it.

Organisations. One kind of link, "holds", covers every post and job, with its capacity. A duty can bind a person where a law does (a Form 700 filer, a lobbyist).

Law and courts. A law's in-force dates can point at its enactment event. Court filings and orders become events, and settlements become money facts.

Analysis and questions. A total stays a calculation and is never stored again as a fact. Spreadsheet figures are content, read when a member asks. The assistant gains the new reads as a recorded widening.

Built to carry the full ladder. Every fact has one home, enforced by checks in the store itself. One id scheme covers every kind of record. Today ids break past 9,999 a year, and that is fixed first. C1

Design Requirement 6, refined

Requirement meaning

The question. Design Requirement 6 lets a published case name individuals "only in their official capacity in connection with specific documented actions", but its examples name only the role ("the Finance Director certified the ACFR"). The record now holds who decided, signed or carried out each act. So may a published case give the person's name, or only the title? And what about people outside any public role, such as a relative, a settlement payee or a sole-proprietor vendor? On one side: accountability that survives staff turnover, and the fact you singled out, that someone signed an order against policy. On the other: defamation if the wrong person is named, and the safety of people who hold no public role.

  • (a) Adopt the draft: "Work products document institutional actions and compliance, and the people who carried them out. A person is named, by name and role, in connection with a specific documented act they performed or took part in: deciding, authoring, signing, approving, implementing (e.g., "Finance Director Jane Doe certified the ACFR"; "the order was signed by Deputy Director John Roe, contrary to policy §4.2"). Accountability belongs to the role and the institution, and also to the person who acted; staff turnover does not extinguish either. If the institution cannot answer for actions taken by predecessor occupants of a role, that failure of institutional recordkeeping is itself a compliance issue. A person outside any official, professional or public role is identified in a published work product only where a documented act of theirs bears on the finding."
  • (b) Title by default. Name only elected and appointed officials and department heads, or where the source itself names the person in the act.
  • (c) Leave the requirement unchanged.

Recommend (a), with two additions to confirm. First, a person in a public or professional role may also be named for a documented position or tie that bears on the finding, not only an act. For example: the councilmember's brother who directs the hauler's parent company, or a registered lobbyist for a client. Otherwise such a person reads only as "a relative of the councilmember". Second, the rest of the requirement stands: the metadata, and disclosing the group's and members' ties to those it examines. One filter checks every person a case names before publication and asks for the basis. Documents the case relies on travel whole and unredacted, as your rulings already require.

C3

The words members see

UX

The question. Members need plain words for what they will now see, and several obvious words are taken ("action", "case", "docket", "obligation", "ledger", "budget"). A clashing word misleads members and the readers of published cases.

  • Connections is the umbrella, with these kinds:
    • positions and memberships
    • ties (family, partners, associates a document states)
    • took part (voted, signed, decided, wrote, carried out, was copied)
    • money (paid, gave, received, transferred)
    • event links (authorised, answered, amended, reversed, gave as the cause)
    • law links
    • owes
    • mentioned together
    • same person?
    • overlaps ("both on the 7-member Police Commission, 2019")
    Each page about a subject, person, fund, contract or proceeding has a "Connections" place and an "Explore connections" act. Never "knows", "network", "conflict" or "suspicious".
  • Events and timeline, with two lanes: "what they did" and "what we did".
  • Money words by context: proposed, adopted and amended for budgets; committed, spent and paid for spending; billed and collected for revenue. A member's followed flow is a "money trail". Never "ledger", "diverted" or "misused" in Civicsmith's own words.
  • People, members and sources (already ruled). Position, career, credentials, memberships, interests, and a person page.

Recommend all, for the design stream to render.

C4

What exploring connections may pass through

Doctrine

The question. The registry's declared groupings ("stands in for", "is part of", "overlaps") are, by a standing rule, never walked, so that a label cannot stand in for evidence. Some connections are computed rather than held: "mentioned together", "met by this event", "in force on that date". Walking a declared grouping could chain into paths no document supports. Leaving out the computed kinds breaks chains like "this payment, under this resolution, against this restriction".

  • (a) Walk every evidenced kind and the computed kinds, each showing how it was derived. Declared groupings are shown beside a node but never walked. Hypotheses are never steps.
  • (b) Also walk declared groupings.
  • (c) Held kinds only.

Recommend (a). It keeps the reason for the old rule and gives you your chains.

C5

How a person's identity is established

Doctrine

The question. People rarely carry an identifier in a document. Under today's rule nearly every person match is grade C (a name) or D (a member's word). What may establish that two records are the same person? Published, a wrong link is defamation. Never being able to say "this is the same Michael Houston" leaves careers and connections unusable.

  • (a) Only an identifier at both ends.
  • (b) Also a name match corroborated by a cited fact true at both documents' dates (the same post, the same licence or filer number) earns grade B. It is a recorded claim with its evidence, linked and never merged. A member's word stays D.
  • (c) A member's confirmation counts as established.

Recommend (b). It is how investigators identify people. Civicsmith's own words never say "is the same person", only "claimed the same person, grade B, because …".

C6

Who inside the group sees facts about people

Policy · safety

The question. Facts read from public documents are shared evidence. A member's testimony, identity claims and interest checks inside a hidden project are that project's own thinking. Who sees which? A member who joined to infiltrate the group would see every person fact shared group-wide. Scoping everything to projects would split one person into many records.

  • (a) Everything group-wide.
  • (b) The registry is group-wide. Facts from public documents follow their document's visibility. Testimony, claims, checks and hypotheses inside a hidden project stay fenced and uncounted. Members' ties and the link from a source to a person get their own narrowest visibility. The exposure is explained at setup.
  • (c) Every person fact limited to its project.

Recommend (b).

C7

Members' own ties

Requirement meaning

The question. Design Requirement 6 requires disclosing ties between the group or its members and those it examines. But members are pseudonymous, and nothing holds their ties today. How are they recorded and disclosed? Honest disclosure is at stake on one side, a member's safety on the other.

  • (a) Each member declares their own ties (employer, relative, business), seen by them and administrators. A case concerning that entity discloses the tie at the attribution level the member chooses. Each publishing member attests at publication that they have no undeclared tie, vendors named in the money included.
  • (b) Administrators may record members' ties.
  • (c) No model; disclosure in prose.

Recommend (a).

C8

Questions the machine raises about people and money

Doctrine

The question. Should the machine ever put "worth a look" in the queue? Examples: a councilmember voting on a contract with a firm that paid her; contracts clustered just under a bid threshold; payments past a commitment. It would catch what members miss, but your rulings say the machine does not choose what to look into, and such items can read as accusations.

  • (a) Only checks a member declares for people, and detectors a member switches on for money. Each is defined by its data and states its denominator. Each raises "Noticed" with its cited derivation, never "conflict" and never a score. None is shown before a measured false-alarm test.
  • (b) The machine raises them on its own.
  • (c) None.

Recommend (a).

C9

How the group looks people up

Policy

The question. Much of a career lives on people's own pages and on login-gated platforms. Looking someone up can tip them off. Automated scraping against LinkedIn's terms ended in a permanent injunction and $500,000 (hiQ v. LinkedIn). Complete careers are at stake on one side, legal exposure and the group's safety on the other.

  • (a) Public registers and public pages by default. A private individual's own site or a login-gated platform only by a member's own act in their own browser. Never automated collection against a platform's terms. No paid people-search vendor. Data-use limits kept (FEC). Members' lookups never logged.
  • (b) Allow automated collection of public profiles.

Recommend (a).

C10

Removing a personal fact, and lawful demands

Policy

The question. The record may hold any cited personal fact (K1455), and it is append-only and published in signed editions. What may remove a fact, and how far does removal reach? California's Gov. Code 7928.215 requires any person or association to take down an official's home address or phone number within 48 hours of the official's written demand.

  • (a) Remove, leaving a marker, only material that is unlawful to hold, confidential material, a recorded court order, or a lawful demand of a kind the jurisdiction profile lists. Addresses and phone numbers are never published. A wrong match or a person's request is otherwise corrected forward, with notice. Removal reaches this instance, future exports and future editions. Past published editions stay as they are, and a later edition states the correction.
  • (b) Also remove on any person's request.
  • (c) Also withdraw published editions.

Recommend (a). It mirrors your open B18 (an order is complied with, never silently).

C11

A timeline in a published case

Requirement scope

The question. Should a published case carry the timeline of its findings? A timeline helps readers understand sequence. But it names people in a sequence, and must be reproducible.

  • (a) Yes. It is frozen at signing, each item shows its source, the two lanes are never mixed, and people appear as C1 rules.
  • (b) No; members write sequence in prose.

Recommend (a).

Decided at my level, reported

Order and modules. The new modules events, money, people and explore, plus a small connection grammar. Events replaces the planned chronology. Every upward need is met by registration. A minimal events store comes first, because other modules depend on it.

One home per fact. Payer and payee live on the money fact, never on the event. A duty cites amounts and never holds one. A total is never re-entered as a fact. Hypotheses live with the inquiry, and the store refuses them in evidence.

Exploring. Up to 8 steps by default and 10 at most, 1,000 links per step, 5,000 nodes in total, and very common nodes (a city-wide fund) named rather than walked. Your seven-step chain fits, and is measured on real data first.

The machine. It writes only facts carrying a source's own identifiers (Legistar meetings and votes, licence and filer records), and only for what a member follows or asks for. It proposes everything else, labelled.
