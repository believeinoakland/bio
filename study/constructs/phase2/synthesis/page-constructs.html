Capability architecture · BOB #112 · 5 October 2026

Civicsmith Constructs

What Civicsmith must understand to support the work groups bring to it: time, organisations and their obligations, people, events, money, law, courts, analysis, and questions in plain words. For each: where it stands today, the full ladder of capability it is to reach, one architecture that carries all nine, the stages in which to build it, and the decisions that are yours.

How this was established

Readers read every source whole: the canon, all your rulings, the design stream's journeys and use cases, and the requirements of every module that bears on these constructs, each checked against the code. Analysts researched how investigators, auditors, journalists and watchdog groups actually do this work, and the standards they use. One analyst modelled the constructs as one system. Reviewers checked every claim against the sources, the code and the web, and walked real cases end to end: the sewer-fund transfers over nine fiscal years (FY 2012–2021); a councilmember's career meeting a contract's award, change orders and payments, with a family tie; and the police consent decree's tasks tracked through events and payments. The evidence is on branch study/constructs. The canon form is the capability ladders document.

The answer

Today Civicsmith supports each of these only at its lowest levels, and a member can reach even less than is built. No member can yet ask the assistant a question in plain words.

Connections are the heart of an investigation. A donation, a vote, an award, a payment, a post held, a family tie, an act against a rule: every kind of relationship keeps one home. All of them share one shape and one way to explore them. Each step is cited and graded, and the weakest step governs. The machine never asserts a connection on its own and never scores anyone for being "connected". A chain like this one is one exploration:

Donorgave to Campaign committeeacts for Councilmembervoted aye Voteauthorised Awardconcerns Contractpaid under Payments from Fund 1010

One thread runs through the civic record: what a body owes, to whom, by when, under which authority. Organisations and people say who owes it and who acted. Law supplies the authority. Time supplies the due date. Events say what actually happened. Money says what was promised and paid. Courts supply orders that create duties. Analysis measures performance. Questions read all of it.

The substrate comes first. Each stage builds services, not screens, and publishes them so the design work knows what it can build on.

The ladders, rung by rung

How to read a ladder

Each rung is defined by what a member can do, so climbing a rung means a new kind of work a group can finish. Rungs build on each other. Built means the code exists and passes its tests; usable today means a member can do it through today's screens. Every construct is to reach L5, in dependency order, within realistic resources and without slowing everyday use.

Time

Target: L5. The first stages reach L3, and L4 for document dates and timelines; L5 follows once the assistant runs.

L0

Record time. Prove what existed when. Every capture is timestamped, every version of a page is kept, and the record's own history is dated and never rewritten.

"What did the budget page say when we captured it on 3 March?"

Usable todayTarget
L1

Stated dates. Type a date, with its reason, on the group's own action. The system marks it overdue the day after and reminds you only when you asked.

"Remind me on 14 October that the Clerk's reply is due."

Usable todayTargetOverdue is marked on the UTC day, hours early: a correction.
L2

Computed deadlines on the local calendar. The system proposes the due date and counts it the way the rule does. That covers calendar or business days, the office's own holidays, time zone and hours, and rolling past a weekend. It covers extensions, and periods counted back from an event or in hours. It also counts dates derived from the event they must precede (K1431). Where the calendar cannot settle it, it says "undetermined, because…".

"Our records request went in Thursday 19 March. When is the City late?" Answer: Monday 30 March, ten days, rolled past Sunday, per CCP §12.

Partly builtTargetBusiness days and office holidays are built, none of it is reachable, and the roll-forward, the local day and extensions are missing.
L3

The civic calendar and what bodies owe. Before anything is published, follow a body's meeting schedule. Track what it owes: the agenda before a meeting, the minutes after, reports, budgets before their fiscal year, promised dates, contract dates. Each is measured from the real event's date. Fiscal years. Which law was in force, and who held an office, on a given date.

"Minutes for the 28 July committee meeting were due 18 August and aren't up." "The staff report has to be out before the 12 June vote."

Partly builtTargetOnly fragments: overdue findings measured from when the group captured a document, not from the meeting.
L4

Time as evidence. Use a document's own dates, including approximate ones ("about 2019–2021"), as graded evidence. Build a cited timeline across documents. Tell legitimate change over time from contradiction. Measure lateness as a pattern with its denominator. Read a page or a search as it stood on a date.

"Minutes were late at 38 of 41 meetings since January 2024." "The emergency was declared two weeks after the contract was signed."

Partly builtTargetOnly the version history of pages, and one action's chronology in the counsel packet.
L5

Assisted. Ask in plain words and get cited answers. Proposed dates are labelled as the machine's, for a member to adopt.

"What is due this week?" "What was in force when the contract was signed?"

Not builtTarget

Organisations

Target: L5. The first stages reach L4 for one city's bodies; sharing between groups follows (B22, ruled).

L0

Free text. A body or office is only a name typed into a field.

The capture says "City of Oakland".

Usable todayTarget
L1

A registry. Register bodies, offices, people, funds and contracts, with their other names. Documents are matched to them with a grade saying how sure the match is. "Everything about X". Address an action to an office.

"Show me everything we hold about the Controller's Bureau."

Usable todayTargetThe correction acts have no screen.
L2

Structure as of a date. Dated, sourced lines between bodies and offices: part of, reports to, oversees, appoints, funds, contracts with or acts for, responsible for, holds the records of. Who held each office, and when. Renames and mergers. The identifiers source systems use.

"Who was Finance Director when the 2022 ACFR was certified?" "Who audits the Port?"

Not builtTargetOnly an undated "member of" exists.
L3

Obligations held. Who owes what to whom, by when, and on what authority: a law, an order, a contract clause, a commitment, or the event it must precede. Duties, prohibitions and powers, each with its source in force. An office's duties and powers in one place.

"The franchise says the hauler must report tonnage monthly. Hold that as an obligation."

Not builtTargetDeclared flows exist, but with no one who owes and no source.
L4

Declared against observed. The system works out each obligation's occurrences: met, late, overdue, discharged or undetermined, and why. An overdue one raises a question in the queue, never a verdict. It reads which powers are in force, walks chains of accountability as of a date, and counts patterns.

"Minutes for the 4 August meeting are 21 days overdue." "Who answers for this, then and now?"

Partly builtTargetA fragment only: progression overdue findings.
L5

A shared civic model. Structure and obligation registers shared between groups, in standard formats.

"Every group working on the Port uses the same map of who reports to whom."

Not builtTarget

L0

Names in text. A person is a name found in documents.

Searching for "Jane Doe".

Usable todayTarget
L1

A registry of people. Register a person with their other names; documents are matched to them with a grade saying how sure the match is; "everything about this person".

"Everything we hold about Jane Doe, each match graded."

Partly builtTargetThe person kind exists on the Subjects screen; nothing reads a person as a person.
L2

Positions, career, credentials, statements. Who holds each post, by name, and who held it before. Jobs inside and outside government; schools and degrees; licences with their status; military service; memberships of associations, clubs and political groups. An organisation's staffing on any date. Everything a person said or did, in date order. Each fact cited and dated.

"Who is the deputy director of city planning, and what did they do before?"

Not builtTarget
L3

Interests and money. Statements of economic interests, gifts, campaign contributions given and received, lobbying registrations, company officer roles. A possible conflict raises a question in the queue, never a verdict.

Not builtTarget
L4

Who knows who. Overlaps between people over time: the same employer, unit, board, school and years, campaign or address, each cited. Shown as facts, never as "they know each other", and never as a ranking of who is most connected. Patterns such as regulators moving to the companies they regulated.

"The planning director and the lobbyist both worked at Smith & Co. from 2015 to 2018."

Not builtTarget
L5

Assisted, imported, watched. Ask about a person in plain words; import public registers (campaign finance, lobbying, licensing, company filings); be told when a watched register changes.

"Tell me if she files a new statement of interests."

Events

Target: L5. A minimal store of events comes first, because law, organisations and money point at events; timelines and duties met by events follow; relations between events and patterns after.

L0

Dates in documents. Dates are text; every capture is timestamped; the group's own histories are dated.

"What did the page say on 3 March?"

Usable todayTarget
L1

Events held. Record what happened from cited passages: a meeting, vote, signing, award, payment, order or statement, with its date-time and precision, place, participants by role (including who decided, wrote, signed or carried it out) and what it concerns. Several documents can attest one event. A document's own dates are held separately from the events they may attest.

"Council approved the hauler's contract on 12 March 2019 (minutes, item 7)."

Not builtTargetFragments only: the government act judged in a determination; votes left as text in minutes.
L2

Timelines and process. A cited timeline for any scope, with "before", "after" or "undetermined"; events placed on a declared process so missing, overdue and out-of-order steps count from the event's own date.

"Was the emergency declared before or after the contract was signed?"

Not builtTarget"Overdue" is counted today from when the group captured a document.
L3

Relations and what should have happened. One event authorising, answering, amending or reversing another, each cited and graded; cause only as a source states it; a duty's due date matched to the events that met it, recorded as met or overdue.

"This payment was made under that resolution."

Not builtTarget
L4

Reconstruction and patterns. Who was sent, copied or present, and when; statements in order; anomalies of sequence and lateness with their denominators, raised as questions; a timeline in a published case.

"Minutes were late at 38 of 41 meetings since January 2024."

Not builtTarget
L5

Assisted, imported, watched. Events and relations proposed from documents, labelled, at a member's request; "what happened between X and Y" in plain words; imports from structured sources.

"What happened between the audit and the budget vote?"

Not builtTarget

Money

Target: L5. Money facts come early, with calculation; trails, reconciliation, rules and commitments next; what money buys and delivers after.

L0

Figures cited. Cite the passage, cell, range or table that holds an amount; formulas and hidden sheets are evidence.

"Cite the ACFR's transfer figure."

Usable todayTarget
L1

Money facts held. An amount as its source states it, with its kind (revenue, spending, transfer, payment, contribution and others), how far it got (proposed, adopted, committed, paid), its accounting basis, period, payer, payee and fund, one source and a grade. Totals only within one kind, stage, basis and period.

"FY2023-24: $2.1M transferred from the Sewer Service Fund to the General Purpose Fund."

Built, not reachableTargetOnly figures after a determination, and fee quotes.
L2

Flows and trails. Money followed from its sources through funds to payees; a money trail a member follows across fiscal years and changes of label; a payment's chain of authority; two sources of one figure reconciled, naming the difference (basis, period, rounding).

"Do the transfers after FY2021 continue the franchise fee under new labels?"

Not builtTarget
L3

Rules and commitments. Restricted funds, fee limits, thresholds and transfer authority held as law; commitments set against payments, change orders included; any mismatch raised as a question, never a violation.

"The contract was $2.0M; we've found $3.4M paid; $0.6M of change orders held."

Not builtTarget
L4

What money buys and delivers. Purchases and quantities; unit costs over years; budget against actuals across years; patterns with denominators; fact-based rankings.

"Cost per pothole filled, 2019 to 2025."

Not builtTarget
L5

Captured, assisted, watched. Whole ledgers and registers captured as tables and read into facts when a member asks; proposals from budget books, labelled; told when a watched source changes.

"Where did the sewer fund's money go in FY23?"

Law

Target: L5. The first stages reach L3 in the record and L4 in what the AI prepares; imports and legislative watching follow.

L0

Law as plain evidence. Capture a code page, an ordinance or a contract and cite passages, like any document. Nothing knows it is law.

"Cite paragraph (b) of this captured page."

Usable todayTarget
L1

Law named. Type citations: an action's governing laws, a deadline's basis. Ordinance numbers and code sections are recognised inside documents. Find every document that cites a section.

"Every agenda item and staff report that cites Ordinance 13579."

Usable todayTargetOnly just: citation strings on actions, and references and backlinks.
L2

Law held, during investigation. Declare a standard from its captured text: citation, issuer, level, the period it was in force, and what replaced it. Ask "was it in force on this date?" A question, a flow or a deadline can cite a held standard. The assistant proposes candidate standards, with their captured text.

"Which ordinance says how fast potholes must be fixed? Hold it as our standard."

Built, not reachableTargetBuilt, but after Publication: no screen, and investigation cannot use it.
L3

Law structured and versioned. An ordinance's versions over time, and its sections. Amendments, repeals and recodification are links that cite the amending text. Definitions and cross-references. Which law ranks above which. How far behind the code publisher is, stated.

"The transfer was in FY22. What did §13.04.080 say then, and what changed it?"

Not builtTarget
L4

Law applied and explained. The assistant prepares a side-by-side for a member to evaluate: what the law requires, what the city did, what is unknown. It proposes which rule may prevail and gives a labelled reading of a rule or a charge. Determinations come after publication, then breach actions.

"What's this sewer maintenance charge on my water bill?"

Partly builtTargetDeterminations and the comparison store are built, with no screen and no AI caller.
L5

Law shared and watched. Import state and federal law, watch legislatures for amendments, and link court interpretations.

"Tell us when Council amends the section our finding cites."

Courts

Target: L5. The first stages reach L3; precedent and procedural reasoning (B12 (iv)) follow.

L0

Documents. Capture any court document and cite a passage. Record the group's own filing, or a decision, as correspondence on its action.

"Attach the judge's order to our petition."

Usable todayTargetWatching a court page is built but cannot be switched on.
L1

Court-aware reading. Court documents are read by type: a register of actions, an order, an opinion, a grand jury or audit report with its findings and recommendations. Case numbers and citations are recognised. A citation is verified against a held copy, or marked "not verified". A decision can be held as a standard.

"Paragraph 12 of the order says the City must produce within 30 days."

Partly builtTargetA decision can be held as a standard, with pin cites, but nothing is reachable.
L2

A proceeding followed. A proceeding is a subject: court, number, kind, and parties by role (bodies and people, each with their role). Related cases are linked. Each capture of the court's register flags new entries. Stages are tracked, with missing and overdue. Dates carry the order that set them. The group's own action links to its proceeding.

"The city is a defendant in the sewer-fee suit. Where does it stand? Tell me when the judge rules."

Not builtTarget
L3

Duties from proceedings. Paragraphs of orders and decrees, and grand jury and audit recommendations, are held as obligations an office owes by a date. Required replies, such as a grand jury response within 60 or 90 days, run on a clock. A monitor's or auditor's reported status is quoted beside the group's own reading.

"Did the Council answer the grand jury within 90 days, and did it do what it said?"

Not builtTarget
L4

Interpretation and precedent. A decision is linked to the provision it interprets, with its later history (reversed, vacated). The assistant proposes precedent, labelled.

"Is this case still good law, and which provision did it read?"

Not builtTarget
L5

Procedural reasoning and patterns. Filing windows and standing questions. Patterns across proceedings.

"What has the city paid in settlements since 2019?"

The architecture

One architecture for all nine

The modules are in one order, and a module may use only those before it. Where a later module needs to feed an earlier one, it registers with it. Law, local calendars and calculation move down from the Action layer, so investigation can use them.

One home per fact. Each fact lives in exactly one place, enforced by checks in the store itself. Payer and payee live on the money fact; a duty cites amounts and never holds one; a total is a calculation and never a stored fact; a document's own date and an event are two things.

Shared models. One registry of subjects, where records are linked and never merged. One way of saying when something was true, as a date-time with its precision and zone, or as a pointer to an event. One grade family. One id scheme. One table declaration that governs export and removal.

Hunches and signals. Hunches, hypotheses and the machine's signals (importance, suspicion, a possible cause, combined scores) live in their own layer, labelled, each showing its method. Members may build on them, but they are never facts or evidence and never move a finding.

Built for real volumes. For one city: about 190 meetings and 850 matters a year, a payroll of about 5,000, and 100,000 to 450,000 payments a year in comparable cities. Ledgers stay captured tables and are read when a member asks. Heavy work runs in slices or off to the side, so members are never slowed.

How to proceed

Stages, in dependency order

Stages, not a tranche plan: none opens until you say so. Each stage measures first and builds services that the design work then builds screens on.

0

Runnable and correct

The assistant runs on the group's or a member's own Claude account, with usage recorded and a ceiling. The defects below are fixed, ids no longer break past 9,999 a year, and every module's tables travel with a group's export. Measurements first: money sources, how well dates are read, real chains of connections.

1

The shared ground, identity and amounts

The calendar engine, calculation, the connection grammar, a minimal store of events, dated lines between bodies, offices and people, and law and local facts moved down. One rule for naming people in published work. Then people's posts and careers, and money facts. Unlocks: a records request's due date from the law; who decided and signed an act; "who is the deputy director of planning, and who held it before"; the founding case's transfers held with stage and basis; "ask the record" in plain words.

2

What bodies owe, timelines, trails and connections

Duties met or missed, with a plan step that fires when a reply is overdue. Timelines and a body's meetings and votes. Following a court case. Money trails, reconciliation, rules and commitments. Interests and ties. The one exploration of connections. Unlocks: "was the transfer authorised?"; committed against paid on a contract; a donation-to-fund chain as one exploration; a member's own workbook in the record.

3

Applied and assisted

The assistant applies law, deadlines and figures from the system, and proposes people, events and figures at a member's request. Patterns, budget and financial-report readers, unit costs, precedent, charts.

4

Exchange

Importing public registers (campaign finance, lobbying, licences, company filings) and state and federal law. Standing questions. Sharing between groups.

Not choices

Corrections to make

Defects and wrong wording found along the way. None needs your decision. Each becomes an entry for the first tranche you open.

  1. The deadline calculator counts from the wrong receipt, so Oakland's one deadline rule never computes when the City says nothing.
  2. No roll-forward past weekends and holidays, and the weekend is written into the code instead of the jurisdiction profile.
  3. "Overdue" is marked on the UTC day, seven to eight hours early for a California office. It should use the office's local day.
  4. "Overdue" in progressions counts from when the group captured a document, not from the event.
  5. Search date ranges drop the last day; impossible dates such as 31 February are accepted.
  6. The Oakland profile ships its deadline rule and its five offices without a source.
  7. Record ids break past 9,999 a year: twelve checks in the code refuse the 10,000th id.
  8. A group's export leaves module tables behind, including the registry of people, so a case cannot yet be fully recreated without Civicsmith.
  9. The assistant code accepts only an API key, though the setup page rightly asks for a Claude subscription; its model calls have no caching, usage record or spending bound.
  10. Some canon documents still cite California law directly, which belongs in jurisdiction profiles.
Already ruled
  • Scope: every construct reaches L5, staged by dependency, within realistic resources and without slowing everyday use.
  • People: tracked fully, including careers, credentials, memberships, interests and ties, with no special rule for family or sensitive facts. Who decided, wrote, signed or carried out an act is recorded.
  • Names: events and timeline; members, sources, people; the construct called money.
  • Money: followed whoever's it is, the group's own included, with no mark that it is the group's.
  • Time: every world fact is a date-time with its precision; due dates may follow from dependencies, and are recorded as met or not, so a plan step can wait on them.
  • Hunches and signals: hunches, hypotheses and the machine's signals have a place, labelled, but are never facts or evidence.
  • Rankings: rankings by a measured quantity are analysis, stored and kept up to date.
  • Extraction: targeted, at a member's request, and ongoing.
  • Connections: the heart of the architecture.
  • What the machine may say about law (B12): nothing from the model's own knowledge; labelled, cited readings of held statutes, ordinances and policies, with "legal information, not legal advice"; court rulings quoted only, until their accuracy is measured; procedural facts shown as facts; procedural reasoning laid out condition by condition, never what to file.
  • The assistant can run on a Claude subscription; the substrate comes first.
  • The assistant's account (B16): the assistant is optional, offered at setup, where the administrator is told that questions, and the material read to answer them, go to Anthropic under the group's account; the group's account serves everyone, and a project's or member's own account takes precedence where set.
  • What the assistant may suggest (B17): suggestions are optional, because each one costs model use. They are off until switched on by whoever's Claude account carries the assistant: the group's administrator for the group's account, or the project or member for their own. When on, suggestions come only from material the member brought or chose, are labelled, and are adopted only by the member.
  • A court order to remove or redact a published case (B18): the way to comply is designed and built now. An order addressed to the group is complied with, never silently: the order is captured, a signed docket entry names it, and the edition is stamped. Material a court later seals, or unseals, follows the same principle.
  • Standing questions (B21): a member may write a standing question with a cadence and an end date. A saved search re-runs on the schedule first, at almost no cost; the AI runs only when that finds something new, read-only, bounded, within the use limit of the account that carries the assistant, and its answer reaches the queue labelled as the assistant's. Every other AI run still starts only when a member acts. The standing question is the member's own, seen only by them.
  • Sharing between groups (B22): a group shares a pack (a body's structure, obligations, held law, people, events, money trails) only by an explicit act, as it publishes a case. A receiving group accepts it by a reasoned act; the pack is recreated in its record, never installed, with no inherited grade. What it holds about people travels with it, each fact with its citation, adopted by the same reasoned act.
  • Naming people in published cases (C1): a person materially involved is named, by name, title or role as the finding needs, with their documented acts, the positions they held, and their ties and financial interests where these bear on the finding. Naming consistently lets readers and other groups see the same person, or people with material ties, across cases that seem unconnected. A person outside any public or professional role is named only where their act, tie or interest bears on the finding. Every named person has a recorded basis, checked before publication.
  • The rules on people (C2): members may search, sort and rank the people the record holds by any held fact; judgment scores only as labelled signals. A harmed party may be a person, named in published text only with consent or where Design Requirement 6 allows. The group's action is addressed to an office. Who decided and signed a government act is recorded, while the finding still judges the office. "Never name an individual except in official role" binds only Civicsmith's own voice and published pages. The old limits on holding people are retired, except that imports never bulk-copy home addresses or phone numbers. The other rows as recommended.
  • The words members see (C3): "Connections" is the umbrella, with its kinds (positions and memberships, ties, took part, money, event links, law links, owes, mentioned together, same person?, overlaps), a "Connections" place on every subject page and an "Explore connections" act; never "knows", "network", "conflict", "suspicious" or "most connected". "Events and timeline", with lanes "what they did" and "what we did". C4

What exploring connections may pass through

Doctrine

The question. The registry's declared groupings ("stands in for", "is part of", "overlaps") are, by a standing rule, never walked, so that a label cannot stand in for evidence. Some connections are computed rather than held: "mentioned together", "met by this event", "in force on that date". Walking a declared grouping could chain into paths no document supports. Leaving out the computed kinds breaks chains like "this payment, under this resolution, against this restriction".

  • (a) Walk every evidenced kind and the computed kinds, each showing how it was derived. Declared groupings are shown beside a node but never walked. Hypotheses are never steps.
  • (b) Also walk declared groupings.
  • (c) Held kinds only.

Recommend (a). It keeps the reason for the old rule and gives you your chains.

C5

How a person's identity is established

Doctrine

The question. People rarely carry an identifier in a document. Under today's rule nearly every person match is grade C (a name) or D (a member's word). What may establish that two records are the same person? Published, a wrong link is defamation. Never being able to say "this is the same Michael Houston" leaves careers and connections unusable.

  • (a) Only an identifier at both ends.
  • (b) Also a name match corroborated by a cited fact true at both documents' dates (the same post, the same licence or filer number) earns grade B. It is a recorded claim with its evidence, linked and never merged. A member's word stays D.
  • (c) A member's confirmation counts as established.

Recommend (b). It is how investigators identify people. Civicsmith's own words never say "is the same person", only "claimed the same person, grade B, because …".

C6

Who inside the group sees facts about people

Policy · safety

The question. Facts read from public documents are shared evidence. A member's testimony, identity claims and interest checks inside a hidden project are that project's own thinking. Who sees which? A member who joined to infiltrate the group would see every person fact shared group-wide. Scoping everything to projects would split one person into many records.

  • (a) Everything group-wide.
  • (b) The registry is group-wide. Facts from public documents follow their document's visibility. Testimony, claims, checks and hypotheses inside a hidden project stay fenced and uncounted. Members' ties and the link from a source to a person get their own narrowest visibility. The exposure is explained at setup.
  • (c) Every person fact limited to its project.

Recommend (b).

C7

Members' own ties

Requirement meaning

The question. Design Requirement 6 requires disclosing ties between the group or its members and those it examines. But members are pseudonymous, and nothing holds their ties today. How are they recorded and disclosed? Honest disclosure is at stake on one side, a member's safety on the other.

  • (a) Each member declares their own ties (employer, relative, business), seen by them and administrators. A case concerning that entity discloses the tie at the attribution level the member chooses. Each publishing member attests at publication that they have no undeclared tie, vendors named in the money included.
  • (b) Administrators may record members' ties.
  • (c) No model; disclosure in prose.

Recommend (a).

C9

How the group looks people up

Policy

The question. Much of a career lives on people's own pages and on login-gated platforms. Looking someone up can tip them off. Automated scraping against LinkedIn's terms ended in a permanent injunction and $500,000 (hiQ v. LinkedIn). Complete careers are at stake on one side, legal exposure and the group's safety on the other.

  • (a) Public registers and public pages by default. A private individual's own site or a login-gated platform only by a member's own act in their own browser. Never automated collection against a platform's terms. No paid people-search vendor. Data-use limits kept (FEC). Members' lookups never logged.
  • (b) Allow automated collection of public profiles.

Recommend (a).

C10

Removing a personal fact, and lawful demands

Policy

The question. The record may hold any cited personal fact (K1455), and it is append-only and published in signed editions. What may remove a fact, and how far does removal reach? California's Gov. Code 7928.215 requires any person or association to take down an official's home address or phone number within 48 hours of the official's written demand.

  • (a) Remove, leaving a marker, only material that is unlawful to hold, confidential material, a recorded court order, or a lawful demand of a kind the jurisdiction profile lists. Addresses and phone numbers are never published. A wrong match or a person's request is otherwise corrected forward, with notice. Removal reaches this instance, future exports and future editions. Past published editions stay as they are, and a later edition states the correction.
  • (b) Also remove on any person's request.
  • (c) Also withdraw published editions.

Recommend (a). It mirrors your B18 ruling (an order is complied with, never silently).
