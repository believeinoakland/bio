2. **California home-address law is misstated** (§1 "Legal limits"). The study says Gov. Code 7928.205 bars "state and local agencies, not private groups". In fact Gov. Code **7928.215** says: "No person, business, or association shall publicly post or publicly display on the internet the home address or telephone number of any elected or appointed official if that official has made a written demand". The material must come down within 48 hours, it must not be transferred to anyone else, and a demand lasts four years (https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/government-code/gov-sect-7928-215/; https://law.justia.com/codes/california/2024/code-gov/title-1/division-10/part-5/chapter-14/article-3/section-7928-215/). Section 7928.210 separately bars posting with intent to threaten. **Correction:** a group *is* bound once it receives a demand. This bears on three things. (a) The `address_as_stated` / `contact_as_stated` person-fact kinds. (b) P-B9: on lawful demand, a fact must be removed from public display (public-read, published editions' outward surfaces). That is a removal class P-B9 (a) leaves out, and its "not on a person's request" recommendation is wrong as a general rule. (c) B22 sharing, because the statute forbids transfer after a demand. The product must stay jurisdiction-free, so the mechanism is generic: a "lawful demand" class in I-19 or B18, with the profile holding which demands bind and their periods (D99).
3. **The Form 700 source is misgrounded** (needs E1 and H4, §5.1, §5.4 `monitoring`, stage 2). ## Verdict
