## Cross-construct observations
- DEC-4's chain-of-derivation model (each step attributed, dated, can only weaken) is a reusable pattern for any derived fact about a person, event or amount.
- DEC-5 explicitly links document metadata (EVENTS: edits with author and instant) to the entity axis (PEOPLE/ORGS): "'the actions of people and departments' is the ENTITY AXIS (M4, D-71, D-83). A person and an office are entities; 'who edited what, when' is a reading resolved ACROSS documents" (l.268–274). D-71: cross-document resolution is "the single largest piece of manual work in case development".
- DEC-9 progressions (expected stages vs threaded documents, exception discharge) are the existing "what should have happened against what did" machinery for EVENTS, and the sole-source procurement example ties it to MONEY.
- DEC-6: person, contract and fund already sit in the one entities registry, so PEOPLE and MONEY objects start as entity kinds subject to bias declarations.
- DEC-10's ruling makes time-bound expected events (progression stages with due-by) notify the member who connected them to a case; the per-case aggregation means EVENTS findings are routed through QUESTIONS/inquiry structures (Focus/Project).
- DEC-11's two scenarios frame the PEOPLE design tension (document fact vs person pattern via indexing); Bob resolved it toward indexing for public records (DEC-5), consistent with K1452/K1455 later.
- DEC-14's IMPACT vs OUTCOMES split is a ready doctrine for EVENTS causation: events (outcomes) carried at full strength as dated facts; causal links between events (authorises/causes) unproven unless an outside source states them — consistent with DEC-77 (10) cause doctrine in the brief.
