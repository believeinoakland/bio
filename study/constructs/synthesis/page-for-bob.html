The Constructs Study

You asked whether Civicsmith understands time, organisations, law, court cases, analysis and plain-language questions well enough for the work groups will bring to it. Twenty readers, six analysts and three reviewers read the canon, your rulings, the design session's journeys and the code, and checked them against how watchdogs, auditors and courts actually work. This page is the answer, with the decisions that are yours. You have ruled B1: every area goes to its full ladder. Your ruling on B8 added a seventh area, people, carried through every other area below.

The answer

Your sense is right on all six. The product today supports each of these areas only at its lowest levels, and a member can reach even less than is built. No member can ask the assistant a question in plain words, on any copy.

One thread joins them: what a body owes, to whom, by when, under which authority. Organisations supply who owes it, and people supply who held each office and who acted. Law supplies the authority. Time supplies the due date. Courts supply orders and reports that create duties. Analysis measures performance against a bar. Questions read all of it.

The structural finding. Law, local calendars, deadlines and calculation all sit in layer 9, after Publication. So investigation, where the work happens, cannot use any of them. The recommended architecture moves the record's understanding down into layer 5 and leaves acting where it is. A determination still rests on a published finding, and the machine still never concludes.

The substrate comes first. Each stage builds services, not screens, and publishes them so the design work knows what it can do and continues on top of them. Any help from the assistant also waits on one fix: it must run on the group's own Claude account, which the design already allows and the code does not yet.

Your corrections, applied

The assistant runs on a Claude subscription (K1429). You settled this at DEC-55 in August, and Anthropic's own pages confirm it today: the Agent SDK and apps built on it draw on a subscription's usage limits. The first version of this page said the opposite. A reviewer misread the 15 June pause, which only withdrew a planned separate credit, and I did not check the reading against DEC-55. The setup page's wording stands. The fault is in the code: it accepts only an API key, so it gains a subscription path, with an API key kept as an option.

Substrate first (K1430). The stages below are the substrate. A screen is never a precondition of a stage; the UX work continues on what each stage publishes.

Dates that follow from dependencies (K1431). A budget must be in place before its year begins; a report must be out before the meeting that decides on it. A due date's basis can now be a rule, a body's commitment, a dependency (the event it must precede, and why), or the group's own window. A dependency date moves with the event it serves. Missing one is a dated fact about sequence ("the report was posted two hours before the vote"), never a violation unless a rule also applies.

The ladders, rung by rung

How to read a ladder

A ladder measures one area of capability. Each rung is defined by what a member can do. So climbing a rung means a new kind of work a group can finish: not more code, but more of the job done.

Rungs build on each other. L3 assumes L0 to L2. L0 is always "the system holds documents and knows nothing more"; L5 is always the most ambitious form, usually with the assistant doing much of the work. The rungs are specific to each area, so L3 for Time is not comparable to L3 for Law.

Each rung is judged two ways. Built means the code exists and passes its tests. Usable today means a member can actually do it through today's interface. The gap between the two is mostly missing screens and an assistant that is not yet deployed. The gap between what is built and the target is the substrate work this study proposes.

The target is now every rung (B1 (c), your ruling): each area reaches L5, in dependency order, within realistic resources and without slowing everyday use. The stages say which rungs come first.

The obligation, once. Three studies designed the same thing in three places. It becomes one record in one module (duties). Members see your word "obligation" (DEC-107) for a body's duty and "power" for what an office may do. Each obligation names who owes it (an office or body, or a person where a law binds them, such as an official who must file a statement of interests), to whom, what, its source in force (a law, an order, a contract clause or a commitment), and how its due date is computed: from a rule, from the body's own commitment, or from the event it must precede, such as a report needed before a vote. Whether each occurrence was met, late or overdue is worked out when read, never stored. "Overdue" raises a question; it never asserts a violation.

One way to ask "as of a date". Laws in force, who held an office, what a line between bodies was, and a proceeding's status are all read the same way, and each answer says "undetermined" when the dates don't settle it.

B12

What the machine may say about law and rulings

Doctrine · the line on practising law

The question. Members will ask what the law means for them: "Can the City charge this fee?" "Do we still have time to sue?" How far may the assistant go in answering? If it says too little, it is useless for the core work. If it goes too far, it gives legal advice it has no standing to give, and a confident wrong answer could cost a group its case or a deadline. Option (iv) concerns the hardest form of this question: reasoning about the group's own legal position.

  • (i) Closed book: no fact and no rule from the model's own knowledge; where nothing is held it says "not held" and offers the act that would find it.
  • (ii) Explaining: (a) quote only; (b) a labelled, cited reading of held text, quoted beside it, stating its limits and "legal information, not legal advice", never "the law is", a member's rights, a likely outcome or what to file; (c) free explanation.
  • (iii) Procedural facts from the profile (which form, which venue, which deadline) shown as facts, never as recommendations.
  • (iv) New with L5: procedural reasoning for the group's own situation (can we still file, do we have standing, have we exhausted the remedies first). The machine lays out each condition with its source and the facts in the record that bear on it, each marked met, unmet or undetermined as a computed fact (a filing window counted from dates). It never says "you have standing" or what to file, and it points to counsel.

Changed by B1 (c). Courts L5 needs (iv); recommend it on these terms. Rulings stay quote-only until their accuracy is measured: that is a quality gate, not a missing capability.

Recommend (i) yes; (ii)(b) for statutes, ordinances and policies, with rulings quote-only until acceptance is measured; (iii) yes; (iv) yes, on those terms. Legal research tools built on retrieval are still wrong one time in six or more, and New York City's chatbot taught businesses illegal practice. An attorney-supervised mode waits until a group with a lawyer member asks.

B16

The assistant's account: optional, and whose

Policy · UX

The question. The assistant sends each question, and the material it reads to answer it, to Anthropic, under a Claude account that someone pays for. Must every group use the assistant, or does each group choose at setup? And whose account carries the group's use: one account for the group, or each member's own?

Settled: the assistant runs on the group's or a member's own Claude account, a subscription or an API key (DEC-55, K1429). Anthropic's conditions: the account is signed in through Anthropic's own sign-in, and Civicsmith never stores a Claude password or session; Pro and Max limits assume one person's ordinary use, so a group sharing one assistant fits a Team plan or members' own plans.

  • (i) The assistant is optional, offered at setup; the administrator is told then that questions, and the material read to answer them, go to Anthropic under the group's account.
  • (ii) The group's account serves everyone; a project's or member's own account takes precedence where set.

Recommend both. (The daily use ceiling and the administrator's monthly view I have decided: K1450.) On a subscription the cost is the plan's price; the open question is whether a group's use fits its limits, which stage 0 measures.

B17

Doctrine

Your rule DEC-27 lets it only organise what the member has said. Loosening it makes the assistant far more useful.   • (ii) Suggestions: (a) structure only what the member said; (b) labelled suggestions drawn only from material the member brought or chose, each adopted by the member; (c) free suggestions.

B18

A court order to remove or redact a published case

Policy

The question. A court may order a group to take down or redact a case it has published, for example on a defamation claim or a privacy order. A published case is signed and meant to stay fixed. Should the product have a built way to comply with such an order (take the case down or redact it, openly, with a record of why), and should it be built now or only when the first order arrives?

  • (a) Defer the path until the first such order is served on a group, and record the principle now: an order addressed to the group is complied with, never silently; the order is captured, a signed docket entry names it, and the edition is stamped.
  • (b) Design the path now.

Changed by B1 (c). A court order arrives with a deadline and legal stakes: exactly the moment "sorry, I can't do that yet" is least acceptable. The path is small. Now recommend (b): design and build it.

Recommend (b). Material that a court later seals, or unseals, joins the same principle.

B21

Standing questions: the AI on a schedule

Doctrine · new with L5

The question. May the AI run on a schedule, with no member asking at that moment? Your current rule (D13) says it never starts on its own.

Questions L5 is "tell me if the Clerk misses this date" or "tell me when anything new bears on this question". Your provisional rule today is that no AI run starts on its own (D13).

  • (a) Keep D13: standing questions are watched mechanically only (saved searches and the queue), and the AI runs when a member opens the result.
  • (b) A member authors a standing question with a cadence and an end date. A saved search re-runs on the schedule first, which costs almost nothing; the AI runs only when that finds something new, read-only, bounded, within the group's use ceiling, and its answer reaches the queue labelled as the assistant's.
  • (c) Standing AI runs without these limits.

Recommend (b). It gives the capability you want while keeping cost and speed in hand: most scheduled checks never wake the AI. It lifts D13 only for questions a member wrote, and only to read.

B22

Sharing between groups

Policy · new with L5

The question. Once one group has mapped how a body is organised, or entered a city's code as law, may another group reuse that work, and on what terms? Sharing saves real effort. But taking another group's unchecked work as true could carry its errors into your own findings.

Organisations L5 and Law L5 let groups share what they have built: the structure of a body (who reports to whom, who holds what), registers of obligations, and held law.

  • (a) A group shares a pack only by an explicit act, as it publishes a case; a receiving group accepts it by a reasoned act (DEC-96), and the pack is recreated in the receiving record, never installed, with no inherited grade; holders travel only under B8.
  • (b) Automatic sharing within a coalition.
  • (c) No sharing.

Recommend (a). It is how cases already move between groups, so it adds a capability without a new trust model.
